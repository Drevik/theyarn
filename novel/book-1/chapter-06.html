Chapter 6: Research and Revelations

Chapter 6: Research and Revelations

The library smelled of dust and secrets.

Drevik descended the spiral staircase carved into living stone, each step taking him deeper beneath the university campus. The air grew cooler, carrying the scent of aged paper and something else—something that prickled at the back of his neck. Old magic. The kind that seeped into walls over centuries and never quite faded.

The staircase emptied into a chamber that stole his breath.

Shelves stretched upward into shadows that the flickering amber lights couldn't penetrate. Rolling ladders clung to brass rails along the stacks, and leather-bound volumes lined every surface in neat, weathered rows. The space dwarfed any library he'd seen above ground—a cathedral of knowledge hidden beneath the mundane world.

"You're late."

He turned toward the voice. Ivy sat at a reading table near the center of the room, her pale fingers wrapped around a cup of tea that steamed in the cool air. In the dim light, her silver-blonde hair seemed almost luminous, and her gray eyes tracked his approach with an intensity that belied her small frame.

"I had to make sure I wasn't followed," Drevik said, sliding into the chair across from her. "After what happened at the ceremony—"

"I know what happened." Ivy's voice dropped lower. "Everyone knows. The question is whether you understand what it means."

The weight of her gaze settled on him. This wasn't the shy, stammering woman he'd glimpsed during his initiation. In her domain, surrounded by books and the comfortable weight of knowledge, she held herself differently. Straighter. More certain.

"That's why I'm here." He leaned forward, forearms resting on the worn wood of the table. "You said you could help me understand the warning."

"I said I might be able to." She reached beneath the table and produced a leather satchel, worn soft with age. "What I'm about to show you could get us both stripped of our standing. Or worse."

"Ivy—"

"Do you understand what 'worse' means in the Order?" She met his eyes, and he saw fear there—genuine fear. "We don't just lose membership. We lose memories. They have ways of making people forget."

The silence stretched between them.

"I need to know," Drevik said finally. "Whatever the risk."

Ivy studied him for a long moment. Then she nodded, more to herself than to him, and opened the satchel.

The book she withdrew was different from the others he'd seen in the Order's collection. Its cover was plain black leather, unmarked by title or symbol, but the pages inside were thick vellum covered in cramped handwriting. Some entries had been pasted in—clippings from newspapers, photographs, fragments of documents with official seals partially visible.

"This is a compilation," Ivy explained, her voice barely above a whisper. "Records that were supposed to be destroyed but... weren't. I found it hidden in a false panel behind the stacks on restricted texts. Someone wanted this information preserved."

She turned to a page near the middle and slid the book toward him.

Drevik's breath caught.

A photograph had been mounted there, slightly yellowed with age. Two figures stood before a building he recognized—the old chapel on the north end of campus, now used for storage. The man had dark hair and striking green-gold eyes that seemed to look through the camera, through time itself. The woman beside him was smaller, with a determined set to her jaw and hands that gripped a bundle wrapped in blankets.

A baby. They were holding a baby.

"Markinsoul," Ivy said softly. "That's the name they gave when they joined. But before that—" She flipped to another page, a clipped document with portions blacked out. "Before that, they were the Thornes. A family line the Order thought had been eliminated three generations ago."

Drevik's hands trembled as he traced the edge of the photograph. "My parents."

"I thought so." Ivy's voice carried a gentleness he hadn't expected. "The resemblance is unmistakable."

"What happened to them? The official story was an accident—"

"The official story is always an accident." Ivy turned more pages. "But this compilation suggests otherwise. Your parents discovered something about the Order's leadership. Something about connections between certain Elders and the Shadowlands breaches they're supposed to prevent."

The room seemed to contract around him. "They were killed?"

"I can't prove it. The relevant pages were removed before I found this." She gestured to a section where stubs of torn paper jutted from the binding. "But someone wanted you to know the partial truth. Otherwise, this book would have been destroyed entirely."

Drevik sat back, trying to process the flood of information. His grandmother had told him his parents died in a car accident when he was two. Just two young people, gone too soon, leaving their son to be raised by a woman who loved him fiercely but never spoke of what came before.

"Why would someone want me to know?"

"That's the question, isn't it?" Ivy's gray eyes held his. "The Order isn't unified. There are factions, alliances, rivalries that span generations. Someone is positioning you for a purpose. Whether that purpose serves you or serves them..."

She let the sentence hang.

Drevik looked down at the compilation again. His parents' faces stared back at him, frozen in a moment of hope. They'd been young. Younger than he was now, with their whole lives ahead of them. And they'd held him in their arms, wrapped in blankets, unaware of whatever fate was already closing in.

"What did they discover?" he asked.

"I don't know." Ivy's brow furrowed. "But I found something else. Something that concerns you directly."

She reached into the satchel again and produced a folded map. When she spread it across the table, Drevik recognized the campus layout—the buildings, the pathways, the green spaces between. Red marks dotted certain locations, and his patrol route from the assignment ceremony was traced in careful black ink.

"Your assigned patrol." Ivy traced the line with one finger. "It passes through three marked zones."

"Marked how?"

"Active breach points." She looked up at him, and the fear was back in her eyes. "Places where the barrier between our world and the Shadowlands has worn thin. Where entities sometimes slip through."

The temperature in the library seemed to drop several degrees.

"New acolytes don't patrol breach zones," Drevik said slowly. "We're not trained for that. We don't have the skills—"

"Exactly." Ivy's voice was grim. "Whoever made your assignment either made a catastrophic error, or they intended for you to encounter something you couldn't handle."

"Someone wants me dead."

The words fell into the silence between them. Saying them aloud made them real in a way they hadn't been before.

"I can't be certain." Ivy folded the map, her movements precise and careful. "But the pattern is troubling. Your parents were eliminated for what they knew. You're their son, possibly carrying their abilities—abilities the Order finds threatening. And now you've been assigned to a patrol route that would put you in mortal danger before you've completed basic training."

Drevik pushed back from the table and stood, pacing between the shelves. His mind raced through possibilities. Elder Vance had made the assignments personally, pulling names from what he'd claimed was random selection. But nothing in the Order was random. Every ritual, every ceremony, every seemingly arbitrary choice served a purpose.

"Who else knows about this?"

"About your parents? Perhaps a dozen people, most of them Elders." Ivy's gaze followed him as he moved. "About the patrol assignment? Only those who have access to the breach zone maps. Wardens and above."

"That narrows it."

"It narrows it to people who have every reason to protect the Order's secrets." Ivy stood as well, her slight frame seeming smaller against the towering shelves. "Drevik, I'm taking a risk just showing you this. If anyone discovers—"

"Why are you helping me?"

The question stopped her. She wrapped her arms around herself, a gesture that made her look younger, more vulnerable.

"Because I know what it's like to have questions you can't ask." Her voice was quiet. "My mother was an archivist too. She found things she shouldn't have found, asked questions that made people uncomfortable. One day she was sent on a research mission to a remote site, and she never came back."

"Ivy..."

"The official story was a Shadowlands encounter." Her smile was bitter. "But my mother was the most cautious woman I ever knew. She wouldn't have ventured near a breach zone without full preparation and backup. Someone sent her there deliberately. Someone who wanted her silenced."

Drevik stopped pacing. The parallels were too obvious to ignore—mothers killed in supposed accidents, children left with questions no one would answer. Ivy had lost her mother the same way he'd lost his parents, and she'd been living with that knowledge far longer.

"You think the same people are behind both."

"I think the Order has rot in its foundation." Ivy met his eyes steadily. "I think someone has been eliminating members who get too close to certain truths for generations. And I think you've been marked as the next target."

The words settled over him.

"Then why assign me to a dangerous patrol? Why not just... eliminate me quietly?"

"Because your death has to look like an accident. Or a training mishap. Something that won't raise questions." Ivy returned to her chair, lowering herself with a weariness that spoke of years carrying this burden. "A new acolyte dying on patrol would be tragic but not suspicious. Especially if he'd shown unusual ability but hadn't completed his training. The Order would mourn, investigate briefly, and move on."

Drevik resumed his seat across from her. The compilation lay between them, his parents' faces watching from the photograph. They'd been young, hopeful, holding their infant son. They'd had no idea what was coming.

"Who can I trust?"

The question came out rougher than he intended. Ivy's expression softened.

"Trust is dangerous in the Order. Even the people who seem supportive may be serving their own agendas." She hesitated. "But there are others who have reason to question the official narratives. Others who've lost people to 'accidents' that don't add up."

"Like you."

"Like me." She offered a tentative smile. "And perhaps the Vane woman. Seraphina. Her family has been at odds with the current leadership for years. And Lexi Thornwood—"

"Lexi?" Drevik's surprise was genuine. "She's been nothing but hostile since I arrived."

"Her mother is an Elder." Ivy's tone carried a warning. "But Lexi herself has questioned certain decisions. She's not her mother's puppet, whatever appearances might suggest. She may be watching you for her own reasons, not the ones you assume."

Drevik thought of Lexi's sharp green eyes, the way she'd looked at him during the ceremony. He'd interpreted her intensity as suspicion, perhaps hostility. But what if there was more to it?

"The way you describe it, everyone has hidden motives."

"Welcome to the Order." Ivy's smile was wry. "The trick is learning which motives align with your survival."

He looked at the compilation again. So many pages had been torn out, so many truths redacted. But someone had preserved this much—had hidden it where Ivy would find it. Someone wanted him to know about his parents, about the pattern of eliminations, about the danger he was in.

"Who left this book for you to find?"

"I don't know." Ivy's voice dropped to a whisper. "But they're still in the Order. They've been leaving me clues for years, guiding me toward information that's been buried. I think they're building toward something. A reckoning, maybe. And I think you're part of it."

The weight of her words pressed against him. He'd come here looking for answers about a warning during his initiation ceremony. Instead, he'd found a conspiracy that spanned generations, a target on his back, and a mystery that reached into his own bloodline.

"What do I do now?"

Ivy reached across the table. Her fingers brushed against his hand—a light touch, barely there—but he felt the warmth of her skin like a brand. She pulled back quickly, color rising in her cheeks.

"Learn." Her voice was slightly unsteady. "Train. Grow stronger. The Order values power above all else. If you become valuable enough, you become harder to eliminate quietly. And when the time comes..."

"When the time comes?"

"You'll know who to trust." She met his eyes again, and something shifted in her expression. The fear was still there, but beneath it was something else. Determination, maybe. Or something warmer.

"You're taking a risk, helping me," Drevik said quietly. "If they're watching..."

"They watch everyone." Ivy's hand moved toward his again, then retreated. "But the library has... advantages. Certain sections are shielded from surveillance. Old protections, built into the walls before the current leadership took power. As long as we meet here, in the right places, we can speak freely."

"Shielded how?"

"Blood wards. Lay them yourself and they answer to you alone." Ivy gestured to the walls. "The founders of the Order believed knowledge should be protected from those who would suppress it. They built safeguards into the very architecture."

Drevik looked at the shelves with new appreciation. The library wasn't just a repository—it was a fortress. A place where truth could survive even when those in power wanted it buried.

"Can you teach me? The blood wards, the protections?"

Ivy's smile widened. "That's why I brought you here." She stood, moving toward the shelves with a confidence that transformed her bearing. "Follow me. There's something else you need to see."

He followed her deeper into the stacks, past sections labeled in languages he didn't recognize. The light grew dimmer as they walked, the amber lamps giving way to shadows that seemed to breathe with ancient power. Finally, she stopped before a shelf that looked no different from any other.

"This section is warded. Nothing that happens here can be observed from outside." She pressed her palm to a symbol carved into the wood—a crescent moon wrapped in thorns. "When you're ready, you can claim your own space. Create your own sanctuary."

The symbol pulsed faintly beneath her hand, and Drevik felt the brush of magic against his senses. Old, deep, patient.

"How?"

"Blood and intent." Ivy withdrew a small knife from her pocket—the blade no longer than her finger. "A drop is enough. You press it to the ward and speak your purpose. The magic accepts you or it doesn't."

"And if it doesn't accept me?"

"Then you're not meant to have a sanctuary here." Her gray eyes held his. "But I think you will be. The wards know bloodlines. They remember those who came before."

His parents. The thought struck him. Had they stood where he stood? Had they pressed their palms to symbols and spoken their purpose?

Ivy offered him the knife, handle first. Her fingers brushed his as he took it—a lingering contact this time, deliberate despite the flush that crept up her neck.

"Try it."

Drevik pressed the blade to his palm. The sting was brief, a single drop of blood welling against his skin. He reached for the symbol beside Ivy's and pressed his palm flat against the wood.

"I claim this space for knowledge and protection," he said, the words coming without thought. "For truth that cannot be silenced."

The wood warmed beneath his hand. The symbol began to glow—faintly at first, then brighter, a deep amber that matched the library's lamps. Power rushed through him, settling into his bones with the weight of centuries.

"Interesting," Ivy breathed. "The wards recognize you. That's... rare."

"What does it mean?"

"It means this library knows your blood." She was staring at him with something like wonder. "It means you belong here."

Drevik withdrew his hand. The cut had already healed, leaving no mark—but he could feel the connection now. A thread of power linking him to the walls, the shelves, the accumulated knowledge of generations.

"Thank you." He turned to Ivy, seeing her properly for the first time. Not just a shy archivist, but a woman who'd risked everything to bring him here. A woman who understood loss and questions and the weight of forbidden truth.

Her cheeks colored under his attention. "I— there's more I need to tell you. But not here." She glanced toward the shadows between the shelves. "Someone's watching. Not the wards—someone physical. I've noticed the signs for weeks. We need to be careful about when we meet and where."

"Then how do I find you?"

"I'll send a message through the usual channels. A book request with a specific call number." Her smile was fleeting. "Archivists communicate in our own language. No one else will understand."

"Ivy." He caught her arm as she turned away. Her skin was warm through the thin fabric of her sleeve. "Why are you doing this? Really?"

She looked at his hand on her arm, then up at his face. For a moment, her mask slipped entirely, and he saw the loneliness beneath—the years of carrying her mother's death in silence, the fear that had become her constant companion, the hope that flickered despite everything.

"Because you're the first person who might actually do something about it." Her voice was barely audible. "Because when I saw you at your initiation, I recognized something. You're not just here by accident. You're meant for something. And I want to be part of it."

She pulled away gently, but her eyes stayed on his a moment longer.

"Be careful, Drevik. The patrol assignment can be changed if you petition the right people. Find an excuse—illness, injury, family emergency. Buy yourself time to train. To grow stronger." Her voice steadied. "And watch the women who watch you. Not all attention is hostile. Some of us are hoping you succeed."

Then she was gone, disappearing between the shelves with a silence that spoke of years learning to move unnoticed.

Drevik stood alone in the warded section, the compilation tucked under his arm, the weight of revelation settling onto his shoulders. His parents had been killed for what they knew. Someone in the Order wanted him dead. And a shy archivist with gray eyes and a backbone of steel had just become his most unlikely ally.

He looked down at the photograph again. His mother's jaw, determined even in a moment of joy. His father's eyes, green-gold and piercing, seeing futures perhaps even then.

What did you find? he asked the silent faces. What truth was worth dying for?

The library offered no answers. But somewhere in its depths, hidden behind wards and blood and centuries of accumulated knowledge, the truth waited.

And Drevik Markinsoul intended to find it.

The journey back to the surface felt longer than the descent. Drevik climbed the spiral staircase in a daze, his mind turning over everything Ivy had revealed. His parents murdered. His patrol assignment sabotaged. A conspiracy spanning generations.

The Order of the Eclipsed Veil had seemed like an answer to questions he'd carried his whole life—a place where he might belong, where his strange abilities would be understood and valued. Instead, it had become something far more dangerous. A nest of vipers, each one coiled and waiting to strike.

He emerged into the night air, the campus spread before him under a sky thick with stars. The moon was waning, a thin crescent that cast weak silver light across the buildings and pathways. Somewhere in those buildings, students were studying, partying, living their mundane lives with no idea of what lurked beneath their feet.

How many of them were like him? Carrying abilities they didn't understand, drawn to secrets they couldn't name? How many would the Order recruit, use, and discard?

The questions followed him across the quad. He kept to the shadows, practicing the stealth the Order had begun teaching him, testing whether anyone was following. Twice he caught movement in his peripheral vision—other students, most likely—but he couldn't shake the feeling of eyes on his back.

When he finally reached his dorm room, he collapsed onto his bed without bothering to turn on the light. The compilation lay beside him, his parents' faces visible in the glow from the window.

His phone buzzed. A text from a number he didn't recognize.

You were in the library a long time. Careful. Curiosity killed more than cats.

He stared at the message, heart pounding. Someone had seen him enter. Someone had tracked how long he'd stayed. The Order's reach extended further than he'd imagined.

His thumb hovered over the screen, but there was no point responding. Whoever had sent this knew where he'd been, knew what he might have learned. The warning was clear: we're watching.

A second message arrived.

But curiosity also reveals truth. The right questions in the right ears. You're not alone in this.

Drevik read the words twice. The first message had been a threat. The second... something else. An offer? A promise?

He typed a reply: Who is this?

No answer came. He waited, phone glowing in the darkness, but the screen stayed silent.

Finally, he set the phone aside and closed his eyes. Sleep would be impossible now, but he needed to rest, needed to think. Tomorrow he would petition to change his patrol assignment. He would find an excuse, buy himself time. And he would watch the women around him—Lexi's hostility, Seraphina's intensity, Ivy's quiet courage—searching for the truth beneath the surface.

I'm meant for something, Ivy had said. And I want to be part of it.

The Order had brought him here for a reason. His parents had died for a reason. And somewhere in the web of secrets and lies, the truth waited to be uncovered.

Drevik Markinsoul was done being a pawn.

It was time to learn the game.
