Chapter 20: Through the Veil

Chapter 20: Through the Veil

The breach screamed as Drevik stepped through it.

Not a sound—not exactly. More like the feeling of a scream pressed against his skin, cold and wrong and hungry. The mortal world fell away behind him in a rush of displaced air and Lexi's voice calling his name, and then he was elsewhere.

The Shadowlands stretched before him in eternal twilight.

Drevik's knees buckled. He caught himself on one hand, fingers sinking into ground that wasn't ground—something between ash and velvet, gray-black and faintly luminescent. The air tasted of copper and ozone and something older, something that made his hindbrain scream at him to run.

He didn't run. He'd known what he was walking into.

Mostly.

Above him, the sky was a bruise. No sun, no moon, no stars—just endless gradients of purple and gray that shifted like something breathing. The landscape rolled away in impossible geometries, hills that curved the wrong way, shadows that fell toward the light instead of away from it. In the distance, structures that might have been buildings or might have been bones rose from the obsidian plain, their shapes suggesting architecture that human eyes weren't meant to understand.

Beautiful, some part of him thought. In the way a knife is beautiful before it opens your throat.

He pushed himself upright. Behind him, the breach hung in the air like a wound—a ragged oval of light that shouldn't exist here, spilling mortal-realm illumination into the eternal dusk. Through it, he could see the ritual chamber distorted as if through frosted glass. Lexi's face, her mouth moving. The others around her, their expressions ranging from terror to determination.

But their voices were gone. The breach was a window, not a door—not anymore. He'd known that too. To close it properly, he had to push from this side while they pulled from theirs.

Work fast, he told himself. Time moves differently here.

The entity found him before he took his third step.

It didn't approach. It arrived—one moment the space before him was empty, and the next it wasn't. The creature existed in the spaces between seeing, a presence that his eyes kept trying to slide past. It was darkness given form, hunger given purpose, and when it turned what might have been its attention toward him, Drevik felt his shadow magic writhe in response.

"Little thing." The voice came from everywhere and nowhere, vibrating through the ground and the air and his bones. "You carry the taint of the Veiled Ones."

Drevik's foresight activated without his permission—a cascade of possible futures flickering through his mind. In most of them, he died in the next thirty seconds. In some, he died more slowly. In a precious few—

He grabbed onto those few and held.

"I carry the weight of the Order," he said, surprised at how steady his voice sounded. "And you're going back where you belong."

The entity laughed, and the sound was thunder without clouds, lightning without rain. "I was summoned, little thing. Called by one of your own. I belong where I am wished."

"Corvin didn't know what he was calling." Drevik shifted his stance, feeling the Shadowlands' strange physics resist his movements. Everything here was heavier, slower, as if the realm itself wanted to drag him down and keep him. "He thought he was summoning power he could control."

"He summoned hunger." The entity drifted closer—or the darkness around Drevik deepened; he couldn't tell the difference. "I have fed on your kind before. The ones who call themselves Veiled. They taste of ambition and fear."

Drevik's foresight pulsed. Now, it whispered. Duck left.

He ducked left.

A tendril of absolute darkness sliced through the space where his head had been, and the air itself seemed to scream. Where the tendril touched the ground, reality peeled, revealing layers of nothing beneath nothing.

"Interesting." The entity's attention sharpened—Drevik felt it like a blade against his throat. "You see the paths."

"I see enough."

He didn't see enough. That was the problem. His foresight showed him possibilities, but the Shadowlands bent probability in ways his mortal mind couldn't fully process. Every future he glimpsed had ten more branching from it, and half of them ended with his death in the next instant.

But some of them didn't. Some of them—

Lexi, he thought, and the image steadied him. Her face in the moment before he'd stepped through the breach. The fear she'd tried to hide. The pride she hadn't bothered to conceal.

Go, she'd said, her hand on his chest, over his heart. And come back.

In the mortal realm, Lexi stood before the breach with her arms spread and her eyes blazing.

"You can't close it." Elder Corvin—the real Corvin, the one who'd been freed from his brother's control when the ritual circle shattered—shook his head. "The energy required—"

"I'm not closing it." Lexi's voice cut through the chaos like her blade cut through shadow. The ritual chamber was in ruins, the floor cracked, the walls weeping something that looked like blood but wasn't. The other acolytes huddled near the entrance, too injured or too afraid to approach. "I'm keeping it open."

"The breach is destabilizing." Seraphina Vane moved to Lexi's side, her silver hair wild, her hands still glowing with the remnants of binding magic. "Even if we could hold it, the Shadowlands will—"

"I don't care what the Shadowlands will do." Lexi didn't look away from the oval of wrong light. Through it, she could see shapes moving—Drevik's silhouette against the twilight, and something larger, something darker. "He's coming back. I won't let you close it before he does."

"Lexi." Corvin's voice softened. "Time moves differently there. Hours could pass before—"

"Then we wait hours."

"The breach won't hold that long."

"Then we make it hold." She turned to Seraphina. "You're a Vane. Your family has been binding things for centuries. Bind this."

Seraphina's lips curved—not quite a smile, but close. "You're asking me to anchor a hole in reality."

"I'm asking you to help me save him."

Something passed between the two women—acknowledgment, perhaps. Or challenge.

"Get the others," Seraphina said finally. "Anyone who can still channel. We'll need a circle."

Lexi moved without hesitation, grabbing acolytes by the arm, pulling them into position. Some were crying. Some were bleeding. All of them looked at the breach with naked terror.

But they moved when Lexi told them to move. They took their places in the circle. And when Seraphina began to chant, their voices rose with hers.

In the Shadowlands, Drevik was dying.

Not quickly—the entity seemed to prefer playing with its food—but inevitably. Each attack he dodged cost him something. Energy. Will. The ability to see the paths ahead clearly.

"You cannot win." The entity circled him now, a darkness that moved against the wind. "Even your foresight cannot show you every possibility. Eventually, you will miss one."

"Eventually." Drevik's breath came hard, his body aching from the constant evasion. "But not yet."

He needed to end this. He needed—

His foresight flickered, and he saw it.

Not a weakness exactly. But a connection. The entity had been summoned—called through a breach by Corvin's brother, anchored to the mortal realm by blood and will. That anchor was gone now, shattered when the ritual circle broke. But the entity had fed on the energy of the summoning, and that feeding had left a trace.

A trail.

Drevik could see it—a thin line of wrongness leading from the entity back toward the mortal realm, toward the breach. The creature was still connected to the summoning site, still tied to the place where it had been called.

That's how you send it back, his foresight whispered. Not by fighting it. By following the thread.

He didn't hesitate. He stopped dodging.

The entity's next attack caught him across the chest—not a direct hit, but close enough. Pain bloomed through him, cold and wrong, and he felt something tear inside that shouldn't tear. But his hands closed around the thread of connection, and he pulled.

The entity screamed.

It was worse than the breach's scream—worse than anything Drevik had ever heard. It was the sound of something being forced somewhere it didn't want to go, and the Shadowlands themselves seemed to recoil from it. The ground cracked beneath his feet. The sky above him tore.

But he kept pulling.

"NO." The entity's voice had lost its thunder. It was desperate now, a thing that had been hungry for so long and now faced starvation. "I will take you with me. I will—"

Drevik pulled harder.

His foresight showed him what would happen next. The entity would be drawn back through the breach—if he could reach the breach himself in time. The closing would happen from both sides at once, and he needed to be in the threshold when it did. Not on this side. Not on that side.

Between.

He ran.

"Something's happening." Lexi pressed her hand against the breach, ignoring the way the edges bit into her palm. "He's—"

The breach pulsed.

Light and darkness spiraled through the oval, and the entity's scream echoed through the ritual chamber. The acolytes in the circle cried out, their connection to the magic wavering.

"Hold!" Seraphina's voice cracked like a whip. "Whatever comes through, you hold!"

Lexi didn't look back. She couldn't. All she could see was the breach, and through it—

Drevik.

He was running toward her, his face set in grim determination, his chest dark with something that might have been blood but moved wrong. Behind him, the entity writhed, fighting against a force it couldn't see.

"Get back!" Drevik's voice, thin and distant, coming through the breach like sound through water. "It's going to—"

The breach contracted.

"No!" Lexi threw herself forward, as if she could hold it open with her bare hands. "Drevik—"

He jumped.

Time stretched. Lexi watched him sail through the air, the twilight of the Shadowlands behind him, the entity reaching for him with tendrils of absolute darkness. He was going to make it. He was going to—

A tendril caught his ankle.

"NO—" Lexi's scream tore from her throat.

Drevik twisted in midair, his hand closing around something at his belt—a knife, she realized, one of the ritual blades they'd brought to the chamber. He drove it down into the tendril, into himself, into the connection between him and the entity.

And he pulled.

The entity shrieked. The breach screamed. And Drevik came through.

He hit the ground hard, rolling, and the breach slammed shut behind him like a door being kicked. The last thing Lexi saw through it was the entity's form dissolving, pulled apart by the closing, its hunger finally silenced.

Then there was silence.

Lexi dropped to her knees beside him.

"Drevik. Drevik—"

His eyes were closed. His chest—she couldn't look at his chest. The wound was too deep, too wrong, still leaking something that moved on its own.

"Medical," she heard someone say. Her voice, maybe. Or Seraphina's. "We need—"

Drevik's eyes opened.

Green-gold, they caught the light of the ruined ritual chamber, and for a moment Lexi saw something else in them. A flicker of twilight. A shadow that moved against the light.

Then it was gone.

"Hey," he said. His voice was a ruin.

"You absolute idiot." Lexi's hands hovered over him, afraid to touch, afraid not to. "You could have died. You should have died. What were you thinking?"

"Thinking..." Drevik coughed, and the sound was wet and wrong. "Thinking I'd come back."

"You came back." Lexi's vision blurred. She blinked furiously, refusing to cry. "You came back, you bastard. You actually came back."

His hand found hers. His fingers were cold, but they curled around hers with familiar strength.

"Told you," he said. "Always."

She laughed—or sobbed, she couldn't tell the difference anymore—and pressed her forehead to his shoulder. He smelled of copper and ash and something else, something she didn't recognize. Something that made her shadow magic stir in response.

Later, she told herself. We'll figure out what that means later.

Right now, he was alive. He was here. He'd come back.

That was enough.

Drevik woke in the infirmary three days later.

The wound on his chest had healed faster than it should have—another mystery to add to the growing list. The healers had done their work, but they'd looked at him strangely afterward, exchanging glances he wasn't supposed to see.

He didn't tell them about the dreams.

He'd been dreaming of the Shadowlands every night since he came back. Not nightmares exactly—more like memories. The twilight sky. The alien geometry of the landscape. The entity's voice, still echoing somewhere in the back of his mind.

You carry the taint of the Veiled Ones, it had said. And now Drevik carried something else too.

He could feel it. A piece of the Shadowlands, lodged somewhere between his heart and his soul. It didn't hurt. It didn't feel wrong, exactly. It felt like a door that had been left open, letting in a draft from somewhere else.

Later, he told himself. Figure it out later.

Lexi was sitting beside his bed when he opened his eyes.

"You're awake." She didn't sound surprised—she'd been here every time he'd woken, he was told. She'd barely left the infirmary since they'd brought him in. "How do you feel?"

"Like I fought a shadow entity and won." He tried to sit up, winced, and settled for propping himself on his elbows. "How long?"

"Three days." Her hand found his, the touch almost casual. "The Order is... impressed."

"The Order can—" He stopped himself. "What happened to Corvin's brother?"

"Dead." Lexi's expression flickered. "The ritual backlash when the breach closed. There wasn't enough left to bury."

Drevik absorbed that. He hadn't wanted the man dead—but he couldn't exactly mourn either. "And Corvin?"

"Recovering. He's been cleared of wrongdoing—his brother was controlling him through blood magic." She paused. "He's been asking about you. He wants to thank you."

"Later." Drevik's eyes found hers. "Right now, I want to know why you're here."

Lexi's jaw tightened. "You almost died."

"I know."

"You almost died, and the last thing I said to you before you went through that breach was go. Not—"

"Lexi."

She stopped. Her eyes were too bright, her expression too controlled. He'd never seen her like this—unmade by something as simple as his survival.

"Come here," he said softly.

She hesitated. Then she leaned down and pressed her lips to his.

The kiss was gentle at first—tentative, as if she was afraid he'd break. But Drevik's hand came up to cup the back of her head, his fingers tangling in her hair, and the kiss deepened. She tasted like coffee and exhaustion and something uniquely her, and he drank her in like water after a drought.

When they finally broke apart, her forehead rested against his.

"I thought I'd lost you," she whispered. "When you went through that breach, I thought—"

"You didn't lose me." His thumb stroked her cheek. "I told you I'd come back."

"You almost didn't."

"But I did." He kissed her again, softer this time. "I came back for you."

She pulled back to look at him, her green eyes searching his face for something. Whatever she found made her expression shift—relief and wonder and something deeper, something she wasn't ready to name.

"You're an idiot," she said, but she was smiling. "A brave, stupid, wonderful idiot."

"I love you too."

The words slipped out before he could stop them. He hadn't meant to say it—not yet, not like this, not while he was lying in an infirmary bed with a wound that shouldn't have healed and dreams that weren't entirely his own.

But Lexi didn't flinch. Didn't pull away.

Instead, she kissed him again, and this time there was nothing gentle about it. This was a claiming—a promise made with lips and teeth and tongue. When she finally drew back, her breathing was ragged.

"Say it again," she demanded. "When you're not high on healing potions."

"I'll say it every day," he promised. "If you'll let me."

She laughed—a real laugh, bright and surprised. "You're going to be trouble, aren't you? More than you've already been."

"Probably."

"Good." She settled into the chair beside his bed, her hand still holding his. "I like trouble."

The ceremony was held a week later.

Drevik stood in the Order's central chamber, surrounded by faces that had become familiar over the past months. Elder Corvin presided, his voice steady despite the shadows under his eyes.

"Drevik Markinsoul," the Elder intoned. "You entered the Shadowlands and returned. You faced an entity of hunger and darkness and prevailed. You have proven yourself worthy of advancement."

The words were ritual, but Drevik felt their weight. He'd earned this—not just the rank, but the right to stand here. The right to call himself something more than an acolyte.

"By the authority vested in this Order, I name you Seeker. May you walk in shadow and emerge in light."

The title settled over him like a mantle. Seeker—the rank Lexi had held when they'd first met. The rank that meant he was trusted to operate alone, to make decisions in the field, to represent the Order in matters that mattered.

Lexi caught his eye across the chamber and smiled. She was a Seeker still, though she'd been offered advancement to Warden. She'd refused it, she'd told him later. She wanted to stay where she was a little longer.

With you, her smile said. Wherever you go.

The ceremony continued—formal words, formal gestures. But Drevik barely heard them. His attention was on the shadows at the edge of his vision, the way they seemed to lean toward him now, as if recognizing him as one of their own.

The Shadowlands left something in you, his foresight whispered. A gift. Or a curse. You'll find out which soon enough.

He pushed the thought away. Whatever had changed in him could wait. Right now, he had a celebration to attend, a woman to hold, and a future that—for the first time in his life—felt like something he wanted rather than something he feared.

Later, there would be questions. The entity had spoken of his parents, of things he didn't understand. The Order had secrets upon secrets, and he'd barely scratched the surface. The Shadowlands had marked him, and eventually, he'd have to discover what that meant.

But that was later.

Tonight, he was alive. He was loved. He was home.

And that was enough.

The dream came on the first night of the new moon.

Drevik stood in the Shadowlands, but they were different now. The twilight sky swirled with colors he'd never seen before, and the ground beneath his feet hummed with power that felt almost familiar.

You carry our mark now, something whispered from the darkness. You are ours.

He woke with a gasp, his hand pressed against his chest. The wound was completely healed—but beneath his fingers, he could feel it. A pulse of shadow, beating in time with his heart.

In the bed beside him, Lexi stirred.

"Drevik?" Her voice was thick with sleep. "What's wrong?"

He looked at her—really looked, letting his new Sight trace the patterns of shadow and light that surrounded her. She blazed with it, her bond to him a golden thread that connected them heart to heart.

"Nothing," he said, and meant it. "Just a dream."

She made a skeptical sound but settled back against his shoulder. Her hand found his chest, over the place where the shadow-pulse beat.

"Your heart's racing," she murmured.

"I know." He pressed a kiss to her hair. "Go back to sleep."

She did, her breathing evening out within minutes. Drevik stayed awake, watching the shadows dance across the ceiling.

What are you? he asked the darkness inside him.

It didn't answer—not in words. But he felt it stir, felt it settle deeper into his bones, and knew that whatever it was, it wasn't going anywhere.

Later, he told himself again. We'll figure it out later.

But even as he thought it, he knew there would be no later. The Shadowlands had marked him, and sooner or later, he'd have to discover what that meant.

For now, though, he had Lexi's warmth beside him and the Order's protection around him and a future that stretched before him full of possibility.

He closed his eyes.

And let the darkness carry him into sleep.
