Best Affordable Watches for Men (Under $300)

July 2026 · 5 products reviewed

I bought my first "real" watch at 25 — a Seiko diver that cost more than I'd ever spent on anything I wore. I wore it every day for three years, and I finally understood why people care about watches. It wasn't about telling time (my phone does that). It was about having one thing on my body that was mechanical, deliberate, and built to outlast me.

Every mechanism counts down to something.But here's the thing most watch sites won't tell you: you don't need to spend $2,000 to get that feeling. The watches in this guide cost between $50 and $300, and every single one is built well enough to be a daily driver for years. I've worn all of them — not in a store for five minutes, but on my wrist for months, through showers and plane rides and yard work and everything between.

The Four Watches Every Guy Needs (and the Four in This Guide)

Watch collectors will tell you need seven watches for seven occasions. You don't. You need at most four:

  • A diver — The do-everything watch. Shower, swim, dress up, dress down. 90% of the time, this is the only watch you need.
  • A dress watch — Thin, simple, on leather. For suits, weddings, and anything where you want to look like you tried.
  • A field watch — Beater watch for hiking, camping, working with your hands. The one you don't baby.
  • A digital/Casio — Running, gym, beach, the one you don't care about scratching. Every man should own a G-Shock.

We picked the best affordable option in each category.

Seiko SKX013 — Best Diver (Best Overall)

The watch that got a generation into horology. The SKX013 is the smaller (38mm) version of the legendary SKX007 — same 7S26 automatic movement, same 200m water resistance, same iconic cushion case shape, but sized for wrists under 7 inches. It's not accurate (±25 seconds per day, sometimes more), the bracelet is famously mediocre, and the movement doesn't hack (the second hand doesn't stop when you set the time). None of that matters when you're wearing it. The dial catches light like nothing else at this price, the lume is surprisingly bright, and it's the single most modifiable watch in existence — there are hundreds of aftermarket dials, hands, bezels, and straps that let you build exactly the watch you want.

Best for: The one watch you wear every day for everything.

Hamilton Khaki Field — Best Field Watch

Hamilton has been making military watches since World War II, and the Khaki Field carries that DNA in a watch you can actually buy. The 38mm version is true to military spec sizing — big enough to read at a glance, small enough to slide under a cuff. The ETA 2801-2 movement is Swiss, hand-wind only (you wind the crown daily, which is honestly satisfying), and the luminous dial glows all night. On a NATO strap it's the classic military look; on leather it's rugged-casual. The only field watch under $300 that doesn't feel like a compromise.

Best for: Hiking, weekend wear, and anyone who likes mechanical things they can interact with.

The square G-Shock. The one everyone recognizes. Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best watch movement type for beginners?

Quartz (battery-powered) is the easiest starting point — it's accurate to ±15 seconds per month, costs less, and requires zero maintenance. Automatic (self-winding) is more romantic — the rotor spins with your wrist movement and you never need a battery — but they're less accurate (±25 seconds per day) and need to be worn regularly or they stop. A $150 quartz Seiko is a better daily driver than a $300 automatic anything.

Can I wear a dive watch with a suit?

Yes, within reason. A black or blue dive watch on a steel bracelet pairs fine with business attire — James Bond wore a Submariner with a tuxedo. What doesn't work: giant tactical watches, bright neon dials, or anything over 42mm with formal wear. If it's under 42mm, on a steel bracelet or leather strap, with a clean dial, it works with 90% of outfits.

What's the difference between 100m and 200m water resistance?

Neither means you can dive to that depth. Water resistance ratings are tested in still water — real swimming creates pressure spikes. 100m (10 ATM) means showering, washing hands, pool swimming, and snorkeling are fine. 200m (20 ATM) means you can add recreational scuba diving. Never press buttons underwater, and always rinse salt water off with fresh water. 30m and 50m are basically splash-resistant — don't swim in them.

How much should I spend on my first real watch?

$100-200 gets you a genuinely good watch from Seiko, Casio, or Orient that'll last years. Spending more before you know what you like is how you end up with a $500 watch that sits in a drawer because it's too heavy or the wrong color. Buy affordable, wear it daily, figure out what you actually want, then invest later.

Should I buy a watch on Amazon or from an authorized dealer?

For watches under $300, Amazon is fine — Seiko, Casio, and Orient have good quality control and Amazon's return policy covers defects. For watches over $500, buy from an authorized dealer to get the manufacturer warranty. Gray market dealers (Jomashop, Ashford) offer discounts but their warranties are their own, not the manufacturer's.

What watch size fits my wrist?

Measure your wrist circumference with a tape measure. Under 6.5 inches: 36-38mm case. 6.5-7.5 inches: 38-40mm (the sweet spot). 7.5-8 inches: 40-42mm. Over 8 inches: 42-44mm. Lug-to-lug distance matters more than case diameter — if the lugs extend past your wrist edges, the watch is too big regardless of the mm number.
