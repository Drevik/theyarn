Best Electric Pressure Cooker Alternatives to the Instant Pot

Published: July 20, 26 | Products Reviewed: 4

The Instant Pot franchise dominates the modern kitchen counter for a reason. It’s incredibly versatile—it can pressure cook, slow cook, sauté, and steam all in one unit. But if you already own an IP, or if its price point is out of budget, you don't have to buy another one. There are genuinely excellent alternatives designed to hit different needs: some focus purely on deep slow cooking, others provide the necessary pressure with a lower entry cost. This guide tests the best gadgets that serve as viable replacements without sacrificing kitchen performance.

We focused our testing not just on which unit was 'best,' but which one was *the right* fit for your specific cooking style, whether that's bone broth slow-cooking or quick weeknight meals.

The Best Budget Multi-Cookers (Low Cost Entry)

If the $90+ price tag of the Instant Pot is too much, you don't need to compromise on functionality. Several brands offer dedicated electric multi-cookers that capture about 80% of the IP’s core functions for a fraction of the cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need a pressure cooker if I have an air fryer?

Not necessarily. An air fryer excels at dry, crispy heat (roasting, dehydrating). A pressure cooker is designed to rapidly cook liquids under high pressure, which creates tender results in minutes that would take hours otherwise.

Are electric multi-cookers safe for glass cookware?

Most quality brands include safety measures and recommend specific pot materials. Always follow the manufacturer's guidelines, particularly regarding maximum temperature and liquid levels.

How do I choose a capacity (quart size)?

For two people, 3 to 4 quarts is fine. For a family of four or more, stick to at least a 6-7 quart model to avoid cooking in multiple batches.
