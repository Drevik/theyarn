Chapter 9: Unquiet Hours

Chapter 9: Unquiet Hours

The ceiling of Drevik's dorm room had become a map of his own restlessness.

Two hours past midnight. Three hours until the mission briefing. Six hours until they crossed into the Shadowlands proper, and he still couldn't silence the noise in his head. Shadows pooled in the corners of his room—deeper than natural darkness, alive with the ambient magic that had seeped into his bones over months of training. They whispered in frequencies just below hearing, and for once, he welcomed the distraction.

His phone buzzed against his chest.

Can't sleep either?

Lexi.

He typed back without lifting the device: Shouldn't you be resting?

Her response came immediately. Shouldn't you?

He smiled despite himself, despite everything. Despite the weight of what tomorrow promised and the things he'd been running from since Lexi had kissed him in the training hall three days ago. Since Mira had pressed him against the library shelves and demanded he stop hiding. Since Ivy had looked at him with those knowing eyes and told him she understood—whatever he needed to figure out.

Since he'd realized that running wasn't working anymore.

His phone buzzed again.

Common room. Ten minutes. Don't make me come up there.

That sounded like a threat.

It was.

Drevik set the phone aside and stared at the ceiling one more moment. The shadows rippled in response to his mood—agitated, uncertain. He needed to center himself before the mission, needed clarity. Instead, he was drowning in women who made him feel things he'd spent years teaching himself not to feel.

Belonging was dangerous. He'd learned that young. But the Order had a way of dismantling every lesson his grandmother had taught him about emotional self-preservation.

He pulled on a black henley and jeans, stepped into the hallway, and let the shadows carry him down three flights of stairs to the common room in the compound's residential wing. The space was typically warm—leather couches, fire pit that burned with enchanted flame, bookshelves lined with Order histories. Tonight, it felt like walking into a trap.

Lexi sat on the central couch, her auburn hair loose around her shoulders. Mira occupied the armchair to the left, her dark curls tied back, her expression sharp even in the low light. Ivy perched on the windowsill, pale and watchful, moonlight catching the silver threads in her white-blonde hair.

Three women. One man who had no idea how he'd gotten here.

"You're late," Lexi said.

"Two minutes," he countered. "And I didn't agree to anything."

"No," Mira agreed, her voice carrying that familiar edge. "You didn't agree to anything. That's rather the point."

Drevik remained standing, arms crossed. The shadows clustered near his feet like anxious pets. "I'm not doing this tonight. We have a mission in six hours—"

"Which is exactly why we're doing this tonight," Lexi interrupted, rising from the couch. The fire pit painted her features in shifting gold and amber. "In case any of us don't come back."

The words landed like stones dropped into still water. Drevik felt the ripples spread through his chest.

"That's dramatic," he said quietly. "Even for you."

"The Shadowlands don't care about dramatic." Lexi moved toward him, each step deliberate. "You've read the briefing. You know what we're walking into. And you've spent the last three days avoiding every single one of us."

"I've been preparing—"

"You've been hiding." Mira stood from the armchair, her movements fluid and dangerous. "You've been retreating into duty because you don't want to face what's happening between us."

Drevik's jaw tightened. "There is no 'us.' There's a mission, and there are complications I don't need right before—"

"Stop."

Ivy's voice cut through the tension—soft, but carrying an authority that silenced everyone. She hadn't moved from the windowsill, but her pale eyes were fixed on Drevik with unsettling intensity.

"Stop pretending you're the only one struggling," Ivy continued. "Stop acting as though your confusion gives you the right to dismiss our feelings."

Drevik turned to face her fully. Of the three, Ivy was the quietest—the least likely to push, the most likely to retreat. If she was confronting him now, something had shifted.

"I'm not dismissing anything," he said. "I'm trying to keep focus on what matters."

"What matters." Ivy tilted her head, her expression unreadable. "You mean the Order? The mission? The heroic acolyte who doesn't need emotional entanglements?"

"I didn't say that—"

"You didn't have to." Ivy rose from the windowsill, her movements graceful and silent. "You think we haven't noticed? You think Lexi hasn't been pacing her room every night since the training hall? You think Mira hasn't been snapping at everyone who crosses her path? You think I haven't been—" She stopped, drawing a breath. "We're all waiting for you to decide. And you won't even acknowledge we're waiting."

The silence that followed was thick enough to cut.

Drevik looked between them—Lexi with her fierce defiance, Mira with her sharp challenge, Ivy with her quiet devastation. Three women who had, impossibly, inexplicably, set their sights on him. Three women he couldn't stop thinking about, even when he tried.

"I don't know what you want me to say," he finally managed.

Lexi made a sound of frustration and stepped forward, closing the distance between them until she stood directly in front of him. Her green eyes held firelight and fury and something deeper—something that made his chest tight.

"Say you feel it," she said. "Say you've been thinking about that kiss since it happened. Say you haven't been able to focus because every time you close your eyes, you see me. Or Mira. Or Ivy. Say you're just as tangled up in this as we are."

Her voice dropped on the last words, vulnerability cracking through the demand.

Drevik looked down at her—this woman who had been his rival, his mentor, his fiercest defender. Lexi, who had trained him when no one else would take him seriously. Lexi, who had believed in him before he'd believed in himself.

"I've been thinking about that kiss," he admitted, his voice rough. "Every night. Every time I try to meditate. Every time the shadows get too quiet."

Lexi's breath caught. The anger in her expression flickered, replaced by something rawer.

"Then why—"

"Because I can't afford to want this." The words came harder now, torn from somewhere deep. "Because the mission tomorrow isn't routine. Because the Shadowlands don't forgive mistakes, and I've already lost people I—"

He stopped, but the damage was done. The name hung unspoken in the air. His grandmother. His parents. Everyone who had ever mattered.

Lexi's expression softened. She reached up, her hand settling against his jaw, and he let himself lean into the touch despite every instinct screaming at him to pull away.

"You think caring makes you weak," she said quietly. "You think loving people gives the universe leverage to hurt you."

"Don't—"

"It's not weakness." Her thumb traced his cheekbone. "It's the only strength that matters. And if we're walking into the Shadowlands tomorrow, I refuse to leave things unsaid."

Drevik's control cracked. He'd been holding himself together through weeks of training, months of adjustment, years of careful emotional distance. Lexi's touch, her words, the fierce determination in her eyes—it was too much.

His hand came up to cup the back of her neck, and he pulled her to him.

The kiss was desperate and angry and relieved all at once. Lexi responded instantly, her fingers sliding into his hair, her body pressing against his with a hunger that matched his own. She tasted like cinnamon and challenge, like every argument they'd ever had transformed into something electric.

When they finally broke apart, both breathing hard, Drevik became aware of the others still watching.

Mira's expression had shifted—still sharp, but with an edge of satisfaction. Ivy's pale eyes had gone soft, her earlier intensity gentling into something that looked almost like hope.

"Finally," Mira said. "That wasn't so difficult, was it?"

"That was a beginning," Lexi corrected, still pressed against Drevik's chest. "Not an ending." She pulled back enough to meet his eyes. "I need to know where we stand. All of us. Because I won't compete—"

"You're not competing." Drevik's voice came out steadier than he expected. "I don't want you competing. I want—" He stopped, trying to articulate something he'd never allowed himself to examine.

"What do you want?" Mira asked, moving closer. "Not what you think you should want. Not what the Order expects. What do you want, Drevik?"

The question hung in the air like a challenge.

He looked at each of them in turn. Lexi, fierce and protective, who had been his anchor through chaos. Mira, brilliant and demanding, who pushed him harder than anyone and made him want to rise to meet her. Ivy, quiet and perceptive, who saw through every defense and offered understanding instead of judgment.

"I want to stop being afraid," he said finally. "I want to let myself care about people without waiting for the universe to take them away. I want—" His voice roughened. "I want you. All of you. And that terrifies me more than anything in the Shadowlands."

The confession settled into the silence like a stone finding water.

Mira's smile sharpened. "Good. Honesty. That's a start."

She moved toward him with purpose, and Drevik had a moment to register Lexi stepping aside before Mira's hand fisted in his collar and pulled him down to her level.

"If you think," she murmured against his lips, "that you can kiss Lexi and leave me waiting indefinitely, you understand less about women than I thought."

Then she kissed him—different from Lexi's desperate hunger, more controlled and deliberate, like she was conducting an experiment and satisfied with the results. Her lips moved against his with precision, and Drevik felt himself respond despite the exhaustion, despite the emotional upheaval. Mira kissed like she did everything—with intelligence and intent, like she was mapping his responses and filing them away.

When she released him, her eyes were bright with satisfaction.

"Acceptable," she pronounced. "Room for improvement."

"Your ego is going to be a problem," Drevik heard himself say.

"It already is." Mira stepped back, smoothing her blouse. "But at least now we understand each other."

Drevik stood in the center of the room, caught between three women, his heart racing and his defenses in ruins. He felt like a man who'd stepped off a cliff and discovered he didn't know how to fly.

Then Ivy rose from the windowsill.

She approached with none of Lexi's fury or Mira's challenge. Her movement was patient, inevitable—like moonlight creeping across a floor.

"Drevik." Her voice was barely above a whisper. "May I?"

He swallowed hard and nodded.

Ivy's hand came up to rest against his chest, over his heart. Her touch was gentle, but her eyes held an intensity that cut through every wall he'd tried to build.

"I'm not like them," she said softly. "I won't demand or challenge or push. But I won't disappear either. I've been invisible my whole life—too quiet, too strange, too sensitive to the Shadowlands' call. I won't be invisible with you."

Her hand pressed firmer against his chest.

"I'll wait," she continued. "I'll give you time to figure out how this works. But I won't be a secret, and I won't be an afterthought. Do you understand?"

Drevik looked into her pale eyes and saw the depth of feeling there—the years of being overlooked, the fierce determination to matter. Ivy, who had every reason to hide, was choosing to stand in the light and ask for what she needed.

"I understand," he said, and meant it.

Ivy nodded once, then rose on her toes to press a kiss to his jaw—brief and gentle, but weighted with promise. When she pulled back, her smile was small but real.

"Good," she said. "Now we should discuss the mission. Because there are things you need to know before tomorrow."

The shift in atmosphere was immediate. Lexi straightened, her expression sharpening. Mira's playfulness vanished, replaced by the focused analyst. Ivy moved toward the couch, her earlier vulnerability tucked away behind professional focus.

Drevik followed, grateful for the change in subject even as part of him reeled from what had just happened. He settled into an armchair, and the three women arranged themselves around him—Lexi on the couch's edge, Mira in the adjacent chair, Ivy standing by the fire.

"The Shadowlands briefing was incomplete," Lexi said without preamble. "The Elders don't want to spook the acolytes, but there are things we need to prepare for."

"The Veiled Ones have been tracking increased activity near the border zones," Mira continued. "More breaches, more entities crossing into the mortal realm. Something is destabilizing the barrier."

"That's why we're going in," Drevik said. "To reinforce the anchor points."

"That's part of it." Ivy's voice was quiet but certain. "But there's something else. Something they didn't include in the official briefing."

Drevik's shadows stirred, responding to his sudden tension. "How do you know this?"

Ivy's smile was thin. "My sensitivity to the Shadowlands has certain advantages. I hear things the Elders don't realize I can hear. I see things they think are hidden."

"What things?"

Lexi leaned forward. "The entity that's been destabilizing the barrier—it's not random. Someone is directing it. Someone with knowledge of Order magic."

"Traitors?" The word tasted wrong in Drevik's mouth.

"Or True Believers," Mira said grimly. "There's a faction within the Order that thinks we've grown too cautious. They want to open the borders completely, let the Shadowlands merge with our reality."

"That's insane."

"That's fanaticism." Lexi's voice was hard. "And if they're willing to sacrifice acolytes to achieve their goals, we need to be prepared for more than routine reinforcement."

Drevik absorbed this, his mind racing through implications. The mission had seemed dangerous but manageable—a standard test of acolyte readiness. Now it sounded like walking into an ambush.

"Why tell me now?" he asked. "Why not bring this to the Elders?"

"We did," Lexi said. "Elder Thornwood listened. He promised to increase security protocols."

"But you don't think that's enough."

"I think there are Elders we can trust and Elders we can't." Mira's expression was grim. "Until we know which is which, we handle this ourselves."

Drevik nodded slowly. It made a terrible kind of sense—the Order had survived for centuries by being paranoid, by assuming infiltration was constant. Why would now be different?

"There's more," Ivy said quietly. All eyes turned to her. "The coordinates we're meant to reinforce—they're near the sector where your mother was last seen."

The room seemed to contract. Drevik felt the shadows around him surge, responding to the sudden spike of emotion.

"My mother?"

"Officially, she was lost during a standard reconnaissance mission. Unofficially—" Ivy hesitated. "Unofficially, there are rumors. Whispers among the older members. Your mother wasn't just reconnaissance. She was looking for something. Or someone."

"Who?"

"We don't know." Lexi's voice was gentle now, careful. "But the coordinates for tomorrow's mission put us within range of her last known location. If you wanted to investigate..."

Drevik's mind raced. His mother had been a mystery his entire life—her existence confirmed only through Order records, her fate unknown. His grandmother had refused to discuss her, and his father was a question mark. The possibility of answers, of truth...

"The Elders would never authorize a detour," he said.

"No," Mira agreed. "They wouldn't. Which is why we're not asking."

Drevik looked at the three women—each determined, each ready to risk censure for him. It was more than he deserved, more than he'd ever expected to find when he'd stumbled into this world.

"Why?" he asked. "Why risk this for me?"

Lexi's expression softened. "Because you've earned it. Because you deserve to know what happened to your family. Because—"

"Because we care," Ivy finished simply. "Isn't that enough?"

Before Drevik could respond, the common room door swung open.

Seraphina Vane stood in the doorway, her dark hair disheveled, her clothes marked with travel. She looked like she'd run the entire way from wherever she'd been—her eyes wild, her breathing ragged.

"Seraphina?" Drevik stood. "What are you doing here? You're supposed to be at the northern temple—"

"Forget the temple." She stepped inside, her gaze sweeping the room before landing on him. "I got your message about the mission coordinates. I've been trying to reach you for hours."

"We've been... discussing," Lexi said carefully.

Seraphina's laugh was sharp and humorless. "Discussing. Good. Then you know about the coordinates. About Drevik's mother."

"We know," Mira confirmed.

"Then you know why I'm coming with you." Seraphina moved toward Drevik, her intensity palpable. "The Elders denied my request, but I'm going anyway. I have to."

"Why?" Drevik asked. "What's in that sector that's so important to you?"

Seraphina's eyes met his, and for a moment, the mask she always wore slipped. Beneath it was something raw—grief and determination and desperate hope.

"Because I'm the only one who can guide you to where your mother was last seen," she said. "Because I was there when she disappeared. Because I've spent fifteen years trying to find a way back to that place, and now you're walking straight into it."

The silence stretched, heavy with revelation.

"You knew my mother?" Drevik's voice came out rough.

"I served with her. I trained under her." Seraphina's jaw tightened. "And I watched her walk into a trap that was meant for me. Every day since then, I've been trying to make it right."

Drevik felt the world shift beneath his feet. The shadows around him churned, responding to emotions too tangled to name.

"Tell me everything," he said.

Seraphina nodded. "I will. But not here. Not with ears that might report to the wrong people." Her gaze swept the room again, landing on each of the women in turn. "All of you need to hear this. Because tomorrow, we're not just reinforcing anchor points. We're going to find out what happened to Elara Markinsoul—and we're going to make whoever's responsible pay."

Drevik stood in the center of the room, surrounded by women who had chosen to stand with him, facing a mystery that had haunted his entire life. The mission had transformed into something else entirely.

"Then let's move," he said. "We have six hours, and I want to know everything before we cross into the Shadowlands."

Seraphina smiled—sharp and dangerous and fierce. "Finally. Someone who understands."

The shadows around Drevik's feet settled into stillness. For the first time in days, the noise in his head had quieted into focus. Tomorrow would bring danger, revelation, possibly death. But tonight, he had clarity.

Tonight, he had people willing to fight beside him.

Tonight, for the first time in longer than he could remember, he wasn't facing the darkness alone.
