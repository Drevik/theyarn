Chapter 7: Academic Pressure

Chapter 7: Academic Pressure

The fluorescent lights of the library hummed with a frequency that made Drevik's teeth ache.

He'd been staring at the same page of his Art History textbook for forty-five minutes, and the images were starting to move. Not because of any mystical phenomenon—simply because his exhausted brain was projecting shadows where none existed. After three nights of Shadowlands training followed by dawn patrol and then classes, his grasp on reality had become... tenuous.

"Drevik."

He blinked. The woman across from him had been talking for several minutes. He knew this because her lips had been moving, and occasionally sounds emerged. He just couldn't recall any of them.

"I'm sorry," he said, rubbing his eyes. "What?"

Marissa sighed, tapping her pen against her notebook. "I asked if you wanted to grab coffee. You've been dissociating for the past hour, and honestly? You look like you're about to pass out."

"I'm fine."

"You're really not." She closed her notebook, concern flickering across her features. "Is everything okay? You've been weird lately. Weirder than usual, I mean."

Drevik forced a smile that felt brittle on his face. Marissa Chen had been in his study group since freshman year. She was pre-law, aggressively organized, and had never once suspected that her classmate spent his nights fighting shadow creatures in a parallel dimension. He wanted to keep it that way.

"Just stressed about finals," he said. "You know how it is."

"Finals." She raised an eyebrow. "We're studying Art History, Drevik. We're not exactly cramming for the MCATs."

The irony wasn't lost on him. While Marissa worried about baroque versus rococo, he was learning to walk through walls and bind interdimensional entities. The academic pressure should have felt manageable by comparison.

It didn't.

"Maybe I need that coffee after all," he conceded, standing too quickly. The room tilted. He grabbed the edge of the table.

Marissa was watching him with an expression he couldn't quite read. "Are you sure you're okay? Because if you're having some kind of breakdown—"

"I'm fine," he repeated, more sharply than intended. He caught himself, softening his tone. "Sorry. I haven't been sleeping well. Coffee would help."

Her eyes lingered on him a moment longer before she shrugged. "Fine. But if you collapse in the middle of the library, I'm putting that in my closing argument someday."

The campus coffee shop was a fifteen-minute walk across the quad, and Drevik used every step to reassemble his composure. The autumn air helped—crisp and clean, carrying the distant sound of a practice session from the athletic fields. Normal. Mundane. The world that existed outside the Order's shadow.

He just wished it felt more real.

"That's the third time you've checked your phone in two minutes," Marissa observed as they entered the coffee shop. "Expecting an important call?"

Drevik pocketed the device, heat rising to his cheeks. He hadn't been checking for messages. He'd been checking for shadows. The darkness in his peripheral vision had become persistent lately—a side effect of extended time in the Shadowlands, Lexi had explained. His mind was learning to see things that human eyes weren't meant to see.

It made focusing on Art History remarkably difficult.

"Habit," he said. "What's your order?"

They found a corner table, and for the next hour, Drevik attempted to absorb information about Renaissance painters while his brain screamed about everything he was ignoring. The Order expected him to train four nights a week now. His classes expected him to remain conscious during lectures. His normal friends expected him to show up for things. And somewhere in the back of his mind, the darkness whispered that none of this mattered—that the Shadowlands were the only reality, and everything else was just a dream he'd wake from eventually.

"You're doing it again," Marissa said, breaking through his thoughts.

"Doing what?"

"Staring into space like you're looking at something nobody else can see."

Drevik's throat tightened. If only she knew how literal that was.

Before he could respond, the door chimed. He glanced up automatically, scanning entrances and exits—a habit drilled into him by Lexi—and his heart stuttered.

Seraphina Vane walked through the door like she owned the air itself.

She wore normal clothes, relatively speaking. Dark jeans, a cream-colored sweater, ankle boots. Her silver-blonde hair fell loose around her shoulders, catching the afternoon light. But there was nothing normal about the way every head in the coffee shop turned to follow her progress. She moved with the liquid grace of something not entirely human, and Drevik felt the familiar pull in his chest—the strange magnetic attraction that had existed between them since their first meeting.

She wasn't supposed to be here. Seraphina operated in different circles, higher echelons of Order business. Seeing her on campus felt like spotting a tiger in a grocery store.

Their eyes met across the crowded room.

A smile curved her lips—not warm, exactly, but knowing. She didn't approach him. Didn't acknowledge him beyond that single glance. She simply ordered at the counter, collected her drink, and settled at a table on the opposite side of the shop, pulling out a book as though she belonged there.

"Drevik." Marissa's voice cut through his daze. "Who is that?"

"No one," he said too quickly.

Marissa's gaze sharpened with the intensity of someone who'd been waiting for exactly this kind of drama. "That's not 'no one.' That's someone who just walked in looking like a supermodel and made eye contact with you like you share secrets. Do you share secrets?"

"We've met." Drevik kept his eyes firmly on his textbook. "She's... acquainted with some people I know."

"Acquainted." Marissa leaned forward, abandoning all pretense of studying. "Is she why you've been distracted lately? Because if you're blowing off study sessions for a girl—"

"I'm not." The denial came out sharper than intended. "I don't even know her that well."

"Could've fooled me." Marissa glanced at Seraphina again, then back at Drevik. "She's still looking at you."

He didn't need to check. He could feel her attention like a physical weight, pressing against his awareness. The darkness inside him stirred, responding to something it recognized.

"Maybe you should go talk to her," Marissa suggested. "Get it out of your system so we can focus on Renaissance art."

"There's nothing to get out of my system."

"Then prove it." Marissa stood, gathering her things. "I need to use the restroom. When I get back, I expect you to either be fully present or have some kind of explanation. Your choice."

She walked away, leaving Drevik alone at the table with his textbook and the weight of Seraphina's gaze.

He lasted approximately thirty seconds before he looked up.

She was already watching him, her pale blue eyes reflecting the ambient light with an intensity that made his breath catch. She tilted her head slightly—a question, an invitation, or perhaps a command. With Seraphina, it was difficult to tell the difference.

Drevik rose from his table before he consciously decided to move.

The walk across the coffee shop felt longer than the walk across campus. He was aware of every person watching him, every whisper that might be about him, every shadow that might be something more. Seraphina remained seated, perfectly at ease, as though his approach was exactly what she'd planned.

"Unexpected," he said, stopping beside her table. "What are you doing here?"

"Getting coffee." She gestured to her cup. "Is that not allowed?"

"You know what I mean."

Seraphina's smile deepened. "Perhaps I wanted to see how you were adjusting. The Shadowlands training has been... intense lately."

"That's one word for it."

"Sit." It wasn't a request.

Drevik sat, acutely aware of how this must look to anyone watching. A beautiful woman, intimate conversation, the kind of scenario that spawned rumors. He thought of Lexi, who was probably training somewhere on campus right now, completely unaware that Seraphina had appeared like a summoned spirit.

"You look exhausted," Seraphina observed, studying him over the rim of her cup. "The darkness is wearing on you."

"I'm managing."

"Managing." She set down her cup, folding her hands on the table. "You know, I can feel it when you're in the Shadowlands. Your presence... echoes. It's quite distinctive."

The admission sent a shiver down his spine. Seraphina had never been clear about the exact nature of her interest in him. She appeared and disappeared like the moon behind clouds, offering cryptic insights and lingering touches that left him confused and wanting.

"I didn't realize you were tracking my movements," he said carefully.

"Tracking is such a clinical word. I prefer... attuned." Her pale eyes held his. "The Order has high hopes for you, Drevik. Higher than you realize. The darkness inside you is growing, and that growth requires... cultivation."

"Is that why you're here? Cultivation?"

"Partly." She leaned back in her chair, and he noticed how the ambient light seemed to bend around her, softening edges and deepening shadows. "I also wanted to see you in your natural habitat. You look remarkably ordinary in this setting. It's charming."

Drevik couldn't decide if that was a compliment or an insult. "I'm a college student. This is what college students do."

"Mmm." Her gaze drifted across the coffee shop, landing briefly on Marissa's empty chair. "Your friend seems curious about me."

"She's suspicious. I've been... distracted lately."

"Because of the Order."

"Because of everything." The admission slipped out before he could stop it. "I'm trying to balance two completely different lives, and I'm not sure I'm succeeding at either."

Seraphina was quiet for a moment. When she spoke again, her voice had lost some of its playful edge. "The duality you're experiencing is temporary. Eventually, you'll learn to integrate both aspects of yourself. The darkness will stop feeling like a separate presence and start feeling like... you."

"Did that happen for you?"

Something flickered across her features—vulnerability, perhaps, or pain. It vanished before he could be certain. "I've always been what I am. The darkness and I have never been separate."

Before he could respond, movement caught his eye. Marissa had returned from the restroom and was standing near their table, her expression a complex mixture of curiosity and challenge.

"I see you've made a new friend," she said, her tone carefully neutral.

Drevik stood, feeling suddenly like he was balancing on a knife's edge between worlds. "Marissa, this is Seraphina. We have... mutual acquaintances."

Seraphina rose as well, extending her hand with a grace that made the simple gesture look ceremonial. "A pleasure. Drevik's mentioned you."

Marissa shook the offered hand, her eyes widening slightly—probably at the coolness of Seraphina's skin, or the strength in her grip. "I doubt that. He barely mentioned you at all, which is interesting considering how you just looked at each other."

"Marissa—"

"I'm going to head out," Marissa interrupted, gathering her bag. "You clearly have... things... to discuss. Just do me a favor and actually show up to the study session tomorrow. Professor Harrison is already giving you the side-eye."

She left before Drevik could respond, weaving through tables toward the exit. He watched her go, a pit forming in his stomach.

"She's protective of you," Seraphina observed. "That's good. You'll need people who care about your survival."

"Was that a threat?"

"An observation." Seraphina collected her cup, preparing to leave. "Be careful, Drevik. The darkness is changing you faster than you realize. Your control is slipping."

"I have it under control."

"Do you?" She reached out, her fingers brushing his jaw with a touch so light it might have been a shadow. "Then why are your hands shaking?"

He looked down. She was right. A fine tremor ran through his fingers, invisible unless someone was looking closely. He hadn't even noticed.

"The more you deny the darkness," Seraphina continued, her voice dropping to a murmur, "the more it will demand from you. Stop fighting it. Let it flow through you instead of trying to contain it."

"How do you know so much about what I'm experiencing?"

"Because I've watched others walk this path." Her hand fell away from his face. "Most of them didn't survive. You have a chance to be different—if you stop pretending you're still just a college student with normal problems."

She turned and walked out of the coffee shop without a backward glance, leaving Drevik standing alone at a table that suddenly felt very exposed.

He was still reeling from the encounter when his phone buzzed.

Training tonight. 9 PM. Don't be late. —Lexi

The message was typical Lexi—direct, efficient, with no wasted words. But reading it after Seraphina's appearance, something twisted in his chest. Lexi would not be pleased to know Seraphina had been on campus. She viewed the older woman's interest in Drevik with deep suspicion.

Not that Drevik entirely understood that interest himself.

He left the coffee shop and headed toward his next class, trying to shake off the encounter. The campus looked different now—the shadows between buildings seemed deeper, the light harsher. Every person he passed might be an Order member, or something worse.

Stop it, he told himself. You're being paranoid.

But was it paranoia if the threats were real?

His Art History lecture passed in a blur. Professor Harrison's voice droned on about perspective and composition while Drevik's mind kept drifting to shadowy corridors and silver-blonde hair. He took notes automatically, his hand moving while his thoughts raced.

After class, he made his way to the small study room in the library where Lexi had agreed to meet him. She'd offered to help him prepare for his Art History final, claiming she'd taken the class two years ago and aced it. He suspected she just wanted an excuse to spend time together outside of Order business.

He found her already there, books spread across the table, her auburn hair pulled back in a practical ponytail. She looked up as he entered, and her face softened in a way that still caught him off guard.

"You're late," she said, but there was no real accusation in her tone.

"Got held up." He dropped into the chair across from her. "Ran into... something unexpected."

Lexi's eyes sharpened. "What kind of unexpected?"

He considered lying. It would be easier. But the words wouldn't come.

"Seraphina," he said finally. "She was at the coffee shop."

The softness vanished from Lexi's expression, replaced by something harder. "On campus? Why?"

"She said she wanted to see how I was adjusting. And something about feeling my presence in the Shadowlands." He paused, watching Lexi's jaw tighten. "Is that... normal?"

"Nothing about Seraphina is normal." Lexi's voice had an edge he hadn't heard before. "She's been involved with the Order for decades, but no one knows exactly what she is. Some people think she's not entirely human."

"She said something similar. About always being what she is."

Lexia's frown deepened. "What else did she say?"

"That the darkness is changing me. That I need to stop fighting it." He met Lexi's eyes. "She said my hands were shaking."

The admission hung between them. Lexi reached across the table, taking his hands in hers. Her touch was warm—warmer than Seraphina's—and familiar in a way that settled something inside him.

"You're pushing yourself too hard," she said quietly. "The training, the classes, everything. You need rest."

"I can't rest. Finals are next week, and the Shadowlands mission—"

"Will happen whether you're rested or not." She squeezed his hands. "Let me help you. Not just with studying. With everything."

The offer was genuine, and he could see how much it cost her to make it. Lexi wasn't the type to ask for permission or offer support easily. She was the type to take charge and assume everyone would follow.

"I don't want to burden you," he said.

"You're not a burden." Her voice softened further. "You're never a burden. I wouldn't have offered if I didn't want to help."

He studied her face—the determined set of her jaw, the vulnerability she tried so hard to hide, the way her green eyes held his without flinching. She was offering him something beyond academic support. She was offering him partnership.

"Okay," he said. "Help me."

They studied for three hours.

Lexi was a demanding tutor, drilling him on dates and movements and artistic techniques until his brain felt like overworked muscle. But underneath the academic rigor, there was a comfortable rhythm to their interaction—a domesticity that felt foreign and precious.

At some point, she moved her chair beside his, leaning over his notes to point out details he'd missed. Her shoulder pressed against his, warm and solid, and he found himself breathing easier than he had in days.

"You're overthinking this," she said, tapping his notebook. "The Baroque period isn't about memorizing facts. It's about understanding the emotional intent. These artists were trying to make people feel something."

"Kind of like shadow magic," he said without thinking.

Lexi paused. "What do you mean?"

"I mean..." He struggled to articulate the thought that had been forming. "In the Shadowlands, emotion shapes reality. Fear creates monsters. Hope creates paths. The Baroque artists were doing something similar—using contrast and movement to manipulate emotional response."

Lexi stared at him for a long moment. Then, slowly, a smile spread across her face—the kind of genuine smile she rarely showed anyone.

"That's actually brilliant," she said. "I never thought about it that way, but you're right. The same principles apply. You're understanding shadow work better than you realize."

The praise warmed him more than it should have. "I have a good teacher."

"Flattery will get you everywhere." But she was still smiling, and the tension that had gripped her since he'd mentioned Seraphina seemed to have loosened.

They returned to studying, but the dynamic had shifted. Every accidental touch felt charged—her hand brushing his as they reached for the same book, her breath warm against his cheek as she leaned in to read a passage. He was acutely aware of her proximity, the scent of her hair, the way her presence made the shadows feel less threatening.

"Drevik." Her voice had dropped to barely a whisper.

He turned to find her face very close to his, her green eyes searching his features with an intensity that had nothing to do with academics.

"You have no idea how hard it is," she said, "pretending this is just studying."

His heart stuttered. "Lexi—"

"I know we said we'd take things slow. I know you're dealing with more than any acolyte should have to handle. But being around you, watching you push yourself to the breaking point, not being able to—" She stopped herself, looking away. "I'm sorry. I shouldn't."

"Shouldn't what?"

"Complicate things." She started to pull back, but he caught her hand.

"You're not complicating things." His voice came out rough. "You're the only thing keeping me grounded."

The admission hung between them. Lexi's gaze returned to his face, and he saw the moment her restraint crumbled.

"Then let me ground you," she whispered.

She kissed him.

It wasn't their first kiss, but it felt different—less hesitant, more desperate. Her lips pressed against his with a hunger that matched the darkness pulsing inside him. He pulled her closer, his hand tangling in her hair, and she made a soft sound that sent heat flooding through his body.

For a moment, the shadows retreated entirely. There was only Lexi—warm and real and his—and the way she felt against him, and the way she whispered his name like it meant something sacred.

Then his phone buzzed, shattering the moment.

They broke apart, both breathing heavily. Lexi's cheeks were flushed, her lips slightly swollen, her eyes still dark with wanting.

"You should get that," she said, but her voice was unsteady.

Drevik glanced at his phone. A message from Professor Harrison.

Mr. Markinsoul, please come to my office immediately. We need to discuss your recent academic performance.

He stared at the screen, a cold weight settling in his stomach.

"What is it?" Lexi asked.

"Professor Harrison wants to see me." He looked up at her. "Now."

Professor Harrison's office was on the third floor of the humanities building, a cramped space filled with books and artifacts accumulated over decades of teaching. The professor herself was a small woman with sharp eyes and an intimidating reputation for detecting academic dishonesty.

She gestured for Drevik to sit without looking up from the papers on her desk.

"Mr. Markinsoul," she said finally, removing her glasses to study him directly. "Do you know why I asked to see you?"

"I can guess."

"Your midterm paper was exceptional. Thoughtful, well-researched, showing genuine insight into the material." She paused. "Your recent work, however, has been... inconsistent. You've missed assignments. Your participation in class has declined markedly. And today, you spent the entire lecture staring at the wall."

Drevik's throat tightened. "I've been dealing with some personal issues."

"So I gathered." Professor Harrison leaned back in her chair. "I'm not without understanding, Mr. Markinsoul. College is a stressful time. Many students struggle with balancing their responsibilities. But I've noticed a pattern with you that goes beyond typical academic pressure."

"What kind of pattern?"

"You disappear for days at a time. You return looking exhausted, sometimes injured. Your friends say you've joined some kind of... organization." Her eyes sharpened. "I want to give you the benefit of the doubt, but I need to know what's really going on."

The weight of the question pressed against Drevik's chest. He couldn't tell her the truth. He couldn't tell her anything. The Order's secrecy was absolute, and revealing even a fraction of the truth would put her in danger.

"I joined a martial arts group," he said, falling back on the cover story Lexi had helped him construct. "The training is more intense than I expected. I'm learning to manage my time better."

Professor Harrison studied him for a long moment. "A martial arts group."

"Yes."

"That meets at night."

"Yes."

"That leaves you looking like you haven't slept in a week." She stood, moving to her window. "Mr. Markinsoul, I've been teaching for thirty years. I've seen students struggle with drugs, with relationships, with family problems. I've never seen someone look the way you do right now unless they were in genuine danger."

"I'm not in danger."

"Then prove it." She turned back to face him. "Ace your final exam. Show up to class. Stop looking over your shoulder like something's hunting you." Her voice softened slightly. "Whatever you're involved in, if it's hurting you, there are resources. People who can help."

The irony of her words was almost painful. She thought he needed counseling, or medical attention, or any of the normal solutions for normal problems. She had no idea that the help he needed came from a secret organization that operated beneath the university itself.

"I appreciate your concern," he said carefully. "I'll do better."

Professor Harrison's expression suggested she didn't entirely believe him, but she nodded. "See that you do. You have potential, Mr. Markinsoul. Don't waste it."

He left her office with his mind racing. The conversation had shaken something loose—a growing awareness that his double life was becoming increasingly difficult to maintain. How long could he keep this up before everything fell apart?

The walk back to his dorm felt longer than usual. The sun had set while he was in Harrison's office, and the campus had transformed into a landscape of pools of light separated by deep shadows. He caught himself scanning the darkness automatically, looking for threats that shouldn't exist in the normal world.

His phone buzzed again. Lexi.

How did it go?

Fine. She's suspicious but bought the cover story.

Good. Get some rest. I'll see you at 9.

He pocketed the phone, but he didn't head back to his dorm. Instead, he found himself walking toward the library, drawn by some instinct he couldn't name. The building was still open, though sparsely populated at this hour. He made his way to a quiet corner on the third floor, settling into a chair where he could see the entrance.

He needed to think. To process. To figure out how to balance the impossible demands on his time and energy.

But as he sat there, the exhaustion he'd been fighting all day finally caught up with him. His eyes grew heavy. The darkness behind his eyelids seemed welcoming, almost comforting, and he felt himself drifting toward it.

The dream came quickly.

He was standing in the Shadowlands, but different than he'd ever seen them. The twilight landscape stretched before him, dotted with ruins that pulsed with dark energy. In the distance, something massive moved—a presence that made the shadows themselves recoil.

"You're changing," a voice whispered. It sounded like Seraphina, but distorted, as if speaking through water. "The darkness is becoming you."

Then Lexi appeared before him, her green eyes wide with fear. "Drevik, run. It's hunting you. It's been hunting you all along."

"Lexi—" He reached for her, but she dissolved into shadows.

The massive presence drew closer. He could feel it now, a pressure against his consciousness that made his bones ache. It wasn't just a creature. It was looking for him. Specifically. Personally.

Drevik Markinsoul, a voice echoed in his mind, ancient and terrible. I see you at last.

He woke with a gasp.

The library was dark. Somehow, hours had passed without him noticing. His phone showed 11:47 PM—he'd missed his training session with Lexi entirely.

Damn it.

He stood too quickly, his head spinning. The dream still clung to him, its images lingering at the edges of his vision. The Shadowlands had felt so real, and that presence—

It's hunting you.

He needed to tell someone. Lexi would know what it meant. Maybe the Order had encountered entities that could track acolytes through dreams.

He made his way toward the exit, his footsteps echoing in the empty corridor. The fluorescent lights had been dimmed for nighttime, casting long shadows across the floor. He moved through them automatically, his mind still processing.

The attack came without warning.

One moment he was walking; the next, something cold and dark wrapped around his ankle, yanking him off his feet. He hit the ground hard, his phone skittering across the floor. Shadows surged around him, coalescing into a shape that shouldn't exist outside the Shadowlands.

A Shadeling. Here. On campus.

Impossible, his mind screamed. The creatures couldn't cross over without a breach point. The Order maintained wards specifically to prevent—

The Shadeling lunged.

Drevik rolled aside, feeling the rush of cold air as it passed where his throat had been. He scrambled to his feet, calling on his training. Shadow Walking was useless here—he had nowhere to walk to. But combat techniques...

The Shadeling circled, its form shifting and flowing like liquid darkness. It was smaller than the ones he'd faced in the Shadowlands, but no less deadly. Its eyes—if it had eyes—fixed on him with unmistakable intent.

It wasn't attacking randomly. It was attacking him.

"You followed me," he said aloud, understanding crashing through him. "From the Shadowlands. You followed me here."

The creature didn't answer—couldn't answer—but its intent was clear. It had tracked him through whatever connection he'd formed during training, and now it was here, in the library, in the middle of the campus where thousands of people slept, unaware.

Drevik's hand closed around a letter opener someone had left on a nearby desk. Not ideal, but better than nothing. The Shadeling coiled to strike again, and he prepared himself—

The lights flickered.

The Shadeling hesitated, its form wavering. Something about the electrical current disrupted its manifestation. Drevik filed that information away, his mind racing.

Then the creature lunged again, and there was no time for thinking—only survival.

He killed it with the letter opener through its core, the motion instinctive despite his exhaustion. The Shadeling dissolved into smoke, leaving him standing in the dim library with his heart pounding and his hands shaking.

His phone lay cracked on the floor, screen shattered but still functional. He picked it up, finding three missed calls from Lexi and a string of increasingly concerned messages.

Where are you?

Training started 20 minutes ago.

Drevik, answer me.

Something's wrong. I'm coming to find you.

He typed a response with trembling fingers, his heart still racing from the attack.

Library. Third floor. Shadeling found me. It's dead.

The response came immediately.

Stay there. I'm bringing backup.

Drevik leaned against a bookshelf, trying to steady his breathing. The darkness inside him pulsed in rhythm with his heartbeat, stronger than it had been before the fight. The creature's death had fed it somehow, and he could feel the power settling into his bones.

It's hunting you.

Lexi's dream-words echoed in his mind. The Shadeling hadn't been random. It had been sent, or drawn, specifically to him. And if one could find him...

More would follow.

The campus was no longer safe.
