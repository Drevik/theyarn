Chapter 19: Extraction

The Shadowlands had teeth.

Drevik felt them sinking into his consciousness with every step—serrated edges of hunger and malice that scraped against the barriers he'd hastily constructed around his mind. The realm knew what he carried now, and it wanted to tear it free.

"Move faster." Mira's voice cut through the twilight gloom, her usual composure fractured by urgency. Blood seeped between her fingers where she clutched her side, the wound leaking shadows instead of red. "The breach is destabilizing. We have maybe twenty minutes before it collapses entirely."

Twenty minutes. Drevik's legs burned from the sprint across the obsidian plains, his lungs gasping against air that felt more like breathing water. Behind them, the horizon had begun to curdle—darkness gathering into shapes that hadn't been there moments before.

Seraphina stumbled, and he caught her arm before she could fall. The mysterious woman who'd haunted his dreams for weeks before he'd ever met her in the flesh now looked painfully mortal—dark circles under her eyes, her silver hair tangled with debris from their flight through the Crystalline Forest.

"I can make it," she insisted, pulling away from his support. Pride sharpened her words, but exhaustion softened them. "Worry about Lexi."

As if summoned by her name, Lexi's weight shifted against Drevik's shoulder. He'd refused to let anyone else carry her after the battle at the nexus point—her wound too precarious, the shadow-poison too volatile to risk jostling. The gash across her ribs had started as something small, but the Entity's touch had corrupted it into something that pulsed with sickly violet light.

"I'm fine," Lexi muttered, the words slurring. Her auburn hair was plastered to her forehead with sweat, her green eyes unfocused. "Stop... stop looking at me like that."

"Like what?" Drevik tightened his grip around her waist, feeling the tremor that ran through her frame.

"Like you're already planning my funeral."

Despite everything—the creatures closing behind them, the threshold shrinking with every passing second, the chaos that awaited on the other side—something warm flickered in his chest. Even half-delirious with pain, Lexi was still fighting. Still fierce.

"Planning would imply I've accepted the possibility," Drevik said, adjusting her arm across his shoulders. "I don't accept anything that ends with you dead. Too stubborn."

A ghost of a smile crossed her face. "Asshole."

"Takes one to know one."

The terrain shifted beneath them—smooth obsidian giving way to crystalline formations that jutted from the ground like broken teeth. They were approaching the boundary zone, where the Shadowlands pressed closest against the mortal realm. The air here tasted of copper and ozone, and the sky above had begun to ripple with visible waves of distortion.

"There." Mira pointed toward a shimmer in the darkness—a tear in reality that flickered like a dying flame. "The threshold. It's still open."

Something shrieked behind them.

Drevik didn't look back. He didn't need to. He could feel the Entities gathering, their attention fixed on him with predatory focus. The power he'd absorbed at the nexus point marked him now—made him a beacon in the eternal twilight.

Come to us, little acolyte. Let us show you what you truly are.

The voices slithered through his mental barriers, sweet and terrible. He understood now why the Elders spoke of temptation—why the Order's teachings emphasized discipline above all else. The Shadowlands offered exactly what he'd always wanted. Belonging. Purpose. Power.

You could rule here. You could burn brighter than any Veiled One. You could keep them all safe forever—all your women, all your loves. Just let go.

"Drevik." Seraphina's hand closed around his wrist, her grip surprisingly strong. "Stay with us. Whatever they're showing you—"

"How did you know?"

"Because I can see it in your eyes." She stepped closer, and for a moment the exhaustion fell away, revealing the woman who'd walked through his dreams with cryptic warnings and riddles. "The same thing calls to me. Every time I Shadow Walk, I feel it pulling harder. The realm wants us, Drevik. It's been waiting for people like us for centuries."

"People like us?"

"Those who can see beyond the veil. Those who can shape it." Her eyes held his, unflinching. "That's why we can't stay. That's why we have to run."

The first Entity materialized from the shadows to their left—a mass of writhing darkness that resolved into something vaguely humanoid, its face a smooth expanse of nothing where features should have been. It moved without sound, gliding across the crystal formations with terrible grace.

Mira's blade caught it mid-strike, shadows against shadows, and the Entity shattered like glass. But three more were already forming.

"Go!" Mira shouted. "The threshold won't wait!"

Drevik ran.

Lexi's weight pulled at his shoulder, her breathing ragged and shallow against his neck. Every step sent jolts of pain through his body, but he pushed harder, faster—his newly enhanced abilities screaming against the strain of holding two forms of magic simultaneously. The nexus power burned through his veins like liquid fire, and the Shadowlands responded to it. Paths opened before him that hadn't existed moments before. Shadows bent around his footsteps, hiding their passage.

You're learning, whispered a voice in his mind—not the Entities' seductive calls, but something older. Something that felt like approval. Good. You'll need this.

He didn't question it. Questioning meant slowing down, and slowing down meant death.

The threshold grew larger with every stride—a wound in reality that leaked mortal light into the eternal twilight. Through it, he could see the ritual chamber they'd left behind: stone walls covered in protective sigils, the bodies of the Order's advance team sprawled across the floor, and a figure standing at the center, arms raised, surrounded by a circle of flickering candles.

Ivy.

She'd stayed behind to keep their exit open. She'd been alone for hours, maintaining a breach that should have required at least six acolytes. Her face was pale as parchment, blood trickling from her nose, her whole body trembling with the effort of holding reality apart.

"Ivy!" Seraphina's voice cracked on the name. "Hold on! We're coming!"

Through the threshold, Ivy's eyes found them—wide with relief and something else. Fear. Not for herself, but for them.

"They're coming," she gasped, her voice carrying through the breach like it came from underwater. "More of them. I can feel them gathering on this side. Drevik, you have to—"

The threshold flickered.

"NO!" Mira lunged forward, her shadows wrapping around the edges of the breach, lending it strength. "Don't you dare close it! Don't you dare—"

"Not... closing..." Ivy's words came through gritted teeth. "Holding... but something's... wrong..."

Drevik felt it too. A pressure building against the threshold from the mortal side—not Entities, but something else. Something that felt disturbingly familiar.

The Order, he realized with cold clarity. They're trying to seal us in.

"FASTER!" He poured more power into his legs, into his lungs, into the shadows that wrapped around their small group. Lexi cried out as the pace increased, her wound tearing further, but there was no choice. No option except forward.

The Entities were closing from behind. He could feel them now, dozens of them, their attention fixed on the threshold like predators who'd cornered prey. If the breach closed before they reached it—

"GO!" Mira grabbed Seraphina's arm and threw her through the threshold. The mysterious woman tumbled into the ritual chamber, rolling across the stone floor, gasping as mortal air filled her lungs.

Drevik was five feet away. Four.

The threshold shrank by half.

"Drevik!" Ivy's voice rose to a scream. "I can't hold it—the Elders are pushing against me—they want it closed—"

Three feet. Two.

Lexi's body went limp against his shoulder, unconsciousness finally claiming her. Her weight increased, dragging at his stride, and he felt her starting to slip—

Mira appeared at his side, her hands closing around Lexi's other arm. "Together," she snarled, blood still seeping between her fingers from her own wound. "Don't you dare let her go."

One foot.

Drevik threw himself forward, Lexi's body clutched against his chest, Mira's hands gripping them both, and they crashed through the threshold like swimmers breaching the surface of a frozen lake.

The mortal realm hit him like a wall.

Air—real air, clean and sharp and tasting of dust and candle smoke—filled his lungs. The stone floor of the ritual chamber was cold against his back, solid and real in ways the Shadowlands never were. For one perfect moment, he simply lay there, breathing, feeling the weight of Lexi's unconscious form across his chest, the burning in his muscles slowly beginning to fade.

Then the screaming started.

Drevik scrambled upright, keeping Lexi against him, his eyes sweeping the chamber for the source of the chaos. What he saw made his blood freeze.

The ritual chamber was in ruins. The protective sigils had been torn from the walls, their silver lines now nothing but scorch marks on ancient stone. The bodies of the advance team lay scattered like broken dolls, their faces contorted in expressions of terror. And standing at the far end of the room, surrounded by a dozen acolytes in full combat gear, was Elder Kestran.

His face was unreadable, but his eyes—his eyes held something Drevik had never seen in an Elder before.

Fear.

"Secure the breach," Kestran ordered, his voice flat. "All remaining shadow-casters, reinforce the boundary. Nothing gets through."

"But the others—" one of the acolytes began.

"There are no others." Kestran's gaze found Drevik, and something flickered there—recognition, perhaps, or calculation. "The Shadowlands are lost. Everything beyond this threshold is now enemy territory."

"Lost?" Mira pushed herself to her feet, swaying slightly. Blood dripped from her side, pooling on the stone. "We just crossed through. The breach is holding. If you'd give us time to—"

"The breach is an open wound." Kestran's voice cut through her words like a blade. "Every second it remains, more of the Shadowlands bleeds through. We've already lost three acolytes to possession. The entities are using it as a doorway." He turned to the acolytes. "Seal it. Now."

"You'll kill Ivy!" Seraphina staggered forward, her earlier gracelessness forgotten in the face of the threat. "She's anchored to the threshold—if you close it while she's still connected—"

"Then she should have released her hold." Kestran didn't look at Ivy, who knelt in the center of her circle, her hands still raised, her eyes rolled back in her head as she maintained the spell that had saved them all. "The needs of the Order supersede any individual."

Something broke inside Drevik.

It wasn't anger—anger was too simple, too clean. This was something older and colder, a clarity that cut through exhaustion and pain like ice through flesh. He understood, in that moment, why the Order had survived for centuries. Why it had grown cruel.

It wasn't about protecting people. It was about protecting power.

"No."

The word fell into the chamber like a stone into still water. Every eye turned to him—acolytes, Elder, his fellow survivors. Even Ivy, lost in her trance, seemed to stir at the sound.

Kestran's expression didn't change. "You are an acolyte, Markinsoul. You do not give orders. You follow them."

"I'm not giving an order." Drevik rose to his feet, keeping Lexi cradled against his chest. His shadow stretched across the floor, responding to the power that still hummed through his veins. "I'm making a choice."

He walked toward Ivy's circle.

Every acolyte in the room moved to stop him—shadows coiling, hands reaching for weapons, faces set with the rigid certainty of those who followed orders without question. But before any of them could cross the distance, before Kestran could give the command that would end Ivy's life, Drevik's shadow moved.

It didn't spread like normal shadow. It didn't cling to the floor or hide in corners. It rose—a column of darkness that stretched from his feet to the ceiling, blocking the acolytes' path, forming a wall between them and Ivy's circle.

The room fell silent.

"Impossible," Kestran breathed. "That's not—no acolyte can—"

"I told you." Seraphina's voice was soft, but it carried in the sudden stillness. "I told you what he was becoming. What he found at the nexus." She moved to stand beside Drevik, her hand finding his shoulder. "The Shadowlands didn't want to keep him, Kestran. They wanted to follow him."

The Elder's face went even paler. "You touched the core."

"I didn't just touch it." Drevik's shadow writhed, reshaping itself into something that made the acolytes retreat another step. "I absorbed it. Part of it, anyway. Enough to understand what your Order has been missing for centuries." He met Kestran's eyes over the barrier. "The Shadowlands isn't our enemy. It's our mirror. And you've been trying to fight your own reflection."

"Pretty speeches won't save her." Kestran's voice had gone hard again, the momentary fear buried beneath centuries of discipline. "The threshold must close. If you've truly absorbed what you claim, then you know the cost of leaving it open. Every moment, more entities slip through. More innocents die. The balance—"

"Is already broken." Mira had moved to Ivy's other side, her hands positioning themselves above the unconscious woman's temples. "The breach at the nexus point changed everything. Closing this threshold won't fix it—it'll just force the pressure somewhere else. Somewhere we can't predict."

"Then we find that somewhere and we seal it too."

"You'll be chasing leaks forever." Drevik knelt beside Ivy, keeping his shadow-wall in place. "The Order's approach has been wrong from the start. You treat the Shadowlands like a disease when it's actually a circulatory system. You don't cure it by cutting it out—you heal it by changing the flow."

Kestran stared at him. For a long moment, no one moved. The only sounds were the flickering candles and Ivy's shallow breathing.

Then, slowly, the Elder lowered his hand.

"Show me."

The command hung in the air. Drevik felt the weight of every eye in the room—acolytes ready to strike if he made a wrong move, his fellow survivors swaying on their feet, Ivy's life depending on his next words.

"I need to reach into the threshold," he said carefully. "Ivy's anchored to it—if I can extend my shadow through the connection, I can stabilize the breach without closing it. Turn it from a wound into a... a doorway. Something controlled."

"And if you're wrong?"

Drevik looked down at Lexi's face, pale and still against his chest. At Ivy, whose nose had begun bleeding freely, whose life was slipping away with every second the threshold remained unstable. At Mira and Seraphina, both wounded, both watching him with expressions that mixed fear with desperate hope.

"Then I die with them."

The words settled into his bones as he spoke them. True words. Honest words. He would rather burn himself to nothing than watch another person he loved die because he'd been too slow, too weak, too afraid to try.

Kestran studied him for a long moment. Then, impossibly, something shifted in the Elder's ancient eyes.

"Proceed."

Drevik didn't waste the opportunity.

He closed his eyes and reached—not outward, but inward, into that place where the nexus power had taken root. The core of Shadowlands essence that now lived inside him, waiting to be used. It rose at his call like a living thing, eager and hungry and terribly, beautifully vast.

His shadow extended.

Not across the floor—through it. Through reality itself, threading into the threshold where Ivy's consciousness was tangled. He could feel her there, a bright thread of will holding open a door that should have been impossible for any single person to manage. She was fading, her grip slipping, but she hadn't let go. She'd held on for him. For all of them.

I've got you, he thought, wrapping his shadow around her essence. Let go now. I'll take it from here.

For a moment, nothing happened. Then, slowly, Ivy's bright thread began to release its hold, transferring the weight of the threshold into Drevik's waiting shadow.

It was like catching a falling mountain.

Drevik's body locked as the full force of the breach slammed through him—not pain, exactly, but pressure. Immense, crushing pressure that made every breath a battle. The threshold wanted to close. The entities on the other side wanted to push through. The laws of reality themselves screamed that this was wrong, impossible, forbidden.

Wrong, he agreed, sweat breaking across his forehead. But not impossible. Watch me.

He didn't fight the pressure. He shaped it. The power he'd absorbed at the nexus responded to his will, turning the chaotic energies of the breach into something ordered. Something controlled. Like water through a pipe, like electricity through a wire, like—

Like love through a bond.

The thought came unbidden, and with it came understanding. The Order had been wrong about so many things, but this most of all: they treated power as something to be hoarded, controlled, locked away. But power wasn't static. It was a relationship—a flow between source and recipient. The threshold wasn't a wound to be closed; it was a connection to be balanced.

Give and take. Push and pull. The way of shadows.

He felt the breach stabilize. The screaming pressure eased into something manageable, something sustainable. The doorway remained open, but now it was his doorway—controlled by his will, maintained by his shadow, powered by the core of Shadowlands essence that now lived inside him.

Drevik opened his eyes.

The ritual chamber was silent. The acolytes stood frozen, their weapons half-drawn, their faces masks of shock. Kestran had gone very still, his expression unreadable.

But Drevik only had eyes for the women in front of him.

Ivy had slumped forward the moment the weight transferred, her body finally releasing the strain it had carried for too long. Mira caught her before she could hit the ground, her earlier rivalry forgotten in the face of shared survival.

Seraphina had moved to Lexi's side, her hands hovering over the wound that still pulsed with corrupted violet light. "The shadow-poison is spreading. We need to treat this now, or—"

"I can help." Drevik's voice came out rougher than he intended, hoarse with exhaustion. "Let me try."

He didn't wait for permission. Kneeling beside Lexi, he placed his hand over her wound and pulled. The nexus power responded, drawing the shadow-poison out of her flesh and into his own shadow, where it could be safely dispersed. Lexi gasped, her eyes flying open, her body arching off the floor as the corruption left her.

"Drevik?" Her voice was weak, confused. "What... where..."

"Shh." He brushed the hair back from her face, his thumb tracing the line of her jaw. "I've got you. You're safe."

"Like hell I am." But she didn't pull away from his touch. "You look terrible."

"Look who's talking."

A ghost of her usual fire flickered in her green eyes. "I've been better." Her gaze swept the room—the acolytes still frozen, the Elder watching with an unreadable expression, the other women gathered close. "We made it?"

"We made it."

"Good." She closed her eyes again. "Because if you ever put me through something like that again, I'll kill you myself."

"Lexi—"

"I mean it." Her hand found his, squeezing weakly. "Don't ever do that to me again. Don't ever..." Her voice cracked. "Don't ever make me watch you almost die."

The words hung between them, weighted with everything they hadn't said. Everything they'd been dancing around for weeks, hiding behind rivalry and banter and the careful distance they'd maintained.

Drevik bent his head and kissed her.

It wasn't gentle. It wasn't hesitant. It was desperate and raw and tasting of blood and shadow, his lips crushing against hers like he was trying to pour everything he couldn't say into that single point of contact. For one heartbeat, she lay frozen—then her hand came up to fist in his hair, pulling him closer, kissing him back with equal ferocity.

Someone made a sound behind them. Mira, maybe, or Seraphina. Drevik didn't care. All that mattered was Lexi's mouth against his, her fingers in his hair, the fierce pounding of her heart beneath his palm.

When they finally broke apart, both gasping, her green eyes were bright with something that looked dangerously close to tears.

"You're an idiot," she whispered.

"I know."

"A complete and total idiot."

"I know."

"I..." She swallowed hard. "I can't believe you waited until now to do that."

"Bad timing?"

"The worst." But she was smiling, and her hand was still tangled in his hair, and for one perfect moment nothing else mattered.

Then Kestran cleared his throat.

Drevik turned, his body positioning itself between Lexi and the Elder without conscious thought. His shadow rose behind him, a silent warning.

"The threshold," Kestran said, his voice carefully neutral. "It's stable?"

"For now." Drevik rose, keeping one hand on Lexi's shoulder. "I can hold it indefinitely, but it's not a permanent solution. The breach at the nexus point needs to be addressed, or the pressure will keep building until something breaks."

"And you know how to address it?"

"I have ideas." He met the Elder's eyes steadily. "But I'll need access to the Order's archives. The real ones, not the sanitized versions they show acolytes. And I'll need help." His gaze swept the room, landing on each of the women in turn—Lexi, Mira, Seraphina, Ivy. "All of them."

Kestran was silent for a long moment. The acolytes shifted uneasily, their weapons still half-raised, their attention jumping between the Elder and the shadow-wrapped acolyte who'd just done the impossible.

"Very well," Kestran finally said. "But understand this, Markinsoul: whatever you've become, whatever power you now wield—you are still bound by the Order's laws. The threshold may be stable, but the consequences of tonight's events have only begun to unfold." His gaze hardened. "The other Elders are missing. Vanished from their chambers sometime during your... excursion. The Veiled Ones have been silent for weeks. And the breach at the nexus point is spreading faster than our projections anticipated."

The words settled into Drevik's chest like stones.

"Missing?"

"All of them. Every Elder who was not in this chamber tonight has disappeared. No sign of struggle. No trace of shadow-walking. Simply... gone." Kestran's mask slipped for just a moment, revealing the fear beneath. "Whatever is coming, Markinsoul, it has already begun. You may have bought us time with your stunt, but time is all it is."

Drevik looked down at Lexi, still pale and weak but watching him with steady eyes. At Mira, supporting an unconscious Ivy. At Seraphina, her mysterious air replaced by something raw and vulnerable.

He'd survived the Shadowlands. He'd mastered powers that should have been impossible. He'd held the threshold and kissed the woman he loved and proven himself stronger than anyone had expected.

But as Kestran's words sank in, as the weight of what came next settled onto his shoulders, Drevik understood that everything before this moment had been nothing but preparation.

The real test was just beginning.

"Then we'd better get started," he said, and his shadow rose to meet the coming storm.
