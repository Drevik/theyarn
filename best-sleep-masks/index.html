Best Sleep Masks for Better Sleep (That Don't Press on Your Eyes)

July 2026 · 5 products reviewed

I worked night shifts for two years and tried every sleep mask on the market. Most of them had the same problem: they pressed directly on my eyelids, which made my eyes feel weird and gave me blurry morning vision like I'd slept with my contacts in. The ones that didn't press on my eyes let light in from the bottom. Finding a mask that blocks all light AND doesn't touch your eyes felt like asking for a unicorn.

Then contoured sleep masks became a thing, and the whole category changed. Here's what actually works.

What Actually Matters

  • Total blackout: A sleep mask that lets in a sliver of light from the bottom is worse than no mask — your brain notices the inconsistency. The mask should seal completely around your nose and cheeks. Masks with nose wire adjustments (like the Manta) seal better than flat masks.
  • No pressure on eyes: This is the #1 reason people give up on sleep masks. If you can feel the mask pressing on your eyelids, it's the wrong design. Contoured masks with raised eye cups solve this completely — you can open your eyes underneath them.
  • Adjustable strap: One-size-fits-all is a lie. Head sizes vary enormously, and the strap needs to be adjustable without creating pressure points on your temples. Dual-strap designs stay in place better than single elastic bands.
  • Breathability: If you run hot at night, memory foam masks will make you sweat. Silk and cotton breathe better. If you sleep in a cold room, foam provides welcome insulation.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do sleep masks really help you sleep?

Yes, especially for day sleepers, city dwellers with streetlights, or anyone sharing a room with a light-on reader. They block 99-100% of light, signaling your brain to produce melatonin. Studies show improved sleep quality and faster time to fall asleep, particularly for shift workers and travelers.

Why do sleep masks hurt my eyes?

Most masks press directly on your eyelids. Contoured masks (like the Manta) have raised eye cups that sit away from your eyes, giving you room to blink. If your current mask hurts, switch to a contoured design.

What material is best for a sleep mask?

Silk is most comfortable against skin and causes least friction. Cotton is breathable and washable. Memory foam blocks the most light but can feel warm. If you run hot, go silk or cotton. For maximum blackout, go foam.

Can I wash a sleep mask?

Yes — hand wash in cool water with mild soap, lay flat to dry. Most masks lose elasticity after machine washing. Replace every 6-12 months when elastic stretches or fabric shows wear.

How do I keep a sleep mask from falling off?

Adjust the strap tighter (but not so tight it gives you a headache). Dual-strap designs stay in place better than single elastic bands. Side sleepers should look for masks with thin side panels.

Are expensive sleep masks worth it?

Up to $30, yes — better materials, better light blocking, and contoured eye cups make a real difference. Above $30, you're paying for brand or luxury materials that don't improve sleep quality.

Can side sleepers use sleep masks?

Yes, with the right mask. Contoured masks with thin side panels (Manta, JVKE) work best. Silk masks also work because they slide against the pillow. Avoid masks with thick padding on the sides.
