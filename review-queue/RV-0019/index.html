Core idea

You must recognize that your "default" settings regarding sex and love are not biological truths, but historical leftovers. Much of what you consider "natural" is actually an inherited blueprint from agrarian-era social structures that no longer serve your current reality. To move toward non-monogamy, you cannot simply delete old myths; you must actively replace them with intentional, chosen beliefs to avoid a psychological free fall. The goal is to transition from a reactive state—governed by invisible, inherited rules—to a proactive state governed by a pragmatic, ethics-based framework.

Key concepts and terms

  • The Invisible Premise: The unexamined assumption that lifelong, monogamous, heterosexual marriage is the only "normal" or "natural" way to exist. It operates in the background of your psyche until it causes a conflict or a "trip."
  • The Free Fall: The state of psychological instability that occurs when you reject old social dogmas but have not yet established a new, functional belief system to replace them.
  • Pragmatic Ethics: An ethical framework based on observable consequences rather than metaphysical "rules." It focuses on harm reduction and the promotion of well-being rather than adherence to abstract moral laws.
  • The Harm Test: A diagnostic tool used to evaluate an action or relationship by asking: Is anyone being harmed? Can this harm be avoided? Are the risks known and minimized?
  • The Growth Test: The positive counterpart to the Harm Test, focusing on whether an action facilitates learning, joy, and personal expansion.
  • Active Collaboration (Consent): A definition of consent that moves beyond "permission." It is an ongoing, communicative process designed for the mutual benefit, well-being, and pleasure of everyone involved.
  • Abundance Mindset: The belief that love and sexual energy are not finite resources that must be hoarded, but are instead entirely available and can increase through sharing.

Techniques worth drilling

  1. The Myth Deconstruction Protocol
  • Step 1: Identify the "Natural" Rule. Listen to your internal monologue for phrases like "It’s just how it is," "That’s not normal," or "You shouldn't feel that way."
  • Step 2: Trace the Origin. Ask yourself if this rule is based on a current physical reality or an inherited social structure (e.g., "Does this rule apply to me because of biology, or because of how I was raised?").
  • Step 3: Label the Myth. Explicitly name the rule as a "myth" rather than a "fact" to strip it of its authority.
  1. The Ethical Diagnostic (The Two-Way Test)
  • Step 1: Apply the Harm Test. Before making a change in your relationship structure, list every potential point of friction. Ask: Who is being hurt? Is the harm avoidable through communication? Are all parties aware of the risks?
  • Step 2: Apply the Growth Test. Evaluate the potential upside. Ask: Is this helping someone grow? Is this increasing the total amount of joy or learning in the situation?
  • Step 3: Balance the Equation. If the harm is avoidable and the growth is significant, proceed with a plan to mitigate the harm.
  1. The Belief Replacement Method
  • Step 1: Isolate the Vacuum. Identify a specific belief you have recently discarded (e.g., "I must be someone's 'only' to be valued").
  • Step 2: Draft a Replacement. Write a new, proactive belief that is grounded in your current reality (e.g., "My value is intrinsic and is not determined by the number of people who love me").
  • Step 3: Test for Utility. Ensure the new belief is functional. A good replacement belief should provide a "floor" for your psyche so you do not drift into the "free fall."
  1. The Active Consent Check-In
  • Step 1: Move from Permission to Collaboration. Instead of asking "Is this okay?", ask "How can we make this experience better for both of us?"
  • Step 2: Monitor the "Ongoing" Aspect. Treat consent as a live stream, not a recorded file. Periodically pause during an interaction to check if the "active collaboration" is still functioning for all parties.

Practice drill

The Rule-to-Replacement Audit

This week, you will perform a targeted audit of one specific inherited rule you still obey (e.g., rules about jealousy, rules about how much time you must spend with a partner, or rules about "proper" sexual behavior).

  1. Document: Write down the rule exactly as you hear it in your head.
  2. Analyze: Run that rule through the Harm Test (Is it causing unnecessary harm?) and the Growth Test (Is it preventing you or others from growing?).
  3. Rewrite: Write one sentence that represents a chosen belief you will adopt in its place. This new belief must be actionable and focused on the well-being of all involved.

Example: "I must hide my attraction to others to protect my partner's feelings" $\rightarrow$ "I will communicate my attractions transparently so we can build trust through honesty and manage any resulting jealousy together."

Critiques and caveats

  • The Risk of Negligence: Using a "Pragmatic Ethics" framework (the Harm Test) can lead to a dangerous "ends justify the means" mentality if you become too focused on the "Growth Test" while ignoring subtle, long-term emotional erosion.
  • The Complexity of Minimization: The instruction to "minimize risks" is difficult in practice. You cannot eliminate all risk in human connection; over-focusing on risk mitigation can lead to paralysis or hyper-vigilance, which can stifle the very "growth" you are seeking.
  • The Burden of Responsibility: Transitioning to "Active Collaboration" places a heavy cognitive and emotional load on the individual. It requires high-level communication skills that may not be present in all participants, potentially leading to a breakdown in the "Harm Test" if one party is unable to engage.

Reading notes

This section provides the psychological foundation for the rest of the book. While later chapters offer specific "how-to" logistics for managing multiple partners, chapters 2 and 3 focus on the internal reconfiguration required to make those logistics even possible. You cannot implement the techniques in the later chapters if you are still operating under the "Invisible Premise" discussed here.
