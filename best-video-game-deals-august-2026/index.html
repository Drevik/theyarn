🏆 Best Overall: Elden Ring Nightreign (PS5)

From Software's standalone co-op experiment landed at $60 and has been sliding down the price curve ever since. Right now you can grab the PS5 standard edition for around $25, sometimes dipping to $20 with an Amazon coupon. That's a 58 to 67% discount on a game that's barely a year old and still has an active player base.

Nightreign strips the open-world exploration of Elden Ring and replaces it with a roguelike loop: three-player squads drop into a shrinking map, loot up, and fight a boss before the night kills everyone. It's faster, tighter, and way more accessible than the main game. You don't need to have beaten Margit to enjoy this.

  • 3-player co-op roguelike set in the Elden Ring universe (works great solo too)
  • Dozens of runs worth of content; each session runs 30 to 45 minutes
  • Amazon Exclusive Deluxe Edition (ASIN: B0DVMVRN9Z) includes extra characters and a mini soundtrack for ~$30
  • Also available on Xbox Series X (ASIN: B0DWXZJ85P) at similar pricing

Honest downside: The roguelike structure means you're repeating the same map tiles. If you need the sprawling exploration of the original Elden Ring, this won't scratch that itch. The difficulty also spikes hard in later rounds. Expect to fail a lot before things click.

You're getting a 20 to 25 hour action RPG with genuinely impressive visuals for less than a fast-casual lunch.

Best Remake: Metal Gear Solid Delta: Snake Eater (PS5)

Konami's Unreal Engine 5 remake of the greatest stealth game ever made has been on my watch list since launch. The original MGS3 is a masterpiece of jungle infiltration, boss fights that double as character studies, and a story about loyalty that still hits. The Delta remake rebuilds the whole thing in modern visuals while keeping the gameplay bones intact.

Right now the Day One Edition (ASIN: B0D6PD4795) is hovering around $25 on Amazon, down from $69.99. That's a 64% discount. For context, this game launched in September 2025 and hasn't seen a price this low until now. The Tactical Edition (ASIN: B0D4RJGFDT), which bundles the original MG1/MG2 games, is $25.38.

  • Full UE5 visual overhaul: the jungle looks phenomenal with ray-traced lighting
  • Gameplay feels faithful: CQC, camouflage system, survival mechanics all intact
  • Available on PS5 and Xbox Series X at similar pricing
  • The CQC alone is worth the price of admission. Grabbing guards feels incredible on DualSense.

Honest downside: The voice acting is a mixed bag. Some original cast returned, some didn't, and the tonal shifts are noticeable if you played the 2004 version. The camera controls also feel dated in spots. This is a remake, not a reinvention, and certain design decisions from 20 years ago haven't aged well.

Borderlands 4 launched in September 2025 at $69.99 for the standard edition and has been on a slow, steady price decline ever since. Amazon currently has it at $24 to $30 depending on the day, which is a 40 to 52% drop. That's fast for a game that isn't even a year old.

Best RPG Remaster: Dragon Quest III HD-2D Remake (PS5)

Dragon Quest III is the game that defined the JRPG template in 1988. Square Enix's HD-2D remake, the same engine style as Octopath Traveler, gives it a visual glow-up while preserving the class system and exploration loop that made the original legendary. Amazon has the PS5 version (ASIN: B0D71JQXR4) at around $20, down from $59.99. The Switch version (ASIN: B0D7GXMDTF) is hovering around $39.

At $20 for PS5, you're getting a 40+ hour JRPG with modern presentation and classic mechanics. The class system alone (pick a hero, recruit a party, change classes mid-game) carries more depth than most modern RPGs bother with. No microtransactions, no battle pass, no live-service noise. Just a complete game.

  • HD-2D visual style looks incredible: pixel art meets 3D depth of field
  • Full class system with mid-game class changing for party customization
  • No microtransactions, no DLC: the whole game is on the disc
  • PS5 version at ~$20 is the best price-per-hour ratio on this list after Lost Soul Aside

Honest downside: It's a faithful remake of a 1988 game, which means the encounter rate can feel relentless by modern standards. There's no "disable random battles" toggle. If you need QoL features like auto-battle or speed-up options, the options here are limited compared to something like the recent Final Fantasy Pixel Remasters.

Best Dark Horse Pick: Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (PS5)

Clair Obscur came out of nowhere. A debut game from Sandfall Interactive built on Unreal Engine 5, it delivers turn-based RPG combat with real-time action inputs. Think a JRPG with Souls-like dodge and parry mechanics layered on top. The art direction is wild: Belle Époque aesthetics filtered through a surreal, painterly nightmare. It's one of the best-looking games of 2026.

The PS5 standard edition (ASIN: B0DK6225DY) is sitting around $37 on Amazon with a coupon, down from $49.99. The Xbox version (ASIN: B0DK61BM3R) is at a similar price point. For a game that launched at full price and earned widespread critical praise, that's a solid entry fee.

  • Turn-based combat with real-time dodge/parry: the hybrid system is genuinely fresh
  • Some of the best art direction in any 2026 release (screenshots don't do it justice)
  • 30 to 40 hour campaign with meaningful side content
  • The combat depth rewards replay and experimentation with party builds

Honest downside: The parry timing windows are punishing, and the game doesn't explain its mechanics well. Expect to spend the first few hours confused about which stats matter and how the skill tree actually works. The story also takes about 10 hours to hit its stride. The opening is slow by design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are physical game discs still worth buying?

Yes, if you care about resale value or sharing games with friends. Discs also avoid the storage-eating problem of 80GB+ digital installs. The trade-off is convenience: you have to swap discs and wait for patches either way. If you're someone who sells games after finishing them, a physical copy at $25 that you can resell for $15 effectively costs you $10. That math doesn't work with digital.

How low can newly released games actually drop in price?

It depends on the publisher and how fast sales slow down. First-party Nintendo games barely budge. Mario Kart World is still $51 on a good day. But third-party AAA titles like Borderlands 4 or Elden Ring Nightreign can hit 40 to 60% off within six months of launch, sometimes faster on Amazon. The key is watching for the second price drop, not the first. The initial 20% off sale is usually followed by a 50% off sale two months later.

Should I wait for Black Friday instead of buying now?

If a game is already 50% or more off, pull the trigger. Prices that low rarely stick around. Black Friday might save you another $5 on the same title, but you're gambling on stock levels and paying four more months of opportunity cost. You could have been playing it instead of staring at your backlog.

Is the Razer BlackShark V2 X good enough for competitive FPS?

For the price, yes. The 7.1 surround sound and cardioid mic punch above the $40 price point. You won't get the same positional precision as a $150 headset with dedicated DAC, but for casual-to-mid-tier competitive play, it holds its own. The key limitation is the 3.5mm connection: you're not getting virtual surround on console without the USB adapter Razer sells separately.

What's the single best game deal right now?

Lost Soul Aside at $10 (from $60) is absurd value for a brand-new PS5 action RPG. If you own a PlayStation, grab it before the sale ends. It's the kind of discount that usually only shows up on games that are two years old and forgotten, not on something released in 2025.

Are Amazon game prices reliable or do they fluctuate?

They fluctuate constantly. Amazon uses dynamic pricing, so a $10 price today could be $30 tomorrow. If you see a deal that's 50%+ off, bookmark it and buy within 24 hours. I've verified every price in this article as of August 19, 2026, but I can't guarantee they'll hold. CamelCamelCamel is your friend for historical price tracking.
