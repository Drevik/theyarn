Chapter 13: Loyalties Tested

Chapter 13: Loyalties Tested

The words of his mother's letter burned in Drevik's memory like acid etched into glass.

They're using you, my son. The Order doesn't protect the balance—they exploit it. And you're the key to something much darker than they'll ever admit.

He stood at the window of the safehouse, watching shadows pool in the alley below. The lunar cycle was waxing, which meant his power was building, but it also meant the Shadowlands grew restless. Everything grew restless during the waxing moon.

"You've read it seventeen times."

Drevik didn't turn at Lexi's voice. The letter was creased now, the paper worn soft from his fingers. "Something doesn't make sense. If she knew the Order was corrupt, why leave me with them? Why let me be raised as an acolyte at all?"

"Maybe she didn't have a choice." Lexi moved to stand beside him, close enough that he could feel the warmth radiating from her skin. "Maybe she was trying to protect you the only way she knew how."

"By lying to me my entire life?" Drevik finally turned to face her. In the dim light of the safehouse, Lexi's auburn hair caught the glow of the streetlamps outside, turning it to burnished copper. Her green eyes held that familiar intensity, but beneath it, he saw something else. Uncertainty. The same uncertainty that had been gnawing at him since they'd found the letter hidden in his mother's effects. "That doesn't feel like protection. It feels like abandonment."

Lexi's jaw tightened. "You don't know what it's like—the pressure inside the Order. The choices people make to survive." She crossed her arms, a defensive posture he'd learned to read over the months. "My mother made choices too. Choices I didn't understand until I was older. Sometimes the people who love us hurt us because they don't see another way."

"Is that supposed to make me feel better?"

"No." Her voice softened. "It's supposed to make you feel less alone."

Before Drevik could respond, the door to the main room swung open. Mira swept in, her dark hair pulled back in a severe knot, her expression carved from the same stone as the Order's founding pillars. Behind her came Sera, her mysterious gaze moving over the room like she was cataloging exits.

"We need to talk," Mira said. "The mission parameters have changed."

The five of them gathered around the table in the safehouse's main room: Drevik, Lexi, Mira, Sera, and Kade—the last minute addition to their team, a Warden with more experience than the rest of them combined. Kade's silver-streaked hair and scarred hands spoke to decades of service, but his eyes remained sharp, missing nothing.

Mira spread a map across the table, marking positions with quick, precise movements. "The extraction point is here. We have twelve hours before the lunar peak, which means we need to move soon if we're going to reach the shrine before the Shadowlands breach."

"Hold on." Drevik's voice cut through her tactical planning. "We're not just going to continue like nothing happened."

Mira's dark eyes fixed on him. "The mission—"

"The mission was based on intelligence from the Order." Drevik stepped forward, planting his hands on the table. "Intelligence that my mother's letter suggests might be compromised. We don't even know what we're really walking into."

"The shrine contains an artifact," Mira said, her tone carefully controlled. "An artifact that could stabilize the breach points. That hasn't changed."

"But why us?" Lexi spoke up, her voice sharp. "Why send a team of junior members for something this important? If the Order has Elders, Wardens with decades of experience—" She turned to Kade. "No offense."

"None taken." Kade's weathered face remained impassive. "She's asking the right question."

Mira's composure cracked, just slightly. "The mission requires specific abilities. Shadow Walking precision. Veil Sight acuity. You were selected because you have the necessary skills."

"Or because we're expendable." Drevik straightened. "That's what my mother's letter implies. That the Order sends acolytes on dangerous missions to test them—to see who survives. Who's useful."

"That's not—" Mira started.

"It is." Sera's soft voice silenced the argument. The mysterious woman had been quiet since they'd arrived at the safehouse, but now she leaned forward, her pale eyes reflecting lamplight like mirrors. "You know it's true, Mira. You've known since the beginning."

The silence that followed stretched thin as spider silk.

"What is she talking about?" Lexi's hand moved to the knife at her hip—a reflexive gesture, born from years of Order training. "Mira?"

Mira's face went still. Too still. The kind of stillness that preceded an explosion.

"The Order has... factions," she said finally. "Different chapters with different philosophies about how to maintain the balance. The Northern Chapter believes in testing members through practical challenges. The Southern Chapter favors tradition and careful study. The Eastern Chapter—" She stopped herself.

"The Eastern Chapter believes the Order needs new leadership." Sera's voice remained soft, but an edge ran beneath it. "They believe the current Elders have lost sight of the true purpose. And they've been watching Drevik since his Initiation."

Drevik felt the blood drain from his face. "What?"

"You have abilities that haven't been seen in generations." Mira's mask was cracking now, fragments of emotion showing through. "The Sight isn't just about perceiving the Shadowlands. You can see possibilities. Futures. That makes you valuable to people who want to reshape the Order."

"I was sent to evaluate you." The words came out flat, controlled. "To determine if you would be receptive to the Eastern Chapter's goals. To see if you could be... recruited."

The safehouse suddenly felt smaller, the walls pressing in. Drevik stared at Mira—the woman who had trained him, pushed him, challenged him. The woman he had started to trust.

"You were spying on me."

"I was doing my duty." Mira's chin lifted. "The mission parameters changed when your mother's letter surfaced. The Northern Chapter wants you contained. The Eastern Chapter wants you cultivated. And the Southern Chapter wants to use you as a bargaining chip in their political games."

"And you?" Drevik's voice came out harder than he intended. "What do you want?"

For the first time since he'd known her, Mira's composure shattered completely. Her carefully maintained mask fell away, revealing something raw beneath. Something that looked almost like pain.

"I wanted—" She stopped. Swallowed. Started again. "I was supposed to report on your abilities. Your weaknesses. Your loyalties. But you weren't supposed to be—" She turned away, her shoulders rigid. "You weren't supposed to matter."

Lexi moved first, positioning herself between Drevik and Mira. Her protective instincts had sharpened over the months they'd served together, and Drevik had learned to recognize the set of her shoulders, the angle of her stance. She was ready for a fight.

"What game are you playing?" Lexi demanded. "What do the Eastern Chapter really want?"

"They want change." Sera answered when Mira remained silent. "They want to tear down the old hierarchy and build something new. And they believe Drevik could be the catalyst."

"Or the excuse." Kade spoke for the first time since the confrontation began. His gravelly voice carried the weight of long experience. "A figurehead is useful. But figureheads can also be disposed of when they've served their purpose."

Drevik's head was spinning. The letter. The factions. Mira's betrayal—or was it something more complicated? He looked at each member of the team in turn, trying to see past the surface.

"The mission," he said slowly. "The shrine. The artifact. What's really there?"

Mira turned back to face him, and something in her expression had shifted. The professional mask was gone, replaced by something more vulnerable.

"There is an artifact," she said. "But it's not what we were told. The shrine contains a binding circle—one of the oldest in existence. The Order wants to use it to seal something away. Something that's been trying to break through for centuries."

"What kind of something?" Lexi's hand hadn't left her knife.

"A Shadowlands entity," Sera said. "One of the Old Ones. The Order has been keeping it contained for three hundred years, but the seals are weakening. The Eastern Chapter believes it should be destroyed entirely. The Northern Chapter wants to study it. And the Southern Chapter—" She smiled, a thin expression without warmth. "The Southern Chapter wants to make a deal with it."

The implications settled over the room like a suffocating blanket. Drevik thought about his mother's letter, about the warnings she'd left behind. About the Order he'd trusted—the structure he'd climbed, the people he'd believed in.

"They're using me," he said. "All of them. I'm just a tool to whichever faction wins."

"Yes." Mira's agreement came without hesitation. "You are. To them."

"But not to you?"

The question hung in the air between them. Mira's dark eyes met his, and Drevik saw something there that surprised him. Something that looked almost like the same feeling he'd been trying to suppress for months.

"No," she said quietly. "Not anymore."

The argument that followed lasted for over an hour.

Kade wanted to abort the mission entirely. "We don't have enough information," he insisted. "Walking into a three-hundred-year-old binding circle with factions fighting over the outcome? That's suicide, not strategy."

Sera argued for continuing. "The shrine is real. The threat is real. Whether or not we trust the Order's motives, the entity in that circle needs to be dealt with. If the seals fail, we're looking at a breach event that could consume the entire city."

Lexi wanted to know who they could trust. "If Mira was sent to evaluate Drevik, who else has an agenda? How do we know you're not working for the Southern Chapter?" she demanded, turning to Sera. "How do we know Kade isn't Northern Chapter's backup plan?"

"Because," Kade said dryly, "if I were Northern Chapter, I'd have killed the boy already and collected the reward for his body."

That silenced the room.

Drevik stepped away from the table, needing space to think. The safehouse felt suffocating, every shadow a potential threat. He moved toward the narrow hallway that led to the bedrooms, but stopped when he felt a hand on his arm.

Lexi's grip was firm, her fingers pressing into the muscle above his elbow.

"We need a moment," she announced to the room. "Don't follow."

She pulled him down the hallway and into the smaller of the two bedrooms, closing the door firmly behind them. The room was sparse—a narrow bed, a wooden chair, a window overlooking the alley. But it was private, and that was all they needed.

"Talk to me." Lexi's voice was quiet now, stripped of the aggression she'd shown in the main room. "What are you thinking?"

Drevik laughed, the sound hollow. "I'm thinking that everything I believed about my life is a lie. The Order, my training, my mother—" He shook his head. "I don't know what's real anymore."

"I'm real." Lexi stepped closer, close enough that he could see the gold flecks in her green eyes. "This is real. Whatever else is happening, that hasn't changed."

"Hasn't it?" Drevik's voice cracked. "You're Order-born. Your mother is an Elder. Everything you've ever believed—" He pulled away, turning toward the window. "How can I ask you to choose between me and everything you were raised to believe in?"

"You're not asking." Lexi moved behind him, her warmth pressing against his back. Her arms wrapped around his waist, pulling him against her. "I'm telling you. I made my choice months ago, Drevik. Before I even understood what I was choosing."

He turned in her arms, facing her. The lamplight from the window caught her features, illuminating the fierce determination in her expression.

"The Order raised me," she continued. "They trained me, shaped me, gave me purpose. But they didn't teach me this." She pressed her hand flat against his chest, over his heart. "They didn't teach me what it feels like to care about someone more than duty. More than hierarchy. More than survival."

"Lexi—"

"Let me finish." Her eyes glistened, but her voice remained steady. "My whole life, I've been told that the Order comes first. That personal attachments are weaknesses to be overcome. That love is a luxury we can't afford." She swallowed hard. "But watching you—watching you fight, watching you grow, watching you become the person you were meant to be—I realized something."

"What?"

"The Order is wrong." The words came out fierce, almost angry. "Not just about you. About everything. About me." She pulled back slightly, her hands moving to frame his face. "They taught me to protect the mission at all costs. But they never taught me that some things are worth more than any mission."

Drevik stared at her, his heart pounding against his ribs. He'd known, somewhere in the back of his mind, that this was coming. That the tension between them had been building toward something inevitable. But he hadn't expected it to happen like this—in a cramped safehouse room, surrounded by enemies and uncertainty, with the weight of betrayal pressing down on them from all sides.

"I don't know what happens next," he admitted. "I don't know who to trust or what path to take. But I know—" He stopped, gathering his courage. "I know I don't want to face it without you."

Lexi's expression softened. "You won't have to."

She kissed him.

It wasn't their first kiss—that had happened weeks ago, in the aftermath of a mission that had nearly killed them both. But this was different. That first kiss had been desperate, fueled by adrenaline and fear. This kiss was deliberate. A choice. A declaration.

Lexi's lips moved against his with purpose, her hands sliding from his face to his shoulders, pulling him closer. Drevik responded without thinking, his arms wrapping around her waist, drawing her body against his. The heat of her seeped through their clothes, and he felt himself hardening in response.

"Lexi," he murmured against her mouth. "We shouldn't—not now—"

"Why not?" She pulled back just enough to meet his eyes. Her pupils were blown wide, dark with desire. "We could be walking to our deaths. We could be facing the end of everything we've believed in. Why shouldn't we take this moment?"

She had a point. Every argument he could make about timing, about appropriateness, about the others in the next room—all of it seemed meaningless against the reality of their situation. The mission was compromised. Their loyalties were divided. The Order they'd both served was fractured by internal politics that saw them as nothing more than tools.

But this—whatever was growing between them—this was real.

Drevik stopped fighting it.

He pulled Lexi back to him, capturing her mouth in a kiss that was deeper, more demanding than before. She responded with equal intensity, her fingers working at the buttons of his shirt with practiced efficiency. He felt the fabric part, felt the cool air against his skin, and then her hands were on him, palms flat against his chest, sliding over the muscles he'd built through months of training.

"You've gotten stronger," she whispered, breaking the kiss to trace a line down his sternum.

"You've gotten more impatient."

She laughed—a genuine sound that surprised them both. In the darkness of their situation, the laughter felt like a small rebellion. A refusal to let the circumstances strip them of joy.

"Maybe I'm just tired of waiting." Lexi's hands continued their exploration, sliding around to his back, pulling him toward the narrow bed. "Maybe I've decided that some things shouldn't wait."

The bed creaked as they fell onto it, Lexi beneath him, her auburn hair spreading across the thin pillow. Drevik braced himself on his arms, looking down at her. In the dim light filtering through the window, her face was half in shadow, half in light. The duality suited her—the Order soldier and the woman who had chosen to stand against everything she'd been taught.

"I don't have protection," he said, the practical words feeling absurd in the moment.

Lexi reached into the pocket of her jacket, which had fallen to the floor beside the bed, and produced a small packet. "Acolyte training teaches us to be prepared for everything."

Drevik laughed, the sound lighter than he'd expected. "You planned this?"

"I hoped." Her expression grew serious again. "I've been hoping for a while now."

The admission sent a pulse of heat through him. Drevik leaned down, pressing his lips to the curve of her neck, feeling her pulse quicken beneath his mouth. Her hands found his belt, working the buckle with efficient movements, and then she was pushing his pants down, her fingers wrapping around him.

He groaned against her skin, the sensation of her touch almost overwhelming after months of tension. "Lexi—"

"Let me." She shifted beneath him, guiding him to her entrance. "I want to feel you. Just you."

Drevik pushed forward, sinking into her warmth, and the world narrowed to this moment—her body beneath his, her breath hot against his ear, her hands clutching at his shoulders. He moved slowly at first, giving her time to adjust, but she was having none of it.

"Faster," she demanded, her hips rising to meet his. "I don't want slow. I want—"

She didn't finish the sentence, but he understood. She wanted intensity. She wanted passion. She wanted to feel something real in the midst of all the lies.

He gave her what she wanted.

Their bodies moved together with increasing urgency, the narrow bed creaking in protest. Drevik buried his face in the curve of her neck, breathing in the scent of her—sweat and leather and something floral beneath it all. Lexi's legs wrapped around his waist, pulling him deeper, her breath coming in sharp gasps.

"Look at me," she commanded, and he raised his head to meet her eyes. "I want to see you."

He held her gaze as they moved, watching the pleasure build in her expression. Her green eyes darkened, her lips parted, and when she finally came, it was with his name on her lips—a sound he would remember for the rest of his life.

He followed her over the edge moments later, the release crashing through him like a wave.

They lay together afterward, tangled in each other and the thin blanket, their breathing gradually slowing. Drevik's mind was quiet for the first time since he'd read his mother's letter. The noise of betrayal and uncertainty had faded, replaced by the simple reality of Lexi's warmth against his side.

"This doesn't solve anything," he said eventually.

"I know." Her fingers traced idle patterns on his chest. "But it reminds me what I'm fighting for."

They emerged from the bedroom to find the others waiting in tense silence.

Mira stood by the window, her posture rigid. Sera had taken a position near the door, her mysterious gaze fixed on something only she could see. Kade remained at the table, his scarred hands folded in front of him.

Drevik felt heat rise to his face, but Lexi showed no embarrassment. She walked into the room with her head held high, moving to stand beside him like she belonged there. Like she'd always belonged there.

"We've made a decision," Drevik said, addressing the room. "We're continuing the mission."

Kade raised an eyebrow but said nothing.

"The shrine is real," Drevik continued. "The threat is real. Whatever the Order's motives, whatever the factions want—the entity in that binding circle needs to be dealt with. We can't just walk away."

"And after?" Mira asked. "What happens when we've completed the mission?"

"We figure out which side we're on." Drevik met her eyes, seeing the complex emotions she was trying to hide. "Or maybe we decide we don't need sides. Maybe we find our own path."

Sera smiled, a thin expression that held more warmth than he'd seen from her before. "That sounds like something the Eastern Chapter would say."

"Maybe it is." Drevik straightened. "But it's my choice. Not theirs. Not the Northern Chapter's. Not anyone's but mine."

The room fell silent, processing his declaration. Then Kade stood, his weathered face unreadable.

"Well then," he said. "We have a mission to complete. I suggest we start moving."

They gathered their gear in efficient silence—weapons, supplies, communication devices. The mission had been compromised, but they were still trained acolytes. They still had a job to do.

Drevik was checking his Shadow Walking focus when Mira approached him. Her expression was carefully controlled, but he could see the cracks in her composure.

"I meant what I said earlier," she told him quietly. "You weren't supposed to matter."

"But I do."

"Yes." Her voice dropped lower. "You do. And that complicates things for me in ways I didn't expect."

"Your report to the Eastern Chapter—"

"I haven't filed it." Mira's jaw tightened. "I've had opportunities. Chances to send information that would have given them leverage over you. And I haven't taken any of them."

"Why?"

She looked away, her dark eyes fixed on something in the distance. "Because somewhere along the way, you became more than an assignment. You became—" She stopped herself. "You became someone I don't want to see hurt."

Before Drevik could respond, she turned and walked away, joining Sera at the door. But her words lingered, adding another layer to the tangled web of relationships he was navigating.

Lexi appeared at his side, her hand finding his. "You okay?"

"I don't know." He squeezed her fingers. "Ask me when this is over."

"If it's ever over."

"Then I guess we'll have to keep asking."

They moved out into the night, the waxing moon casting silver light across the city streets. The Shadowlands were stirring—he could feel it in the tension in the air, the way shadows seemed to move at the edges of his vision. The breach was coming, and they were walking toward it with eyes open.

The attack came without warning.

They were two blocks from the shrine when the shadows around them began to move. Not the natural movement of light and darkness—something else. Something wrong.

"Contact!" Kade's voice cut through the night air, and then the shadows were exploding outward, forming shapes that shouldn't exist outside of nightmares.

Shadowlands entities. At least a dozen of them, their forms flickering between dimensions, half-materialized and half-ghost. They moved with terrifying speed, flowing around corners, emerging from alleys, pouring out of the darkness between streetlights.

Drevik's Veil Sight activated automatically, and he saw them fully—not just the shadow forms, but the writhing darkness beneath. These weren't minor entities, the kind that occasionally slipped through natural breach points. These were hunters. And they were tracking something.

"Form up!" Lexi's voice was steady despite the chaos. She'd drawn her blade, the enchanted steel gleaming in the moonlight. "Protect Drevik!"

"I don't need protecting—" he started, but then one of the entities was on him, its shadow-wrapped form slamming into his chest.

He went down hard, feeling the cold of the Shadowlands seep through his skin. The entity was trying to pull him across—to drag him into the dimension where physical laws didn't apply, where humans were nothing but prey.

Shadow Walking instinct took over. Drevik reached for the darkness, not to fight it but to become it. His form flickered, destabilizing, and suddenly he was behind the entity instead of beneath it. His blade found its core, and the thing shrieked—a sound that existed somewhere between hearing and feeling—and dissolved.

"One down!" he called, but the others were already engaged.

Lexi moved like a dancer, her blade cutting precise arcs through the air. Each strike was designed to sever the connection between the entity and the physical realm, and her efficiency was terrifying to watch. Kade fought with the methodical brutality of long experience, while Sera used some form of magic Drevik had never seen before—her hands glowing with pale light that seemed to burn the shadows away.

Mira—

Mira wasn't fighting.

She stood at the edge of the battle, her hands raised, and Drevik realized she was chanting. Not attacking. Communicating.

"Mira!" He Shadow Walked to her side, appearing in a flicker of darkness. "What are you doing?"

Her eyes were unfocused, seeing something beyond the physical realm. "They're not attacking randomly. They're here for us specifically. Someone told them where we would be."

The implications hit him like a physical blow. A traitor. Someone on their team was feeding information to the Shadowlands.

"Who?"

Mira's chant ended, and the remaining entities faltered, their coordination breaking. "I don't know. But I can feel the connection—the signal is coming from nearby."

Drevik spun, scanning the battlefield. Lexi was holding her own against two entities. Kade had dispatched another and was moving to assist Sera. Everyone was accounted for. Everyone was fighting.

Everyone except—

His gaze fell on Kade's weathered face, the silver-streaked hair, the scarred hands.

The hands that had stayed folded during the argument. The man who had said nothing during the revelations about the Order's factions.

Kade met his eyes across the chaos, and something cold and knowing passed between them.

Then the older man smiled, raised his hand in a casual salute, and stepped backward into a shadow that shouldn't have been large enough to hold him.

The Shadowlands entities renewed their attack with redoubled fury.

"Kade was the traitor," Drevik breathed, the realization crashing over him. "He's been signaling our position this whole time."

Mira's expression went hard. "Then we finish this without him."

The battle resumed, fiercer than before. But now Drevik knew the shape of the threat—not just the entities in front of them, but the betrayal that had brought them here.

And somewhere in the back of his mind, his mother's warning echoed again.

They're using you, my son.

The worst part was, he was starting to understand exactly how.
