Chapter 8: Seraphina's Secret

Chapter 8: Seraphina's Secret

The coffee shop sat at the edge of campus, wedged between a used bookstore and a psychic's parlor that had never seemed to have any customers. Drevik had walked past it dozens of times without giving it a second thought. Now, standing across the street with the afternoon light slanting low through the autumn leaves, he studied it like it might hold answers to questions he hadn't known to ask until today.

The Black Roast. Clever name for a place that catered to the Order's members, though he suspected most of its regular patrons had no idea what lurked beneath the surface of their everyday lives.

His phone buzzed in his pocket. A single word from an unknown number: Inside.

Seraphina had found him first, of course. She always did.

He crossed the street, pushing through the coffee shop's heavy wooden door. The interior was all exposed brick and warm lighting, the kind of aggressively cozy atmosphere that tried too hard to feel authentic. A handful of students hunched over laptops in worn armchairs. An older man in a tweed jacket read a newspaper by the window, his coffee going cold beside him.

And in the far corner, partially hidden by a shelf of dog-eared paperbacks, sat Seraphina Vane.

She looked different than she had at the gala. Gone were the elegant gown and the confident smirk she wore like armor. Instead, she'd pulled her dark hair into a loose ponytail and dressed in clothes that blended in—dark jeans, a gray sweater that looked soft enough to sleep in, boots that had seen better days. She could have been any student killing time between classes.

Except for the way her eyes tracked every person who entered or left. The way her fingers tapped a restless rhythm against her cup. The tension in her shoulders.

Drevik approached slowly, giving her time to signal if something was wrong. She met his gaze and held up one finger—not wait, but caution. One moment.

He ordered a black coffee he didn't want and settled into the chair across from her only when she finally nodded.

"You came," she said quietly. Her voice was pitched low, barely audible over the hiss of the espresso machine. "I wasn't certain you would."

"You said you had information. About the attack on the ceremony." He wrapped his hands around his cup, letting the heat ground him. "And about me."

Seraphina's jaw tightened. She glanced toward the window, then the door, then the back hallway that presumably led to restrooms and a storage closet. Watching. Waiting.

"I have enemies, Drevik. Enemies who would pay dearly to know I'm sitting here with you right now."

"Then why meet at all?"

She turned back to him, and for the first time since he'd known her, her confident mask slipped. What showed beneath was something raw and frightened and fiercely determined.

"Because you're the only one who might understand what I'm about to tell you. The only one with the power to do something about it."

Her hand moved toward his across the table—stopped just short of touching. "I need your help. And in exchange, I'll give you the truth about everything. The attack. The traitor. And why they targeted you specifically."

Drevik studied her face. The shadows under her eyes suggested she hadn't been sleeping. The slight tremor in her hands spoke of more than caffeine. This wasn't the Seraphina Vane who had charmed and manipulated her way through the Order's social circles. This was someone else entirely—someone who had been hiding in plain sight, just like him.

"Start talking," he said. "We don't have much time."

She exhaled slowly. Nodded.

"Not here. Somewhere with shadows."

The alley behind the coffee shop was narrow and poorly lit, sandwiched between the building and a high brick wall. The afternoon sun couldn't reach it, leaving pools of darkness between the overflowing dumpsters and the fire escapes.

Drevik followed Seraphina into the dimness, his senses sharpening. This was a terrible idea—following a woman he barely trusted into an enclosed space where anyone could be waiting. But the alternative was letting her walk away with information he needed.

She turned to face him, pressing her back against the brick wall. Even in the shadows, he could see her chest rising and falling with quick, shallow breaths.

"What I'm about to show you," she said, "cannot leave this alley. If the wrong people learn what I am..."

"They'll kill you," Drevik finished.

"Worse." Her voice dropped to a whisper. "They'll use me. Study me. Tear me apart to understand how I exist."

"Seraphina—"

She reached up and pulled the hair from her ponytail, letting it fall loose around her shoulders. Then she closed her eyes and stepped deeper into the shadow cast by a fire escape above.

Drevik's breath caught.

Where the darkness touched her skin, she began to glow. A soft, ethereal luminescence emanated from beneath her flesh—not from outside, but from within. Veins of pale violet light traced patterns across her arms, her throat, the delicate lines of her jaw. Her hair seemed to float in currents of invisible energy, and when she opened her eyes, they weren't the dark brown he remembered.

They were silver. Completely, impossibly silver, like polished mirrors reflecting a moon that wasn't there.

"My mother," Seraphina said, and her voice had changed too—layered now, harmonic, like two voices speaking in perfect unison, "was one of the first women to successfully return from the Shadowlands."

Drevik's mind reeled. Return from the Shadowlands. The parallel dimension that gave the Order its power, that harbored creatures of nightmare and entropy. The place where Veiled Ones went and never came back.

"That's impossible," he said. "No one returns from—"

"She did." Seraphina stepped forward, and the glow intensified. "Three days she was gone. Three days in a realm where time moves differently, where reality itself is fluid. When she came back, she was... changed. Altered. And pregnant with me."

She held out her hand, palm up. The light beneath her skin pulsed in rhythm with something deeper than a heartbeat.

"I am half human, half Shadowlands entity. The only one of my kind to survive birth. The only one the Order doesn't know about."

Drevik stared at her glowing form, trying to process what he was seeing. "How is that possible? How has no one discovered you before now?"

Seraphina's glow dimmed slightly as she pulled back from the edge of revelation. The silver faded from her eyes, leaving them brown again—but Drevik had seen what lurked beneath the surface now. He couldn't unsee it.

"My mother hid me. Taught me to control it, to suppress the glow, to blend in." Her voice was bitter. "She made me promise never to reveal what I am. And then she died, and I was left alone with a secret that could destroy me."

"Why tell me?"

She met his eyes directly. "Because you have the Sight, Drevik. You can see possibilities. You can see the paths that others miss." She moved closer, and he caught her scent—something floral and dark, like night-blooming jasmine mixed with ozone. "I've watched you. Studied you. You're different from the others in the Order. You question things. You see through lies."

"That doesn't explain why you're trusting me with your life."

Seraphina's expression shifted—vulnerability bleeding through the careful mask.

"Because I'm tired of being alone." The words came out rough, almost angry. "I'm tired of hiding. And because you're the first person I've met who might actually be able to help me instead of using me."

She was close now. Close enough that Drevik could feel the heat radiating from her body—warmer than it should be, like she was running a constant low fever. Her heartbeat, when he focused on it, was slower than a human's. Much slower. And that strange, layered quality to her voice...

"You said you had information about the attack," Drevik managed. "About a traitor."

Seraphina nodded, but her gaze lingered on his face with an intensity that made his skin prickle.

"The breach at the ceremony wasn't random. Someone in the Order created it deliberately. They weakened the Veil from inside, allowing entities to pass through."

"Who?"

"I don't know the name. But I know they're planning something bigger. The full moon ceremony in three weeks—they're going to use it to open a permanent gate."

Drevik's blood ran cold. A permanent gate between the mortal realm and the Shadowlands would mean chaos. Entities flooding through unchecked. Thousands of deaths before the Order could respond.

"Why tell me this? Why not go to the Elders?"

Seraphina laughed—a brittle, hollow sound. "The Elders? Half of them would order my execution for what I am. The other half would hand me over to researchers who'd treat me like a lab rat." She shook her head. "No. You're the only one I can trust with this, Drevik. You and your strange gifts and your ridiculous sense of honor."

She reached out and touched his arm. Her fingers burned against his skin, that fever-heat seeping through his sleeve.

"I need protection. A place to hide when things get dangerous. Someone who can shield me from both the entities that hunt me and the Order members who'd destroy me." Her eyes searched his face. "In exchange, I'll be your eyes and ears. I can sense Shadowlands energy in ways even the Veiled Ones can't. I'll help you find the traitor."

Drevik's mind raced through the implications. An alliance with Seraphina would give him an incredible advantage—but it would also paint a target on his back. If the Order discovered he was sheltering a half-Shadowlands being, they'd demand answers he couldn't give. If the traitor learned she was sharing information...

"You're asking me to keep a secret from everyone I'm supposed to trust."

"I'm asking you to trust your instincts over your orders." Seraphina stepped even closer. Her body heat washed over him like a wave. "I've seen how you look at me, Drevik. I've felt the pull between us."

His throat tightened. "That's not—"

"Don't." Her voice softened. "Don't pretend you don't feel it. I'm part Shadowlands entity. I can sense these things—the way your energy reaches for mine when we're close. The way your heartbeat quickens."

Her hand slid up his arm to his shoulder, and Drevik's pulse did exactly what she said it would. Quickened. His body responding to her proximity even as his mind screamed warnings.

"You're asking a lot," he said roughly.

"I'm offering a lot." She rose on her toes, bringing her face level with his. Her breath was warm against his lips. "Partnership. Protection. And something more, if you want it."

Drevik's hand came up without his permission, settling at the curve of her waist. Her body was so warm—impossibly, intoxicatingly warm—and she leaned into his touch like she'd been starving for it.

"This is dangerous," he said.

"Everything worth having is dangerous."

Her lips brushed against his cheek, feather-light, and the touch burned through him like a brand. Heat flooded his body, pooling low in his belly, and he had to fight the urge to pull her fully against him. To take what she was offering and damn the consequences.

Then she pulled back, and the moment broke.

"Think about it," she whispered. "I'll find you again in a few days. We can discuss terms."

She turned to leave the alley, her silhouette fading back into the shadows—

And stopped.

Her entire body went rigid. The glow beneath her skin flickered, violet light pulsing in a rapid, panicked rhythm.

"Something's here," she breathed. "Something from the Shadowlands."

Drevik's senses sharpened. He reached for his own power, letting it rise to the surface, and felt it—a wrongness at the edge of the alley. A presence that didn't belong in the mortal realm.

"Entity," he said. "Tracking you?"

Seraphina's silver eyes flared. "They can sense my blood. They always know where I am eventually."

A shape coalesced in the shadows at the alley's entrance. Vague at first, then solidifying—something with too many limbs and a mouth that opened sideways. It made no sound, but Drevik felt its hunger like ice water in his veins.

"Can you shadow-walk?" he asked urgently.

"Not fast enough. It would follow."

Drevik made a decision.

"Come here." He grabbed Seraphina's arm and pulled her against him, wrapping his other arm around her waist. Her body molded to his, fever-warm and trembling. "Hold your breath."

"What—"

"Trust me."

He reached for the darkest shadow in the alley—the deep pool of black beneath the fire escape—and pushed.

The world inverted. Cold darkness swallowed them, and for a heartbeat they were nowhere and everywhere at once. Drevik felt Seraphina's gasp against his chest, felt her fingers dig into his shoulders, felt the impossible heat of her body as they tumbled through the space between spaces.

Then they emerged on the other side of the wall, in a narrow service corridor behind the coffee shop's storage room.

Drevik pressed Seraphina against the wall, his body covering hers, both of them breathing hard. Her glow had faded to almost nothing, but her eyes were still silver, still fixed on his face with an expression he couldn't quite read.

"You took me through the shadow," she whispered. "I didn't think that was possible. Shadowlands blood is supposed to react badly to—"

"I didn't have time to worry about reactions." He was still pressed against her, still feeling the rapid rise and fall of her chest against his. "Are you hurt?"

She shook her head slowly. Her hands were still gripping his shoulders, her fingers curled into the fabric of his jacket.

"No one's ever done that for me before." Her voice was strange—soft, wondering. "No one's ever put themselves between me and the things that hunt me."

Drevik became suddenly, acutely aware of how close they were. Her breasts pressed against his chest, her hips cradled against his. Her lips were inches from his, parted slightly, and her breath came in quick, shallow pants that had nothing to do with fear.

He should step back. Put distance between them.

He didn't.

"Thank you," Seraphina breathed. She rose on her toes again, and this time when her lips met his cheek, she lingered. The kiss burned through his skin like a fever dream, and he felt her tremble against him.

When she pulled back, her eyes were still silver, still holding that strange, vulnerable light.

"The next breach," she said, her voice barely audible, "will be during the full moon ceremony. The traitor plans something big." She paused, her fingers still tangled in his jacket. "And they know about your parents, Drevik. Whatever happened to them—it's connected to what's happening now."

Drevik's blood went cold. "What do you mean?"

But Seraphina was already pulling away, already retreating into the shadows of the corridor.

"I'll find you," she said over her shoulder. "Watch for me. And be careful who you trust."

Then she was gone, slipping through a door that led to the street, leaving Drevik alone with the phantom heat of her body against his and the echo of her final words ringing in his ears.

They know about your parents.

He sagged against the wall, his heart pounding, his mind racing through the implications. Seraphina Vane was half-Shadowlands being. A traitor was working to open a permanent gate. And somewhere in the chaos, the truth about his parents—about who they were and what had happened to them—was waiting to be discovered.

The full moon ceremony was in three weeks.

He had that long to find the traitor, protect Seraphina, and uncover the secrets that had been buried for twenty-one years.

Drevik pushed off the wall and headed for the street, his coffee going cold in the shop across the way, his life getting more complicated by the hour.

And somewhere in the back of his mind, he could still feel the burning touch of Seraphina's lips against his skin.

He made it halfway across campus before Lexi found him.

She fell into step beside him without a word, her auburn hair catching the afternoon light. Her green eyes swept over his face, reading something in his expression that made her jaw tighten.

"You met with her." Not a question.

Drevik kept walking. "I had to."

"You're putting yourself in danger."

"I'm putting myself in a lot of danger. That seems to be the theme lately."

Lexi grabbed his arm and pulled him to a stop. Her grip was firm, her expression fierce.

"Seraphina Vane is not what she seems. My mother's files mention her—flagged as unstable, potentially dangerous."

"Your mother's files mention a lot of things." Drevik met her gaze steadily. "Does she know about the traitor? About the full moon ceremony?"

Lexi's eyes narrowed. "What are you talking about?"

"Someone inside the Order is working to open a permanent gate during the ceremony. Seraphina confirmed it. She also confirmed that the breach at the gala wasn't an accident—it was a test run."

Lexi's hand loosened on his arm. The anger in her expression shifted to something closer to alarm.

"That's... if that's true..."

"It is true." Drevik gently removed her hand from his sleeve. "I know you don't trust her. I know there's history between you two that I don't understand. But she gave me information we need, and she's putting herself at risk to help us."

"What does she want in return?"

The question hung in the air between them. Drevik thought about Seraphina's confession, her vulnerability, the way she'd looked at him in the alley. The heat of her body against his.

"Protection," he said finally. "And trust."

Lexi studied him for a long moment, and he saw something flicker in her eyes—not jealousy, exactly, but something adjacent to it.

"You're playing a dangerous game, Drevik."

"I know." He started walking again, and this time she fell into step beside him. "But I don't have a choice. The full moon ceremony is in three weeks. If we don't find the traitor before then, everything falls apart."

Lexi was quiet for several steps. Then: "My mother's files. There might be something in them about previous breaches, about patterns that could point to who's responsible."

"Can you get them?"

"I already have them. I just didn't know what I was looking for." She glanced at him sidelong. "I'll go through them tonight. See if anything matches what Seraphina told you."

Drevik nodded. "Thank you."

They walked in silence for a while, the campus sprawling around them, students hurrying to their next classes, completely unaware of the darkness gathering just beyond the veil of their everyday lives.

"Drevik," Lexi said quietly. "What else did she tell you?"

He hesitated. The secret of Seraphina's heritage wasn't his to share. But the final words, the warning about his parents...

"She said the traitor knows about my parents. That what happened to them is connected to what's happening now."

Lexi's step faltered. She reached out and touched his arm again—not grabbing this time, just resting her fingers against his sleeve.

"We'll figure this out." Her voice was fierce. "Whatever secrets they're hiding, whatever they've buried—we'll find the truth."

Drevik looked at her, at the determination in her green eyes, and felt something loosen in his chest. He wasn't alone in this. Whatever came next, whatever dangers waited in the shadows, he had people beside him.

"I know we will," he said.

And together, they walked toward the gathering dark.
