PC Gaming Deals Under $200: What Still Makes Sense in October 2026

October 21, 2026 · 4 deals tracked · By Marcus Chen

I've been buying PC parts since a 3dfx Voodoo2 took an entire summer of lawn-mowing money in 1998, and I have never seen a market this badly skewed. Graphics card prices are averaging around 57% over MSRP right now. The RTX 5060 Ti 16GB, a card that launched at $429, sells for $730 or more at mainstream retailers and tracker averages have pushed past $800. DDR5 has it worse. A 32GB kit of DDR5-6000 that cost about $104 in November 2024 runs $460 to $560 today, and flagship 2TB NVMe drives that dipped to $128 in 2023 now sit near $370. The memory shortage is real, AI datacenters are eating the wafer supply, and the spot market does not care about your wishlist.

Which flips the shopping logic upside down. In a normal October I'd steer you toward a GPU first and call the rest accessories. This month the honest deals live at the edges of your setup, the monitor, the headset, the controller, the mouse, where manufacturers don't bake VRAM or NAND into the bill of materials. Everything below costs less than $200 on its own, and the whole four-item loadout still costs less than two-thirds of what a single RTX 5060 Ti goes for this week. That's not an exaggeration; that's just the arithmetic of a broken quarter.

A note on method, because deal roundups lie by osmosis. Sites copy each other's numbers and a two-day-old buybox price becomes "found: $X" within an afternoon. I don't trust that pipeline. The Parts to Refuse Until Prices Fold

Now the list your wallet asked me to write. Discrete graphics cards: refuse. RTX 5060 Ti 16GB stock runs $730 to $820 against a $429 MSRP, the RTX 5070 has drifted past $800, and the 5090's floor sits north of $6,100 where it has no business being. System RAM: refuse. That $559.99 Corsair Vengeance 32GB DDR5-6000 CL30 kit isn't a deal, it's the new normal, five times what the same class of kit cost two years ago. Flagship SSDs: think hard. The Samsung 990 Pro 2TB is a wonderful drive and still costs $370 to $390, nearly triple its 2023 low. None of these numbers will improve because you clicked on them at 11pm. The only winning move against a supply-driven spike is not to play.

The exception that proves the rule is the prebuilt. System integrators bought their VRAM and DDR5 before the spot market went vertical, so a finished tower still carries prices that make component shoppers wince with envy. The ABS Cyclone Aqua (Core 5 120, RTX 5060, 16GB, 1TB) dropped to $849 at Walmart this month, which is more machine than a $700-and-up Steam Machine at less money, and MSI's Codex R2 with the same GPU and DDR5 hovered around $1,099 to $1,199 on Amazon. A prebuilt means MSI's warranty instead of yours, and bloatware you'll spend an evening deleting, but it also means a graphics card you didn't buy at 70% over MSRP. Right now those towers are the least dishonest objects in PC hardware.

What about Black Friday? Deal-season history favors exactly what this guide covers: consoles, games, and peripherals get their deepest cuts in November, while the memory-starved categories may stay flat or drift higher. My working plan for the season is to spend under $250 on the edges now, put the GPU budget in a drawer, and let the spot market lose its mind on its own schedule. Hardware you need today is not a deal-hunting item; hardware you want next quarter almost always is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why buy peripherals now but not GPUs or RAM?

Because the spike is memory-specific. GPU pricing carries VRAM, SSD pricing carries NAND, and RAM is itself the commodity going vertical. A monitor, headset, controller, or mouse contains none of those, so its price follows launch cycles instead of the AI build-out. Right now that divide is the whole market story.

Is a curved 300Hz VA monitor worth it at $141?

As a value pick, yes, and it's the longest-lived item in this guide. You get 2560x1440 at 300Hz over DisplayPort with a height-adjustable stand. The compromises are real: VA smears slightly in dark scenes, the curve isn't for everyone, HDMI caps at 144Hz, and the HDR is ceremonial. A display also outlives two or three GPUs, so the money survives the spike.

Does the 8BitDo Ultimate 2C work on Xbox?

No. It's not Xbox-licensed. On Windows it's a standard XInput controller and works everywhere XInput works, plus Switch and Bluetooth devices. Series X|S consoles only accept licensed pads, so Xbox households should grab a first-party controller on sale instead.

Will GPU and RAM prices fall before Black Friday?

Nobody honest will promise it. This hike is supply-driven, not a retail pricing game, so a November banner doesn't automatically fix it. Black Friday's best cuts have historically landed on consoles, games, and peripherals anyway. If you must have a GPU this calendar year, a prebuilt is currently the only path where the math still works.
