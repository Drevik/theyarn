Chapter 5: The First Oath

Chapter 5: The First Oath

The blood in Drevik's veins had turned to ice water.

He stood at the threshold of the central chamber, staring up at the domed ceiling where someone had painted constellations that didn't exist in any sky he'd ever known. The stars spiraled inward toward a dark center—an eclipse, he realized. The point where all light vanished.

"You're going to stare at it all night, or are you coming in?"

Lexi's voice cut through the fog in his head. She stood beside him in ceremonial robes the color of deep wine, her auburn hair pulled back from her face in a way that made her look older. More severe. The green of her eyes caught the candlelight and threw it back like polished glass.

"Sorry." Drevik stepped forward, his own black robes swishing around his ankles. "Just trying to memorize the ceiling in case I don't survive this."

"Comforting." She fell into step beside him, her shoulder brushing his. "The Oath isn't death. It's just... permanent."

"That's what everyone keeps saying about everything in this place."

The chamber was circular, ringed with pillars of black stone that seemed to drink the light from the hundreds of candles scattered throughout. At the center stood an altar—or what Drevik assumed was an altar. It looked more like a slab of obsidian that had grown directly from the floor, its surface carved with symbols that shifted when he looked at them too long.

Twelve figures waited in a semicircle behind the altar. The Elders, he'd been told. Men and women of various ages, their faces masked by shadows that had nothing to do with the lighting. At the center stood a woman who needed no introduction—the Veiled One, the Order's supreme leader. Her features were impossible to focus on, as if his eyes simply refused to hold her image.

"Kneel," Lexi whispered.

Drevik dropped to one knee, the cold stone seeping through the thin fabric of his robes. Lexi knelt beside him, close enough that he could smell something sharp and clean beneath the incense—wintergreen, maybe. Or just the scent of her skin.

"Acolyte Drevik Markinsoul." The Veiled One's voice came from everywhere and nowhere, resonating in his chest like a second heartbeat. "You stand before the Order of the Eclipsed Veil to bind yourself to our purpose. Do you come freely, or do you come by coercion?"

"Freely." The word scraped out of his dry throat.

"Do you understand that this bond, once forged, cannot be broken save by death or the judgment of this Order?"

"I understand."

"Do you swear to hold our secrets, guard our boundaries, and serve the balance between worlds until your final breath releases you?"

"I swear."

The Veiled One raised one hand, and the shadows around the room seemed to lean inward. "Then take the hand of your sponsor and speak the words that will seal your fate."

Lexi's fingers found his.

The moment their skin touched, something electric shot up Drevik's arm. Not painful—startling. Like touching a live wire wrapped in velvet. He heard Lexi's sharp intake of breath and knew she'd felt it too.

Her eyes met his, wide for just a second before the careful mask slid back into place.

What was that?

No time to ask. The words were rising in his throat unbidden, as if someone had written them on the inside of his skull.

"By shadow's grace and moon's embrace, I bind myself to this Order's face. My secrets theirs, my silence deep, my promises eternal keep. Through darkness walking, light I'll seek, until my spirit's final speak."

The words tasted like copper on his tongue. As the last syllable left his lips, the symbols on the obsidian altar flared silver-white, and Drevik felt something wrap around his heart. Not physically—but something deeper. Something that had found a place inside him and settled there like a stone dropped into still water.

"It is done." The Veiled One lowered her hand. "Rise, Acolyte Markinsoul. You are one of us now."

The ceremony had taken less than twenty minutes.

The aftermath lasted hours.

Drevik sat on a bench in one of the compound's many corridors, his head between his knees, trying to remember how to breathe normally. The black robes had been exchanged for his regular clothes, but nothing felt regular anymore. The weight in his chest was constant—a presence that pressed against his ribs like a second skeleton.

"You look like you're about to vomit."

He lifted his head. Lexi leaned against the wall across from him, arms crossed, watching him with an expression caught between amusement and something softer.

"Feel like it too." He straightened, running a hand through his hair. "Is it always like this? This... heaviness?"

"You get used to it." She pushed off the wall and walked toward him, stopping just outside what he was beginning to think of as her personal space. "The Oath settles after a few days. Becomes background noise."

"Background noise." Drevik laughed, but it came out strangled. "Great. So I'll just have a magical collar around my soul that I can eventually learn to ignore."

Something flickered across her face. "It's not a collar. It's a promise."

"A promise I can't break."

"No." Her voice was quiet. "You really can't."

He stood, frustration pricking at him. "And what happens if I do? If I slip up, say something I shouldn't, break some rule I don't even know exists yet?"

Lexi didn't answer. Her silence was answer enough.

Drevik moved past her, heading for the corridor that would lead him back to the surface, back to the normal world where magic wasn't real and people didn't bind their souls to shadow cults—

Pain exploded through his chest.

He doubled over, gasping, his vision going white at the edges. It felt like someone had reached inside him and squeezed his heart until it nearly burst. The weight of the Oath had been a presence before; now it was a weapon, crushing him from the inside out.

"Drevik!" Lexi's hands were on his shoulders, steadying him. "What—what did you do?"

"I don't—" He couldn't finish the sentence. The pain was receding, leaving him shaking and sweat-soaked. "I was just... I was trying to leave."

"Trying to—" Her eyes went wide. "You were thinking about leaving the Order?"

"I was thinking about going back to my dorm!"

The pain spiked again, less severe this time but still enough to make him stagger. Lexi caught him, her arm wrapping around his waist to hold him upright.

"Stop," she hissed. "Stop thinking about it. The Oath punishes intent, not just action. If you're genuinely considering breaking your bond—even subconsciously—it will correct you."

"Correct me." Drevik wheezed the words. "That's what we're calling it?"

"Focus on something else." Her voice was close to his ear, her breath warm against his skin. "Think about... about your classes. About anything that ties you to this life."

He tried. He thought about his art history final, about the coffee shop on campus that never got his order right, about the way his grandmother used to sing while she cooked—

The pain faded. His heartbeat steadied.

Lexi guided him back to the bench, and this time she sat beside him. Close enough that their knees touched.

"The Oath interprets through your own mind," she said quietly. "If you genuinely want to leave, it will stop you. But if you're just... overwhelmed, scared, needing space—it can tell the difference. Usually."

"Usually."

"It takes practice." She was looking at her hands, folded in her lap. "I've seen grown men cry during their first week. One of them tried to run. He made it three steps before the Oath put him on the ground."

Drevik's mouth was dry. "What happened to him?"

"He learned." Her jaw tightened. "Or he didn't, and he died during his first mission because he was too focused on escaping to pay attention to the shadows."

The words hung between them. Drevik studied her profile—the strong line of her nose, the tension in her shoulders, the way she wouldn't quite meet his eyes.

"You really hate that I'm here," he said. "Don't you?"

"I don't hate you."

"That's not the same thing."

Lexi was silent for a long moment. Then, so quietly he almost missed it: "I had a trainee once. Before you."

The confession hung in the air. Drevik waited.

"His name was Marcus." Her voice was flat, controlled. "He was born into the Order like me. We grew up together. Trained together. When I was promoted to Seeker, they assigned him to me as my first acolyte."

She finally looked at him, and her eyes were raw. "I was supposed to protect him. Teach him. Keep him alive long enough to become a Seeker himself."

"What happened?"

"We were patrolling during a new moon. Standard reconnaissance—nothing dangerous. But there was a breach we didn't see coming. A shadow creature had slipped through somewhere in the city, and we walked right into its path." Her hands had clenched into fists. "I told him to run. He stayed to cover my retreat."

Drevik didn't need her to finish. He could see it in the way she held herself, the defensive curl of her shoulders.

"He didn't make it," she said. "And every time I look at you, I remember that I'm the reason someone's son didn't come home."

The weight of her words settled over them. Drevik understood now—the sharpness in her voice when she corrected him, the way she pushed him harder than seemed fair. It wasn't distrust. It was fear.

"Lexi." He kept his voice gentle. "I'm not him."

"I know."

"And you're not going to get me killed."

"You don't know that."

"No." He reached out, hesitated, then let his hand settle over hers where it rested on her knee. Her skin was warm. "But you're going to teach me how to survive. And I'm going to listen."

Her eyes searched his face, looking for something—mockery, maybe, or empty promises. Whatever she found there made her exhale slowly.

"You're really not what I expected," she said.

"Is that a compliment?"

"I haven't decided yet." But her fingers turned under his, clasping back. The touch was brief—just a moment of contact—but something in it resonated. The same electric warmth he'd felt during the ceremony, muted now but still present.

She pulled back first, standing abruptly. "Come on. There's someone you need to meet before your first patrol."

The library was tucked into a corner of the compound that Drevik hadn't explored yet, hidden behind a door that looked like solid stone until Lexi pressed her palm to a specific spot and the wall simply... moved.

Beyond it lay a space that shouldn't have been possible. Shelves stretched toward a ceiling lost in shadow, filled with books and scrolls and objects Drevik couldn't name. The air smelled of old paper and something sweeter—jasmine, maybe, or honeysuckle.

"Ivy?" Lexi's voice echoed slightly. "Are you in here?"

A head popped out from between two towering bookcases. Dark hair cut in a sharp bob. Round glasses perched on a nose dusted with freckles. A face that might have been anywhere from twenty to thirty, with an expression of perpetual curiosity.

"Finally!" The woman—Ivy—emerged fully, clutching a leather-bound tome to her chest. "I've been waiting for hours. Is this the new one?"

"Drevik Markinsoul." Lexi gestured. "Ivy Chen. She runs the archives and handles mission briefings for acolytes."

"Pleasure to meet you." Ivy pushed her glasses up her nose and smiled. It was a warm smile, genuine in a way that made Drevik's chest ache. "Welcome to the Order. Try not to die in your first month—it's embarrassing for everyone."

"I'll... do my best?"

"She's joking." Lexi's voice was flat. "Mostly."

Ivy set the tome on a nearby table and began rifling through its pages. "Your patrol assignment came through this morning. New moon is in three days, which means you'll be walking the eastern boundary of the campus. Standard reconnaissance, minimal risk."

"Minimal risk." Drevik repeated the words. "That's what Lexi said about her last mission with a trainee."

Ivy's hands stilled. Her eyes lifted to meet his, and something shifted in her expression. "You've been told about Marcus."

"Heard about it, more like."

"Then you understand why Lexi is the way she is." Ivy's voice was gentle. "She blames herself. She'll always blame herself. But she's also the best trainer the Order has ever produced. If anyone can keep you alive, it's her."

She pulled a folded parchment from the tome and handed it to Drevik. "Your patrol route. Memorize it before the new moon and destroy the paper. We don't leave evidence lying around."

Drevik unfolded the parchment, studying the lines that traced a path through the campus. "Eastern boundary... this goes through the old cemetery behind the chapel."

"Cemeteries are thin spots," Ivy said. "The veil between worlds is weaker there. We patrol them to make sure nothing slips through."

"Charming."

"You'll get used to it." Ivy smiled again, that warm, genuine expression that made Drevik want to trust her immediately. "And if you have questions, I'm always here. The archives are my domain, but I make exceptions for people who bring me coffee."

"I'll remember that."

"See that you do." She turned back to her books, dismissing them with the ease of someone who had far too much to do and not enough time to do it. "Good luck, Acolyte Markinsoul. You'll need it."

Lexi walked him back toward the surface in silence. The compound's corridors seemed longer in the aftermath of the Oath ceremony, the shadows deeper. Or maybe Drevik was just seeing them more clearly now.

"The pain will fade," Lexi said as they climbed the stairs that led to the hidden exit. "The Oath is aggressive at first because it's establishing boundaries. Once you understand what you can and can't do, it stops being so... insistent."

"And if I want out later? After I've proven myself?"

She stopped at the top of the stairs, her hand on the door that would lead them back to the ordinary world. Her profile was lit by the single bulb overhead, casting half her face in shadow.

"There is no out, Drevik. Not really. Once you're bound, you're bound for life. The only release is death." Her voice was soft. "That's why the Oath ceremony is so important. That's why we make sure initiates understand what they're agreeing to before they speak the words."

"Did you understand? When you took your Oath?"

"I was born into this." She opened the door, letting in the cool night air. "I never knew anything else."

They emerged into a narrow alley between two campus buildings, the entrance to the compound hidden behind a illusion that made the wall look like solid brick. Drevik's dorm was a ten-minute walk from here. His art history textbook was waiting on his desk. His normal life was right there, just within reach.

But the weight in his chest reminded him that normal wasn't really an option anymore.

"I'll see you for training tomorrow," Lexi said. "Six AM. Don't be late."

"Lexi." He caught her arm before she could disappear back into the compound. "Thank you. For telling me about Marcus."

She looked at his hand on her sleeve, then up at his face. "Don't make me regret it."

She pulled free and stepped back through the hidden door. It closed behind her with a sound like a sigh.

Drevik stood alone in the alley, the parchment with his patrol route pressed against his chest. Three days until the new moon. Three days until his first real mission.

He started walking toward his dorm, his mind racing through everything he'd learned. The Oath, the weight in his chest, Lexi's confession, Ivy's briefing—

Ivy.

Something about their meeting tugged at his memory. She'd been so focused on the briefing, so professional. But there had been a moment, just before he left, when her eyes had met his and held.

He pulled out the parchment to study it again, memorizing the route while it was still fresh.

A folded piece of paper fell out from between the layers.

Drevik frowned, bending to pick it up. It hadn't been there before—he would have noticed. Which meant Ivy had slipped it to him during the briefing, quick enough that even Lexi hadn't seen.

He unfolded it, the streetlamp overhead casting just enough light to read the words.

The patrol route you were given is wrong. Don't follow it. Come see me before the new moon.

Drevik stared at the message, his heart rate spiking.

Wrong.

He looked back toward the alley where the compound entrance lay hidden, then down at the note in his hand.

The weight of the Oath pressed against his ribs—not painfully, but insistently. A reminder that he was bound now, that his choices weren't entirely his own.

But Ivy was part of the Order. Ivy had given him this note. Ivy, with her warm smile and her freckled nose and her knowledge of things that shouldn't be known.

He folded the paper carefully and tucked it into his pocket.

Three days until the new moon.

He had until then to figure out who, exactly, he could trust.

The walk back to his dorm passed in a blur. Drevik's mind churned through possibilities, each one more unlikely than the last. Ivy was the Order's librarian—why would she give him a false patrol route? Was it a test? A trap? A genuine warning from someone who saw something others didn't?

He let himself into his room, grateful that his roommate was out, and sat on the edge of his bed with his head in his hands.

The Oath hummed in his chest. Not painful—just present. A constant reminder that he wasn't alone in his own body anymore.

You asked for this, he reminded himself. You wanted to belong somewhere.

But belonging had a price. He was just beginning to understand how steep that price might be.

His phone buzzed. A text from an unknown number.

Training tomorrow. 6AM. Don't eat breakfast beforehand—you'll probably lose it. - L

Drevik almost smiled. Lexi's version of encouragement.

He typed back: Looking forward to it.

His phone buzzed again, almost immediately.

You're a terrible liar, Markinsoul.

He was still smiling when he fell asleep, the weight of the Oath settling into his bones like something that had always been there, just waiting for him to notice.

The dreams came in fragments.

Shadow creatures with too many eyes. Lexi's voice calling out a warning. A path through a cemetery that led somewhere the map didn't show. Ivy's hand pressing a note into his palm, her fingers lingering longer than necessary.

And beneath it all, a pulse of magic that beat in time with his own heart.

He woke before dawn, the Oath humming in his chest, and knew that nothing would ever be simple again.
