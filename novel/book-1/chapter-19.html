Chapter 19: The Traitor Revealed

Chapter 19: The Traitor Revealed

The corridor stretched before them like a throat, all shadow and stone, and Drevik couldn't shake the feeling that they were walking directly into something that would swallow them whole.

"Left at the next junction," Seraphina whispered, her voice barely disturbing the air. The mysterious woman moved as if she were part of the darkness itself, her dark hair blending with the shadows that clung to the walls. "Two guards. They haven't seen us yet."

Drevik nodded, feeling the weight of the evidence crystal in his pocket. Inside it, preserved through magic he didn't fully understand, Corvin's confession played on an endless loop—his voice, his words, his treachery made manifest. They just had to get it to Elder Thornwood before Corvin realized how much they knew.

Lexi pressed close to his side, her shoulder brushing his with each step. The contact shouldn't have felt so grounding, not when they were creeping through the Order's compound with every shadow potentially hiding an enemy. But her warmth anchored him in a way nothing else could.

I should tell her, he thought, not for the first time. If this goes wrong, if we don't make it—

"Stop," Ivy breathed, her hand catching Drevik's arm. The small blonde's eyes had gone distant, that particular look she got when her peculiar gifts showed her something the rest of them couldn't see. "They know we're coming. Corvin—he told them Drevik's the traitor. They're mobilizing."

Drevik's stomach dropped. Of course Corvin had contingencies. A man who'd spent years plotting the collapse of the Veil wouldn't leave his position to chance.

"How many?" Lexi asked, her hand already moving to the blade at her hip.

"Eight. No, ten." Ivy's face paled. "Coming from three directions. We have maybe thirty seconds before—"

The first guard rounded the corner.

Drevik didn't think. He reached for the shadows—the power that had always come so naturally to him, the gift that made him either a prodigy or a freak depending on who you asked—and pulled.

The guard's shadow reached up and wrapped around his ankles like hands. He went down hard, his weapon clattering across the stone floor, and before he could cry out, Seraphina was there. A touch to his forehead, a whisper of words Drevik couldn't quite hear, and the man went limp.

"Sleep," she said simply. "He'll wake with a headache and no memory of the last hour."

"One down," Lexi muttered, already moving. "Nine to go."

Then they were upon them.

Fighting alongside Lexi was unlike anything Drevik had experienced.

They moved together without words, without signals, as if some part of them had already merged into a single unit. When a shadow-blade came for his left side, her knife was there to deflect it. When she overextended toward a guard's throat, he caught the enemy's counter-strike with shadow wrapped around his forearm.

How do we know each other so well? The question flickered through his mind even as he ducked under a swinging fist. We've barely trained together.

But it wasn't training. It was something deeper. Something that made his chest ache even as his body moved through the violent choreography of survival.

Ivy fought with a ferocity that belied her small frame, her hands glowing with pale light as she struck pressure points and sent guards crumpling. Seraphina moved like smoke, always where the enemy least expected, her fingers leaving unconscious bodies in her wake.

But it was Lexi who held Drevik's attention. Lexi, with her auburn hair coming loose from its braid. Lexi, with her green eyes sharp and focused. Lexi, who put herself between him and danger without a second thought.

A guard broke through their formation, his blade aimed at Drevik's chest. Time slowed. Drevik saw the strike coming, knew he couldn't move fast enough—

Lexi's body slammed into his, spinning them both aside. The blade caught her arm instead of his heart.

"Lexi!"

"I'm fine." She was already back on her feet, blood running down her forearm, her eyes never leaving the enemy. "Focus, Drevik. We're not done."

But something in Drevik cracked at the sight of her blood. A wall he'd been carefully maintaining, a distance he'd been keeping, crumbled.

She could have died. She would have died for me.

The fight ended three minutes later. Ten guards lay unconscious or groaning on the stone floor, and Drevik's breath came in ragged gasps. The shadow-magic always took something from him—a little piece of his energy, a fragment of his warmth. Using it this extensively left him hollow.

Lexi's hand found his, her fingers threading between his own.

"We need to keep moving," she said quietly. Her other arm hung at her side, the wound still bleeding freely. "Elder Thornwood's chambers are another two floors down."

"Your arm—"

"Will survive until we've stopped a Shadowlands breach." Her green eyes met his, and in them he saw everything she wasn't saying. "Priorities, Drevik."

He wanted to kiss her. The absurdity of the timing—standing in a corridor surrounded by unconscious bodies, his best friend bleeding, a traitor to confront—didn't make the urge any less powerful. He wanted to press his lips to hers and pour everything he couldn't articulate into that contact.

Instead, he squeezed her hand once and let go.

"Then let's finish this."

They made it to Elder Thornwood's chambers without further incident, which should have been the first sign that something was wrong.

The heavy wooden doors stood open. Candlelight spilled into the corridor, warm and golden, casting dancing shadows across the stone. Inside, Drevik could hear voices—low, calm, the kind of conversation that happened over tea, not during crises.

He signaled the others to stop, pressing himself against the wall beside the doorway. Lexi mirrored his position on the opposite side, her knife ready.

"—understand your concerns, Elder, but I'm afraid you've been misled."

Corvin's voice. Smooth, cultured, reasonable. The voice of a man who had never once raised it in anger because he'd never needed to. The voice of a man who could convince you the sky was green if it served his purposes.

"Corvin, I've known you since you were a child. I trained you. I—"

"And that's precisely why this hurts." A pause, the sound of movement. "I need you to sign this order, Elder. The one that transfers gate authority to my office. Just until this business with the Markinsoul boy is resolved."

Drevik's blood ran cold. Gate authority. With that, Corvin could open any of the Order's sealed passages to the Shadowlands. All of them. At once.

Ivy was right. He's not just weakening the Veil—he's planning to tear it apart completely.

Drevik looked at Lexi. Her face had gone pale, her jaw tight with barely contained fury. She knew what gate authority meant. She'd grown up in this world, understood its structures and safeguards better than he ever would.

Her father, he realized suddenly. Elder Thornwood is Lexi's father.

The realization hit him like a physical blow. He'd known, abstractly, that Lexi came from an Order family. But seeing her face now, watching her listen to a traitor threaten her father, understanding the impossible position she was in—

She met his eyes. Something passed between them, silent and complete. I have to do this, her gaze said. I have to face him.

I'm with you, his answered. Whatever happens.

Drevik stepped through the doorway.

The scene inside was worse than he'd imagined.

Elder Thornwood sat behind his desk, his face drawn and pale, his hands flat on the wood as if to steady himself. The man looked older than Drevik remembered, the lines around his eyes deeper, his silver hair thin. But his spine was straight, his chin lifted. Whatever Corvin had done to him, he hadn't broken.

Corvin stood behind the Elder's chair, one hand resting casually on the old man's shoulder. In his other hand, he held a blade—not steel, but shadow-glass, the Order's most dangerous substance. One touch of that blade to bare skin would sever a person's connection to magic forever.

And pressed against Elder Thornwood's throat was the edge of that blade.

"Drevik." Corvin's smile was genuine, almost warm. "I wondered when you'd arrive. You've been so very busy tonight, haven't you? Running through my halls, incapacitating my people, carrying evidence you think will save you."

"Evidence that will hang you," Lexi spat, stepping up beside Drevik. Her voice shook with barely contained rage. "Let my father go, you monstrous—"

"Ah, Alexa." Corvin's eyes moved to her, and something in his expression shifted. Not cruelty—something worse. Pity. "Your father has been a good man. A faithful servant of the Order. But his generation's time is ending. The Veil has held for too long, kept us too separate from the source of our power."

"You're insane." Seraphina's voice came from somewhere behind Drevik. "The Shadowlands will consume everything. You can't control what comes through a full breach."

"Can't I?" Corvin's smile widened. "You assume I'm opening the gates without preparation. But I've been planning this for decades, Seraphina. I've made arrangements. The entity that comes through will be bound to me—will become me. Immortal, unstoppable, powerful beyond your comprehension."

Drevik's shadow-sense flared. Somewhere below them, he could feel it—a pressure building, a wrongness pressing against the Veil. Corvin had already started. Whatever he needed gate authority for, he'd found another way to begin.

"You've been weakening the barriers for years," Drevik said, the pieces falling into place. "The incidents—the breaches we've been sent to contain. You caused them."

"Of course I caused them." Corvin laughed, a sound like breaking glass. "Every breach thins the fabric. Every creature that slips through leaves a wound that doesn't quite heal. And you, my talented young acolyte—you've been my unwitting assistant. Every time you sealed a breach, you left a little piece of your power behind. A little fragment of potential that I could use later."

The words hit Drevik like ice water. He's been using me. From the beginning.

"What do you want?" Drevik forced himself to keep his voice steady, even as his mind raced through possibilities. "You have the Elder at knifepoint. You've been planning this for decades. What could you possibly need from me now?"

Corvin's eyes gleamed.

"Your blood, Drevik. The Markinsoul bloodline carries something special—something I couldn't have predicted. Your mother had it too, before she died. The ability to see futures, to walk paths that haven't yet been taken. With that gift added to the ritual, I won't just control what comes through the breach—I'll be able to choose which future becomes real. I'll be a god."

The world narrowed to a single point. Drevik could feel the other people in the room—Lexi's barely contained fury, Ivy's terror, Seraphina's calculating stillness—but they seemed very far away.

My mother.

"She knew what you were planning," Drevik said, his voice strange and distant. "That's why she died."

"She knew too much," Corvin agreed. "And she refused to join me. A waste, truly. But her son—her talented, powerful, useful son—might prove more reasonable." The shadow-glass blade pressed closer to Elder Thornwood's throat. "Surrender, Drevik. Give me your cooperation, and I'll let your friends live. Refuse, and I'll take what I need from your corpse."

Drevik looked at Lexi.

Her green eyes were bright with unshed tears, her face pale, her injured arm still bleeding. But beneath the fear, beneath the pain, he saw something else. Trust. Absolute, unwavering trust.

Whatever you decide, her eyes said. I'm with you.

He made his choice.

The vision hit him like lightning.

One moment he was standing in the Elder's chamber, watching Corvin's smile spread across his face. The next, he was somewhere else entirely—standing at the edge of a wound in reality, a tear in the fabric of the world that glowed with sickly purple light.

Through the tear, he could see the Shadowlands. Not the distant glimpses he'd caught before, but the true depth of it—a twilight realm of twisted geometry and hungry things, of shadows that moved with their own will and eyes that watched from the darkness.

And something was coming through.

The entity was vast. Vast in a way that made Drevik's mind stutter and skip, unable to process what it was seeing. It was shadow and hunger and ancient, patient malice, and it had been waiting for this moment for longer than humanity had existed.

In the vision, Drevik saw himself standing at the breach's edge. He saw Lexi behind him, bleeding from a wound he couldn't see. He saw Seraphina and Ivy collapsed on the ground, their eyes glassy and distant.

And he saw what he had to do.

The breach couldn't be closed from this side—not fully, not permanently. The entity had already pushed too much of itself through. But if someone went through to the other side, if they pulled the breach closed from within the Shadowlands...

They could seal it. Forever.

The vision shifted, showing him the aftermath. The Order rebuilding. Lexi, her face lined with grief, rising through the ranks. His name spoken with reverence and sorrow. A memorial, carved in shadow-glass, bearing his image.

And then—further out, harder to see—something that made his heart clench.

He wouldn't die.

The vision wasn't clear on what happened after he stepped through. But it showed him enough. He wouldn't die. He would be trapped, stranded in the Shadowlands, but he would survive. And if he survived...

There might be a way back.

The vision released him.

Drevik staggered, catching himself on the doorframe. The others were staring at him—Lexi with concern, Ivy with knowing eyes, even Corvin with something like curiosity.

"Interesting," the traitor murmured. "You saw something, didn't you? A gift like yours can't help but show you paths. Tell me, Drevik—what did you see? Your victory? My defeat?"

Drevik straightened slowly. His heart was pounding, his hands shaking, but his mind was clear for the first time since this nightmare began.

"I saw how this ends," he said quietly. "And I choose it."

Corvin's smile faltered. Just for a moment, just a fraction, but Drevik saw it.

"You choose—"

Drevik moved.

He didn't think about the shadow-glass blade at Elder Thornwood's throat. He didn't calculate odds or plan contingencies. He simply moved, reaching for every scrap of power he'd ever touched, every shadow he'd ever walked through, every future he'd ever seen.

The room went dark.

Not dim—dark. Absolutely, completely dark, as if someone had snuffed out every light in existence. Drevik heard Corvin's startled cry, heard Elder Thornwood's chair scrape back, heard Lexi's sharp intake of breath.

And in that darkness, Drevik walked.

Shadow-walking was usually a slow process—step into one shadow, step out of another. But this wasn't usual. This was every shadow at once, every darkness he could reach, and he poured himself into it like he'd never done before.

He emerged behind Corvin.

The traitor was still holding his blade, still reaching for the Elder, but his eyes were wide with shock, his smile finally gone. Drevik's hand closed around Corvin's wrist, his grip iron-tight.

"You killed my mother," Drevik said, his voice flat and cold. "You used me. You threatened everyone I love."

Corvin's eyes met his. There was fear there now—real fear, the fear of a man who had finally lost control.

"The breach," Corvin gasped. "It's already opening. You can't stop it. No one can."

"Maybe not." Drevik squeezed, feeling bones grind together. Corvin's hand opened, and the shadow-glass blade fell. "But I can stop you."

He didn't kill him. Some part of him wanted to—that dark, angry part that had lived in his chest since his grandmother died, since he'd been alone in the world. But killing Corvin wouldn't bring his mother back. It wouldn't undo the damage.

Instead, he pulled.

Shadow wrapped around Corvin like chains, binding his arms to his sides, his legs together, his mouth shut. The traitor fell to the floor, writhing silently, his eyes still wide with terror.

"Lexi!" Drevik turned, already moving. "Your father—are you—"

But Lexi was already there, crouched beside the Elder's chair, her hands checking him over with practiced efficiency. Her father's hand came up to cup her face, a gesture so tender it made Drevik's chest ache.

"I'm fine, little star," Elder Thornwood said, his voice rough. "Thanks to him." His eyes met Drevik's. "You have my gratitude, Acolyte. And my attention. We need to talk about—"

"The breach," Drevik interrupted. He could feel it getting stronger, the wound in reality widening somewhere below them. "It's opening. Corvin started the ritual before he came here. We don't have much time."

Ivy appeared at his elbow, her face pale but determined.

"It's in the lower sanctum. The old gate—the one they sealed a hundred years ago. He's been weakening it for years, and tonight he broke the final lock." Her eyes met Drevik's. "It's bad. Really, really bad."

"Then we stop it." Lexi was on her feet, her knife back in her hand, her expression fierce. "We've stopped breaches before."

"Not like this." Seraphina's voice was quiet, her face grave. "This isn't a breach. This is an invasion. The entity Corvin made his deal with—it's already coming through. And if it finishes crossing..."

"Then we don't let it finish," Drevik said.

He looked at each of them in turn. Ivy, small and scared but standing tall. Seraphina, mysterious and powerful and finally showing something real beneath her mask. Elder Thornwood, wounded but still fighting. And Lexi—Lexi with her blood-stained arm and her tear-bright eyes and her unwavering faith.

I have to do this, he thought. I have to go through. But first—

He crossed to Lexi in three steps and pulled her into his arms.

The kiss was desperate and fierce and nothing like he'd imagined. It tasted of blood and fear and something sweeter underneath—hope, maybe, or love, or the shape of a future they might still have.

"I have to go through," he breathed against her lips. "I have to close it from the other side. I saw—"

"No." Lexi's hands fisted in his shirt, pulling him closer. "No, Drevik, you can't—the Shadowlands will—"

"I won't die." He cupped her face, making her look at him, making her see the truth in his eyes. "I saw it. I'll be trapped, but I'll survive. And if I survive, I can find my way back. There are paths between worlds, Lexi. Paths I can walk now." His voice cracked. "But I have to go. There's no other way."

Lexi's tears spilled over, tracking down her cheeks. But she didn't argue. She'd grown up in the Order; she understood sacrifice. She understood duty.

"Then I'm coming with you."

"You can't." It killed him to say it. "The sealing requires shadow-magic. Real, powerful shadow-magic. You'd be stranded with me, and someone needs to—someone needs to tell the Order what happened. Someone needs to make sure Corvin pays for this."

"Someone else can do that." Lexi's voice was raw. "Drevik, please—I can't lose you. Not now. Not when I just—"

"I know." He pressed his forehead to hers, breathing her in. "I know, Lexi. I love you too."

The words hung between them, simple and devastating.

"Then don't go," she whispered. "Stay. We'll find another way. We always—"

"There isn't one." He kissed her again, softer this time, trying to say everything he couldn't put into words. "But I'll come back. I promise you. I will find my way back to you."

Lexi made a sound that was half-sob, half-laugh.

"You'd better." She pulled back, wiping her face with her uninjured hand. "Because if you die in there, I'll find a way to bring you back and kill you myself."

Drevik smiled. It was the most real thing he'd felt all night.

"I'm holding you to that."

The lower sanctum was worse than the vision had shown.

The old gate dominated the chamber—a massive arch of black stone covered in symbols that seemed to writhe when Drevik looked at them. Through the arch, the Shadowlands pressed close, reality thinning and tearing as something vast pushed its way through.

The entity was already partially manifested. Tendrils of shadow-darkness reached into the mortal realm, tasting, probing, spreading. Where they touched stone, it blackened and crumbled. Where they touched air, it grew cold and thick.

"Go," Drevik told the others. "Get the Elder to safety. Warn the Order. And make sure Corvin doesn't get away."

"We're not leaving you," Ivy said, her voice trembling but firm.

"You have to." Drevik turned to face them, standing between them and the breach. "If you stay, the entity will use you against me. It'll find a way to keep the gate open. You have to go. Now."

Seraphina's hand found his shoulder, her touch surprisingly gentle.

"The Shadowlands are not kind to mortals," she said quietly. "Even those with shadow-gifts. Be careful, Drevik. And..." She hesitated. "Come back."

"I will."

Ivy threw herself at him, her small arms wrapping around his waist in a fierce hug.

"You saved my life," she whispered against his chest. "More than once. I won't forget it."

"Neither will I." Drevik kissed the top of her head. "Now go. Both of you. Please."

They went. Seraphina's arm around Ivy's shoulders, guiding the smaller woman toward the exit. And then it was just Drevik and the breach and the entity pressing against the wound in the world.

This is it, he thought. This is what I was made for.

He walked toward the gate.

The entity noticed him. He felt its attention like a physical weight, like standing beneath a waterfall of ice water. It was curious, he realized. It had never encountered someone like him—someone who could walk between worlds, who could see futures, who carried shadow-gifts strong enough to challenge its own.

You cannot stop me, little shadow-walker, it seemed to say. The voice wasn't words, exactly. It was more like understanding, pressed directly into his mind. The breach is made. I am coming.

"Not if I close it." Drevik reached out with his power, feeling the edges of the tear, the places where reality had been worn thin. "Not if I seal you on the other side."

You would trap yourself. You would be stranded in eternal twilight, surrounded by things that would devour you in moments.

"I'll take my chances."

He stepped through the gate.

The Shadowlands were everything he'd feared and nothing he'd imagined.

Twilight stretched in every direction—a sky that was neither day nor night, a horizon that curved wrong, a landscape of twisted shapes and impossible angles. The air tasted of copper and shadows, and every sound seemed to come from everywhere at once.

Behind him, he could see the breach—the wound in reality that led back to the mortal world. The entity was still pressing through, its vast form making the opening bulge and stretch.

Now, Drevik thought. Before it's too late.

He reached for the edges of the breach. Not with his hands—with his power, his will, everything he had. The shadow-magic came, responding to his need, and he felt the wound start to close.

The entity screamed.

It wasn't a sound. It was a pressure, a force, a rage that threatened to tear his mind apart. The thing thrashed against the closing breach, trying to push through before it was too late, but Drevik held on. He held on with everything he had.

Close, he willed the breach. Close, damn you. CLOSE.

The wound in reality began to seal.

The entity's tendril was cut off, severed from its main body, and it writhed in the mortal world like a snake with its head removed. Drevik felt it being pulled back, felt the pressure easing, felt the barrier between worlds reasserting itself.

No! The entity's voice was rage and hunger and desperation. You cannot—

But he could. He was. The breach was closing, layer by layer, the Veil knitting itself back together.

And then, with a sound like a thunderclap, it sealed.

Drevik stood in the Shadowlands, alone.

The twilight pressed around him, heavy and thick. In the distance, he could see shapes moving—things that lived in this realm, things that would be very interested in a mortal who'd just trapped himself in their domain.

I did it, he thought, almost surprised. I actually did it.

His legs gave out. He collapsed to his knees on ground that felt like packed ash, his body shaking with exhaustion, his power completely spent.

Now I just have to survive long enough to find a way back.

He looked up at the sky—at the strange, lightless sky that seemed to go on forever—and thought of Lexi. Her face, her voice, her arms around him. The taste of her kiss.

I promised, he reminded himself. I promised I'd come back.

Somewhere in the distance, something howled.

Drevik pushed himself to his feet, swaying slightly, and began to walk.

Back in the Mortal Realm

Lexi stood at the sealed gate, her hand pressed against cold stone.

"He's gone," she whispered. The words tasted like ash. "He's really gone."

"We'll find him." Ivy's voice was thick with tears. "The Order has resources, ancient texts, ways to—"

"Stop." Lexi's voice came out harder than she intended. "Just... stop. I need a minute."

Ivy fell silent. Behind them, Order members were flooding into the sanctum, drawn by the surge of power, the closing of a breach that shouldn't have existed. Elder Thornwood was among them, leaning heavily on Seraphina's arm, his face pale but determined.

But Lexi couldn't focus on any of that. She could only stare at the sealed gate and feel the space where Drevik should have been.

He said he'd come back, she reminded herself. He promised.

But promises were just words. And the Shadowlands were not kind.

A hand touched her shoulder. She turned to find her father standing there, his face lined with something she'd never seen in him before—genuine, unguarded emotion.

"I owe that boy my life," Elder Thornwood said quietly. "And more than that—I owe him the truth. About his parents. About what the Order has been hiding." His jaw tightened. "When we bring him back—and we will bring him back—he deserves to know everything."

Lexi's eyes stung. She blinked rapidly, refusing to cry in front of the gathering crowd.

"He told me he loved me," she said, her voice barely audible. "Right before he walked through that gate. He told me he loved me, and then he left."

Her father's expression softened.

"Then we bring him home," he said firmly. "And you tell him again."

Lexi nodded, swallowing hard. Her hand fell away from the sealed gate, her fingers curling into a fist at her side.

Drevik was out there, somewhere in the eternal twilight, fighting to survive. And she was here, safe and whole and useless.

But not for long.

I'm coming for you, she promised silently, staring at the stone as if she could see through it to the realm beyond. Whatever it takes, however long it takes—I'm coming for you.

The Order's members began to swarm around the sealed gate, examining it, discussing it, planning. Behind them, Corvin was being dragged away in shadow-chains, his face still frozen in that expression of shocked terror.

It was over. The breach was sealed. The traitor was caught.

But for Lexi, nothing was resolved.

Drevik was gone.

And she would move heaven and earth to get him back.
