Best French Press Coffee Makers (And How to Use Them Right)

July 2026 · 5 products reviewed

I've been making French press coffee every morning for eight years, and I still remember the first time I did it wrong. Boiling water straight from the kettle, pre-ground coffee from a can, and a four-minute steep that produced something I can only describe as "bitter disappointment in a cup." The French press is simple — but simple doesn't mean foolproof.

The grounds know more than the cup.The best French press makes coffee that's rich, full-bodied, and actually tastes like the beans you bought. The worst one gives you a cup of murky grounds water with a side of burnt rubber. After testing nine presses over six months, here's what actually matters and what to buy.

What Makes a Good French Press?

Three things separate a great French press from a mediocre one:

  • Filtration: The number one complaint about French press coffee is grounds in the cup. A good filter system (or double filter) keeps grounds out without needing a secondary pour-through filter. The Espro P7's micro-filter is in a different league.
  • Heat retention: Glass looks nice but loses heat fast. If you're not pouring immediately, double-wall stainless steel keeps coffee hot for 30+ minutes instead of 10. Single-wall glass is fine if you drink right away.
  • Durability: Glass presses shatter. It's not a question of if, but when — usually when you're washing it and it slips. Steel presses are functionally indestructible. If you're clumsy (like me), factor this in.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much coffee do I use in a French press?

Start with a 1:15 ratio — 1 gram of coffee for every 15 grams of water. For a standard 34oz (1 liter) French press, that's roughly 65 grams of coffee (about 2/3 cup of whole beans). Grind coarser than you think — it should look like coarse sea salt, not sand.

How long should I steep French press coffee?

4 minutes. Set a timer. Most people over-steep and get bitter coffee, or under-steep and get weak coffee. Four minutes at the right grind size gives you full extraction without the harshness.

Why does my French press coffee have grounds in it?

Two reasons: your grind is too fine, or your filter screen is worn out. French press requires coarse grind. Replace the filter screen every 6-12 months (they're $5-8) and the grounds issue mostly disappears.

Is French press coffee bad for cholesterol?

Unfiltered coffee contains cafestol, a compound that can raise LDL cholesterol. If you have high cholesterol, paper-filtered coffee removes most of it. For most people drinking 2-3 cups a day, the effect is small. Talk to your doctor if you're concerned.

Can I use a French press for cold brew?

Yes. Use a 1:8 ratio of coffee to cold water, stir, put the lid on with the plunger up, and refrigerate for 12-16 hours. Press and pour. Dilute the concentrate with water or milk to taste.

How do I clean a French press?

Dump grounds in the trash (not the sink). Rinse with warm water, add a drop of dish soap, plunge a few times to clean the screen, rinse thoroughly. Deep clean monthly: disassemble the filter screens and soak in white vinegar for 15 minutes.

Glass or stainless steel French press?

Glass lets you see the coffee and doesn't retain flavors, but it breaks. Stainless steel is indestructible and keeps coffee hot longer, but you can't see inside. If you knock things over, get steel. If you're careful and like watching the brew, get glass.
