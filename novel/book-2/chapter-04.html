Chapter 4: Forbidden Pages

Chapter 4: Forbidden Pages

The restricted section of the Order's archives smelled of dust, old paper, and something darker—something that made the hair on Drevik's arms stand up even before he stepped through the threshold.

He'd received Ivy's message three hours ago, the slip of paper appearing under his dorm room door sometime after midnight. Midnight. East alcove. Come alone. Don't be late. No signature, but he'd recognized her handwriting from the labels she'd been teaching him to read—the archaic script the Order used for their oldest records.

Now he stood in the shadow of a towering bookshelf, waiting, every sense on alert. The archives spread before him like a labyrinth, shelves stretching up into darkness that the flickering wall sconces couldn't fully penetrate. Somewhere deeper in the stacks, something whispered—a sound like pages turning in an empty room.

"You came."

Drevik spun, heart hammering, and found Ivy pressing her finger to her lips. She emerged from between two shelves like she'd materialized from the shadows themselves, her dark hair pulled back in a tight braid, her round glasses catching the amber light. The robes she wore were simple acolyte grey, but she'd pinned them close to her body with a leather sash—practical for moving quickly.

"I wasn't sure you would," she continued, her voice barely above a breath. "After what happened at the ceremony, I thought maybe you'd—"

"Ivy." Drevik kept his voice low, matching hers. "You asked me here. Whatever you have to say, say it."

She glanced over her shoulder, then gestured for him to follow her deeper into the stacks. "Not here. Somewhere the wards don't reach."

Wards?

The word sent a chill down his spine. He'd learned about the Order's protective enchantments in his first week—magical barriers that tracked movement, recorded conversations, and ensured that nothing within the compound went unnoticed by those who knew where to look. If Ivy was taking him somewhere beyond their reach, whatever she had to show him was dangerous enough to get them both in serious trouble.

He followed her anyway.

The deeper they went, the older the archives became. The neatly organized shelves gave way to cramped aisles between shelves of warped wood, filled with scrolls and bound volumes that looked like they'd crumble at a touch. The air grew colder, and Drevik's breath fogged in front of his face. The whispering sound he'd heard earlier grew louder—not words, exactly, but something close to language. Something that made his shadow magic stir beneath his skin.

"You can hear it, can't you?" Ivy glanced back at him, her brown eyes catching a glint of something in the darkness. "The records from before the Reformation. They're not... quiet. Not like the new ones."

"Before the Reformation?"

"Eighty years ago." She turned a corner, leading him down a spiral staircase carved into the stone floor. "When the Order restructured after the last major breach. They locked away everything that didn't fit the official narrative. Burned some of it. Hid the rest down here."

The stairs emptied into a small circular chamber, lit by a single candle that Ivy must have left earlier. A reading desk sat in the center, already piled with books and loose papers. The walls were lined with shelves, but these were different from the ones above—metal boxes with intricate locks, each one marked with symbols Drevik couldn't read.

Ivy moved to the desk and began sorting through the materials, her hands moving with practiced efficiency. "I've been coming here for months. Ever since you were initiated. I told myself I was just curious about the new acolyte everyone was whispering about, but that wasn't the whole truth." She looked up at him, her expression unreadable in the candlelight. "I recognized your name."

Drevik's throat tightened. "Markinsoul isn't exactly common, but—"

"Your mother's name was Sera Markinsoul. She was a Seeker in the Order, twenty-two years ago." Ivy pushed a leather-bound volume toward him across the desk. "She disappeared during a breach event. Officially, she was declared lost to the Shadowlands, her spirit torn apart before she could be retrieved. But that's not what happened."

The book sat between them, its cover unmarked save for a single symbol burned into the leather—a crescent moon wrapped in what looked like thorny vines. Drevik's hand trembled slightly as he reached for it.

"How do you know this?"

"Because I found her research notes." Ivy opened the book to a page marked with a faded ribbon. "She was investigating a pattern of breaches that didn't match the Order's understanding of Shadowlands dynamics. The official position has always been that breaches are random—tears in the veil caused by the natural ebb and flow of power between realms. But your mother discovered something different."

Drevik looked down at the page. The handwriting was dense, cramped, and interspersed with diagrams and calculations he couldn't fully follow. But certain words jumped out at him: deliberate, artificial, controlled.

"She thought someone was creating the breaches on purpose," he said slowly, the implications settling over him. "Someone in the Order."

"She did more than think it. She proved it." Ivy pulled out another document—a folded letter, its edges worn from handling. "This was meant for the Veiled Council. A formal accusation. But she never delivered it. Three days after she wrote this, she disappeared."

Drevik took the letter, his eyes scanning the faded ink. His mother's voice emerged from the page—formal, measured, but carrying an undercurrent of fear that resonated across the decades. I have identified the pattern. The breaches follow lunar cycles, yes, but their timing corresponds to specific rituals performed by members of our own Order. I do not yet know who is responsible, but I know the mechanism. Someone is weakening the veil intentionally, using the Order's own power against it.

"She was murdered." The words came out flat, certain. "Someone found out what she knew, and they silenced her."

"I believe so." Ivy's voice softened. "I'm sorry. I know this isn't—"

"Don't." Drevik looked up, meeting her eyes. "Don't apologize. You're the first person who's told me anything real about my mother since I joined this Order. Everyone else acts like she never existed."

"Because they're afraid." Ivy stepped closer, her hand resting briefly on his arm. The touch sent a warm current through him, not entirely magical. "The people who did this are still here. Still in power. If they know you're asking questions—"

"Why are you telling me this?" Drevik turned to face her fully, suddenly aware of how close they were standing in the small chamber. The candle cast shifting shadows across Ivy's face, softening the severity of her features. "You've been gathering this information for months. You said so yourself. Why risk everything to help me?"

Ivy was quiet for a long moment. When she spoke, her voice was barely audible.

"Because I know what it's like to lose someone and have everyone pretend they never existed."

The raw pain in her words cut through Drevik's suspicion. He waited, giving her space to continue.

"My brother. Three years older than me. He was a Warden—one of the youngest ever promoted to that rank. Brilliant, talented, everyone said he'd be an Elder someday." Ivy's hand dropped from his arm, and she turned away, adjusting her glasses in a gesture that seemed more about having something to do with her hands than any practical need. "He discovered something in the archives, too. Not about the breaches—I don't know exactly what. He never told me. But I watched him change over the course of six months. Growing more paranoid, more afraid. And then one night he walked into the Shadowlands and didn't walk out."

"Ivy..."

"They said it was suicide. That the pressure of his position broke him, that he simply gave up." She turned back to face him, her jaw set with determination that transformed her nervous energy into something fierce. "But I knew my brother. He didn't give up. He found something, just like your mother did, and it got him killed. And I've spent every day since then trying to figure out what it was."

The chamber felt smaller suddenly, the walls pressing in. Drevik was acutely aware of Ivy's proximity, the way the candlelight caught the curve of her cheek, the intensity in her eyes that had nothing to do with scholarly interest. She was sharing more than information—she was sharing a wound, one that matched his own.

"You think the same people are responsible," he said. "The ones who killed my mother."

"I think the breaches, your mother's death, my brother—it's all connected. The pattern she identified twenty-two years ago? It's happening again." Ivy moved back to the desk, pulling out a rolled parchment covered in dates and symbols. "I've been tracking breach reports for the past six months. The timing matches the lunar cycle exactly, but that's not the significant part. Look at the locations."

Drevik unrolled the parchment, his eyes scanning the marked positions on a hand-drawn map. Each breach was labeled with a date and a location—and every single one had occurred within a mile of the university campus.

"They're getting closer."

"They're converging. Whatever they're doing, whoever is creating these breaches, they're building toward something. And the center of the pattern—" Ivy pointed to a spot on the map, marked with a red circle. "—is right here. The compound beneath the university. The heart of the Order's power."

A chill ran through Drevik that had nothing to do with the cold air. His mother had been right. Someone in the Order was deliberately weakening the veil—and they were using the Order's own headquarters as the focal point.

"Why didn't anyone else notice this? The Elders, the Wardens—"

"Maybe they have." Ivy's voice dropped even lower. "Maybe some of them are in on it. Or maybe they've just been told not to look too closely. Either way, you need to be careful. You're an acolyte, which means you have access to parts of the compound that initiates don't. People talk around you. They assume you're too new, too unimportant to matter. If you keep your eyes open, you might see things that others miss."

Drevik looked at the map again, then at the stack of documents Ivy had assembled. His mother's notes. The breach pattern. The letter that had never been delivered. It was more than he'd learned in weeks of training, more than anyone had ever told him about the Order or his own family.

And Ivy had been collecting it all, piece by piece, for months. For him.

"Why me specifically?" he asked. "You could have taken this to someone with more authority. A Warden, an Elder—"

"And watched them bury it, like they buried everything else?" Ivy shook her head. "No. You're an unknown quantity. You're outside the power structure, outside the families that have controlled the Order for generations. And you have a personal stake in this. Your mother—" She hesitated, then stepped closer again. "You deserve to know the truth. And maybe... maybe you're the one who can finally do something about it."

The air between them seemed to thicken. Drevik was suddenly aware of how alone they were in this hidden chamber, surrounded by secrets that could get them both killed. Ivy's bravery, her months of careful research, her willingness to trust him with knowledge she'd risked everything to uncover—it was more than professional courtesy. More than shared interest.

He reached out, brushing a strand of hair from her face. The gesture was impulsive, driven by something he didn't fully understand, but Ivy didn't pull away. Her breath caught, her eyes widening behind her glasses.

"Drevik..."

"I know." His voice was rough. "I know this isn't—I know we're in the middle of something dangerous. But I need you to know that I understand what you're risking. And I'm not going to let it be for nothing."

Ivy's hand came up to cover his, pressing his palm against her cheek. The contact sent a jolt through him—warmth and shadow mixing in his blood, his power responding to the closeness of another magical being. Her eyes fluttered closed for just a moment, and when she opened them again, there was something new in her expression. Something that looked like hope.

"I've been so alone with this," she whispered. "For three years, I've been gathering pieces of a puzzle I couldn't solve by myself. And then you walked into the initiation ceremony, and I saw your name, and I thought—maybe this is it. Maybe this is the moment everything changes."

"It will." Drevik's thumb traced the line of her jaw. "Whatever's happening in the Order, whatever killed my mother and your brother—we're going to find out. And we're going to stop it."

Ivy leaned into his touch for a moment longer, then reluctantly pulled back. The practical part of her reasserted itself, the nervous scholar returning to the surface.

"We should go through the rest of these notes quickly. I've already extended the ward bypass longer than I should have—"

A sound from above made them both freeze.

Footsteps. Heavy, deliberate, moving through the stacks above their heads.

Drevik's heart slammed against his ribs. "Did anyone follow you?"

"I don't think so. I was careful, I—" Ivy's face went pale. "The shadow alarm. I disabled the primary wards, but there's a secondary system that triggers if someone accesses the metal boxes. I didn't think anyone would—"

Another set of footsteps joined the first. Voices, muffled but audible through the stone.

"—definitely came this way. The wards lit up like a festival display."

"Who has access this time of night?"

"That's what we're about to find out."

Ivy moved quickly, shoving documents into Drevik's hands. "Take these. Your mother's notes, the breach map, the letter—everything that proves the pattern. Hide them in your room, somewhere the searches won't find them."

"What about you?"

"I'll stay. Cover our tracks, make it look like I was doing independent research. I have permission to be in the archives—I can explain away a late-night study session." Her hands trembled as she gathered the remaining papers. "You don't have that luxury. If they find you here, especially with these documents—"

"I'm not leaving you to face them alone."

"Drevik." Ivy grabbed his arm, her grip surprisingly strong. "You don't understand. If they find out what you know, what you're carrying, they'll kill you. Not officially, not publicly—but you'll disappear, just like your mother. Just like my brother. And then everything I've done, everything I've gathered, will be for nothing."

The footsteps were closer now, descending the spiral stairs. Drevik could hear the creak of leather, the soft clink of metal—the sounds of Wardens in full gear.

"Ivy—"

"Go." She pushed him toward the back of the chamber, where the shadows pooled thickest. "Use your gift. Walk through the shadows. Get out of here."

"You said there were wards—"

"The wards are disabled, and the shadow alarm only tracks physical movement through the archives. If you walk through shadow, you'll bypass everything." Her eyes were bright with fear, but her voice held steady. "I'll find you tomorrow. We'll figure out the next step. Just go."

Drevik clutched the documents against his chest. He could hear the Wardens' voices clearly now, calling out to each other as they reached the bottom of the stairs.

Ivy shoved him hard, her hands against his chest. "Go. Now."

He reached for the shadows, felt them respond to his will, wrapping around him like cold water. But even as the darkness began to swallow him, he saw Ivy turn toward the chamber entrance, straightening her spine, arranging her face into an expression of innocent confusion.

And then the shadows took him, and the world fell away.

He emerged in his dorm room, the documents still clutched against his chest, his heart pounding so hard he could feel it in his throat. The familiar surroundings felt surreal after the hidden chamber, the candlelight, the weight of secrets revealed.

Drevik moved to his bed and began pulling up the loose floorboard he'd discovered his first week—a hiding spot he'd never thought he'd need. His hands shook as he placed the documents inside, covering them with a spare shirt, replacing the board, spreading his rug back over the spot.

Then he sat on his bed, his head in his hands, and tried to process everything he'd learned.

His mother had been murdered. Someone in the Order was creating breaches deliberately. The attacks were converging on the compound. And Ivy—quiet, nervous Ivy who worked in the archives and helped him with his translation exercises—had been carrying the weight of that knowledge alone, for months, waiting for someone she could trust.

He thought about the way she'd looked at him in the chamber. The way she'd leaned into his touch. The fear in her eyes when she'd heard the Wardens approaching—not for herself, but for him.

She's been alone with this for three years. Just like I've been alone since my grandmother died.

Drevik stood, pacing the small room. He wanted to go back, to make sure Ivy was safe, to stand beside her when she faced the Wardens. But he knew she was right. If they found him with the documents, everything would be lost. His only chance to help her was to stay hidden, to preserve the evidence, to wait for her to contact him tomorrow.

Assuming she could.

Assuming they didn't arrest her, or worse.

Someone in the Order is creating breaches deliberately.

His mother had discovered the same pattern twenty-two years ago. She'd tried to report it, and she'd vanished. Now it was happening again, and the people responsible were still in position, still pulling strings, still killing anyone who got too close to the truth.

Drevik thought about Lexi, about the way she'd looked at him during training—suspicious, challenging, but with something else beneath it. He thought about the other acolytes, the initiates, the Wardens and Elders who moved through the compound with their secrets and their power struggles.

How many of them knew? How many of them were complicit?

And how could he possibly know who to trust?

A knock at his door made him freeze.

He counted to three, forcing his expression into something neutral, before opening it.

Lexi stood in the hallway, her auburn hair loose around her shoulders, her green eyes sharp with something he couldn't read. She was still dressed in her training clothes—fitted pants, a sleeveless tunic that showed the defined muscles of her arms.

"Drevik." Her voice was carefully controlled. "You're awake late."

"So are you."

She didn't smile. "There's been a security incident in the archives. Someone triggered the shadow alarm. The Wardens are investigating."

Drevik kept his face still. "That sounds serious."

"It is." Lexi's eyes searched his face, looking for something. "They've detained one of the archive workers. A woman named Ivy. Do you know her?"

Detained.

The word hit him hard. He let a flicker of confusion cross his face—not hard to manage, given the circumstances.

"Ivy? She's helped me with some translations. I wouldn't say I know her well. Why?"

Lexi was quiet for a long moment. When she spoke again, her voice had softened slightly, though her expression remained guarded.

"She was found in the restricted section, past the disabled wards. The Wardens think she was stealing information." Another pause, weighted with meaning. "They're questioning her now. But I thought you should know. In case you have any... insight."

Drevik forced himself to hold Lexi's gaze. "I don't know why she would do something like that. She always seemed dedicated to the Order."

"People are not always what they seem." Lexi's voice carried an edge. "Sometimes they hide their true purposes. Sometimes they let others take risks for them while they stay safe in the shadows."

The accusation in her words was clear. But was she accusing him of being involved, or warning him that Ivy had taken a fall for someone else's benefit?

"I appreciate you telling me," he said carefully. "If there's anything I can do to help—"

"There isn't." Lexi stepped back, her expression closing off. "This is Warden business now. The best thing you can do is stay out of it. Focus on your training. Don't draw attention."

She turned to leave, then paused, looking back over her shoulder.

"Whatever you're involved in, Drevik, be careful. The Order doesn't take kindly to acolytes who overstep. And I'd hate to see you make a mistake you can't come back from."

She walked away before he could respond, her footsteps fading down the corridor.

Drevik closed his door and leaned against it, his heart racing. Ivy was in custody. The Wardens were questioning her. And Lexi—whatever her motivations—had just given him a warning that felt far more significant than a casual concern for a fellow acolyte.

He thought about the documents hidden under his floor. About his mother's research, the pattern of breaches, the conspiracy that had killed her and countless others.

This is bigger than I understood. Bigger than I'm ready for.

But he couldn't walk away now. Ivy had risked everything to bring him this information, to trust him with secrets she'd carried alone for years. He wouldn't let her sacrifice be meaningless.

Tomorrow, he would find a way to help her. He would begin unraveling the truth about his mother's death. And he would discover who in the Order was worth trusting—and who wanted him dead.

Drevik climbed into bed, but he knew sleep wouldn't come. The shadows in his room seemed deeper than usual, and he couldn't stop thinking about Ivy's face as she'd pushed him toward safety—brave, terrified, and utterly alone.

He wouldn't let her stay that way.

Not if he could help it.
