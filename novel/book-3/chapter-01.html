Book 3: The Veiled Truth

Chapter 1: New Responsibilities

His mother's voice haunted the spaces between sleep and waking.

Trust no one.

Drevik opened his eyes to darkness and the unfamiliar ceiling of his Seeker quarters. The shadows in his room no longer pressed with menace—they bent toward him like plants seeking light, drawn by something in his blood that had changed during the ascension ritual. His new rank came with new senses, new weight, new responsibilities that pressed against his chest like invisible chains.

He sat up, pushing aside sheets that still smelled faintly of Mira's healing herbs. The bed felt wrong without the warmth of another body beside him. Three nights since his promotion, and he still woke reaching for someone who wasn't there.

The women had established a rotation. Katryn's idea, of course—color-coded calendars and designated nights that made his love life look like a committee meeting. Last night had been Mira's turn. Tonight belonged to Seraphina. Tomorrow, Lexi would claim what she considered her rightful place at the center of his attention.

Four women. Four bonds. Four potential weaknesses his mother had warned him about.

He swung his legs over the edge of the bed and planted his feet on cold stone. The Order's compound never warmed completely, no matter how many heating charms the maintenance workers laid. Something about being underground, cut off from the sun.

His reflection caught in the mirror across the room—taller than average, lean build, dark hair that refused to stay flat. His green-gold eyes looked different now, sharper, seeing things that had been invisible a week ago. The Seeker's gift let him perceive the threads connecting people, the subtle flows of power that animated the world. He'd barely begun learning to interpret what he saw.

A knock at his door. Sharp, authoritative. No hesitation.

"Enter."

Lexi pushed through without waiting for permission. Of course. She'd never let a closed door stop her, and his new rank hadn't changed that particular dynamic. Her auburn hair was pulled back in a tight braid, training clothes already replacing sleepwear.

"You're awake."

"Brilliant observation."

"Smart-ass." She crossed to his window, pulling back curtains to let in what passed for morning light this far underground. "Training in thirty. The Elder wants to see you after. Something about your first assignment."

He stood, stretching muscles that ached from yesterday's patrol. The minor Shadowlands breach he'd handled alone had been straightforward—a minor entity slipping through a thin spot in the barrier, dispatched with efficiency that still surprised him. Seeker status came with power upgrades he was only beginning to understand.

"Which Elder?"

"Thornwood." Her expression flickered. "My father wants to assess his new subordinate personally."

The tension in her voice carried weight. Her father's acceptance of their relationship was conditional at best, grudging tolerance that could vanish if Drevik failed to meet whatever impossible standards the man had set.

"I'll be ready."

"You'd better be." She moved toward him, her stride purposeful. "And Drevik—"

"What?"

Her kiss landed hard, demanding, over before he could deepen it. "Don't forget who you're coming home to."

The training yard buzzed with morning activity. Initiates and acolytes moved through practice forms under the watchful eyes of senior members, the clash of wooden swords and the snap of martial arts strikes creating a rhythm Drevik had grown familiar with over months of hard work.

He crossed the compound with Lexi at his side, acutely aware of the glances that followed them. Word of his promotion had spread quickly—Seeker was the rank where candidates for Elder and Veiled One began to distinguish themselves. The path from Initiate to Seeker was arduous. The path from Seeker to the highest ranks was political.

"You're thinking too loud." Lexi's voice cut through his spiraling thoughts. "I can practically hear the calculations."

"I'm not calculating anything."

"Liar." She elbowed him. "Every person who looks at you is weighing your worth. You're doing the same to them. It's what Seekers do."

She wasn't wrong. His new abilities made it impossible not to read the room—silver threads of loyalty here, black strings of hidden enmity there, the complex web of relationships that made the Order function. Everyone carried invisible marks of their connections. Some shone bright. Others lurked in shadow.

They reached the administrative wing, where the Elders kept their offices. Lexi stopped at the entrance.

"I'll see you tonight."

"Training?"

"Something like that." Her smile held a predatory edge. "Don't let my father intimidate you. He's testing you, but you've already passed the important trials."

"Which ones?"

"The ones that matter to me."

She left before he could respond, her footsteps echoing down the corridor. He watched her go, then turned to face the door that led to Elder Thornwood's domain.

The office of Elder Thornwood smelled of old books and older magic.

Drevik stood before the massive oak desk, noting the way the shadows in the corners bent away from the Elder rather than toward him. A subtle sign of power—something that would have been invisible to him before his promotion.

"Markinsoul." Thornwood's voice carried no warmth. "Sit."

He sat. The chair facing the desk was deliberately lower than the Elder's, forcing visitors to look up. A power play, obvious in its execution.

"Your first official assignment." A folder slid across the desk. "Minor Shadowlands breaches. Three in the past week, each handled by acolytes. The pattern concerns me."

Pattern. Drevik opened the folder, scanning reports that detailed thin spots in reality, minor entities, damage assessments. Taken individually, each incident seemed routine. Viewed together—

"They're forming a circle." He traced the locations on the attached map. "A perfectly even circle around the compound."

"Correct." Thornwood leaned back. "Someone is testing our barriers. Probing for weaknesses. I want to know who, why, and how."

"You suspect deliberate action?"

"I suspect everything, Markinsoul. That's how I've survived this long." The Elder's green eyes—so like Lexi's, yet utterly different—fixed on him. "Your mother had the same instinct. A natural Seeker, before she disappeared."

Drevik's pulse kicked up. He kept his expression neutral. "My mother?"

"A brilliant woman. Vanished before you were old enough to remember. We all assumed she'd fled to the Shadowlands, but no body was ever recovered." Thornwood's gaze sharpened. "Does her name come up often in your conversations with your father?"

"I never knew my father either. Grandmother raised me."

"Of course. My apologies." The apology held no actual remorse. "The point stands. Someone is testing our defenses. Find them. Stop them."

The dismissal was implicit in his tone. Drevik rose, folder in hand.

"One question, Elder."

Thornwood raised an eyebrow.

"Who else knows about this pattern?"

"Three people. Myself. You. And whoever created it." A thin smile. "I suggest you keep that list short."

The first breach site was a coffee shop near campus.

Drevik stood outside the building, noting the way shadows pooled unnaturally around the entrance. Normal humans walked past without noticing, their minds sliding away from the wrongness. But his Seeker's sight caught what they missed—the frayed edges of reality where something had pushed through.

Inside, the coffee shop hummed with morning traffic. Students with laptops, baristas calling orders, the mundane rhythm of academic life. He ordered black coffee he wouldn't drink and settled at a corner table, letting his senses spread.

The thin spot was behind the counter, near the espresso machine. He approached carefully, noting the way the barista's movements created small disturbances in the local reality. Each pass of her hand near the machine revealed a flicker of something else—darkness, depth, the pressure of another dimension pressing against this one.

"Excuse me." He caught her attention with a smile. "Is the machine acting up?"

She looked tired, stressed. "Third time this week. Keeps overheating for no reason."

"I know someone who could take a look." The standard Order cover. "Free of charge."

Her relief was palpable. "That would be amazing. The owner's been talking about replacing it, but those things cost a fortune."

He made a note, ordered a second coffee to maintain his cover, and settled in to wait until the morning rush ended. The thin spot would need careful examination, and he couldn't do that with witnesses.

An hour later, the coffee shop had emptied enough. He approached the espresso machine under the guise of examining it, then let his Seeker's sight take over fully.

The breach point glowed with sickly light visible only to him. Someone had deliberately weakened the barrier here, creating a controlled thin spot. Not random damage—surgical precision. Whoever had done this knew exactly what they were doing.

He reached out with his power, probing the edges of the wound in reality. The signature—

Wrong. Corrupted. He recognized the feel of Seeker work, the careful manipulation of boundaries, but this carried an undertone of Shadowlands influence. As if whoever had made the breach had been touching something from the other side while doing it.

His mother's warning echoed in his mind.

They're coming for what you carry.

He returned to the compound in the early afternoon, mind racing with implications. The breach signatures were wrong. Someone inside the Order had to be involved—the knowledge required to create such precise thin spots wasn't available to outsiders.

His quarters had transformed again.

Every time he left, he returned to find some new addition. Today's contribution: a small shrine in one corner, candles and crystals arranged in a pattern he didn't recognize. Seraphina's work, clearly, but the purpose remained opaque.

"You're investigating the breach pattern."

Not a question. He turned to find Seraphina by his window, her dark hair loose around her shoulders, her expression unreadable as always.

"How did you know?"

"Thornwood asked Katryn to pull the incident reports. She has access to administrative systems." Seraphina moved toward him, gliding more than walking. "The pattern forms a summoning circle."

Ice slid down his spine. "What?"

"A summoning circle. The breach points form a perfect geometric pattern around the compound. Whoever's creating them isn't testing defenses—they're building something. A cage, maybe. Or a gateway."

"How do you know this?"

She smiled, and for a moment her eyes reflected the darkness she'd been born from—a heritage he still didn't fully understand. "The Shadowlands speak to me, Drevik. I hear what they whisper. And right now, they're whispering about your compound."

"Why didn't you tell me sooner?"

"You didn't ask." She crossed to him, her hand finding his cheek. "You're still learning what questions to ask. Give yourself time."

"Time may be exactly what we don't have."

"Then ask better questions."

The evening brought the chaos of household management.

Drevik's small Seeker quarters—generous by Order standards but still cramped for five people—buzzed with competing demands. Katryn had commandeered his desk for her work, multiple screens displaying patrol schedules and supply requisitions and incident reports. Mira organized a corner into a healing station, her herbs and bandages taking up space he hadn't known he had. Lexi's weapons occupied a rack by the door, and Seraphina's mysterious shrine continued to occupy its corner.

Four women, making themselves at home. Four bonds that pulsed in his chest.

"Color-coded calendars," Katryn announced, sliding a tablet toward him. "Green nights are yours alone—recovery time, meditation, personal reflection. Blue is rotation one. Red is rotation two. Yellow is rotation three."

"Four rotations for four people?"

"Weeks cycle through different combinations." Her smile held genuine warmth beneath the efficiency. "Built-in variety, scheduled intimacy, and guaranteed recovery time. I've run projections—satisfaction rates should remain high across all participants."

"Romantic."

"I'm a romantic person." She paused. "I'm also a person who recognizes that logistics kill more relationships than infidelity. This way, nobody fights over time."

"She's not wrong." Lexi dropped onto his couch, still in training clothes, sweat drying on her skin. "The arrangement only works if everyone gets what they need."

Mira emerged from the kitchen with tea that smelled of honey and herbs. "And what do you need, Lexi?"

"Clarity." Her green eyes found Drevik. "I need to know where I stand."

The question hung in the air. He looked around at the four women who'd somehow become his household, each watching him with different expectations.

"You stand with me," he said. "All of you. That hasn't changed."

"Things have changed." Seraphina's voice came from her corner, soft but cutting. "You're a Seeker now. Your responsibilities have expanded. Your power has grown. And you're hiding something."

Silence.

Drevik felt the weight of their attention. Four bonds, four connections—and one secret that could shatter them all.

"I'm handling it."

"Handling what?" Mira's voice held concern, not accusation.

"Something from my past. It's personal."

"Personal." Lexi stood, her movement sharp. "You mean like the voice that came to you two nights ago? The one you pretended didn't happen?"

His jaw tightened. "You heard that?"

"We all heard it. We pretended we didn't because you clearly didn't want to discuss it." She crossed to him, stopping inches away. "But don't think for a second that we're going to let you lock us out. Whatever this is, it affects all of us."

"You don't understand—"

"Then make me understand." Her hand found his chest, pressing against the spot where the power lived. "We're not your enemies, Drevik. We're your partners. Your anchors. Let us help."

The bonds pulsed between them. He could feel each one—Lexi's fierce devotion, Seraphina's mysterious connection, Mira's quiet strength, Katryn's brilliant loyalty. Four threads, all pulling him toward honesty.

But his mother's warning held him frozen.

Trust no one.

"I can't." The words came out rough. "Not yet. I need to understand it myself first."

Lexi's hand dropped. "When you're ready, then."

She walked out. The door closed behind her with a sound like a verdict.

Night fell in earnest, and Drevik found himself alone in more ways than one.

The compound had quieted, most members retired to their quarters. He sat at his desk—not the one Katryn had claimed, but a smaller one by the window—studying the incident reports Thornwood had given him.

A shadow fell across his notes.

"Your guard's down." Seraphina materialized from the darkness by his window. "If I were an assassin, you'd be dead."

"If you were an assassin, you'd have better timing." He gestured at the papers. "Work."

"Or avoidance." She moved closer, her presence carrying the faint chill of Shadowlands energy. "Lexi's upset. She's not handling the sharing well."

"I noticed."

"Have you considered that her feelings matter as much as whatever secret you're keeping?"

The question stung because it was fair. He looked up at Seraphina, taking in the features that seemed to shift between human and something else depending on the light. Her heritage—part Shadowlands entity, part human—gave her perspectives none of them fully shared.

"I'm not trying to hurt anyone."

"I know." She settled beside him, her hip pressing against the desk. "But good intentions don't prevent harm. Sometimes they cause it."

"What would you do?"

Her laugh was soft, bitter. "I'm the wrong person to ask. My response to difficult emotions is to hide in shadows and brood. Not exactly a role model."

"Then why are you here?"

She considered the question. "Because someone needs to remind you that secrets have weight. You carry this alone, and you'll collapse under it. That's not weakness—it's physics."

He let out a breath. "My mother contacted me. The night of my promotion. She spoke through the wards somehow."

Seraphina's expression didn't change. "Your mother, who's been missing since you were a child."

"Yes."

"And she told you...?"

"Nothing useful. Warnings. Vague references to truth and war and keys." His hands tightened on the desk's edge. "She said to trust no one with it."

"Including the four of us?"

"She was very specific about that."

Silence. Then Seraphina nodded slowly.

"Your mother may have reasons we don't understand. She may be right—some secrets are dangerous to share." Her hand found his. "But secrets are also dangerous to keep. Choose your burden, Drevik. Carrying it alone, or carrying the weight of your partners' worry."

"That's not a real choice."

"No. It's not." She squeezed his fingers. "But that's what it means to love people. You don't get easy choices."

Her lips met his, cool and certain. The kiss held no fire—just the steady pressure of someone who understood darkness better than light.

"Stay," he said against her mouth.

"I wasn't planning to leave."

The morning brought new complications.

Drevik woke to Seraphina's warmth beside him and a message waiting on his phone. The anonymous text was brief, its implications vast:

Stop investigating the breach pattern. You're making enemies you can't afford.

He stared at the words, reading them over and over. Someone inside the Order had his personal contact information. Someone inside the Order wanted him to back off.

Seraphina stirred beside him, her dark eyes opening. "What's wrong?"

He handed her the phone without a word.

Her expression hardened as she read. "This came through official channels?"

"Personal line. Untraceable, probably."

"Probably." She sat up, hair falling around her shoulders. "Or whoever sent it wanted you to know they have access to your personal information."

The implications settled in his stomach like cold stone. He'd been a Seeker for three days, and already someone was threatening him.

"We need to tell the others."

"We need to be smarter." Seraphina rose, moving toward her clothes. "Tell Katryn first. She'll know how to trace this, or at least figure out who had access to your contact information."

"And then?"

"Then we decide whether to tell Lexi's father or keep this between ourselves."

He watched her dress, noting the efficiency of her movements. No wasted gestures, no unnecessary motions. Everything about her served a purpose.

"You think the Elder is involved?"

"I think everyone is potentially involved. That's the problem with secrets—they create power vacuums that get filled by ambitious people." She turned to face him. "Get dressed. We have work to do."

He found Katryn in her office—a space she'd claimed as part of their arrangement, claiming she needed quiet for her systems work. Her screens glowed with data streams, patrol reports, and incident logs.

"This your morning coffee?" He set a cup beside her.

"Your morning coffee." She didn't look up. "I don't touch caffeine before noon."

"Then I'll drink it myself."

She glanced at him, her sharp eyes catching something in his expression. "Problem?"

"Several." He handed her his phone. "This arrived this morning."

Her fingers flew across her own device as she examined the message. "Traceable?"

"I don't know how."

"I do." Her screens shifted, code scrolling past. "This was sent through the Order's internal messaging system—see the routing?—but masked to look like an external source. Sloppy work, honestly. Or deliberate."

"Deliberate how?"

"Someone wanted you to know this came from inside, but didn't want to reveal exactly who." Her typing intensified. "I'm checking access logs. Who had your personal contact information?"

"That's a short list."

"Shorter than you think." The screens populated with names. "Three Elders, two Seekers, and the administrative staff who processed your promotion."

"And all of them would know about my assignment."

"All of them would know." She paused, eyes narrowing at something in the data. "Drevik. Someone accessed the restricted archive last night. Elder's section."

"That's impossible. Only Elders have access."

"Someone with Seeker-level clearance made a request override. The request came from your credentials."

His blood went cold. "I was here all night."

"That's my point." She turned to face him fully. "Someone is using your authorization to access things you can't access. And they're doing it under your name."

The Veiled One's journal.

His mother's words surfaced through the chill in his veins. Find the Veiled One's journal. It holds the answers you need.

Someone was already looking for it. Someone was using his clearance to cover their tracks.

"We need to tell Thornwood."

"We need to be careful about telling Thornwood." Katryn's voice held grim precision. "If someone is framing you for restricted access, they're setting you up to take a fall. Reporting this to the Elder could put you on the defensive instead of the offensive."

"Then what?"

"We investigate quietly. Find out who accessed your credentials, when, and from where. Clear your name before anyone accuses you of something." Her hand found his. "And in the meantime, we watch each other's backs. All of us."

"You think one of us could be involved?"

"I think we can't afford to assume anyone is innocent." Her grip tightened. "Including me. Verify everything, Drevik. Trust your instincts. But verify."

The compound around them hummed with morning activity—people moving through hallways, conversations echoing from distant rooms. A normal day in the Order's hidden world, mundane and predictable.

Nothing about this felt predictable anymore.

He found Lexi in the training yard, her body moving through combat forms with mechanical precision. Her strikes landed harder than necessary, her blocks sharper than required. Everything about her posture screamed frustration.

"Can we talk?"

She completed her form before responding. "Talk."

"I'm sorry about last night."

"You don't owe me an apology." Her practice sword slashed through the air. "You owe me trust."

"I'm trying to—"

"Trying isn't doing." She turned to face him, sweat on her face, fire in her eyes. "I understand secrets, Drevik. I understand that some things need to stay hidden. What I don't understand is why you're treating me like a threat."

"I'm not—"

"You are. Every time you close off, every time you deflect, you're telling me that I'm not safe to share things with." Her sword lowered. "I've been protecting you since you arrived here. I've killed for you, bled for you, risked my position in the Order for you. And you still don't trust me."

"Lexi—"

"No." She stepped closer, her voice dropping. "If you're in danger, tell me. If someone's threatening you, tell me. If your mysterious past is coming back to haunt you, tell me. I can't help you if you don't let me in."

The words sat between them, heavy and honest. He looked at her—at the woman who'd gone from rival to partner to something irreplaceable—and felt the weight of his mother's warning crash against the reality of what Lexi was offering.

"There's a journal. The Veiled One's journal. My mother told me to find it before she... before she contacted me."

"Contacted you how?"

"Through the wards. A message from wherever she is."

Lexi absorbed this without flinching. "Your mother, who's been missing for twenty years, sent you a message from beyond the veil telling you to find a restricted artifact."

"Yes."

"And you didn't tell me because?"

"My mother told me to trust no one. She was very specific about that."

Pain flickered across Lexi's face. "Including me."

"Especially you. She mentioned you by name."

That gave her pause. Her sword clattered to the ground, forgotten. "She knows about me?"

"She knows about all of you. All four of us. She said the bonds are real, but anyone can be turned."

"Turned." Lexi's voice hardened. "You think I could be turned?"

"I think I don't know what to think. But I know I can't ignore the warning, and I know I can't ignore what you're offering." He closed the distance between them. "So here's what I'm going to do. I'm going to tell you everything. The message, the journal, the investigation, the anonymous threat this morning. Everything."

"Everything?"

"Everything." He held her gaze. "And then I'm going to ask you to help me figure out who's trying to frame me and what they're looking for. Because I can't do this alone, and you're right—I shouldn't try."

She studied him for a long moment. Then her hand found his face, pulling him toward her.

"Finally," she said against his lips. "You're learning."

By afternoon, all four women sat in his quarters, their expressions varying from concerned to calculating.

Seraphina had known most of it. Katryn filled in the technical details. Lexi brought strategic perspective. And Mira—Mira watched with the quiet attention of a healer assessing a wound.

"Someone accessed the restricted archive using Drevik's credentials." Katryn's screens showed the access logs. "The request originated from terminal seven in the east wing. That terminal was supposedly down for maintenance."

"Convenient." Lexi's arms crossed. "So we have a mystery access point and an anonymous threat. Both pointing to someone inside the Order."

"Someone with enough power to falsify maintenance records." Drevik leaned against his desk. "That narrows the list considerably."

"The journal you're looking for." Seraphina's voice was soft. "What's in it?"

"I don't know. My mother said it holds answers. She didn't say to what."

"And someone else is looking for it." Mira spoke for the first time. "Someone who doesn't want you investigating the breach pattern."

"The breach pattern might be connected." Katryn pulled up the map. "Look—the summoning circle Seraphina mentioned. The breach points form a perfect ring around the compound. But here." She highlighted a point at the center. "The restricted archive is at the exact center. The breaches aren't testing defenses. They're powering something."

Silence fell as the implications settled.

"Someone's creating a ritual." Seraphina's voice held certainty. "Using the breaches to accumulate power. And they need something from the archive to complete it."

"The journal." Drevik stood. "They're looking for the same thing my mother told me to find."

"Which means you're in their way." Lexi's hand found her sword hilt. "And they're trying to remove you."

"Not remove." Mira's voice was steady. "Discredit. If they frame Drevik for unauthorized access, they can eliminate him without raising suspicion."

"So we need to get to the journal first." Katryn's fingers flew across her devices. "I can override the archive locks, but it'll leave traces. We'd have maybe an hour before someone notices."

"Do it." Drevik met each woman's eyes in turn. "All of you. Are you with me?"

Four voices answered as one.

"Yes."

The word hung in the air, solid and certain. Four bonds, four threads pulling him toward a future he couldn't see.

His mother had told him to trust no one.

But she'd also told him the bonds were real.

He was about to find out which truth mattered more.

The plan was simple.

Katryn would create a distraction in the monitoring systems, triggering false alerts that drew security away from the archive. Lexi would provide physical backup in case things went wrong. Mira would monitor Drevik's vital signs remotely through the bond—Seeker abilities had practical applications beyond investigation. And Seraphina...

Seraphina would come with him. Her Shadowlands heritage gave her senses that might detect traps others would miss.

They moved at midnight.

The compound's corridors held the particular silence of sleeping institutions—maintenance systems humming, security patrols moving on predictable rounds. Drevik had memorized the schedule during his acolyte days. Now that knowledge served a different purpose.

Ready? Lexi's voice came through his earpiece, tinny but clear.

"Ready."

Distraction goes live in three... two... one.

The alarms didn't sound—Katryn had disabled the audible alerts—but he felt the shift in power flow as systems rerouted. Somewhere across the compound, security teams would be scrambling toward false breaches.

He and Seraphina moved through the shadows, her presence beside him like darkness folding over darkness. The restricted archive lived in the compound's heart, past the ceremonial chambers, beyond the Elder council room.

The door was locked. The locks were warded. The wards were layered.

"Can you bypass this?" he asked quietly.

Seraphina's hand pressed against the door. Her eyes closed, and for a moment she became something other than human—a bridge between worlds, touching the edges of reality.

"Give me a moment."

He watched the corridor, senses extended. No movement. No sound. Just the weight of darkness and the press of time.

Security team passing your location in four minutes, Katryn reported. Move fast.

"Got it." Seraphina stepped back. The door swung open.

The restricted archive stretched before them—shelves of ancient texts, artifacts sealed in protective cases, the accumulated knowledge of centuries. Somewhere in this room was a journal that might explain everything.

Or nothing.

They spread out, searching. The journal wasn't on any visible shelf—not surprising, given its importance. But Drevik's Seeker abilities let him read traces of power, and something in this room had been accessed recently.

"Here." He stopped before a case that looked empty. The wards around it were fresh—newly reapplied after someone had broken the previous ones.

The case was empty. The journal was gone.

"Someone beat us to it." Seraphina's voice held no surprise. "Or it was never here."

Time's up, Katryn warned. Security coming your way.

They moved. Out of the archive, through corridors that felt longer than before, past traps and wards that Seraphina navigated with instinctive ease.

The women were waiting when they returned to Drevik's quarters. He met their eyes and shook his head.

"Gone. Someone took it before us."

"Or it was never there." Lexi's expression held grim understanding. "Your mother's message. Did she say where the journal was?"

"Only that it was in the archive. She could have been wrong. Or she could have been lied to."

"Or the journal is bait." Mira's voice was soft. "And you just walked into a trap."

Drevik looked around the room—at the four women who'd risked everything on his word, at the bonds that pulsed between them, at the future that suddenly seemed uncertain.

His mother had told him to trust no one.

But she'd also told him the bonds were real.

And right now, those bonds were all he had.

"Then we figure out who's holding the bait," he said. "And we turn the trap back on them."

End of Chapter One
