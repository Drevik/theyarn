Chapter 18: Fratricide

Chapter 18: Fratricide

The first sign of betrayal was silence.

Drevik stood at the window of the safehouse, watching the empty street below. Three days they'd been here—three days of checking shadows, of mapping escape routes, of waiting for the extraction team that Commander Vance had promised. The message had come through Mira's encrypted channel: Team inbound. Trusted operatives only. Expect contact at midnight.

Midnight was seven minutes away.

Behind him, the women moved through the small space with the practiced efficiency of people who'd learned to live ready. Lexi checked her blade for the dozenth time, the soft whisper of steel against leather a familiar comfort. Seraphina sat cross-legged on the floor, her dark eyes distant as she communed with something none of them could see. Mira paced near the back door, her earlier defiance replaced by a coiled tension that made Drevik's skin prickle.

"You're doing it again," Lexi said without looking up.

"Doing what?"

"Thinking so loud I can hear it." She finally met his eyes, and something in her expression softened despite the circumstances. "They'll be here. Vance gave his word."

"That's what worries me." Drevik turned from the window. "Vance gives his word to a lot of people. I'm starting to wonder what it's worth."

Mira stopped pacing. "You don't trust him?"

"I don't trust anyone who hasn't bled for me yet."

The words hung in the air, heavier than he'd intended. Each of these women had bled for him—had risked everything. The weight of that responsibility settled into his bones like ice.

"You trust us," Seraphina said from the floor, her voice carrying that distant quality it always held when she communed with the shadows. "That's enough."

"Is it?" Drevik moved toward the center of the room, positioning himself where he could see both exits. "Three days ago, we were almost killed by people I thought were allies. Now we're supposed to believe a rescue team is coming? Because someone in the Order sent a message?"

"The message came through me," Mira said, her voice sharpening. "I verified the signatures. The encryption keys are legitimate."

"Keys can be stolen." Drevik's hand went to the shadow-blade at his hip, a weapon he'd manifested from his own connection to the darkness. "Signatures can be forged. And people who were supposed to be brothers—" He cut himself off, the old grief rising before he could suppress it.

Lexi stood, crossing to him with purposeful steps. She stopped just short of touching him, her green eyes fierce in the low light. "We don't have time for doubt. If you don't trust the extraction, we find another way out. Right now. Together." She glanced at the others. "All of us."

Drevik opened his mouth to respond when the shadows in the corner of the room shifted.

Not the natural movement of light and darkness—something deeper. Something that made his blood sing and his instincts scream simultaneously. The Veil Sight activated without conscious thought, and suddenly he could see the darkness writhing, taking shape, reaching toward them with fingers made of nothing and everything.

"They're coming," he breathed. "Now. Everyone down—"

The window exploded inward.

Drevik moved on instinct, grabbing Lexi and pulling her behind the overturned couch as glass showered the room. A figure descended through the shattered frame, landing with preternatural grace. Moonlight glinted off familiar features, off dark hair and a face Drevik had seen in the Order's halls a hundred times.

Kestrel. One of Vance's most trusted operatives. The woman who'd trained him in shadow-walking techniques during his first month as an acolyte.

"Hello, boy." Her voice was flat, empty of the warmth he remembered. "Commander sends his regards."

"Kestrel—" Drevik rose from behind the couch, keeping his body between her and the women. "What is this? The extraction—"

"Is real." She smiled, and it didn't reach her eyes. "We're here to extract you. Just not in the way you were hoping for."

More figures materialized from the shadows—four, five, six—spreading through the small space with the coordinated precision of a military unit. Drevik's heart slammed against his ribs as he recognized faces. People he'd trained with. People he'd shared meals with. People he'd trusted.

"How sweet," one of them said—Marcus, an acolyte who'd been just ahead of Drevik in rank. "He looks surprised. Did nobody tell him what happens to problems in the Order?"

"Vance sent you to kill me?" Drevik's voice came out steadier than he felt. "After everything—"

"Vance sent us to contain a threat." Kestrel's hand moved to the blade at her hip. "You've been running with traitors, boy. Associating with known enemies of the Order. Learning things no acolyte should know." Her smile sharpened. "And then there's the small matter of what you are."

"What I am?"

"The Commander has questions. The Elders have questions." She drew her blade, and Drevik felt the shadows recoil from the cold iron. "You can come quietly, answer those questions, and accept judgment. Or we can drag your corpse back and let the mages pull the answers from your cooling blood."

"Those are my only options?"

"Those are your only options."

Drevik glanced back at the women. Lexi had risen, her own blade drawn, positioned to protect Seraphina and Mira. The look in her eyes said she'd seen this coming—had warned him—and she was ready to fight beside him to the death.

Something in his chest settled. Calm spread through his limbs, the strange peace that came when there were no more choices to make. Only action.

"You know," he said, turning back to face Kestrel and the others, "I've spent my whole life being dragged places. Being told where to go, what to learn, who to trust." He let his power uncurl, felt the shadows in the room respond to his will. "I think I'm done with that."

"Then you choose death."

"I choose my own path."

Kestrel moved faster than he'd expected—faster than she'd ever moved in training. Her blade swept toward him in a lethal arc, and Drevik didn't try to block it. He simply... wasn't there anymore.

Shadow-walking in the same room was nearly impossible for most practitioners. The distances were too short, the concentration required too intense. But Drevik wasn't most practitioners. He emerged behind Kestrel, his own blade manifesting from pure darkness, and drove it toward her back.

She spun, parrying with inhuman speed. "You've improved."

"I had good teachers." He pressed forward, their blades meeting in a shower of sparks that shouldn't have been possible with shadow-forged steel. "That's what makes this tragic."

"That's what makes this necessary."

Marcus and two others moved to flank him, but Lexi intercepted them with a feral cry. Her blade sang through the air, catching Marcus's strike and deflecting it with practiced precision. She moved like water, like fury, her auburn hair streaming behind her as she danced between opponents.

"Stay behind me!" she shouted, and Drevik felt a flash of irritation even as gratitude surged through him.

"I can fight—"

"I know you can fight!" She blocked a strike aimed at his blind side, her body pressing against his for a brief moment as she moved. "That doesn't mean you have to fight alone!"

Mira had engaged another operative near the back door, her movements jerky with conflicting emotion. This was her chapter—her brothers and sisters in the Order. Every strike she blocked, every counter she made, cost her something visible in her expression.

"Don't hold back on my account," the operative facing her snarled. "Traitor."

"I'm not the traitor here." Mira's voice cracked, but her blade stayed steady. "The Order was supposed to protect people. Was supposed to stand for something."

"The Order stands for order. Something you've forgotten."

Seraphina remained on the floor, eyes closed, hands moving in complex patterns that made the shadows writhe and twist. Whatever she was doing, it wasn't combat—but the operatives seemed to sense it was dangerous, because two of them broke away from the main fight to rush toward her.

Drevik saw them move. Saw the killing intent in their postures. And something inside him... shifted.

The future split before his eyes—possible outcomes branching like lightning. In one, Seraphina died. In another, she lived but was captured. In a third, he reached her in time but took a wound that would slow him for weeks. In a fourth—

He chose the fifth option.

Shadow exploded outward from him in a wave of pure darkness, freezing everyone in the room for a single heartbeat. In that suspended moment, Drevik moved. Not shadow-walking—something deeper. Something that bent the rules of what was possible. He stepped between moments, between heartbeats, and placed himself in front of Seraphina.

The operatives' blades descended.

Drevik raised his hand, and the shadows obeyed.

Not just the ambient darkness of the room—every shadow, every sliver of shade, every scrap of night responded to his call. They rose like living things, wrapping around the descending blades, catching them in grips that couldn't be broken. The operatives' eyes went wide with shock.

"What are you?" Kestrel breathed from across the room.

Drevik didn't have an answer. He could feel the shadows connected to him now—really connected—in a way they'd never been before. It was like feeling the heartbeat of the darkness itself, like becoming part of something vast and ancient and terrifying.

"I don't know," he admitted. "But I'm done being afraid of it."

He pushed, and the shadows flung the operatives backward, pinning them against the walls with tendrils of pure darkness. Marcus struggled against his bonds, face contorted with rage. The other operatives fought and failed, their own shadow-walking abilities useless against Drevik's command of the substance.

Kestrel alone remained free. She stood in the center of the room, blade lowered, staring at Drevik with an expression he couldn't read.

"You're supposed to be an acolyte," she said quietly. "You're supposed to be untrained, untested. A curiosity, nothing more."

"Looks like you underestimated me."

"Looks like." She raised her blade again, and Drevik felt the shadows strain toward her, eager to bind her like the others. "But I didn't survive this long by fighting fair."

She moved—and so did the shadows. But not Drevik's shadows. The darkness around Kestrel twisted, and suddenly she wasn't where she'd been. She was behind him. Behind Lexi.

Her blade descended toward Lexi's exposed back.

"NO!"

Drevik threw himself forward, shadow-walking without thinking, trying to close the distance. But he was too slow. Too far. The blade was already falling, already—

Lexi turned.

She didn't try to dodge. Didn't try to parry. She simply turned, placing herself between Kestrel and the path to Drevik, and took the blade through her shoulder with a sound that would haunt him forever.

Time stopped.

Drevik watched Lexi's body absorb the impact, watched her eyes widen with shock and pain, watched her mouth open in a silent scream. He saw Kestrel's surprise at the woman's sacrifice, saw the operative's blade lodged deep in flesh and muscle.

Then time resumed, and Drevik's world became rage.

The shadows didn't just respond to him this time—they became him. He felt his consciousness expand, felt the darkness pour through him like water through a broken dam. The room vanished. The safehouse vanished. Everything became shadow and fury and the desperate need to destroy the thing that had hurt her.

He crossed the distance without moving. Grabbed Kestrel's wrist without touching her. Felt her bones crack under the pressure of shadows he didn't remember summoning.

"You touched her." His voice came out wrong—too deep, too resonant, like multiple voices speaking in unison. "You dared to touch her."

Kestrel screamed, and somewhere in the back of his mind, Drevik recognized that sound. It was the scream of someone facing something beyond their understanding. Something beyond human.

"Yield!" The word tore from her throat. "Yield! Please—"

"Drevik, stop!"

Mira's voice cut through the haze. He turned toward her, shadows still writhing around him, and saw her standing over Seraphina, hands raised in a placating gesture.

"If you kill her, you become what they say you are." Mira's voice shook, but her eyes held steady. "Don't give them the excuse. Don't become the monster."

The shadows hesitated. Drevik hesitated. In that moment of stillness, he became aware of the room again—of the operatives pinned against the walls, of Marcus's furious struggles, of Lexi on the floor behind him with blood spreading across her shirt.

"Lexi." The shadows collapsed as his attention shifted, rushing back to him in a wave that left him dizzy and drained. He stumbled toward her, dropping to his knees beside her fallen form. "Lexi, look at me. Stay with me."

Her eyes fluttered open, glassy with pain but still fierce. "Told you," she whispered. "Stay behind me."

"Don't." His voice cracked. "Don't you dare make jokes right now."

"Someone has to." Her hand found his, squeezing weakly. "Takes your mind off the dying."

"You're not dying." He pressed his hands over the wound, feeling the warm blood pulse between his fingers. "I won't let you."

"Drevik—" Seraphina's voice came from behind him, strained but steady. "The wound isn't fatal. Not if we bind it now. Move your hands."

He obeyed, watching as Seraphina crawled forward—when had she moved?—and pressed her palms over Lexi's shoulder. Dark energy flowed between them, not healing but stabilizing, slowing the blood loss to a trickle.

"The blade missed anything vital," Seraphina said, her voice carrying that distant quality again. "But she needs a healer. A real one."

"Then we get her one." Drevik turned toward the operatives, still pinned against the walls. "Where's the extraction point? Where were you supposed to take us?"

Kestrel's face was pale, her broken wrist cradled against her chest. "Commander Vance... he has a facility. Eastern district. Medical wing."

"Then you're going to take us there."

"You'll be walking into a trap."

"I don't care." Drevik rose, and the shadows responded to his anger, rising with him. "You came here to kill me. Instead, you're going to save her. And then you're going to tell me everything you know about what Vance is planning."

Kestrel's eyes met his, and he saw fear there. Real fear, the kind that came from facing something that didn't fit into your understanding of the world.

"Or?" she asked, her voice barely audible.

"Or I let the shadows decide what happens next." He let darkness curl around his fingers, let her see the power he was barely holding in check. "And they're very, very angry right now."

The silence stretched between them. Then Kestrel nodded, a jerky motion that spoke of defeat.

"Fine. I'll take you. But you're making a mistake, boy. Vance has resources you can't imagine. People who can do things with shadows that make what you just did look like a parlor trick."

"Then I'll deal with them too."

"You'll die."

"Maybe." Drevik glanced back at Lexi, pale and still on the floor, and felt his resolve crystallize into something harder than stone. "But not today. Not while she still needs me."

He turned to Mira, who had watched the entire exchange with an expression of mingled horror and wonder.

"Can you walk them to the extraction point? You know the routes better than I do."

"I..." Mira seemed to pull herself together. "Yes. I can get you close. But Drevik, what you just did—the shadows, the way they responded—"

"I know." He kept his voice level, fighting down the panic that wanted to rise at the memory. "I don't know what I am anymore. But I can't think about that right now. Right now, I need to get Lexi somewhere safe."

"Then we move." Seraphina had finished her work, binding Lexi's wound with strips torn from her own shirt. "She can walk, but she'll need support."

"I'll carry her."

"You'll need your hands free in case there's trouble." Seraphina's dark eyes met his, and he saw understanding there. "I know you want to hold her. But you need to be ready to fight."

The words landed like stones in his chest. She was right. He knew she was right. But looking at Lexi's pale face, at the blood still seeping through the makeshift bandages, every instinct screamed to gather her close and never let go.

"Fine." He forced the word out. "Mira, take point. Seraphina, help Lexi. I'll watch our backs."

"And the prisoners?" Mira gestured to the operatives still pinned against the walls.

Drevik looked at them—really looked. Marcus, who he'd shared meals with. Kestrel, who had trained him. Others whose faces were familiar but whose names he'd never learned. They'd come here to kill him. To drag him back for questioning, for judgment, for whatever the Order did to people like him.

But they were also people. People who had lives, families, purposes. People who had believed they were doing the right thing, even if that belief was manipulated.

"Let them go," he said quietly.

Kestrel's eyes widened. "What?"

"I said let them go. The shadows will release you in thirty seconds. After that, you can follow us if you want. Try to complete your mission if you want." He met her gaze steadily. "But I'm not going to kill people I once called friends. I'm not going to become the monster you think I am."

"That's..." Kestrel shook her head slowly. "That's the stupidest thing I've ever heard. You're letting enemies live. Enemies who will come after you again."

"Maybe." Drevik moved to Lexi's side, helping her stand. She leaned against him, warm and alive and breathing, and that was all that mattered. "But at least I'll be able to look at myself in the mirror. Can you say the same?"

He didn't wait for an answer. With Mira in the lead and Seraphina supporting Lexi's other side, they moved toward the back door, stepping over glass and blood and the bodies of people who had tried to kill them.

Behind them, Drevik heard the shadows release their hold. Heard Kestrel's sharp intake of breath, the sound of bodies sliding down walls, the groans of the wounded.

He didn't look back.

The night air hit them like a slap, cold and sharp after the closeness of the safehouse. Drevik breathed it in, letting it clear some of the fog from his mind. The shadows still whispered at the edges of his consciousness, but quieter now, calmer. Waiting.

"You know they'll follow us," Mira said quietly, leading them down an alley that connected to the broader streets. "You showed them what you are. They'll report back to Vance, and he'll send more. Better equipped. Better prepared."

"I know."

"Then why—"

"Because I'm not them." Drevik adjusted his grip on Lexi, feeling her warmth against his side. "I'm not going to become the thing they're afraid of. I'm not going to give them justification."

"You think mercy is weakness." Lexi's voice came out thin, pained, but steady. "It's not. It's the only thing that keeps us human."

Mira turned, and in the moonlight, Drevik saw something shift in her expression. A softening. A realization.

"I didn't understand," she said quietly. "What you were. What you could become. The Order... they told us you were dangerous. A threat. But watching you just now—" She shook her head. "You could have killed all of them. Easily. And you chose not to."

"Would you have? Killed them?"

The silence stretched between them. Then Mira looked away, her voice barely audible.

"I don't know anymore. I thought I did. I thought the Order was right about everything. But watching you spare people who came to murder you..." She looked back, her eyes bright. "That's not the behavior of a monster. That's the behavior of someone worth following."

"I don't want followers. I want to survive. I want the people I care about to survive."

"Then let us help." Mira's voice strengthened. "Let me prove that not everyone in the Order is your enemy. Let me show you that some of us still remember what we're supposed to stand for."

Drevik studied her face, looking for any sign of deception. He saw exhaustion, grief, and underneath it all, a desperate hope.

"Fine," he said finally. "But if you betray me—"

"I won't." She met his eyes steadily. "I'm done betraying people I believe in."

They moved through the streets in silence, a strange procession of the wounded and the weary. Behind them, Drevik heard distant sounds—footsteps, voices, the stirring of pursuit. But they didn't come closer. Not yet.

"Your mother's voice," Lexi said suddenly, her voice barely a whisper. "When you were holding the shadows... I heard it too. Faint, like an echo."

Drevik tensed. "What did it say?"

"It said—" She winced, pain crossing her features. "It said your mercy would be your greatest strength. That you inherited it from her." Her eyes met his. "You hear her voice? Your mother's?"

"Sometimes. When I use the shadows. When I'm afraid." He looked away, uncomfortable with the vulnerability of the admission. "She tells me things. Guides me. I don't know if it's really her or just my mind trying to make sense of what I'm becoming."

"Maybe it doesn't matter." Lexi's hand found his, squeezing gently. "Maybe all that matters is that it helps you choose the right path."

"Does it? The right path?" He gestured at the darkness around them, at the blood on his clothes, at the woman he loved bleeding from a wound she'd taken for him. "I've lost count of the wrong paths I've walked lately."

"Then let us walk with you." Seraphina's voice came soft and certain. "That's what the Order was supposed to be, once. People walking together, sharing burdens. Maybe it can be that again."

"Maybe." Drevik looked up at the sky, at the moon hanging heavy and silver among the stars. "Or maybe it's too late for the Order. Maybe the rot goes too deep."

"Then build something new." Mira's voice carried a fierce determination that surprised him. "Something better. Something that remembers what honor actually means."

Drevik didn't respond. He didn't have answers—only questions, only fears, only the desperate hope that they would make it through the next hour alive.

But as they moved through the shadowed streets, heading toward a confrontation he wasn't sure he could win, he felt something he hadn't felt in a long time.

Hope. Small and fragile, but present.

These women had fought for him. Bled for him. Chosen him over everything they'd known. And somewhere in that choice, in that sacrifice, he found the strength to keep moving forward.

The shadows whispered at the edges of his consciousness, promising power, promising revenge, promising everything he'd ever wanted. But beneath those whispers, quieter but stronger, came his mother's voice.

You are not alone, my son. You were never alone. And that is why you will survive this.

Drevik tightened his grip on Lexi's hand and stepped into the darkness, trusting that the light would follow.

The extraction point was an abandoned warehouse on the eastern edge of the district, exactly where Kestrel had said it would be. Drevik could feel the shadows around it, could sense the people waiting inside—more operatives, more threats, more people who wanted to use him or destroy him.

"Stay close," he murmured to the women. "And be ready for anything."

Lexi's grip tightened on his hand. "Always."

They stepped through the door together, and Drevik felt the weight of what was coming settle across his shoulders like a mantle. Behind them, the shadows followed, patient and hungry, waiting for him to decide what kind of man he would become.

The answer to that question would have to wait.

First, they had to survive.
