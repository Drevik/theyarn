Best Countertop Dishwashers 2026: Tested Picks for Small Kitchens

October 2026 · 6 dishwashers reviewed

Counter space is cheap real estate in a small apartment, but nightly dish duty is expensive in a different currency: your time. A countertop dishwasher promises a real machine wash in a box the size of a small suitcase. After running six popular models through more than 40 hours of cycles (greasy pans, coffee cups, oatmeal bowls, and one deliberately burnt-on cheese bake), we know which ones earn their footprint and which ones are door-flavored paperweights.

The category has matured fast. Five-liter water tanks let these machines run with zero plumbing: you pour water in, close the lid, press start, and walk away. Faucet-connect models still exist and tend to cost less, but the tank models are what apartment dwellers and RV owners actually buy. Here is how the six best options stack up in October 2026.

What Actually Matters in a Countertop Dishwasher

  • Water source: Tank-fed models (5 liters) run anywhere but give you exactly one cycle per fill. Faucet-hookup models run indefinitely off the tap but pin the machine to a sink. Some units do both. If you cook big daily meals, faucet mode saves you two tank refills a day; if you live off-grid or your landlord restricts hookups, a tank is non-negotiable.
  • Capacity honesty: Most of these machines hold four place settings, not six. That translates to two people's daily dishes in one load: a couple of dinner plates, bowls, cutlery, and two pans. The COMFEE' is the outlier with six place settings and room for full-size dinner plates. Check the maximum plate diameter before buying; oversized pans never fit.
  • Wash temperature: Grease dissolves reliably above 140°F. Baby-care cycles that hit 167°F (like the HAVA and NOVETE) come close to sanitizing and cut through egg yolk that warmer-weather quick cycles leave behind. A listed wash temperature is a good sign; a machine that hides its spec sheet is a mild red flag.
  • Drying method: This is the overlooked spec. Residual-heat drying (hot rinse, steam inside a closed box) leaves plastics glossy and wet. Hot-air drying actively pulls water off everything, which is why models with heated drying get shelved dishes while others get dish racks. If your cabinet is full of plastic containers, pay for heated drying.
  • Noise: Good units run around 40 dB, about the volume of a quiet conversation, and you can run them during dinner. Cheap pumps whine and rattle, and a countertop placement amplifies vibration against the backsplash. Run a cycle before guests arrive and you will understand why spec sheets lead with this number.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do countertop dishwashers actually clean as well as full-size dishwashers?

Yes, with realistic expectations. A good countertop unit with dual spray arms and a 60-minute or longer standard cycle cleans greasy plates and glasses about as well as a full-size dishwasher. The tradeoffs are capacity and prep discipline: scrape food before loading, and soak baked-on sauce, because these machines run lower water pressure than built-in models. Quick cycles around 29 minutes usually leave dried egg or oatmeal behind. Run the normal or baby-care cycle for dirty loads and the machines in this guide handle everyday output for one to three people.

Can I use regular dish soap in a countertop dishwasher?

No. Use dishwasher-specific detergent only: pods, gel, or powder, roughly 0.2 to 0.3 oz per cycle. Regular dish soap foams so aggressively that it can seep past the door seals and stress the pump. If your water is hard, add a small amount of dishwasher salt or run a citric-acid cleaning cycle monthly. Most tank-fed models have a small internal detergent compartment, so fill it before starting the cycle rather than sprinkling detergent over the load.

How does the 5-liter water tank work, and how many cycles do you get per fill?

One fill gives you one full cycle. A 5-liter tank holds just enough for a standard wash, and the fill window or indicator on the lid shows the level. Expect to pour in roughly two juice cartons of water before each run, or connect the included inlet hose to a faucet for unlimited supply. Tank mode is what makes these machines viable in dorms, RVs, and rentals where attaching hoses is not an option. Heavy cooking days mean refilling the tank twice.

Will a countertop dishwasher fit my counter and my plates?

Most units measure roughly 17 inches wide, 17 inches deep, and 18 to 19 inches tall, so measure the gap between counter and upper cabinet; you need about 20 inches of clearance for the lid to open fully. Capacity is honest at four place settings (six for the COMFEE'), which covers a couple's daily dishes in one load. Plate diameter matters too: full 12-inch dinner plates fit in the wider COMFEE', while tighter machines want 10 to 11-inch plates or angled loading. Oversized pans usually stay in the sink.

Is a countertop dishwasher cheaper to run than hand washing?

Usually, yes. A standard cycle uses about 5 liters (1.3 gallons) of water, while running the tap through a hand-washing session can use 15 to 20 gallons. Peak power draw is modest, around 950 watts, so a hot cycle costs pennies. If you currently hand wash under running water, a countertop dishwasher pays for itself in water savings within months. If you already wash in a filled basin, the water savings shrink but the time savings stay real.
