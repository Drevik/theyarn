Chapter 8: Competition

Chapter 8: Competition

The Arena of Shadows had existed beneath the university for three centuries, carved from living rock and infused with so much accumulated power that the air itself felt heavy, electric. Drevik stood at the entrance, watching other acolytes filter through the arched doorway ahead of him, each one dressed in the simple gray competition robes that marked them as candidates.

His palms were sweating. He wiped them on the fabric, earning a sharp look from Lexi beside him.

"Stop that," she muttered. "You're making yourself look nervous."

"I am nervous."

"Then stop making it obvious." She smoothed the front of her own robes, the dark blue of a coach rather than a competitor. "You've trained for this. You've shadow-walked farther than anyone in your cohort. Your veil sight is better than most seekers. The only thing that can beat you today is your own head."

Drevik turned to look at her properly. In the torchlight that illuminated the corridor, her auburn hair caught fire at the edges, and her green eyes held a fierce intensity that made something in his chest tighten. She'd been drilling him for weeks, pushing him harder than she'd pushed anyone, staying late to help him refine his techniques.

"Why do you care so much?" he asked.

The question caught her off guard. He could see it in the slight widening of her eyes, the way her lips parted and then pressed together again.

"Because—" She stopped, glancing past him to where other acolytes were gathering. "Because the Order needs competent people on this mission. And because..." She met his eyes again, her voice dropping. "Because I've seen what you can do, Drevik. I've seen you push past every limitation they told you was absolute. You're going to be something remarkable, if you survive long enough to get there."

"If?"

"When." She corrected herself firmly. "When. Now get in there and show them what you are."

The Arena opened into a vast circular space, tiered stone benches rising around a central floor inlaid with silver and obsidian in patterns that hurt to look at directly. The ceiling was lost in shadow, but Drevik could feel the weight of it, pressed down by centuries of accumulated power.

Elders ringed the lowest tier, their masked faces unreadable. Above them, seekers and wardens filled the benches, while acolytes and initiates packed the upper levels. The entire Order, gathered to watch a handful of candidates prove themselves worthy of the Shadowlands mission.

Drevik's name was called third. He descended the stairs to the competition floor, the silver-obsidian pattern warm beneath his bare feet, and took his place in the line of candidates. He counted eight total—four men, four women, each of them the top of their respective training groups.

Including Mira.

She stood two places down from him, her dark hair braided back from her face, her dark eyes tracking every movement in the Arena. When her gaze swept over him, it paused, and her lips curved into something between a smile and a challenge.

He remembered her straddling him in the training room, remembered the way she'd moved, the heat of her, and felt his face warm. Her smile widened, like she knew exactly what he was thinking.

"First trial," announced Elder Corvus, his voice amplified by the acoustics of the space. "Shadow walking precision. You will each transport yourselves to the marked location and return. Speed and accuracy both judged."

A seeker stepped forward, unrolling a scroll. "Drevik Markinsoul. Target location: the reflecting pool in the eastern garden. Return to the center stone."

Drevik nodded, already visualizing the location. The reflecting pool was a hundred yards from the Arena entrance, through three corridors, past the library, in a courtyard that saw little foot traffic. He'd shadow-walked there before, during training with Lexi.

He closed his eyes, felt for the shadows around him—the torches created pockets of darkness, and the Arena itself existed in a permanent twilight that made the power pool at his feet—and pushed.

The world folded.

He opened his eyes beside the reflecting pool, water shimmering under moonlight, and felt the satisfaction of a clean transit. No disorientation, no stagger. He could have walked there faster, but that wasn't the point. The point was precision, and he'd achieved it.

He reached for the shadows again, visualized the center stone in the Arena, and pulled.

The world unfolded.

He stood in the exact center of the silver-obsidian pattern, precisely where he'd intended. Scattered applause rippled through the upper tiers, and he saw Lexi on the bench above, her arms crossed but her expression satisfied.

The other candidates took their turns. Some were faster, some slower. One woman missed her return mark by three feet, earning a frown from the elders. But it was Mira who drew the collective breath.

She didn't just walk. She danced through the shadows, vanishing and reappearing at her target location in the span of a heartbeat, then returning before the echo of her departure had faded. She landed in perfect stillness, not even the tremor of imbalance, and bowed to the elders with fluid grace.

"Flawless," murmured someone behind Drevik. "She's going to be hard to beat."

The second trial tested veil sight—specifically, the ability to perceive Shadowlands creatures that had been bound into the Arena for the competition. Drevik could feel them before he saw them, pressure against his awareness like weights on his skin.

"Identify and locate," Elder Corvus instructed. "We have released five entities into the Arena. You have ten minutes."

Drevik closed his eyes, extending his awareness outward. The first entity was easy—a wisp, barely corporeal, hovering near the eastern entrance. The second was more substantial, a lurker pressed against the northern wall. The third and fourth required more concentration, hidden in the shadows of the upper tiers.

But the fifth—

The fifth was right behind him.

He spun, catching a glimpse of something that defied description—darkness given form, hunger given shape—and then it was gone, dissolved into nothing.

"Five down," he announced. "Four visible, one... mobile."

Elder Corvus nodded, making a note. "Locations?"

Drevik rattled them off, including the moving pattern of the fifth entity. Other candidates made their reports, some missing entities entirely, others confusing shadows for threats. When Mira's turn came, she'd found all five—but she'd also identified a sixth that the elders had hidden as a test of overconfidence.

"Very good," Elder Corvus said, and Drevik watched Mira's smile sharpen with victory.

He was falling behind. He could feel it.

The third trial was combat.

Not lethal—the Order didn't waste its acolytes on death matches—but brutal enough that the infirmary always saw increased traffic after competition days. Drevik was matched against a man named Keston, broad-shouldered and confident, who'd made it clear in the training halls that he considered Drevik an upstart who didn't belong.

"Finally," Keston said, rolling his shoulders as they faced each other. "Thought I'd have to wait until the mission to put you in your place."

"Focus on the match," Drevik replied. "Not your grudge."

"Grudge?" Keston's laugh was short and sharp. "I don't have a grudge. I just don't like pretenders."

The signal came. Keston moved fast, shadow-walking to close the distance in an instant, his fist already swinging toward Drevik's jaw. But Drevik had learned from Lexi—had learned to anticipate the feel of shadow-walking, the subtle displacement of air—and he was already moving, sliding through his own shadow to appear behind Keston.

His strike caught the other man in the kidney. Keston staggered, turned, swung again. This time Drevik blocked, the impact shuddering up his arm, and used the contact to shadow-walk them both to the other side of the Arena.

Disorientation hit Keston harder. His swing went wide, and Drevik's counter connected with his temple. The fight ended three moves later, Drevik's arm locked across Keston's throat, the other man's pride the only casualty.

"Winner: Drevik Markinsoul."

He released Keston and stepped back, ignoring the glare directed at him. His hands were shaking slightly—the shadow-walk had taken more out of him than he'd expected—but he'd won. That was what mattered.

Lexi caught his eye from the benches and gave him a single nod. Pride, he realized. That was pride in her expression.

Mira's fight came next, and Drevik stayed to watch, needing to understand her style before they inevitably faced each other. Her opponent was a woman named Thessa, quick and vicious, who'd already eliminated two other candidates in earlier matches.

Mira didn't shadow-walk. She didn't need to.

She moved like water, flowing around Thessa's attacks, redirecting force rather than meeting it head-on. Her counter-strikes were precise and economical, never wasting movement, always finding the gaps in her opponent's defense. When Thessa tried to use shadow-walking to gain advantage, Mira anticipated every transit, countering before her opponent had fully materialized.

It was beautiful. And terrifying.

The match ended with Thessa on the ground, Mira's foot on her chest, her dark eyes bright with the thrill of combat.

"Winner: Mira Vance."

The final round paired the top two candidates against each other.

Drevik stood across from Mira in the center of the Arena, the silver-obsidian pattern cool beneath his feet. Around them, the gathered Order watched in silence, the weight of their attention pressing down like a physical force.

"No hard feelings," Mira said, her voice pitched for his ears alone. "Whatever happens."

"None taken."

"Good." Her smile flickered, there and gone. "Because I'm going to enjoy this."

The signal came.

Mira moved first, shadow-walking in a pattern that Drevik could barely track—here, then there, then somewhere else entirely, her attacks coming from angles that shouldn't have been possible. He blocked what he could, dodged what he couldn't, and felt each impact in his bones.

She was fast. Faster than anyone he'd faced. And she was holding back—he could feel it in the precision of her strikes, the way she pulled each hit before it could do real damage.

"Not bad," she breathed, appearing beside him, her lips almost touching his ear. "You're still standing. Most don't, by now."

He twisted away, but she was already gone, reappearing behind him. Her kick caught him in the ribs, and he felt something crack. The pain was distant, though, his focus narrowing to the pattern of her movements.

There was a rhythm to it. A pattern. She shadow-walked on a specific timing—three rapid transits, then a pause to reorient. Three, then pause. Three, then—

On her next series, he didn't try to follow. He waited for the pause, anticipated where she'd appear, and shadow-walked to that exact spot.

His fist connected with her jaw.

The shock on her face was worth every bruise she'd given him. She staggered back, hand rising to her face, and Drevik pressed his advantage. He didn't give her time to recover, didn't let her find her rhythm again. He shadow-walked and struck, shadow-walked and struck, driving her back across the Arena floor.

But Mira Vance hadn't reached the top of her cohort by being easy to beat.

She adapted. Her rhythm changed, the timing shifting, and suddenly he was the one on the defensive, blocking attacks that came faster than he could process. Her knee found his stomach. Her elbow caught his temple. Stars exploded across his vision, and he tasted blood.

They circled each other, both breathing hard. Drevik could feel his shadow-walking ability fraying at the edges—the rapid transits were draining his reserves, and he didn't have much left.

"Yield," Mira said, "and I'll make it worth your while."

"What?"

"The mission." She wiped blood from her lip, her dark eyes never leaving his. "Yield now, and I'll owe you a favor. That's not nothing, in the Order."

"I don't want favors." He straightened, ignoring the protest from his ribs. "I want my place."

Her smile returned, sharp and dangerous. "Then come take it."

She came at him in a rush, no more shadow-walking, just pure physical assault. He met her, blocked and countered, felt her strength matching his. They grappled, hands locked, struggling for leverage—and then she did something he hadn't expected.

She kissed him.

It was brief, hard, more bite than softness, and it shocked him enough that his grip loosened. She used the moment to sweep his feet, sending him crashing to the ground, and then she was above him, her weight pinning him down, her hands on his wrists.

"Yield," she said again, breathless.

He could have fought. Could have shadow-walked them both, tried to reverse their positions. But she had him pinned properly, and his reserves were nearly empty, and—truth be told—the kiss had scattered his thoughts more than he wanted to admit.

"Yield," he said, the word bitter in his mouth.

"Winner: Mira Vance."

She held him there for a moment longer than necessary, her dark eyes searching his face for something. Then she was up, offering him a hand, her expression already shifting back to neutral.

"Good match," she said. "You almost had me, at the end."

"Almost isn't enough."

"No." She pulled him to his feet. "It never is."

The rankings were posted an hour later, after the elders had tallied scores from all three trials. Drevik stood with the other candidates, his ribs aching despite the healing draught the infirmary had provided, and waited for the verdict.

First place: Mira Vance.

Second place: Drevik Markinsoul.

Third: Keston Aldric.

Fourth: Thessa Corwin.

He'd lost. He'd known he would, after that final match, but seeing it in writing still stung. Second place wasn't enough—not when the mission was what he needed, not when the answers about his parents were locked in the Shadowlands.

Elder Corvus stood, and the gathered Order fell silent.

"The trials have concluded," he announced. "Results are final. However, given the nature of the upcoming mission, the council has determined that standard selection protocols will be... adjusted."

Murmurs rippled through the benches. Drevik's head came up, hope flickering.

"The Shadowlands mission requires a team of four," Elder Corvus continued. "Two acolytes, one seeker, and one warden. The acolyte positions will be filled by our top two candidates. Mira Vance and Drevik Markinsoul will both participate."

Drevik's heart stuttered. Both. He was going.

"The seeker position will be filled by Lexi Thornwood, whose combat and reconnaissance records make her the optimal choice."

Lexi. He turned, found her in the crowd, saw her expression shift from surprise to satisfaction. She was coming too. Whatever waited in the Shadowlands, he wouldn't face it alone.

"The warden position will be determined by the council within the week. The mission departs on the new moon. Candidates, prepare yourselves."

The gathering began to disperse, but Drevik barely noticed. He was going. He'd failed to win, and he was still going. The relief was so profound it made his knees weak.

"Drevik."

He turned. Mira stood behind him, arms crossed, her expression unreadable.

"Second place," she said. "Not bad for an outsider."

"Not bad for someone who kissed me to win."

Her lips curved. "Would you have preferred I didn't?"

"I would have preferred to win."

"Then next time, don't let a kiss distract you." She stepped closer, her voice dropping. "Or do. I certainly won't mind."

She walked away before he could respond, her stride confident, her victory complete. He watched her go, uncertain whether he'd just been threatened or flirted with—or both.

Lexi found him in the corridor outside the Arena, leaning against the stone wall, trying to convince his ribs that they didn't hurt as much as they thought.

"You're an idiot," she said, without preamble.

"For losing? I noticed."

"For letting her get in your head." She stopped in front of him, her green eyes sharp. "She kissed you, and you froze. I saw it."

"I didn't freeze. I—"

"You froze." She stepped closer, close enough that he could smell the herbs she used in her hair, the faint trace of smoke from the Arena torches. "Next time, when someone tries to distract you with... whatever that was... you ignore it. You focus. You win."

"There won't be a next time. We're both going."

"There's always a next time, Drevik." Her hand came up, hovering near his face, and he saw uncertainty flicker in her eyes. "In the Shadowlands, in the Order, in everything. You can't let people get that close unless you're willing to—"

She stopped. Her hand dropped.

"Unless I'm willing to what?"

"Nothing." She stepped back, her expression hardening into something professional. "Get your ribs looked at. Properly. And start preparing for the mission. The new moon is in two weeks, and I'm not letting you die because you were too stubborn to train properly."

She turned and walked away, and Drevik was left with the ghost of her touch, the echo of her unfinished sentence, and the growing certainty that the mission ahead was going to be more complicated than any of them expected.

He found Ivy in the upper gardens, sitting on a bench that overlooked the Arena entrance. Her silver hair was loose around her shoulders, her dark eyes distant, and she didn't look up when he sat beside her.

"Second place," she said quietly.

"First loser."

"That's not fair. Mira's been training since she could walk. You've been here months." Her hand found his, cool fingers intertwining with his. "You did well."

"I lost."

"You placed. There's a difference." She squeezed his hand. "You're going to the Shadowlands. You're going to find whatever answers you're looking for. And when you come back..."

"When I come back?"

She turned to look at him, and something in her expression shifted—softened, became vulnerable in a way he'd never seen from her.

"When you come back, I'll be here. Waiting." Her thumb traced circles on the back of his hand. "That's all. I just... wanted you to know that."

He didn't know what to say. The words caught in his throat, tangled with feelings he hadn't had time to examine, with fears about the mission and hopes about what he might find and the strange, impossible situation of having multiple women look at him the way Ivy was looking at him now.

"Ivy..."

"You don't have to say anything." She released his hand, rising from the bench. "Just come back. That's all I ask."

She walked away into the darkness, leaving him alone with his thoughts, his aching ribs, and the growing certainty that whatever waited in the Shadowlands, the hardest challenges were still ahead of him.

That night, Drevik dreamed of the Shadowlands.

He stood on a precipice, looking out over a landscape of eternal twilight. Shadows moved in the distance, shapes that defied comprehension, and a voice whispered from somewhere behind him—

You don't belong here.

He turned, but there was no one. Only darkness, and the sense that something was watching. Waiting.

But you will.

He woke with a gasp, his ribs protesting, his skin damp with sweat. The new moon was two weeks away. The mission was coming. And somewhere in the depths of his mind, the shadows were already beginning to stir.
