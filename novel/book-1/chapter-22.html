Chapter 22: New Beginnings

The new quarters were better than he'd expected. Much better than the narrow cot in the initiate dormitory where the ceiling had leaked every time it rained. Drevik stood in the center of his private room—his room, a concept that still felt foreign—taking in the stone walls hung with deep purple tapestries, the narrow window that looked out onto the campus above, the bed that was actually large enough for two.

He didn't let himself think about that last part. Not yet.

A knock at the door broke through his reverie.

"It's open," he called, turning as Lexi slipped inside with the silent grace that came from years of training. Her auburn hair was loose around her shoulders today, a departure from the severe braid she'd worn during their trials. She looked softer somehow. Less like someone preparing for battle.

"Stop brooding," she said, though her smile took any sting from the words. "You've been an official Acolyte for three days. That's barely enough time to brood at all, let alone properly."

"I'm not brooding. I'm... adjusting."

She crossed the room in three quick strides and pressed her palm flat against his chest, right over the new tattoo that marked his collarbone—the stylized crescent moon and veil that declared his rank. Her touch warmed his skin through the fabric of his shirt.

"You're allowed to be happy, you know." Her voice dropped lower. "You earned this."

"I know." He covered her hand with his own. "It just doesn't feel real yet."

"What part? The room? The rank?" Her green eyes searched his face. "Or us?"

"All of it." He exhaled slowly. "A month ago, I was just a college student worrying about finals and student loans. Now I'm part of a secret order that guards reality from shadow monsters, I have magic I barely understand, and you—"

He stopped, uncertain how to finish.

Lexi arched an eyebrow. "Me what?"

"You're here." He said it simply, because the alternative was saying something far more vulnerable. "That's the part that feels least real of all."

For a long moment, she just looked at him. Then she laughed—a real laugh, warm and surprised, nothing like the sharp sound she'd used as armor when they'd first met.

"You're an idiot," she said affectionately. "A romantic idiot, but still."

She kissed him before he could respond. Not the desperate, urgent kisses they'd shared during the chaos of the trials—this was slower, deeper, a promise rather than a claim. Her fingers curled into the fabric of his shirt as his hands found the curve of her waist, pulling her closer.

When they finally broke apart, both slightly breathless, she rested her forehead against his.

"The Order recognizes us as partners now," she murmured. "Bound. Officially entangled in the records."

"I noticed." He smiled against her hair. "Your mother's face when she signed off on it was... memorable."

"She'll come around." Lexi's tone suggested she didn't particularly care either way. "What matters is that no one can separate us now. Not even an Elder."

The weight of that settled over him. In the Order's hierarchy, a recognized partnership meant something beyond mere romance—it was a magical bond, a declaration that their fates were intertwined. If one fell, the other would feel it. If one rose, they would rise together.

"Are you scared?" he asked quietly.

"Of what?"

"Of being tied to me. Of what that means for your future in the Order."

She pulled back just enough to meet his eyes, her expression fierce. "I chose you, Drevik Markinsoul. Not because some prophecy or vision told me to, but because you're worth choosing. My future is whatever we make it together."

He wanted to kiss her again, wanted to lose himself in the certainty she offered, but a knock at the door interrupted them.

Lexi sighed. "I told Ivy to give us more time."

But when she opened the door, it wasn't Ivy on the other side.

Seraphina Vane stood in the hallway, her silver-blonde hair gleaming in the lamplight, her expression unreadable as ever. She wore the dark robes of a Seeker—a rank above Acolyte—and the silver pendant at her throat marked her as one of the Order's情报 gatherers.

"Am I interrupting?" Her voice was smooth, giving nothing away.

"Seraphina." Drevik stepped forward, suddenly conscious of how close he and Lexi had been standing. "No, we were just—"

"I came to congratulate you." Her dark eyes met his, and for just a moment, he thought he saw something vulnerable beneath her careful composure. "On your promotion. And your... partnership."

Lexi's hand found his, her fingers interlacing with his own. "Thank you, Seraphina. That means a great deal."

The two women regarded each other with the careful assessment of those who understood exactly what lay between them. Whatever Seraphina felt—and Drevik still wasn't entirely certain what that was—she kept it hidden behind her customary mask.

"The Elders have requested your presence this evening," Seraphina said, her attention shifting fully to Drevik. "There's a ceremony to formally recognize your new rank. Your grandmother has been invited to attend."

The warmth of the moment evaporated, replaced by a cold knot in his stomach.

"My grandmother?"

"She thinks you've taken an internship with a prestigious research firm." Seraphina's tone was carefully neutral. "The Order maintains several such cover stories for our members with outside families."

"I know, I just—" He ran a hand through his hair. "I haven't seen her since all this started. She probably thinks I've lost my mind, dropping out of my normal classes, disappearing for weeks at a time."

"Then this is an opportunity to reassure her." Seraphina's hand brushed his arm briefly—so quickly he might have imagined it. "You're still her grandson, Drevik. That hasn't changed."

But everything else had, and they all knew it.

The ceremony was brief, held in the same chamber where Drevik had first sworn his oath as an initiate. Elder Thornwood—Lexi's mother—presided, her sharp features inscrutable as she formally acknowledged his advancement. The other Elders watched from their seats along the curved wall, their faces shadowed by their ceremonial hoods.

Drevik barely noticed them. His attention was fixed on the small woman in the back of the room, her gray hair pulled back in its familiar practical bun, her weathered hands clutching her purse like an anchor.

His grandmother looked older than he remembered. More fragile. The realization hit him like a physical blow.

After the formal words were spoken, after the Elders had filed out and Lexi had given him a reassuring squeeze of the hand before slipping away, he finally approached her.

"Gram."

"Drevik." Her voice wavered. "Look at you. All grown up and working for a... a research firm."

The lie tasted like ash in his mouth. "It's a good opportunity."

"I'm sure it is." She reached up to cup his face, her palm warm and papery against his cheek. "You look tired. Are they working you too hard?"

"I'm fine." He covered her hand with his own. "Really. This is just what I needed."

She studied him with the knowing eyes that had seen through every excuse he'd ever made as a child. "You've changed, Drevik. I can see it. Something's different about you."

The mark on his collarbone seemed to burn beneath his shirt. "I've grown up a bit."

"No." She shook her head slowly. "It's more than that. You have a... a weight about you now. Like you're carrying something important."

He couldn't lie to her. Not directly. But he couldn't tell her the truth either. The Order's secrecy was enforced by binding oaths that would literally prevent him from speaking about it to anyone outside their ranks.

"I found my place, Gram." It was as close to honesty as he could get. "For the first time since Mom and Dad... since I lost them... I feel like I belong somewhere."

Her eyes glistened. "Your parents would be proud of you. Whatever this is, whatever you're doing with your life—they'd be proud."

The words landed like a knife between his ribs. He pulled her into a hug, hiding his face against her shoulder so she wouldn't see the emotion that threatened to break through.

"I miss them," he admitted.

"I know, sweetheart." Her arms wrapped around him, strong despite her age. "I miss them too. Every day."

They held each other for a long moment, and Drevik felt the strange dissonance of his two lives—the ordinary world where he was just a college student who'd lost his parents too young, and the extraordinary one where he commanded shadow magic and fought creatures from another dimension.

When she finally pulled back, she was smiling. "Enough of this. You're clearly important now, with your fancy ceremony and your serious colleagues. I saw that girl watching you—the one with the auburn hair. She looked at you like you hung the moon."

Drevik couldn't help but smile. "Lexi. She's... she's important to me."

"I can tell." His grandmother patted his cheek. "She seems fierce. You need someone fierce, Drevik. Someone who'll fight for you when you forget to fight for yourself."

He thought of Lexi facing down her own mother to stand at his side. Of the way she'd thrown herself between him and the Shadow beast during the trials without hesitation.

"She does," he said softly. "She fights for me."

"Good." His grandmother nodded firmly. "Then I approve. Now, when do I get to properly meet her? Not just a wave across a room, but actual conversation."

"Soon," he promised. "Once things settle down."

She gave him a knowing look. "Things never settle down, Drevik. Not when you're young and in love and building a life. You make time for what matters, or you watch it slip away."

The words echoed in his mind long after she'd left, long after the chamber had emptied and he stood alone in the flickering torchlight.

He found Lexi in the training yard behind the compound, going through a series of forms with a blade that gleamed silver in the afternoon light. She moved like water—each motion flowing into the next with practiced grace.

He watched for a moment, appreciating the play of muscle beneath her fitted training clothes, before stepping into the yard.

She paused, lowering her blade. "How did it go with your grandmother?"

"She knows I'm hiding something." He moved to stand beside her, leaning against the weapons rack. "She's too sharp not to notice. But she accepted the cover story, I think."

"Most families do." Lexi wiped sweat from her brow with the back of her hand. "They want to believe their loved ones are safe, doing normal things. It's easier than the truth."

"Does yours? Believe it, I mean?"

Lexi's expression flickered. "My mother is an Elder. There's no pretending in my family. But my father left the Order when I was young. He wanted a normal life." She paused, something complicated moving behind her eyes. "I haven't seen him in years. He couldn't accept what I would become."

"I'm sorry."

"Don't be." Her voice was firm. "I made my choice. This is my family now." She stepped closer, her blade vanishing into the shadows from which all Order weapons came. "You're my family now."

He reached for her, his hand settling on her hip. "I spoke to Ivy earlier. She's been reassigned to the archives—official Acolyte duties."

"Good. She deserves a safe posting after everything." Lexi's hand came up to rest on his chest. "And Seraphina?"

"Remains a mystery, as always." He exhaled. "She wished me well. But there's something she's not saying. Something she wants to tell me but can't."

"Seraphina keeps more secrets than the entire Shadowlands." Lexi's tone was wry. "But she's been loyal to you. Whatever her reasons, she's stood with us when it mattered."

"I know."

They stood in comfortable silence for a moment, the training yard empty around them, the sounds of the campus above filtering down muffled and distant.

"This is just the beginning, isn't it?" Drevik finally said.

Lexi's eyes met his. "For us? Or the danger?"

"Both."

She nodded slowly. "Yes. The trials were just the first test. There will be more—more threats, more challenges. The Order is facing something it hasn't faced in centuries. And you—" She hesitated.

"Me what?"

"You're going to be at the center of it. I've seen how the Elders look at you. How the Veiled Ones watch you in visions. You're not just a new Acolyte, Drevik. You're something more."

The weight of her words settled over him, heavy and certain. He'd known, on some level, since the moment he'd first channeled shadow magic with an ease that shouldn't have been possible. He was different. Special in a way that frightened even the Order's leadership.

"Then I'm glad you're with me." He pulled her closer. "I can't do this alone."

"You won't have to." Her lips brushed his jaw. "I told you. My future is whatever we build together."

He kissed her properly then, in the empty training yard with the afternoon sun warm on their skin. She melted against him, her fingers threading through his hair, her body fitting against his like she'd been made for this exact moment.

When they finally broke apart, both breathing harder, she smiled against his mouth.

"We should continue this somewhere more private."

"My new quarters have an actual door that locks."

"Lead the way, Acolyte Markinsoul."

They made love slowly, taking their time now that urgency wasn't driving them. Drevik memorized every inch of her—the constellation of freckles across her shoulders, the scar just below her left breast from a training accident years ago, the way her breath caught when he kissed the hollow of her throat.

Afterwards, she lay curled against him, her head on his chest, their legs tangled together beneath the sheets. The lamplight cast shadows across the ceiling, familiar patterns that seemed almost to move if he watched them too long.

"Ivy mentioned there's a gathering tomorrow," he said quietly. "All the new Acolytes, being introduced to the broader Order."

"Tradition." Lexi's voice was drowsy, satisfied. "You'll meet people from other compounds. Start building the connections you'll need as you rise."

"Connections." He smiled. "Is that what we call politics in the Order?"

"We call it survival." She propped herself up on one elbow, looking down at him. "The Order isn't unified, Drevik. There are factions, different visions for our future. Some see the Veiled Ones as guides to be followed. Others see them as obstacles to be overcome. You'll need to navigate that."

"And which faction are you in?"

Her eyes gleamed. "I'm in the faction that protects what I love. Whatever that requires."

He pulled her down for another kiss, softer this time. "I don't deserve you."

"No," she agreed. "But you have me anyway."

They drifted off eventually, her warmth a comforting weight against his side. But Drevik's sleep was far from peaceful.

The dream began in twilight.

He stood at the edge of a vast expanse of shadow—not the familiar shadows of the mortal realm, but something deeper, darker. The Shadowlands stretched before him in endless shades of gray and purple, twisted trees rising from ground that seemed to breathe, a sky that held no sun, only the pale glow of a crescent moon.

He'd been here before, in visions during his trials. But this was different. More real.

Something moved in the darkness ahead. Something that smiled with too many teeth.

"Drevik Markinsoul." The voice came from everywhere and nowhere, a whisper that echoed through his bones. "We've been waiting for you."

He tried to move, to step back, but his feet were rooted to the ground. The shadows around him began to shift, forming shapes that might have been human once—or had never been human at all.

"You carry our mark," the voice continued. "You wear our gift. But you don't know what you are, do you? What you were born to become."

"I'm an Acolyte of the Order." His voice came out stronger than he felt. "I serve the Veil."

A laugh, cold and ancient. "The Order. Children playing with power they don't understand. They told you your parents died, didn't they? Told you they were gone forever."

The shadows rippled, and a figure emerged—a woman wrapped in darkness, her features obscured. But as she stepped closer, the shadows fell away like a veil being lifted.

Drevik's heart stopped.

He knew that face. Had memorized it from the few photographs his grandmother had kept, from dreams he'd thought were only dreams.

"Mom?"

Her smile was sad, her eyes holding depths of sorrow and something else—something that might have been hope.

"They lied to you, my son." Her voice was the same voice he remembered from childhood lullabies, from bedtime stories, from a thousand small moments he'd thought he'd forgotten. "I'm still alive. And I need you to find me."

"Where are you? What happened? Dad—"

"The Shadowlands hold many secrets." She reached for him, but her fingers passed through his like smoke. "Not yet, Drevik. You're not ready. But you will be. Grow stronger. Learn what the Order won't teach you. And when the time comes—"

The dream began to fragment, twilight dissolving into darkness.

"Wait!" He reached for her. "How do I find you?"

Her voice echoed as she faded: "Follow the mark. Trust the shadows. And remember—they're not the only ones watching you."

Drevik jolted awake, his heart pounding, his body drenched in sweat. Beside him, Lexi stirred but didn't wake.

The mark on his collarbone burned like fire.

He touched it with trembling fingers, feeling the raised skin of the tattoo, remembering his mother's face, her words echoing in his mind.

I'm still alive.

The Order had told him his parents died in a car accident. Had shown him the records, the grave site, the evidence of their deaths. But the Shadowlands held many secrets, and his mother had appeared to him in a dream that felt more real than any vision he'd experienced.

They had lied to him. About everything.

And somewhere, in a dimension of eternal twilight, his mother was waiting for him to find her.

Drevik lay back down slowly, his mind racing, his heart still pounding. Lexi murmured something in her sleep and curled closer to him, her arm draping across his chest.

He held her tighter than he meant to, needing the anchor of her warmth, the proof that he wasn't alone.

Tomorrow, he would begin his new life as an Acolyte. He would play the Order's games, learn their secrets, rise through their ranks. But now he had a new goal, one that burned brighter than ambition or duty.

He would find the truth about his parents. He would discover what the Order was hiding. And he would rescue his mother from whatever prison the Shadowlands had become.

The mark on his chest pulsed once, like a heartbeat, and Drevik smiled in the darkness.

This is just the beginning.

For all of us.
