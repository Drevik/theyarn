Best Video Game Deals August 2026: Price-Per-Performance Picks That Actually Matter

Published: August 2026 | Product Count: 7 Games | By Marcus Chen

I've been buying games since cartridges had actual gold pins you could see. Back then, a $50 game gave you 40 hours of content and that was that. Now? A $70 game ships with a day-one patch the size of the install itself and half the mechanics locked behind a season pass. The math is broken.

But here's the thing: the deals actually worth paying attention to aren't the ones screaming at you from a banner ad. They're the games that dropped to prices where the hours-per-dollar makes sense, the titles that were already good and are now priced like they should've been at launch. I've sifted through the current Amazon game deals and cut every piece of filler. These seven games are the real value picks for August 2026. No $70 day-one editions padded with cosmetics. No "deals" that are still overpriced. Just games worth your money at these prices.

How I Picked These Deals

Every game here meets three criteria. First, the discount has to be real. I checked price histories, and none of these are fake markdowns from inflated MSRPs. Second, the game has to be genuinely good, not "decent for the price" or "acceptable if you're bored." Third, I note what's wrong with each pick, because no deal is perfect and lying about that doesn't help anyone.

I also cross-referenced against other retailers. Where Best Buy or Walmart beats the Amazon price, I say so. Where Amazon is the clear winner, you'll know that too.

🏆 Best Overall: Elden Ring Nightreign (PS5) at $15 (was $54.99)

This is the deal that made me sit up. FromSoftware's standalone co-op adventure launched at $54.99 and has been sliding down all year. Right now it's sitting at $15 on Amazon, and that's not a typo. You're getting a full FromSoftware action game for less than a movie ticket.

Nightreign takes the combat foundation of Elden Ring and reworks it into a roguelike-ish three-player co-op structure. You pick a character class (Wylder, Guardian, Duchess, and several more) and run three-day cycles of escalating encounters before a boss rush finale. It's tighter and more focused than the open-world Elden Ring, which some people love and others find restrictive.

  • Price: ~$15 at Amazon (ASIN: B0DVNF1F49), lowest recorded price
  • Gameplay: 3-player co-op action with solo option; runs cycle in ~40 minutes
  • Value math: At $15, you need maybe 3 good runs to beat the price of a coffee per hour. That's absurd value.
  • The downside: Solo play is viable but noticeably harder. The game was designed around co-op, and playing alone means enemy health doesn't scale down enough in some encounters. If you don't have friends who game or aren't comfortable with randos, you'll feel the friction. Also, the story is paper thin. Don't come here for narrative.

Metal Gear Solid Delta: Snake Eater (PS5) at $25 (was $69.99)

Konami's remake of what many consider the best stealth game ever made. The original MGS3 is a masterclass in survival mechanics, boss fights, and cold war espionage. Delta keeps the story, the voice acting, and the jungle survival systems intact while rebuilding the graphics and modernizing the controls. It's the right kind of remake, one that respects the source material instead of replacing it.

At $25, you're paying roughly what a PS2 Classic should cost in 2026, but getting a full Unreal Engine 5 rebuild. That's the right price point for a remake, even if some purists will always prefer the original's camera angles.

  • Price: ~$25 at Amazon (ASIN: B0D6PD4795), matches Black Friday low
  • Gameplay: Stealth-action with survival mechanics; 15-20 hour campaign, plus extras
  • What's new: Over-the-shoulder camera (finally), modernized CQC controls, the Snake vs. Monkey mode returns for PS5
  • The downside: The new control scheme is a huge improvement, but some environmental sequences feel awkward with the modern camera. The jungle areas are dense and occasionally frustrating to navigate even with the updated view. Also, no Metal Gear Online equivalent. This is purely the single-player campaign.

Final Fantasy VII Rebirth (PS5) at $19.99 (was $49.99)

Square Enix's second chapter of the FF7 Remake project dropped to $19.99 recently and it's the lowest Amazon has ever listed it. Rebirth takes the combat system from Remake and expands it with open-world zones, a revamped skill tree, and the kind of side content density that will eat your weekends for a month straight.

If you played Remake, this is a no-brainer at $20. If you haven't, grab Remake Intergrade first (also on sale around $19.99) and play them in order. The story doesn't make a lot of sense jumping in at chapter two.

  • Price: ~$19.99 at Amazon (ASIN: B0CGXZQ4CD), all-time low per CamelCamelCamel
  • Gameplay: 60-80+ hours with side content; hybrid action/ATB combat
  • Standout feature: The open-world zones are surprisingly well-paced. Each region feels distinct and the mini-games (queen's blood, chocobo racing, vr combat) are genuinely fun instead of filler
  • The downside: The story goes to some wild places, and not all of them land. Some plot threads are clearly sequel hooks that go nowhere. Performance can chug in certain open-world areas on base PS5. Not unplayable, but noticeable. Also, the camera during indoor combat still gets stuck on walls.

Silent Hill 2 (PS5) at $19.99 (was $39.99)

Bloober Team pulled off something I didn't think was possible: a faithful remake of the 2001 psychological horror landmark that doesn't undermine what made the original special. The combat is tighter (maybe too tight for some purists), the environments are claustrophobic in the best way, and the sound design deserves an award. The PS5 DualSense haptics during otherworld sequences alone make this worth playing on console over PC.

At $20, this is half the launch price for one of the best horror games of the decade. If you've been waiting, stop waiting.

  • Price: ~$19.99 at Amazon (ASIN: B0CMC3C3Z9), 50% off launch price
  • Gameplay: 10-14 hour campaign, 20+ with all endings and secrets
  • What works: The atmosphere is suffocating in the best way. Combat feels weighty and desperate rather than empowering. The puzzle solutions are mostly unchanged from the original, which is the right call.
  • The downside: This is a remake that plays it extremely safe. If you played the 2001 version to death, some sections will feel too familiar. The new voice acting is good but different; Maria's characterization shifts in ways that are debatable. And the combat, while improved, can still feel clunky in tight corridors when the camera fights you.

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (PS5) at $39.99 (was $49.99)

This one snuck up on a lot of people. A turn-based RPG from a smaller studio (Sandfall Interactive) that delivers the kind of art direction and combat depth usually reserved for Square Enix's bigger budgets. The premise (a Paintress who erases people of a certain age each year) is dark and creative. The real-time dodge/parry layer on top of turn-based combat makes every fight feel active instead of menu-driven.

$40 is only a $10 discount, but for a 2025 game that's still climbing bestseller lists, that's solid. This is the kind of title that holds at $50 for months before it drops.

  • Price: ~$39.99 at Amazon (ASIN: B0DK6225DY), first meaningful discount
  • Gameplay: 30-40 hour RPG with real-time defensive mechanics layered on turn-based combat
  • Standout feature: The dodge/parry system in combat makes boss fights feel like rhythm games crossed with JRPG strategy. It's genuinely fresh.
  • The downside: The story starts strong but loses coherence in the final act. Some side quests are textbook RPG filler: go here, collect five widgets, return. The difficulty spikes are real, too. You will hit walls where grinding is the only way forward. Not a dealbreaker at $40, but know what you're getting into.

Metroid Prime 4: Beyond (Switch 2) at $44.99 (was $69.99)

Eighteen years after Metroid Prime 3. Eighteen. Retro Studios finally delivered, and it's good — really good. The Switch 2 edition runs at 4K/60fps in Quality Mode or 1080p/120fps in Performance Mode, and the Joy-Con 2 mouse controls for aiming feel like the future of console FPS input. If you own a Switch 2, this is the system seller.

$45 is a 36% discount on a first-party Nintendo title that's only eight months old. That almost never happens. Nintendo games hold price like gold bullion. Grab this while it's below $50.

  • Price: ~$44.99 at Amazon (ASIN: B0FR9G4N9S), rare first-party Nintendo discount
  • Gameplay: 15-20 hour campaign; first-person exploration with psychic abilities and motorcycle traversal
  • What works: The Joy-Con 2 mouse aiming is transformative. Scanning environments, aiming beams, navigating: it all feels natural in a way stick aiming never did. Planet Viewros is the best-designed Metroid environment since Tallon IV.
  • The downside: The original Switch version is $29.99, so if you don't have a Switch 2, that's the smarter buy. The story is more linear than Prime fans will want. It's Metroid Fusion meets open-world, and the open-world elements feel undercooked. Some NPCs talk too much for a series that built its reputation on isolation and silence. Also, the $44.99 price is for the physical cart; the digital version may differ.

Dragon Quest III HD-2D Remake (PS5) at $19.99 (was $49.99)

Here's the thing about Dragon Quest III: it's one of the most influential RPGs ever made, and it holds up. The HD-2D visual style (think Octopath Traveler but with the charm of 8-bit roots) makes the world feel both nostalgic and modern. The class system, where you build a party of custom characters, is still one of the most satisfying in the genre.

At $20, this is the cheapest it's been. If you've never played a Dragon Quest game, III is the one to start with. It's the beginning of the Erdrick Trilogy and doesn't require any prior knowledge.

  • Price: ~$19.99 at Amazon (ASIN: B0D71JQXR4), 60% off, lowest price recorded
  • Gameplay: 30-50 hours depending on class experimentation; classic turn-based with modern QoL updates
  • What works: The class change system is deeper than you remember. Monster arena and new story content make this more than a visual upgrade. The orchestral soundtrack is gorgeous.
  • The downside: This is still a 30-year-old game at its core. The story pacing is slow by modern standards, random encounters can wear thin, and some quality-of-life improvements don't go far enough. You still can't speed up animations in the original release without mods. Also, the PS5 version has minor texture pop-in that the Switch version somehow avoids.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these game prices current or will they change?

Prices were verified in late August 2026. Amazon game prices shift constantly, sometimes multiple times per day. The deals listed here were live when I checked, but a $15 Elden Ring Nightreign could easily jump back to $40 tomorrow. If you see a price you like, grab it.

Should I buy physical or digital copies of these games?

Physical copies let you resell or lend them, which is the more cost-efficient move long term. Digital is convenient but locks you in. For these sale prices, physical often wins because the discount is deeper and you keep resale value.

Are Switch 2 games worth the premium over original Switch versions?

If you own a Switch 2, yes. The enhanced resolution, faster load times, and Joy-Con 2 mouse controls in games like Metroid Prime 4 justify the upgrade. But if you only have an original Switch, the standard edition at $29.99 is the better deal.

What's the single best deal on this list?

Elden Ring Nightreign at $15 (down from $54.99) offers the highest hours-per-dollar of anything here. It's a full co-op action game from FromSoftware at the price of a lunch. That's the one to grab first if you own a PS5.

Do these games go on sale often?

First-party Nintendo titles like Metroid Prime 4 rarely drop below $45. Third-party games cycle through sales every few months, but the current discounts on Nightreign and FF7 Rebirth are near or at all-time lows. Waiting rarely saves you much more.

Is Elden Ring Nightreign worth playing solo or only in co-op?

You can play solo, and the game scales enemy difficulty accordingly. But Nightreign was built around three-player co-op, and solo runs feel harder and less forgiving. If you have no plans to team up, know what you're signing up for: it's still fun, just punishing.

The Bottom Line

Elden Ring Nightreign at $15 is the deal to beat this month. It's a FromSoftware game for the price of a fast-casual lunch. If you own a PS5 and like action games, this isn't a question. It's a reflex. Grab it before the price crawls back up.

If horror is more your speed, Silent Hill 2 at $20 is the second-easiest recommendation here. And if you want maximum hours for your dollar, FF7 Rebirth at $20 will eat your entire August and thank you for it.
