Chapter 10: Threshold

Chapter 10: Threshold

The ceremonial chamber smelled of burnt sage and something older—something that reminded Drevik of the first time he'd opened his grandmother's attic trunk and found photographs of people long dead. Memory and loss, preserved in dust.

"Remove your shoes." Seraphina's voice echoed against stone walls that hadn't seen sunlight in centuries. "The Shadowlands require skin contact for the crossing. Grounding."

Drevik toed off his sneakers, the cold floor seeping into his soles. Beside him, Lexi was already barefoot, her auburn hair pulled back in a practical braid that exposed the line of her jaw. She caught him looking and raised an eyebrow.

"Focus, Markinsoul."

"Always." But his pulse was hammering against his throat, and he suspected she knew it.

The chamber had transformed since yesterday's preparation. Where before there'd been a simple circle of salt, now intricate symbols carved the floor—spiraling patterns that seemed to shift when he looked too long. Candles burned at each cardinal point, their flames unnaturally still. And in the center of it all, a tear hung in the air like a wound that refused to heal.

Through it, Drevik glimpsed twilight. Not the gentle purple of evening, but something thicker, more substantial—a perpetual dusk that pressed against the edges of the rift like water behind glass.

Mira shifted on his left, her dark eyes tracking the way the shadows moved independent of any light source. "How long has it been since anyone crossed through this threshold?"

"Three months." Seraphina circled the perimeter, checking each symbol with the methodical attention of someone defusing a bomb. "The pathway needs to be reestablished with each crossing. The Shadowlands don't like visitors."

"Then why does it feel like it's calling?" The words left Drevik's mouth before he could stop them.

Everyone stilled.

Seraphina turned slowly, her silver-white hair catching candlelight. "What did you say?"

"The rift." He gestured toward the hanging tear, uncertain how to explain the sensation. "It feels... hungry. Not hostile, exactly. More like it's been waiting."

Lexi moved closer, her shoulder brushing his. "That's not normal," she murmured, low enough that only he could hear. "Most acolytes feel fear at the threshold. Revulsion. Not recognition."

"I'm not saying I want to go through."

"But you don't not want to." Her green eyes searched his face with an intensity that made his chest tight. "Your darkness is responding to it. I can see it in your shadow."

He looked down. His shadow lay stretched across the floor as expected, but the edges rippled. Reached toward the rift like fingers straining for a lover's touch.

"Shit." He took an involuntary step backward, but his shadow held its ground, anchored to something he couldn't see.

"Don't fight it." Seraphina had stopped her circling, her full attention now on Drevik. "Your shadow is trying to cross ahead of you. Let it. The Shadowlands will be... disorienting. Having part of yourself already anchored there will help."

"That doesn't sound concerning at all," Mira muttered, but her hand found Drevik's wrist, grip tight. "What happens if his shadow gets there first and decides it likes the neighborhood?"

"Then we retrieve him and burn the shadow away." Seraphina's tone was clinical, matter-of-fact. "It's happened before. The Order has protocols."

"Comforting."

"It's not meant to be comforting, acolyte. It's meant to be true."

The words hung between them, heavy with implication. Drevik watched his shadow stretch further, the dark shape sliding across the floor with the purpose of a living thing. His stomach clenched, but beneath the fear, something else stirred. Curiosity. Recognition. A strange, magnetic pull that made him want to step forward instead of back.

Lexi's hand slid down to lace through his fingers. "I'll go first. If something goes wrong, I can pull you back."

"Or get pulled in with me."

"I'm stronger than I look."

He met her gaze, saw the determination there, the fierce protectiveness that had defined their relationship since he'd stumbled into this world. "I know you are. That's why you're staying put. If I lose myself in there, someone needs to be anchored enough to drag me home."

Her jaw tightened, but she didn't argue. That was new. In the months since they'd met, Lexi had argued every point, challenged every decision, pushed back against every assertion of authority. Now she stood beside him in the face of something that terrified her—he could smell the fear beneath her skin, sharp and bright—and she let him make the call.

It meant something. He wasn't sure what yet, but it meant something.

"Form the circle." Seraphina's voice cut through his thoughts. "Hands joined. The crossing will feel like drowning. Do not let go of each other, or you'll be lost in the space between."

Drevik gripped Lexi's hand on one side, Mira's on the other. Beyond Mira, Seraphina completed the circle, her palm cool and dry against Mira's skin. The four of them stood at the edge of the ritual space, the rift pulsing before them like a heartbeat.

"Close your eyes," Seraphina instructed. "Breathe. When you feel the pull, step forward. Together. One step at a time."

Drevik closed his eyes.

The darkness behind his lids wasn't empty. Shapes moved there—fragments of memory, pieces of dreams, the shadow-self that had been stretching toward the rift. He could feel it now, the part of him that had already begun crossing. A thin tendril of connection stretched between his body and his shadow, pulling taut as his awareness split.

Come home, whispered something in the dark. Not words, exactly. More like intention, like hunger given form. Come home come home come home—

"Drevik." Lexi's voice, sharp and close. "Your nose is bleeding."

He opened his eyes. A drop of crimson had splashed against his bare foot, bright against the stone. He swiped at his face, felt the wet warmth, the metallic tang that coated his lips.

"It's fine." He didn't know if he was lying. "The threshold is pulling harder than expected."

"Then we don't cross." Lexi's grip tightened. "Seraphina, we abort."

"No." The word came from Drevik's mouth, but it didn't feel like his own. "If we don't cross now, the path closes. The alignment won't return for weeks, and the breach we're tracking will have time to spread. We go."

"Your shadow is eating you from the inside out—"

"I said we go." He looked at Lexi, saw the fear and frustration warring in her expression. "Trust me."

A beat of silence. Two.

Then she exhaled, a sharp hiss through her teeth. "If you die in there, I'm going to be so fucking angry."

"Fair."

They stepped forward.

The world inverted.

Drevik had expected darkness—he'd prepared for the absence of light, for the cold press of shadow against his skin. What he wasn't prepared for was the weight. The Shadowlands pressed against him from all sides, dense and heavy and aware, like swimming through water that knew your name.

Sound worked differently here. When he opened his mouth to breathe, the air tasted of copper and old stone. The ground beneath his feet gave slightly with each step, spongy and uncertain, though when he looked down, he saw only darkness. No floor. No sky. Just endless twilight stretching in every direction, broken by shapes that might have been trees or might have been bones.

"Don't let go." Seraphina's voice came from everywhere and nowhere, muffled by the pressing reality. "The landscape will try to separate you. It's not malicious—the Shadowlands simply don't understand permanence the way we do."

Drevik gripped Lexi's hand tighter, felt her fingers dig into his palm in return. Beside her, Mira had gone pale, her usual sharp edges softened by something that might have been wonder or might have been terror.

"The camp." Drevik's voice came out strange, distorted by the thick air. "Where are we going?"

Seraphina pointed. He followed the line of her finger and saw—something. A structure in the distance, angles wrong, geometry that made his eyes water. It seemed to shift even as he watched, reforming into new configurations like a building dreaming of itself.

"That's not where we're heading." Seraphina started walking, pulling them with her. "The Shadowlands reflect intent. What you're seeing is your mind trying to make sense of the landscape. Focus on my footsteps. Nothing else."

Easier said than done.

The ground pulsed beneath them, each step sending ripples through the darkness. As they walked, Drevik became aware of sounds at the edge of hearing—whispers that slithered away when he tried to focus, laughter that echoed from impossible directions. His shadow had merged with the landscape, and he could feel it now, stretched thin across the Shadowlands like a fishing line cast into deep water.

There you are, said the whisper. We've been waiting.

"Something's talking to me." He kept his voice level, controlled. "In my head."

"What's it saying?" Lexi was pressed against his side now, her body warm despite the ambient chill. Her fear-scent had sharpened, cutting through the copper taste of the air.

"That it's been waiting."

"Tell it to get in line." Mira's attempt at humor fell flat, her voice thin. "We've been waiting too. For weeks. For this whole goddamn mission."

The landscape shifted around them. Drevik blinked, and suddenly trees stood where there'd been emptiness—twisted things with bark like charred skin and branches that moved without wind. They formed a path, or maybe a corridor, their limbs reaching toward each other overhead until the twilight darkened into something closer to true night.

"The Shadowlands are responding to us." Seraphina didn't slow. "Emotions here have weight. Fear shapes the landscape. Stay calm, and the path stays clear."

"Stay calm," Lexi muttered. "In a realm that eats fear and shits nightmares. Sure. Easy."

Drevik pulled her closer, his arm wrapping around her waist without conscious thought. She stiffened, then relaxed into his hold, her body fitting against his like she'd always been there.

"I've got you," he murmured against her hair. "You're not falling behind."

"Neither are you." Her hand pressed flat against his chest, right over his heart. "Whatever's calling you, whatever's pulling at your shadow—I'm not letting go."

He looked down at her. In the strange twilight, her eyes had taken on an almost luminous quality, green flecked with gold. She was beautiful in a way that had nothing to do with the realm's distortions—beautiful in the way of someone who'd chosen to stand beside him despite every reason to run.

"Later," he said, voice rough. "When we get back. We need to talk."

"Talk." Her lips curved, something between a smile and a grimace. "Is that what we're calling it?"

"I'm calling it whatever gets us both out of here alive."

"Then walk, Markinsoul. And don't stop until we're through."

They moved.

Hours passed, or what felt like hours—time moved strangely in the Shadowlands, stretching and compressing in ways that made Drevik's head spin if he thought about it too hard. The landscape continued to shift around them, but Seraphina's guidance kept them on course. When the ground tried to soften into something that would swallow their feet, she found solid path. When the trees pressed too close, their branches reaching with hungry intent, her will cleared the way.

"How do you know where to go?" Mira asked during a rest, her back against one of the twisted trunks. "This place changes every time I blink."

"It responds to certainty." Seraphina stood apart from the group, her attention on the distance. "I know where I'm going because I refuse to be lost. The Shadowlands respect conviction."

"Respect." Drevik tested the word. "That's an interesting choice. Not obey. Not understand. Respect."

"The Shadowlands have their own will, their own desires. They're not a tool to be used—they're a partner to be negotiated with." Her pale eyes found his. "Something you seem to understand instinctively, acolyte. Your shadow has been guiding us for the past hour."

Drevik looked down. His shadow stretched ahead of them, no longer connected to his feet but still tethered to his body by that thin, invisible thread. It had taken on a shape that didn't match his movements—something larger, more defined, moving through the darkness with purpose.

"That's not normal." He'd known it wasn't normal. But saying it aloud made it real in a way it hadn't been before.

"No." Seraphina's expression gave nothing away. "It's not. Your connection to this realm is deeper than any acolyte I've trained. Deeper than most Elders. When we return, there will be questions."

"Questions." He laughed, the sound brittle. "Is that what we're calling them?"

"Interrogation, then. Examination. The Order doesn't take kindly to unknowns." Her lips curved, just slightly. "But that's a problem for later. Right now, we need to reach the breach point before the landscape realizes we've stopped."

They moved again.

The deeper they traveled, the more the Shadowlands seemed to notice them. Drevik felt eyes on his skin—not physical eyes, but the weight of attention, of being observed. The whispers had grown louder, more distinct, and he found himself catching fragments of meaning:

...the one with the shadow...

...finally come home...

...the gate opens...

"There." Seraphina's voice cut through the whispers like a blade. "The camp."

Drevik followed her gesture and stopped.

In a clearing that hadn't existed moments before, a cluster of tents stood arranged in a loose circle. They were ordinary tents—nylon and aluminum poles, the kind you could buy at any outdoor store—but they looked wrong against the twisted landscape. Human geometry imposed on a realm that didn't understand it.

The camp had been abandoned recently. Very recently. A cooking fire still smoldered, smoke rising in a column that seemed to freeze in the air rather than dissipate. A pack leaned against one of the tent poles, its contents spilled across the ground: clothes, a flashlight, protein bars in wrappers that reflected the twilight.

"Someone was living here." Lexi moved forward, her investigation instincts overriding caution. "Recently. There's still coffee in that mug."

Drevik followed her, his shadow stretching ahead like a scout. The ground around the camp was solid—too solid, as if someone had willed it to stay fixed. He knelt beside the fire, felt the warmth still radiating from the coals. "An hour ago. Maybe less."

"Who crosses into the Shadowlands and sets up camp?" Mira circled the perimeter, her movements sharp, predatory. "This place isn't for living. It's for passing through."

"Unless you can't leave." Seraphina's voice had gone quiet. "Unless you're trapped."

Drevik stood, his eyes tracking the ground around the camp. The landscape had begun to respond to their presence, trees pressing closer, shadows deepening. But through the distortion, he could see something else.

Footprints.

Human footprints, leading away from the camp in a direction that made his shadow strain against its tether.

"Someone's been here for weeks." He followed the prints with his eyes, saw them disappear into the darkness ahead. "Living here. Surviving. And recently, they moved deeper in."

"Surviving on what?" Lexi appeared beside him, her shoulder brushing his. "There's no food in this realm. No water. Just... shadow."

"Shadow sustains those who belong to it." Seraphina had gone still, her face unreadable. "If someone has been living here, they've been changed by it. Fed by it. The Shadowlands would have claimed them by now."

"Claimed them how?" Mira's hand had found her blade, a shadow-forged weapon she'd carried since their first mission together. "What comes back from a place like this?"

"Nothing human."

The word hung in the air, heavy with implication.

Drevik looked at the footprints again, at the trail leading deeper into the Shadowlands. His shadow wanted to follow—he could feel it pulling, straining, desperate to chase whatever had made those marks. The whispers had grown clearer, almost audible:

...the lost one...

...the one who stayed...

...the gatekeeper waiting...

"We follow the trail." The decision came from somewhere deeper than his conscious mind. "If someone's been living here, they might have information about the breaches. They might know what's causing them."

"Drevik—" Lexi's hand closed around his wrist. "Your shadow. It's trying to take over."

He looked down. His shadow had grown, stretched, taken on dimensions that didn't match the light. It pooled around his feet like something liquid, reaching upward with tendrils that brushed against his ankles, his calves.

"It's not trying to take over." He could feel it now, the connection between himself and the darkness. "It's trying to protect me. Guide me." He met Lexi's eyes. "The realm recognizes me. I don't know why. But it's not trying to hurt me—it's trying to help."

"That's what they always say." Mira had moved to his other side, her blade drawn but held low. "Right before they get swallowed."

"Then you should probably stay close."

"Like I'd let you walk into the dark alone." Lexi's grip tightened. "You're stuck with me, Markinsoul. Whether you like it or not."

"I like it." The words came out rougher than intended. "I like it a lot."

Her eyes widened slightly, something shifting behind the fear and determination. In the strange twilight, with his shadow reaching for the unknown and the weight of the Shadowlands pressing down, he wanted to kiss her. The desire hit him like a wave—sudden, overwhelming, completely inappropriate for the moment.

Later, he reminded himself. When they got back. When they survived.

"Let's move." Seraphina had already started forward, following the trail of footprints. "Whatever's waiting ahead, I'd rather face it head-on than let the landscape trap us here."

They followed.

The deeper they went, the more the Shadowlands opened around them. The oppressive weight began to lift, replaced by something that felt almost like anticipation. Drevik's shadow stretched further ahead, scouting paths he couldn't see, finding solid ground in the shifting landscape.

And then they found her.

She stood in a clearing that shouldn't exist—perfectly circular, perfectly still, ringed by trees that had stopped moving. Her back was to them, her figure slight, her hair a dark cascade down her shoulders. She wore ordinary clothes—jeans, a t-shirt, sneakers that had seen better days—but she stood in the Shadowlands like she belonged there.

When she turned, Drevik's breath caught.

Her face was young, maybe his age, maybe younger. Her eyes were the problem. They reflected the twilight with an intensity that wasn't human, and when she smiled, her teeth were too sharp, her expression too knowing.

"Finally," she said, her voice carrying across the clearing without rising. "I've been waiting for you, shadow-walker. The realm told me you were coming."

"Who are you?" Seraphina had moved to the front, her stance protective. "How long have you been here?"

"I'm nobody." The girl's smile widened. "I'm everyone. I'm the space between spaces." Her too-sharp eyes found Drevik, pinned him in place. "But you already know that, don't you? You can feel it. The part of you that's already home."

Drevik's shadow had gone still. Completely still, for the first time since they'd crossed the threshold. It stretched toward the girl like a hand reaching for its reflection.

"What are you?" His voice came out steady despite the fear curling in his chest.

"I'm what happens when you stay too long." She tilted her head, a gesture that should have been human but wasn't. "I'm what the Shadowlands make of mortals who refuse to leave. I'm the gate that's been waiting to open."

Behind him, Lexi's hand found his. Her fingers were cold, trembling slightly.

"We're not here for cryptic bullshit," Mira called out, her blade raised. "We're here for answers. Start giving them, or I start cutting."

The girl laughed, the sound echoing in ways that hurt Drevik's ears. "Cutting. Yes. That's what the last ones tried too. The ones who came before you." Her smile faded, replaced by something older, hungrier. "They're part of me now. Part of the landscape. Would you like to join them?"

The trees began to move.

Not the gradual shifting they'd seen before—this was purposeful, predatory. Branches reached toward them like fingers, roots rose from the ground like serpents. The twilight darkened, pressing down, and Drevik felt the weight of the realm settling around them like a fist.

"Run," Seraphina ordered, but the word was barely out of her mouth before the clearing began to close.

Drevik's shadow moved without conscious thought. It spread outward, slammed against the encroaching trees, held them back with a force he didn't know he possessed. The effort sent pain lancing through his skull—hot, bright, blinding—but he held on.

"Go," he gasped. "I'll hold it. Get everyone out."

"Not without you." Lexi refused to release his hand. "I told you. You're stuck with me."

"Lexi—"

"Shut up and run, Markinsoul."

She pulled him forward, and he let her. Behind them, the clearing collapsed, the girl's laughter following them like a ghost as they fled through the twisting landscape.

The Shadowlands had noticed them.

And something in Drevik's blood sang in response.

They didn't stop running until Seraphina found a defensible position—a hollow between two massive trees whose roots formed a natural shelter. They collapsed inside, breathing hard, the sound of pursuit fading behind them.

"That was a gatekeeper." Seraphina's voice was flat. "A human who's been transformed by long exposure to the Shadowlands. They guard territory, control access. She claimed that area."

"She said she was waiting for me." Drevik pressed his back against the wood, feeling his pulse hammer against his ribs. "For us. How would she know we were coming?"

"The realm told her." Seraphina's eyes had gone distant. "Gatekeepers develop a connection to the Shadowlands. They sense changes, movements. If she was waiting specifically for you..."

"Then someone sent her." Lexi had moved to sit beside him, her shoulder pressed against his. "Someone wanted us to find her."

"Or someone wanted to test us." Mira had taken position at the entrance, her blade still drawn. "See how far we'd get before the realm swallowed us whole."

Drevik closed his eyes, felt the weight of exhaustion settling into his bones. His shadow had returned to normal—mostly. It still rippled at the edges, still reached for the darkness beyond their shelter, but it had reconnected to his feet. The pain in his skull had faded to a dull throb.

"We need to keep moving." He opened his eyes. "That thing—the gatekeeper—she'll come looking. And she won't be alone."

"The Shadowlands will be hunting us now." Seraphina stood, her face hard. "We've drawn attention. The realm knows we're here, and it's curious."

"Curious." Mira's laugh was sharp. "Great. The nightmare dimension is curious about us."

Lexi's hand found Drevik's in the darkness. Her fingers were still cold, but steady now. She didn't say anything—didn't need to. The press of her palm against his said enough.

We'll get through this.

I'm not letting go.

He squeezed back, once, and felt her squeeze in return.

"We find the breach," he said, forcing himself to stand. "We close it. We get out. Questions about what I am can wait until we're not being hunted by a nightmare dimension."

"Agreed." Seraphina moved toward the entrance. "The breach is northeast. We move fast, we move quiet, and we don't stop for anything."

They moved.

And as they traveled deeper into the Shadowlands, Drevik felt the realm pressing against his mind, curious, hungry, and waiting.

Welcome home, it whispered. We've been waiting so long.

His shadow rippled in response, reaching toward the darkness with something that felt terrifyingly like joy.

The breach appeared without warning.

One moment they were navigating through trees that moved like living things; the next, the landscape opened into a crater of absolute darkness. The edges were ragged, torn, as if something had ripped through from the other side.

"That's not natural." Mira's voice had gone quiet. "That's a wound."

"Someone forced their way through." Seraphina knelt at the edge, her hand hovering over the void. "From our side. Someone opened a door and didn't close it properly."

The breach pulsed. Drevik felt it in his shadow—a pull, a drag, like gravity had suddenly reversed. The darkness wanted him to fall in, to surrender, to let go.

"That's where the gatekeeper came from." He could see it now—the connection between the woman and the wound. "She fell through. Or was pushed."

"And the Shadowlands claimed her." Lexi's voice was grim. "Turned her into something that doesn't belong anywhere."

Drevik stepped closer to the edge. His shadow stretched down into the void, exploring, mapping. He could feel the shape of the breach—the way it had been made, the carelessness of it, the power that had torn through without regard for consequences.

"I can close it." The words came from somewhere deeper than thought. "My shadow can fill the gap. Seal it."

Seraphina stood sharply. "That's not—"

"I know what it will cost." He could feel it already—the way the darkness wanted more of him, the way it would take and take until there was nothing left. "But if I don't, more gatekeepers will come through. More breaches will open. The Shadowlands are already pushing against the edges of our world."

Lexi's hand closed around his arm. "There has to be another way."

"I don't think there is." He met her eyes, saw the fear and the anger and the something else she refused to name. "I'll be careful. I won't let it take too much."

"You don't know what 'too much' means in a place like this."

"No." He pulled her close, pressed his forehead against hers. "But I know you'll pull me back if I go too far."

Her breath caught. Her hands came up to frame his face, her touch gentle despite the strength in her fingers.

"If you die in there, I'll find whatever afterlife you end up in and drag you back myself."

"That's the most romantic thing you've ever said to me."

"I hate you."

"I know."

He kissed her—brief, fierce, full of everything they hadn't said. Then he stepped back, turned toward the breach, and let his shadow reach for the void.

The Shadowlands rose to meet him.

And Drevik Markinsoul fell into the darkness of his own power, praying he would be strong enough to climb back out.

The sealing took everything he had.

He poured his shadow into the breach, filling the gaps, knitting the edges together with darkness that felt like extension of his own soul. The Shadowlands resisted—they wanted the door to stay open, wanted the connection to the mortal realm—but his will was stronger.

Close, he commanded, and the darkness obeyed.

When it was done, when the breach had smoothed over into nothing, he found himself on his knees, gasping, his vision swimming with colors that didn't exist. Hands pulled at him—Lexi's hands, steady and sure—and then he was being held, carried, supported.

"Stay with us." Her voice came from far away. "Drevik. Stay with me."

"Always," he tried to say, but his voice was gone, lost in the darkness.

The last thing he saw before unconsciousness took him was Lexi's face, her green eyes fierce with tears she refused to shed, her mouth forming words he couldn't hear.

I've got you.

I'm not letting go.

Then the Shadowlands closed around him, and Drevik Markinsoul fell into a darkness that, for the first time, felt like home.

He woke to twilight and the sound of Lexi's voice.

"...don't care what the protocols say. He saved us. He closed the breach. If you want to report him, you'll have to go through me."

"He's waking." Seraphina's voice, calm and measured. "Give him space."

Drevik opened his eyes. The Shadowlands pressed around them, but differently now—softer, less predatory. His shadow had returned to its proper place, stretched beneath him like a sleeping thing.

"Easy." Lexi's face appeared above him, her expression caught between relief and fury. "You've been out for hours. The realm was... it was trying to keep you."

"I closed it." His voice came out rough, damaged. "The breach."

"You closed it." Seraphina knelt beside him, her pale eyes intent. "And in doing so, you formed a connection to this realm that wasn't there before. The Shadowlands know you now. They'll remember you."

"Is that bad?"

"That remains to be seen." She stood. "For now, we need to move. The gatekeeper is still out there, and the realm's attention has been drawn. We need to reach the extraction point before nightfall."

Drevik sat up, felt the world tilt and steady. Lexi's hand remained on his shoulder, anchoring him.

"How do you feel?" she asked, quiet enough that only he could hear.

"Different." He looked at his hands, saw the way his shadow didn't quite match his movements. "Stronger. Like something that was missing finally clicked into place."

"That's what scares me." Her fingers tightened on his shoulder. "You're changing, Drevik. Every time we come here, you lose a little more of what makes you human."

"I'm still here." He covered her hand with his own. "I'm still me."

"For now." Her voice was barely a whisper. "But I've seen what this realm does to people. I've seen what happens when someone stays too long, lets the shadows in too deep." Her eyes met his, fierce and frightened. "I'm not going to watch you become another gatekeeper."

"You won't." He stood, pulling her up with him. "I promise."

"Your promises don't mean anything if you're not human enough to keep them."

It was the most honest thing she'd ever said to him. And it terrified him more than the Shadowlands ever could.

"Then I'll stay human." He released her hand, stepped back. "For you."

She held his gaze for a long moment, something unspoken passing between them. Then she turned away, her braid swinging, her posture rigid with everything she wasn't saying.

"Move out," Seraphina called. "We have a long walk ahead, and the realm is watching."

They moved.

And behind them, in the darkness, something else moved as well—something that had learned Drevik's scent, his shadow, his soul.

Something that would be waiting when he returned.

They reached the extraction point as the twilight deepened—though in the Shadowlands, it was always twilight, always that perpetual moment between day and night. The threshold hung in the air like a mirror, reflecting their world back at them.

Seraphina went first, stepping through with practiced ease. Mira followed, her usual sharp edges dulled by exhaustion.

Drevik hung back, his eyes on the darkness behind them.

"Something's coming." He could feel it now—the attention of the realm, the curiosity that had become something hungrier. "The gatekeeper. She's following."

"Then we close the door before she can get through." Lexi stood beside him, her hand finding his. "Together."

He looked at her—really looked, taking in the determination in her jaw, the fear she refused to acknowledge, the way she positioned herself between him and the darkness.

"I meant what I said." His voice came out rough. "I'll stay human. For you."

"I meant what I said too." Her grip tightened. "I'll drag you back from whatever afterlife you end up in. So don't think death gets you out of this."

"I wouldn't dream of it."

They stepped through together.

And on the other side, in the chamber that smelled of burnt sage and memory, Drevik Markinsoul returned to a world that suddenly felt smaller, dimmer, less real than the one he'd left behind.

His shadow stretched behind him, content.

Waiting.

Home, it seemed to say. We're finally home.

And somewhere in the Shadowlands, something laughed.
