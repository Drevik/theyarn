Book 3: The Veiled Truth

Chapter 2: Unraveling Threads

Drevik woke to Lexi's eyes on him.

She sat on the edge of the bed, already dressed, her expression caught somewhere between contemplation and concern. Morning light filtered through the ward-protected windows—the fake light of the underground compound, but consistent enough to trick the body into routine.

"You're staring," he said.

"I'm thinking." Her hand found his chest, pressing against the spot where the bond pulsed. "There's a difference."

"Doesn't feel different from this side."

Her lips twitched. "How are you feeling?"

The question carried weight beyond its simplicity. After last night—the failed archive break-in, the discovery that someone had beaten them to the journal, the confrontation that followed—he expected tension. Instead, she touched him with the same careful attention she'd always shown.

"Honestly?" He sat up, the sheets pooling at his waist. "Confused. My mother's warning mentioned you specifically. I don't know why."

Lexi's hand withdrew. "That's what I wanted to ask about." Her voice stayed level, controlled. "She said to trust no one, but she singled me out. You, I understand—we're bound. But why me?"

"I don't have answers." He reached for her hand. She let him take it. "If I did, I'd tell you."

"Would you?" The question came out soft. Vulnerable in a way Lexi rarely allowed herself to be. "Last night, you were ready to walk into that archive without us. Without me."

"I asked Seraphina to come."

"Because of her Shadowlands senses. Not because you wanted backup." Her grip tightened. "I've been doing this longer than you. I've killed more entities, handled more breaches, navigated more Order politics. And you still treated me like someone who needed protecting instead of someone who could protect you."

He recognized the pattern now—the accusation beneath the vulnerability. Lexi didn't fear danger. She feared irrelevance.

"I wasn't trying to shut you out." He held her gaze. "I was trying to keep you from having to choose between me and your father."

Something flickered across her face. "My father."

"Thornwood's position on me is conditional at best. If I dragged his daughter into a crime against the Order—"

"His daughter made her own choice." Her jaw set. "You don't get to decide what risks I take. That's not how this works."

"No." He pulled her closer. "It's not. I'm sorry."

The apology landed between them, heavy and honest. She studied him for a long moment.

"Tell me what your mother said. Not the summary—the exact words, as close as you can remember."

He closed his eyes, letting the memory surface. The voice that had come through the wards during his ascension, distorted and distant but unmistakably hers.

"Trust no one with what you carry. The bonds are real—anyone can be turned, but the bonds themselves cannot be faked. Find the Veiled One's journal before they do. And Lexi—"

He opened his eyes. "She said your name. Just your name. Then the connection broke."

Lexi absorbed this in silence. Her thumb traced circles on his hand—a nervous habit he'd learned to read.

"My father's been asking questions about you," she finally said. "About your family. About your mother specifically."

"What kind of questions?"

"The kind that suggest he knows more than he's saying." Her eyes met his. "He let you become a Seeker. That's not nothing. The Elder Council had to approve it, and at least two of them wanted you watched, not promoted."

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because I didn't know if you'd use it against him or not." The admission cost her. "I'm still figuring out where my loyalties land when they conflict."

He understood. Family wasn't simple. Neither was the Order. And their relationship existed in the space between those complications.

"Thank you for telling me now."

"Don't thank me yet." She stood, her composure sliding back into place. "We have a meeting in twenty minutes. All of us. Katryn found something."

The five of them gathered in Drevik's quarters—his Seeker rooms now serving as an informal war room. The space had been designed for one person. Five made it feel cramped.

Katryn had commandeered the largest surface, multiple screens displaying code streams and security logs. Her fingers moved across her tablet, pulling up data with practiced efficiency.

"I've been analyzing the security footage from last night." She didn't look up. "The archives were supposed to be unmonitored during our window. That's why I chose that time."

"But?" Lexi leaned against the wall, arms crossed.

"But the footage from that corridor exists. It just... skips. Exactly seventeen seconds of missing data." Her eyes finally met theirs. "That shouldn't be possible. The Order's security systems don't have gaps—they're designed specifically to prevent that kind of manipulation."

"Someone erased it," Mira said.

"Someone made it look like nothing happened." Katryn pulled up a comparison. "See these timestamps? The system thinks the footage is continuous. But the shadows in the corridor shift. Light sources change. There's a discontinuity in the background data that only shows up if you're looking for it."

Drevik studied the images. "You're saying someone edited the footage to cover our break-in."

"I'm saying someone edited the footage to cover something. It could be our break-in. It could be something else that happened in that corridor at the same time." Katryn's expression held grim precision. "The editing is sophisticated. Whoever did this had Order-level access and technical skills that match or exceed my own."

"That narrows the list." Lexi straightened. "How many people in this compound can do what you do?"

"With this specific system? Three. Me, Elder Thornwood's technical liaison, and a Ward in the security division who's been here for fifteen years." Katryn paused. "But the edit also removed any trace of the person who accessed the journal before us. Which means whoever took it also has the ability to manipulate evidence."

Seraphina had remained silent, positioned by the window where the warded light cast shifting shadows across her features. Now she spoke.

"The Shadowlands are restless today."

All eyes turned to her.

"What does that mean?" Mira asked.

"It means something is coming through. Not a breach—something else." Seraphina's gaze remained fixed on something only she could see. "The barriers between worlds are thinner than they should be. Something is pushing from the other side."

"The breach pattern." Drevik connected the pieces. "Someone is weakening the barriers deliberately."

"Not just weakening." Seraphina turned to face them. "Preparing. They're drawing something through. And the Shadowlands..." She hesitated, her expression shifting to something almost like fear. "They whisper about a cage. A cage that needs a key."

"A cage for what?"

"For someone. Or something." Her voice dropped. "And the key... the key is already inside you, Drevik."

The words settled in the room like a held breath.

"Inside me how?" He kept his voice level.

"I don't know. The whispers aren't clear. But whatever your mother gave you—whatever's in your blood—they need it for something." Seraphina moved toward him, her presence carrying the faint chill of Shadowlands energy. "You're not just a Seeker. You're part of whatever they're building."

Dread coiled in his stomach. His mother's warning—they're coming for what you carry—suddenly made more sense.

"Then we need to figure out what I'm carrying." He looked at each of them in turn. "And we need to do it before whoever's behind this makes their next move."

The summons came at noon.

A messenger appeared at his door—acolyte, young, nervous—with a request that Drevik present himself to Elder Thornwood's office. No explanation. No alternatives.

"He knows," Lexi said after the messenger left. "About last night."

"He suspects." Katryn's fingers continued moving across her tablet. "But he doesn't have proof. If he did, you'd be in a holding cell, not a meeting."

"Should I go?"

"You don't have a choice." Lexi's hand found his arm. "But you don't have to go alone."

The walk to the administrative wing felt longer than usual. Lexi walked beside him, her stride matching his, her presence a steadying weight at his side. They didn't speak. The tension between them had shifted—no longer about trust, but about strategy.

Elder Thornwood's office waited at the end of the corridor, its door unassuming despite the power that lay beyond. Drevik knocked once.

"Enter."

The office hadn't changed since his last visit—old books, older magic, the shadows bending away from the Elder rather than toward him. But something in the air felt different today. Charged. Anticipatory.

"Markinsoul." Thornwood gestured to the chair across from his desk. "Sit."

Drevik sat. Lexi remained standing, positioning herself by the door—close enough to intervene, far enough to not seem like a threat.

"You called for me, Elder."

"I did." Thornwood's green eyes—the same sharp green as his daughter's—fixed on Drevik with uncomfortable intensity. "There was unusual activity in the restricted archive last night."

"Unusual how?"

"Someone accessed the archives using your credentials, Markinsoul. Your authorization codes were logged entering the Elder section at approximately midnight." The Elder's expression didn't change. "At the time, security logs showed you were in your quarters. But logs can be falsified."

Drevik kept his face neutral. "Are you accusing me of something?"

"I'm presenting facts and asking for your interpretation." Thornwood leaned back. "Your credentials were used. The journal of the last Veiled One is missing. And someone—or something—has been creating breach patterns around this compound for weeks."

"You think these things are connected."

"I think someone is orchestrating events. I think that someone has access to Seeker-level systems and Elder-level archives." The Elder's voice dropped. "I think you may be either a participant or a patsy. I haven't determined which."

The accusation hung in the air. Beside the door, Lexi's stance shifted—ready to move if needed.

"If you believe I'm involved," Drevik said carefully, "why summon me instead of having me detained?"

"Because I might be wrong." Thornwood's expression flickered—the first crack in his controlled facade. "And because I remember your mother."

The name landed like a blow.

"My mother?"

"Brilliant woman. Stubborn. Utterly convinced she knew better than everyone around her." Thornwood's gaze grew distant. "She was right more often than she had any right to be. And then she vanished, leaving behind a son who shouldn't have survived the Order's notice but somehow thrived."

Drevik said nothing. The Elder seemed to be working toward something.

"I opposed your promotion. Not because of your family—the Markinsoul bloodline is irrelevant to me—but because you appeared too quickly, advanced too fast. The Order has enemies, Markinsoul. People who would love to place a pawn in our ranks." Thornwood's eyes sharpened. "But Lexi vouched for you. My own daughter, who I trust above all others, swore you were genuine."

"She was right to."

"Perhaps. Or perhaps she's compromised in ways she doesn't understand." The Elder stood, moving toward the window. "Which is why I'm offering you a choice."

"What choice?"

"Help me find whoever's behind this—the journal theft, the breach pattern, the manipulation of our systems—and I'll protect you from the political fallout. Work with me, and your position remains secure."

Drevik's mind raced. The offer was tempting—alignment with Thornwood meant access, protection, resources. But it also meant accepting the Elder's version of events.

"And if I decline?"

"Then you'll face whatever's coming alone." Thornwood turned to face him. "And my daughter will face it with you."

The implicit threat wasn't subtle. Lexi's safety tied to his cooperation.

"I need time to consider," Drevik said.

"You have until tomorrow morning." The Elder's voice hardened. "And Markinsoul—one way or another, I will discover what happened to that journal. Your cooperation would make the process cleaner. Your resistance would make it... complicated."

He found Mira waiting in his quarters when he returned. The others had dispersed—Lexi to training, Katryn to her systems, Seraphina to whatever shadows she occupied when not actively helping. But Mira sat on the edge of his bed, medical bag beside her, her expression serious.

"We need to talk about your health."

"My health is fine."

"That's not what I'm seeing." She stood, pulling a scanner from her bag. "The Seeker ascension changes your physiology. There are adjustments your body makes over weeks, sometimes months. But your readings are erratic."

"Erratic how?"

"Your connection to the Shadowlands should be stabilizing. Instead, it's fluctuating—strong one moment, weak the next." She activated the scanner, running it over his chest. "Hold still."

He let her work, feeling the faint tingle of medical magic probing his systems.

"There." She pointed at the scanner's display. "See these markers? They shouldn't be there."

He studied the readout. Numbers and patterns he didn't fully understand, but one thing stood out—his energy signature was interrupted, jagged where it should have been smooth.

"What does it mean?"

"Something is interfering with your Seeker abilities." Mira's voice held clinical precision. "It could be a side effect of the ascension. It could be a natural variation. But it could also be external."

"External how?"

She set the scanner down. "There's a compound in your blood. Trace amounts—I almost missed it. It's designed to weaken mental barriers, make the subject more susceptible to suggestion."

Cold spread through his chest. "You're saying someone's been drugging me."

"I'm saying there's something in your system that shouldn't be there." Her hand found his. "I don't know when or how. But I need you to think—has anything you've eaten or drunk tasted off? Has anyone had access to your quarters without you present?"

His quarters. Where four women came and went freely. Where Seraphina materialized from shadows. Where Katryn's systems monitored everything.

"I don't—" He stopped. "The tea. Sometimes when I come back, there's tea waiting. I assumed..."

"Who prepared it?"

"Any of them. All of them." His jaw tightened. "I don't know."

Mira's expression softened. "I'm not accusing anyone. But you need to be careful. Your food, your water, anything you consume—know its source. And let me examine you regularly. If the compound is administered again, I'll catch it."

"Can you remove what's already there?"

"Over time. Your body will process it out, assuming no new doses." She squeezed his hand. "But Drevik—if someone is doing this deliberately, they're not trying to kill you. They're trying to control you."

The implications settled like weight on his shoulders. Control. Influence. Make him susceptible to suggestion.

"We need to tell the others."

"No." Mira's voice sharpened. "Not yet. If one of them is responsible, we'd be tipping our hand. Let me monitor the situation first. See if I can trace the source."

"You want me to keep this from them?"

"I want you to survive." Her eyes held his. "You have four bonds. Four people who care about you. But you also have four suspects. And until we know more, we can't afford to trust blindly."

His mother's warning echoed again. Trust no one.

But she'd also said the bonds were real. That they couldn't be faked.

"I'll be careful," he said finally. "But I won't treat them as enemies. Not without proof."

"Then let me get you proof." Mira stood, repacking her bag. "I'll run additional tests. Check the food supplies in your quarters. Look for residues." Her hand lingered on his cheek. "You saved my life in the last crisis. Let me protect yours now."

Evening brought the women together again, this time for an argument that had been building since morning.

Katryn's screens glowed in the corner, data streams reflecting in her glasses. "We're approaching this wrong. We need more intelligence before we act."

"And while we gather intelligence, whoever's behind this makes their next move." Lexi paced the length of Drevik's quarters, her agitation visible in every line of her body. "We should be confronting threats directly."

"Confronting them with what? We don't know who they are. We don't even know what they want."

"They want Drevik." Lexi stopped, turning to face the group. "Seraphina said it—the key is inside him. That makes him the target."

"Which is exactly why we shouldn't charge in blind." Katryn pulled up a map. "Look at the breach pattern. It's evolving. The attacks are getting closer to the compound's center. Someone is tightening a noose around us, and we don't know why."

"Because the journal is the center!" Lexi's voice rose. "The Veiled One's journal is the key to whatever ritual they're building. They need Drevik to find it because his bloodline is connected to it somehow. That's what his mother was trying to tell him."

"Or they're using him as bait." Mira spoke from her position near the door, her voice calm against the rising heat. "The journal might not matter at all. They might be drawing him out deliberately."

All eyes turned to her.

"Why would you say that?" Lexi demanded.

"Because someone accessed the archives using his credentials. Because someone is creating breaches that form a pattern around him specifically. Because everything points to Drevik being central to events—and we don't know if that's by design or by accident." Mira's medical training showed in her clinical detachment. "We need to consider that Drevik might be the trap, not the prize."

The silence that followed was thick enough to cut.

Seraphina finally spoke from her corner. "The Shadowlands don't lie about intent. What's coming for Drevik wants him specifically. Not as bait. As a key." Her eyes found his. "You're what they need. The question is why."

"Then we protect him." Lexi's stance shifted to combative. "That's what we do. We protect him."

"We protect each other," Katryn corrected. "That's how this works."

"We don't even know if we can trust each other." The words came out before Drevik could stop them.

The room went still.

He looked around at the four women—four bonds pulsing in his chest, four people who'd risked everything for him. Mira's warning sat heavy in his mind. Someone had been dosing him with a compound that weakened his mental barriers. Someone with access to his quarters, his food, his trust.

"Someone's been drugging me."

Lexi went rigid. "What?"

"Mira found a compound in my blood. Designed to make me more susceptible to influence." He forced the words out. "It's been administered over time. In my food, my drinks—I don't know how."

"We need to check everything." Katryn was already moving toward his kitchen. "All food sources, all water—"

"Or one of us did it." Seraphina's voice cut through the activity.

Everyone stopped.

"Let's not pretend," Seraphina continued, "that we're all above suspicion. I can move through shadows. Katryn has access to every system in this compound. Mira handles all medical treatments. Lexi has as much access to his quarters as anyone." Her dark eyes swept across the group. "Any one of us could have done it. And if we didn't, we should be asking who else had opportunity."

Dread pooled in Drevik's stomach. He'd known this moment was coming—the moment when suspicion would poison what they'd built. But knowing didn't make it easier.

"Then we find out who." He stood, meeting each of their eyes. "Not by accusing each other. By investigating. By being transparent. By checking everything."

"I can screen everyone's belongings," Katryn offered. "Run the same tests Mira ran on Drevik. See if anyone else has been exposed."

"Do it." He turned to Lexi. "Your father offered me an alliance today. He knows something about last night, but he framed it as a choice—work with him or face the fallout alone."

"That's not a choice," Lexi said. "That's a threat."

"That's what I thought too." He paused. "But he also mentioned my mother. Said he remembers her. That she was brilliant and stubborn and vanished without explanation."

"Your mother is connected to this somehow." Mira's voice was soft. "Whatever's happening—it started before you were born."

"Then we need to understand what she was involved in." Drevik looked at Seraphina. "Can the Shadowlands show us? Her history, what she did?"

Seraphina's expression shifted—something unreadable moving behind her eyes. "The Shadowlands remember everything. But accessing those memories would require entering the Shadowlands directly."

"Can you do that?"

"I can." She rose, moving toward him with fluid grace. "But I can't do it alone. I need an anchor. Someone to pull me back if I lose my way."

"I'll anchor you."

"No." Lexi stepped forward. "You're already compromised—whatever's in your blood, whatever they did to you. You can't be trusted to hold a connection to the Shadowlands without risking yourself." She turned to the others. "One of us needs to be the anchor. Someone stable."

"I can do it." Mira's voice held quiet certainty. "I've studied the medical aspects of Shadowlands contact. I understand the risks."

Seraphina nodded. "Then we do it tonight. When the compound is quiet."

The Shadowlands ritual required preparation—candles, salt, a circle drawn in chalk on the floor. Drevik watched as Seraphina arranged the elements, her movements precise despite their apparent chaos.

"The Shadowlands aren't a place," she explained as she worked. "They're a state of being. A different frequency of reality. To find your mother's trail, I need to step outside this world and look back at it from the other side."

"That sounds dangerous," Mira said.

"It is." Seraphina's smile held no warmth. "The Shadowlands don't like visitors. And they don't like being used as an observation deck. But I have... advantages. My blood carries their resonance. I can move through them in ways pure humans cannot."

She sat in the center of the circle, crossing her legs and closing her eyes. Mira took her position outside the circle, one hand on Seraphina's shoulder, the other holding a charm designed to maintain connection.

"I'm ready," Seraphina said.

The change was subtle at first—a darkening of the shadows in the corners of the room, a drop in temperature that made Drevik's breath mist. Then Seraphina's form began to shift, her edges blurring, her features becoming something other than human.

Mira's grip tightened. "I'm losing her."

"Hold on." Drevik moved closer, his own Seeker abilities reaching toward the disturbance. He could see the threads now—the silver-gold of Seraphina's bond to him, stretching thin as she moved further away, anchored only by Mira's touch.

Then the voice came.

"Drevik."

His mother. But wrong—distorted, layered with harmonics that shouldn't exist in a human voice.

"You're looking for answers in the wrong place."

"Where should I look?" He spoke to the darkness, aware that Seraphina was merely the conduit.

"The key is already inside you. But they want it too. They'll try to take it."

"Who? Who's trying to take it?"

"The ones who remember what your father was. The ones who know what you inherited."

His father. A topic his grandmother had always refused to discuss.

"I never knew my father. What did he give me?"

Silence. Then the voice shifted—becoming Seraphina's again, gasping and straining.

"The Shadowlands... they're pushing back. Something doesn't want us to know. There's a presence here, something powerful—"

The candles flickered. Mira cried out, her hand jerking backward as if burned. And Seraphina's form solidified, her eyes snapping open with a gasp.

She crumpled forward, and Drevik caught her before she hit the ground. Her skin was ice-cold, her breathing ragged.

"Are you all right?" He held her, feeling the tremors that wracked her frame.

"There's something... in the Shadowlands. Something that knows we're looking." Her voice came out broken. "It's been waiting. For you specifically."

"Waiting for me?"

"To come into your inheritance." Her eyes found his, dark and depthless. "Whatever your father gave you—whatever's in your blood—it's connected to something old. Something that doesn't want to be found."

The attack came at midnight.

Drevik felt it before he heard it—a shift in the compound's power, a disturbance in the wards that protected the underground complex. Then the alarms screamed, and everything descended into chaos.

He was out of bed and moving before his mind fully registered the threat. The corridors filled with Order members responding to the breach, their movements coordinated through years of training.

Shadowlands entities. The outer perimeter. Multiple incursion points.

The information flowed through his Seeker senses—the Shadowlands pushing through in three places simultaneously, the compound's defenses struggling to hold.

Lexi appeared at his side, sword already in hand. "This isn't a normal breach."

"No." He could feel it too. "This is coordinated. Deliberate."

They ran toward the nearest breach point, Seraphina materializing from the shadows beside them. Mira and Katryn had their own positions—the compound's medical bay and communications center respectively—but the fight was coming to them.

The first entity was already inside when they arrived. Not the minor creatures Drevik had dealt with before—this one was larger, its form shifting between shapes that hurt to look at. It moved through the corridor with purpose, ignoring the acolytes who tried to slow it.

"Go for the core!" Lexi shouted, engaging the entity with practiced strikes. "The center mass!"

Drevik's Seeker abilities let him see the truth of it—the entity's power concentrated in a knot of darkness at its center. He reached for his own abilities, the shadow magic that had grown since his ascension, and thrust his will toward that core.

The entity screamed—a sound that bypassed ears and struck directly at the mind—and recoiled. But it didn't fall.

They're stronger than before. Seraphina's voice echoed in his head, their bond carrying her thoughts even across the chaos. Someone is feeding them power.

The breach point. He could see it now—a wound in reality where the Shadowlands pushed through, the entity drawing strength from the connection.

"Cover me!" He moved toward the breach, dodging the entity's tendrils, feeling Lexi and Seraphina's attacks forcing it back. His hands pressed against the wound in reality, and he pushed with everything he had.

The barrier sealed with a snap. The entity, cut off from its power source, collapsed into formless darkness.

But there were two more breach points.

They moved. The compound became a blur of combat—Drevik sealing breaches while his partners held off the entities. Katryn's voice came through his earpiece, coordinating response teams, directing forces where they were needed most. Mira's reports came in steady streams—injury counts, stabilization efforts, areas cleared.

By the third breach, Drevik was exhausted. His Seeker abilities demanded more energy than he'd ever pushed through them, and whatever compound had been weakening him left him slower, more vulnerable than he should have been.

And yet, the breaches stopped. The entities fell. The compound held.

He stood in the aftermath, breathing hard, his partners gathering around him. Blood—some his, some not—stained his clothes. The corridor showed signs of the fight: scorch marks on walls, deep gouges where entities had struck, the bodies of fallen acolytes being retrieved by somber teams.

"Everyone okay?" he asked.

Lexi's arm was bleeding from a gash she'd ignored during the fight. Seraphina looked paler than usual. Mira had arrived from the medical bay, already assessing injuries with clinical efficiency. Katryn was reviewing damage reports on her tablet.

But they were alive. All of them.

"That was deliberate," Katryn said, her voice grim. "Three simultaneous breaches, each one stronger than anything we've seen in months. Someone sent those entities."

"Not someone." Drevik felt the truth settling in his bones. "The presence Seraphina felt in the Shadowlands. It knew we were looking for answers. It sent a message."

"A message that said what?" Lexi demanded.

"That we're not as safe as we think." Drevik met her eyes, then each of his partners in turn. "And that whatever's coming for us doesn't need the Shadowlands to reach us. It has agents here already."

Later, when the compound had quieted and the wounded were being treated, Katryn called them together with news that changed everything.

"I've been analyzing the breach pattern," she said, her screens showing the compound's layout with attack points highlighted. "The three incursion sites—they don't form a circle anymore."

"What do they form?" Drevik asked.

Katryn manipulated the display. The three points connected with lines, creating a shape that made his blood freeze.

"A sigil," Seraphina whispered. "They're drawing something."

"Drawing what?" Mira leaned closer.

"I don't know yet." Katryn's fingers moved across her tablet. "But I found something else in the security footage. During the attack, someone accessed the restricted archive again. Using your credentials, Drevik."

"That's impossible—I was fighting for my life."

"Exactly. Which means whoever has access to your credentials used the chaos as cover." She pulled up the footage. "And this time, they disabled the masking. They wanted us to know."

The screen showed a figure moving through the archive corridor during the attack. Cloaked, face hidden, but moving with purpose toward the section where the journal had been stored.

"Can we see who it is?" Lexi asked.

"The figure knows where the cameras are." Katryn enhanced the image. "But there's something else. Look at the way they move."

Drevik studied the footage. The figure's gait, their posture, the way they turned—

Recognition slammed into him like a physical blow.

"That's—" He stopped, the word catching in his throat.

The figure moved like someone he knew. The exact angle of the shoulders, the precise way they navigated the corridor—it was familiar. Too familiar.

"Who?" Katryn asked.

"I'm not sure." The lie came easily, automatic. Because if he was right, if the figure was who he thought it was, then everything he'd believed about his partners was about to shatter.

The figure moved like Seraphina.

But Seraphina had been fighting beside him the entire time. He'd seen her. Felt her presence. Watched her take down entities with her own hands.

Which meant either he was wrong about the figure, or someone was deliberately imitating her. Framing her.

Or there was another option—one he didn't want to consider.

"I need time to think about this," he said. "Don't share this with anyone else yet."

His partners exchanged glances. He could feel their suspicion, their questions. But they didn't push.

Not yet.

His mother's warning echoed one final time. Trust no one. But she'd also said the bonds were real. And right now, those bonds were all he had.

He just didn't know which ones would hold.

End of Chapter Two
