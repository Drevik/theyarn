Chapter 4: Normal Life

Chapter 4: Normal Life

The lecture hall felt like it was underwater.

Drevik blinked at the chalkboard, where Professor Hendricks was writing something about macroeconomic theory, but the words kept sliding around like oil on glass. He'd been awake for thirty-six hours. Maybe forty. The Shadow Walking training sessions happened between midnight and four in the morning, and apparently the Order didn't believe in easing new recruits in gently.

"Mr. Markinsoul?"

He jerked upright. The girl next to him—a business major with a pixie cut and a nose ring—snickered softly.

"Can you explain the concept of market equilibrium?"

Drevik's mouth opened. Closed. He'd read the chapter. He knew this. But his brain felt stuffed with cotton.

"Price point," he managed, voice rough from too much coffee and not enough sleep, "where supply meets demand. No surplus, no shortage. Everyone's happy."

Hendricks raised an eyebrow. "That's... correct, actually. Though I'd appreciate it if you looked at me instead of your desk while answering."

A few students laughed. Drevik felt his ears go hot. Normal. This was supposed to be normal. Just a college junior failing to stay awake in Econ 201, not some initiate of a shadow cult who'd spent the previous night learning to step between dimensions.

The professor moved on, and Drevik slumped back in his seat. His phone buzzed in his pocket. A text from an unknown number:

Training tonight. Midnight. Don't be late. —L

He shoved the phone deeper into his pocket and tried to focus. But his mind kept drifting to the feeling of shadow—how it had wrapped around him like cold water, how the world had folded in on itself when he'd stepped through that first portal. The memory of it was terrifying and exhilarating in equal measure, leaving him with an ache in his chest that felt like hunger.

Focus. This is your life now. Both of them.

The lecture droned on.

After class, Drevik made his way across the quad, weaving through clusters of students who seemed impossibly bright and alive. The autumn sun felt good on his face, warm enough to make him drowsy. He needed food. Real food, not the vending machine snacks he'd been surviving on. And sleep. God, he needed sleep.

"Hey! Wait up!"

He turned. A girl was jogging toward him, her dark hair catching the light. She wasn't someone he recognized, which was unusual—he'd been at Westbrook University for three years, and the campus was small enough that faces became familiar even when names didn't.

She caught up to him, slightly out of breath, smiling in a way that made him acutely aware of the shadows under his own eyes.

"You're Drevik Markinsoul, right? I'm Seraphina. Seraphina Vane. I just transferred."

She extended a hand. He took it automatically. Her grip was firm, her skin cool.

"Sorry, have we met?" He tried to place her face. She was striking—sharp cheekbones, full lips, eyes that seemed to shift between gray and blue depending on the light. A small silver pendant hung at her throat, shaped like a crescent moon.

"No, but I've heard about you." Her smile widened. "Professor Hendricks mentioned you in class last week. Said you were one of his more 'interesting' students. I'm an econ major too."

Drevik felt a prickle of unease. Hendricks had said that? He couldn't recall anything remarkable about his performance in that class. "That's... weird. I'm not exactly a star student."

"Oh, I don't know about that." Seraphina tilted her head, studying him. "I saw your paper on cryptocurrency regulation. You have a way of seeing patterns that others miss."

Now the unease was definite. He'd written that paper sophomore year. For a different professor. In a different department.

"What did you say your major was again?"

"Economics." Her eyes glinted. "Minor in esoteric studies. It's a new program."

There was no esoteric studies minor at Westbrook. Drevik was sure of it.

"Look, I should get going—"

"Let me buy you coffee." She stepped closer, and he caught a hint of her perfume—something floral with an undertone of smoke. "You look like you could use it. Late night studying?"

The question felt loaded. Drevik's pulse quickened.

"I'm fine. Thanks."

"Are you?" Seraphina's voice dropped, just slightly. "Because you don't look fine. You look like someone who's been asked to carry more than they can hold."

He stared at her. The words landed too close to the truth, and for a moment he wanted to tell her everything—the Order, the training, the way the shadows called to him even now, whispering at the edges of his consciousness.

"I'll see you around, Seraphina."

He walked away without looking back. But he could feel her gaze on him, a pressure between his shoulder blades that didn't fade until he'd rounded the corner of the library building.

The training room was in the basement of a building that, on the surface, housed the university's archives. Drevik had walked past it a hundred times without noticing anything unusual. Now he knew better. The door to the basement was hidden behind a shelf of outdated encyclopedias, accessible only to those who knew the pattern of knocks and carried the small obsidian token he'd been given after his initiation.

Lexi was waiting when he arrived.

She stood in the center of the room, arms crossed, wearing the same fitted black clothing she'd worn every time he'd seen her. Her auburn hair was pulled back in a tight braid, and her expression was what he'd come to think of as her default: cold assessment.

"You're late."

"I'm three minutes early."

"Late means not early enough." She uncrossed her arms. "Tell me what you've practiced."

Drevik's body ached from exhaustion, but he straightened. "Shadow Walking. I can do the basic step now, the one you showed me. Move from one shadow to another in the same room."

"Show me."

He looked around the training space. It was a large room, roughly forty feet square, lit by flickering torches that cast pools of shadow into the corners. Stone walls, stone floor, no windows. The air smelled faintly of incense and something older, earthier.

Drevik focused on the shadow in the far corner. He could feel it, even from this distance—a kind of coldness that had nothing to do with temperature. He reached for it with his mind, visualizing the step, the way the darkness would fold around him—

The world shifted.

For a moment, he was nowhere. Everywhere. Floating in a space that wasn't space, surrounded by whispers in languages he almost understood. Then the shadow spat him out, and he was standing in the corner, heart racing, hands trembling.

Lexi nodded. Once.

"Acceptable. Now do it again. The opposite corner."

He did. And again. And again. Each time, the transition became slightly easier, but the cost grew. His legs were shaking now. His vision blurred at the edges.

"Again."

"I need a minute—"

"Now."

He reached for the shadow. It resisted. He pushed harder. The darkness seemed to surge toward him, eager and hungry, and for a moment he was falling, falling through a void that had no bottom—

Lexi's hand closed around his wrist, yanking him back.

"Enough." Her voice was sharp. "You're pushing too hard. The shadow will take everything you give it and ask for more. You have to learn control."

Drevik sagged, catching himself against the wall. "I had it."

"You had nothing. You were about to slip into the Shadowlands." Lexi's grip on his wrist tightened, then released. "That's not a place you want to go unprepared. People don't come back the same."

"What do you mean?"

But she was already moving away, her face closed off. "We're done for tonight. Go home. Eat. Sleep. Tomorrow we work on Veil Sight."

"Lexi—"

"I said we're done."

He watched her, trying to read something—anything—in her posture. She was rigid, her shoulders tight. There was a story there, he realized. Something she wasn't telling him.

"Thank you," he said quietly. "For the training."

She didn't turn around.

He made it halfway across the quad before his legs gave out.

One moment he was walking, the next he was on his knees, the grass cool against his palms. His head spun. The world tilted. He tried to push himself up, but his arms wouldn't cooperate.

"Whoa, hey—"

Hands caught him, strong and careful. He was dimly aware of being guided to a bench, of someone pressing something cold into his hands. A water bottle.

"Drink."

He drank. The water helped, a little. His vision cleared enough to see the person crouched in front of him.

Lexi.

Her face was unreadable, but something flickered in her green eyes. Concern? Annoyance? He couldn't tell.

"You used too much energy," she said. "Shadow Walking drains you. You need to replenish."

"I'm fine."

"You're not fine. You're about thirty seconds from unconscious." She stood, arms crossed again, but her posture had changed. Less rigid. "Can you walk?"

"I think so."

He tried to stand. His legs wobbled. Lexi caught his arm, steadying him.

"I'll take you back to your dorm."

"I can make it—"

"That wasn't a question." Her grip was firm. "Which building?"

Drevik's room was small—a bed, a desk, a window overlooking the parking lot. Lexi guided him inside, deposited him on the bed, and began looking through his mini fridge.

"You have nothing but leftover pizza and energy drinks."

"I haven't had time to shop."

She made a sound that might have been disgust. "Stay here."

She was gone before he could protest. He lay back against the pillow, staring at the ceiling. His whole body felt like it had been wrung out. The shadows in the corners of the room seemed deeper than usual, and he had to remind himself that they were just shadows. Ordinary shadows.

Except nothing was ordinary anymore.

Lexi returned twenty minutes later with a paper bag. She set it on his desk and began pulling out containers—soup, bread, some kind of protein bar.

"Eat. All of it."

"Are you going to watch me?"

"Someone has to make sure you don't die your first week." She pulled his desk chair over to the bed and sat, crossing her legs. "The Order doesn't have the best track record with new recruits."

Drevik forced himself upright, reaching for the soup. It was chicken noodle, still hot. His stomach growled.

"How many recruits have you trained?"

Lexi's expression flickered. "You're the first."

"Really?"

"It's not... I don't usually do this." She looked away. "I requested it."

"Why?"

She was quiet for a long moment. When she spoke, her voice was softer than he'd heard it before.

"Because the last person I was supposed to protect died. And I wasn't there when it happened."

The words hung in the air. Drevik set down the soup.

"I'm sorry."

"It was two years ago. He was a friend. More than a friend, maybe." Lexi's jaw tightened. "He thought he could handle the Shadowlands on his own. He couldn't."

Drevik remembered what she'd said earlier. People don't come back the same.

"Is that why you pushed me so hard? The training?"

"You have potential. More than he did. More than most." She met his eyes, and for the first time, he saw something raw in her expression. Vulnerability. "If you don't learn control, the shadow will consume you. I won't let that happen again."

He wanted to say something—something that would bridge the gap between them, acknowledge the trust she was placing in him. But his eyelids were heavy, and his body was demanding sleep.

"Thank you," he managed. "For everything."

Lexi stood, moving toward the door. But she paused at the threshold.

"Get some sleep. Tomorrow will be harder."

She left the door cracked open. Drevik listened to her footsteps in the hallway, fading into silence.

He woke to dim light and the wrongness of someone watching him.

Drevik's eyes snapped open. For a moment, he didn't know where he was—his dorm room, yes, but something was different. The shadows were too thick. The air too cold.

Then he saw her.

Lexi was in the chair beside his bed, asleep. Her head had fallen back against the wall, her braid loosened, her face relaxed in a way he'd never seen it. She looked younger. Softer. Her lips were parted slightly, and her hand rested near his pillow, close enough that if he moved, his fingers would brush hers.

He lay still, watching her breathe. The cold, distant trainer from the Order was gone, replaced by this—a woman who'd stayed to make sure he was okay. Who'd let her guard down because she was too exhausted to maintain it.

More than a friend, maybe.

The words echoed in his mind. He wondered what that meant. What she'd lost. What she was afraid of losing again.

As if sensing his attention, Lexi stirred. Her eyes opened, unfocused at first, then sharpening as she saw him watching. For a moment, neither of them moved. The silence stretched between them, heavy and strange.

"You stayed," Drevik said quietly.

"You passed out." Her voice was rough. "I wanted to make sure you didn't die in your sleep."

"Did you sleep at all?"

Her gaze slid away. "Someone had to keep watch. The shadows are drawn to you. It makes you a target."

"Lexi—"

"Get dressed." She stood abruptly, the vulnerability gone, replaced by her usual mask. "You have class in an hour. I'll check on you tonight."

She was gone before he could respond. But he noticed that her hand lingered on the doorframe for just a moment, her fingers pressing into the wood as if steadying herself.

The day passed in a blur of lectures and coffee. Drevik found himself looking for Seraphina in each of his classes, but she didn't appear. Had he imagined their interaction? The strange intensity of her questions?

No. She was real. And she knew something she shouldn't.

He was crossing the quad after his last class when Lexi appeared beside him, seemingly out of nowhere.

"We need to talk."

"About what?"

"About the girl you met yesterday. Seraphina Vane."

Drevik stopped walking. "How do you know about that?"

"The Order watches its members. We have eyes everywhere." Lexi's expression was grim. "She's not a transfer student. She's not even enrolled here."

"Then who is she?"

"That's what concerns me. We can't find anything on her. No records, no history, no paper trail. It's like she didn't exist until two days ago."

Drevik remembered the way Seraphina had looked at him. The way her questions had cut too close to the truth.

"What do you think she wants?"

"I don't know. But there's something wrong with her." Lexi's voice dropped. "Stay away from her. Promise me."

"I barely know her—"

"Promise me, Drevik."

He'd never heard her use his first name before. It sounded different in her mouth—urgent, almost desperate.

"I promise."

Lexi nodded, but she didn't seem satisfied. Her eyes kept scanning the quad, watching.

"Go back to your dorm. Don't talk to anyone you don't know. I'll come by tonight for training."

She started to walk away, then stopped. Turned back.

"Drevik."

"Yeah?"

"She's dangerous. I can feel it. And I don't say that lightly."

He watched her go, disappearing into the crowd of students. When she was gone, he let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding.

The sun was setting, painting the quad in shades of orange and purple. Students were heading to dinner, to parties, to the normal routines of college life. Drevik stood still, feeling the weight of his double life pressing down on him.

He turned to head toward his dorm—and froze.

Across the quad, standing perfectly still while students flowed around her, was Seraphina.

She was watching him. And when their eyes met, she smiled.

It was a knowing smile. A hungry smile.

Then the light shifted, just for a moment, and Drevik could have sworn her eyes glowed. A faint silver luminescence that flickered and faded like candlelight.

He blinked. Rubbed his eyes. Looked again.

She was gone.

But the feeling of being watched didn't leave him for the rest of the night.
