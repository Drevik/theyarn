Chapter 17: Maternal Reunion

The safe house was nothing like Drevik had imagined.

He'd pictured something dark and fortified, perhaps an underground bunker bristling with wards and shadow-enhanced defenses. Instead, the address Lexi had memorized led them to a modest cottage on the outskirts of the city, its weathered wooden exterior wrapped in climbing roses that should have been dormant this time of year but burst with impossible crimson blooms.

"That's... not subtle," Seraphina murmured from beside him, her dark eyes scanning the perimeter with professional precision.

"Subtle isn't the same as hidden." Lexi's voice carried a tension that had been building since they'd left the university. She stood at his other side, her athletic frame coiled with readiness. "The roses are a boundary marker. Anyone with Veil Sight would see them as a warning."

"And without Veil Sight?" Drevik asked, though he already knew the answer.

"They'd just see an overgrown garden." Lexi's hand found his, her fingers interlacing with his in a gesture that had become instinctive over the past weeks. "Whatever's inside, she wanted to make sure we found it."

She.

His mother.

The woman who had abandoned him before he could form memories of her face. The mystery that had shadowed every moment of his life, every question about why his grandmother had refused to speak of the past, why his parents' names were absent from every official document, why he'd always felt like he was waiting for something he couldn't name.

Lexi squeezed his hand. "You don't have to do this alone."

"I know." He was grateful for the steadiness in her voice, the way she'd been his anchor through every revelation and reversal since he'd stumbled into the Order's territory. "But I think I need to walk up there myself."

Seraphina's perfectly shaped eyebrow arched. "That's either very brave or very stupid."

"Probably both."

"That's what I like about you, acolyte." The mysterious woman's lips curved into something that wasn't quite a smile. "Very well. We'll maintain perimeter watch. Take whatever time you need."

She and Lexi exchanged a look that communicated an entire tactical plan Drevik wasn't privy to. Then Lexi pressed something into his palm—a small crystal that pulsed with faint luminescence.

"Break this if you need us," she said, her voice dropping to something softer, more intimate. "We'll be there before the shards hit the ground."

The question echoed his earlier thought, completing a circle he hadn't known he was drawing. Where else would she be, this fierce protector who had become so much more than a rival or mentor? Where else would he want her, when the world was spinning in directions he couldn't predict?

His mother's voice whispered through his memory: Trust your heart—it's stronger than any darkness.

For the first time since learning the truth about his heritage, Drevik felt something like peace. Whatever came next, he wouldn't face it alone.

The night wrapped around them, holding them in its embrace, and Drevik fell asleep with Lexi's heartbeat drumming against his chest, counting him into dreams.

Dawn came too quickly.

Drevik woke to an empty bed and the smell of coffee drifting up from somewhere below. For a moment, the peace of the night before held, wrapping him in warmth. Then memory crashed back—his mother, his heritage, the Shadowlands—and the peace shattered.

He dressed quickly, following the scent of coffee to a kitchen that looked like it belonged in a magazine. Seraphina stood at the counter, pouring from an elegant carafe, her dark hair swept up in an artful arrangement that suggested she'd been awake for hours.

"Good morning." Her voice was carefully neutral. "Alexa left early. She said something about checking the perimeter wards."

"She didn't have to do that."

"She wanted to." Seraphina handed him a cup. "How are you feeling?"

The question was loaded, and they both knew it. How did he feel about learning he was half-shadow creature? How did he feel about sleeping with the woman who'd been his fiercest rival? How did he feel about the target that now adorned his back?

"Overwhelmed," he admitted, taking the coffee. "But also... clear. Like something that's always been fuzzy suddenly came into focus."

Seraphina nodded, sipping from her own cup. "Your father's book. Have you looked at it?"

"Not yet." He glanced toward the hallway. "I was going to wait until—"

"Until you had privacy. Wise." She set her cup down, her expression turning serious. "Drevik, what your mother told you changes things. Not between us—I want to be clear about that. But in terms of how the Order will see you, how the Shadowlands will see you... you've become a very valuable piece in a very dangerous game."

"I know."

"Do you?" She moved closer, her perfume subtle and sophisticated. "Because I need you to understand that I'm not offering my support blindly. My family has interests that align with yours. We want the same things—stability, balance, the Order returned to its true purpose. Your existence, your heritage... it could be the key to everything."

"You're saying you're using me."

"I'm saying our goals align." Her hand rose to touch his face, a mirror of the gesture Lexi had made the night before. "The difference is, I'm telling you upfront. I'm not pretending this is purely about affection."

"Is any of it about affection?"

The question surprised him as much as it surprised her. He hadn't meant to ask, but the words had slipped out.

Seraphina's expression flickered. "I'm capable of affection, Drevik. I'm capable of more than you might think." Her fingers traced his jaw. "Last night, you and Alexa... I wasn't asleep. The walls in this house are old, but they're not that thick."

Heat rose to his face. "I—"

"I'm not jealous." She cut him off, though her voice carried an edge. "Or rather, I'm not only jealous. What I am is realistic. You're forming bonds, heart bonds, as your mother called them. Those bonds are power. They're also relationships that require cultivation."

"You're saying you want to be one of those bonds."

"I'm saying I already am." Her eyes held his, dark and depthless. "From the moment you looked at me and saw past the mystery to the woman underneath. You see people, Drevik. That's a rare gift. And it's one I find... compelling."

Before he could respond, footsteps echoed in the hallway. Lexi appeared in the doorway, her hair still damp from a hasty shower, her expression alert.

"The perimeter's clear," she announced, then stopped, taking in the scene—Seraphina's hand on his face, their close proximity. Something flickered in her eyes, but she smoothed it away quickly. "But we have company incoming. Order vehicle, about ten minutes out."

"Friend or foe?" Drevik was already moving, reaching for the coffee he'd barely touched.

"Unknown." Lexi's voice was clipped. "But given that we're in a Vane property that's supposed to be secret, I'm leaning toward foe."

Seraphina's expression had hardened, the intimate moment forgotten. "My family hasn't shared this location with the Order. If they're here, someone's talked."

"Or someone tracked us from the cottage." Drevik's mind raced through possibilities. "My mother said the Shadowlands has interests in me. Maybe they have ways of tracking me that the Order can access."

"Either way, we need to move." Lexi was already heading for the door. "Grab your book. We leave through the back."

The morning erupted into controlled chaos. They gathered what they needed, Seraphina activating secondary wards that would obscure their exit, Lexi scouting the route with the precision of a trained operative. Within minutes, they were slipping out a hidden exit, moving through grounds that would conceal their passage.

As they reached the edge of the property, Drevik glanced back. The manor stood serene and beautiful, giving no indication of the secrets it held.

His mother's final words echoed in his mind: The Order isn't the only ones coming. The Shadowlands has its own plans for you, my son.

Behind him, a car approached on the main road—Order vehicles, probably filled with people who wanted to know what he was, what he could do, what threat he might pose.

Ahead of him, the unknown stretched like a shadow.

And in his pocket, his father's book held answers he wasn't sure he was ready to read.

He took a breath, squared his shoulders, and followed Lexi and Seraphina into the trees.
