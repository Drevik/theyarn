Best Laptop Stands for Remote Work (That Don't Wobble)

I spent two years working from a laptop flat on a desk before I bought a stand. Two years of neck pain, hunched shoulders, and a heat-damaged laptop that throttled under load. The first day I used a stand — screen at eye level, keyboard on the desk where my arms naturally fall — I realized I'd been doing it wrong the entire time. It's the cheapest ergonomic upgrade you can make, and it makes a bigger difference than an expensive chair.

But not all laptop stands are equal. Some wobble when you type. Some don't fit 17-inch laptops. Some look great but block your ventilation. I tested stands across price ranges and use cases to find the ones that actually improve your workday.

What Actually Matters

  • Stability: If the stand wobbles when you type on an external keyboard, it's useless. The stand should hold your laptop rock-steady, even if you bump the desk. Look for wide bases and solid hinges.
  • Adjustability: Your screen height changes depending on your chair, desk, and whether you're sitting or standing. A stand with only one height position is better than nothing, but one with 5+ angles is worth the extra money.
  • Portability: If you commute to a co-working space or coffee shop, weight and foldability matter. The Roost folds to the size of a sunglasses case. The mStand is a solid aluminum brick you'll never move.
  • Ventilation: Laptops get hot. A stand that blocks the bottom vents makes the problem worse. Open-frame stands and ones with ventilation holes are the only acceptable options.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are laptop stands bad for your laptop?

No — they're good for it. Elevating the base improves airflow underneath, which keeps your laptop cooler. Heat is the #1 killer of laptop batteries and components.

Do I need a separate keyboard and mouse with a laptop stand?

Yes, if you're raising the screen to eye level. Typing on a laptop keyboard when the screen is at eye level means your arms are at a 90-degree upward angle — terrible for your shoulders and wrists. Budget $30-50 for a basic keyboard and mouse.

What height should my laptop screen be?

The top of the screen should be at or slightly below eye level, about an arm's length away. If you're tilting your head down to see the bottom, the stand is too low. Most people need 6-8 inches of elevation.

Can I use a laptop stand on a couch or bed?

Some stands work on soft surfaces — the Roost and Nexstand both have legs that straddle cushions. Rigid stands like the mStand need a flat surface. If you work from bed or couch regularly, look for a foldable stand with independent leg adjustment.

What's the difference between a laptop stand and a laptop riser?

Same thing, different names. Both elevate your laptop. The terms are used interchangeably.

Will a laptop stand cool my laptop?

Marginally — by improving airflow. But a stand alone won't fix an overheating laptop. If your laptop runs hot, clean the fans and consider a cooling pad with built-in fans. For normal use, the airflow from a stand is enough.

How much should I spend on a laptop stand?

$20-40 gets you functional (Nexstand, MOFT). $50-80 gets better materials and adjustability (mStand, Roost). $80+ gets premium build quality. More than $100 is paying for aesthetics, not function.
