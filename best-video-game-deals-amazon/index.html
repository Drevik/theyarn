Best Video Game Deals on Amazon Right Now

September 2026 · 8 games reviewed · by Marcus Chen

I've been tracking game prices since before Amazon was the go-to place for physical discs. Back in the day, you scoured Circuit City circulars and hoped. Now the deals come to you, but so does the noise. Half the "deals" on Amazon are inflated MSRPs knocked down to the real street price. The other half are legitimate, honest-to-god discounts on games actually worth playing.

This guide is about that second category. Every game below is something I'd actually recommend at full price, which means the discount is just gravy. I'm not here to push a $70 game that dropped to $60 and call it a deal. I'm here for the 40-60% off stuff that makes your wallet happy and your backlog terrified.

A quick note on pricing: Amazon game prices fluctuate daily. The prices I list are what I saw at the time of writing. If you click through and the price is different, that's Amazon being Amazon. The percentages off are measured against the actual MSRP, not some made-up "list price" that was never real.

How I Pick These Deals

Not every discount makes the cut. Here's what I look for:

  • Real discount vs. fake MSRP. If the "original price" was never the actual going rate, the percentage off is a lie. I verify against price tracking data.
  • Game quality matters. A 70% discount on a 62 Metacritic game isn't a deal. It's a waste of $12. Every game here scored an 80+ from critics, or I've played it myself and can vouch for it.
  • Price-per-hour value. A $20 game you play for 100 hours is a better deal than a $5 game you finish in two. I factor in content volume.
  • Platform availability. I'm focusing on PS5 and Nintendo Switch physical discs, where Amazon's prices are consistently lower than digital storefronts. Xbox and PC deals tend to be better on Steam sales and the Xbox store directly.

With that out of the way, let's get to the games that are actually worth your money this month.

🏆 Best Overall

Elden Ring — PS5

If you somehow haven't played Elden Ring yet, this is your sign. FromSoftware's open-world action RPG launched at $59.99 and currently sits at $19.99 on Amazon. That's 60% off for a game that won Game of the Year, sold 25 million copies, and offers well over 80 hours of content on a first playthrough. The price-per-hour value is almost comical at this point.

The Lands Between is still one of the most impressive open worlds ever built. There's no hand-holding, no quest markers cluttering your screen, no mandatory crafting tutorial. You explore, you fight, you die, you learn. The combat is punishing but fair, the build variety keeps replays fresh, and the world design rewards curiosity in a way most open-world games don't even attempt.

The downside? It's hard. Genuinely, unapologetically hard. If you bounced off Dark Souls or Demon's Souls, Elden Ring won't convert you. The opening hours are brutal before you level up and find better gear. There's no difficulty slider. The story is told through item descriptions and environmental details, which means you'll finish the game understanding roughly 30% of the plot unless you read a wiki. And the camera still gets wonky in tight corridors, a FromSoftware tradition at this point.

But at $19.99, even if you quit after 20 hours, you've still gotten your money's worth. If you stick with it, you've found one of the best games of the generation at a fraction of launch price.

🎮 Best PS5 Exclusive Deal

Returnal — PS5

Returnal was $69.99 at launch. It's now $29.99 on Amazon. For a PS5 exclusive that actually uses the console's features, that's a serious discount. The DualSense haptic feedback and adaptive triggers aren't gimmicks here. They're integral to how the game communicates what's happening. The 3D audio through the PS5's Tempest Engine means you hear enemies before you see them. This is one of the few games that justifies owning a PS5 for its hardware integration alone.

You play as Selene, an astronaut stuck in a time loop on a hostile alien planet. Every death resets the world, rearranging the map and enemies. It's a roguelike built on a AAA budget, which is rare. The bullet-hell combat is fast and fluid. The story is weird and unnerving in the best way. And when you finally beat a boss after 30 attempts, the rush is unlike anything else on PS5.

Here's the catch: it's hard. Harder than Elden Ring in some ways, because you lose your run progress when you die. The roguelike structure means some runs feel like they're over before they start if you get bad room layouts. Load times between deaths are nearly instant, but that just means you're back into the punishment faster. If permadeath mechanics stress you out, this game will be a constant source of anxiety rather than fun.

⭐ Best RPG Deal

Dragon Quest III HD-2D Remake — PS5

Dragon Quest III HD-2D Remake at $24.99 (58% off its $59.99 MSRP) is the kind of deal that makes me want to email everyone I know. This isn't a lazy upscale. Square Enix rebuilt the entire game in the HD-2D engine, the same visual style that made Octopath Traveler look like a living painting. The result is a JRPG from 1988 that looks and plays like it came out this year.

The class system holds up remarkably well. You pick a party of adventurers, assign them classes like Warrior, Mage, Thief, or the game-breaking Sage, and watch their pixel-art sprites bounce around with fluid modern animations. The turn-based combat is simple to understand and deep enough to keep theorycrafters busy. The story, about a hero following in their father's footsteps to defeat a dark lord, is straightforward but sincere in a way that modern JRPGs often try too hard to subvert.

The negatives: it's a remake of a 1988 game, and sometimes that shows. The encounter rate can feel high in certain dungeons. There's no voice acting, just text boxes, which feels odd given the visual upgrade. And if you've played the original, the core content is the same, just prettier. This isn't a full reimagining like FF7 Remake. It's the same game with a gorgeous new coat of paint and quality-of-life improvements like autosave and faster battle speed.

🕹️ Best Switch Deal

Unicorn Overlord — Nintendo Switch

Vanillaware's Unicorn Overlord dropped from $59.99 to roughly $29.99 on Amazon. If you like tactical RPGs and you've been sleeping on this one, wake up. The art direction alone is worth the entry fee. Every character portrait, every background, every animation is hand-drawn with the meticulous craft that Vanillaware is known for. This is the studio that made Odin Sphere and Dragon's Crown, and this might be their best-looking game yet.

The tactical combat puts you in command of squads of units on an overworld map. You position them, set formation priorities, and then watch the real-time resolution play out with the ability to activate skills mid-battle. It's real-time tactics with strategy layer depth, and it works better than it has any right to. With 60+ recruitable characters, there's enormous team-building flexibility.

Now for the real talk. The story is... fine. It's standard fantasy fare with a deposed prince reclaiming his kingdom. Characters are likeable enough but not memorable. The English localization is solid but lacks the punch of the Japanese voice track. The game is also long, 50+ hours for the main story, which is either a selling point or a warning depending on your backlog situation. And the Switch version has occasional frame drops during busy battles with lots of effects on screen. Not game-breaking, but noticeable.

🏆 Best Co-op Deal

Split Fiction — PS5

Split Fiction at $34.99 (30% off its $49.99 MSRP) is the best co-op game deal on Amazon right now. From Hazelight, the studio behind It Takes Two, this is another strictly two-player adventure, and it's just as creative. You play as Mio and Zoe, two writers trapped in a simulation that pulls from their stories. One writes sci-fi, the other writes fantasy, and each level alternates between those aesthetics.

The variety is wild. One minute you're riding a sand shark through a desert, the next you're doing a dance battle with a monkey. Every chapter introduces new mechanics that the game discards as soon as they've been fully explored. It's the video game equivalent of a talented chef who refuses to repeat a course. The Friend's Pass system means only one person needs to own the game, which effectively halves the already-discounted price.

The problems: you need a partner. There's no solo mode, no AI companion, no way to play alone. If you don't have someone to play with, this game is a coaster. The story is earnest but corny in places. Some minigames overstay their welcome by about two minutes. And the PS5 version had a day-one patch that fixed most bugs, but I still hit a handful of animation glitches in later chapters. Nothing game-breaking, but noticeable.

🎯 Best Stealth-Action Deal

Metal Gear Solid Delta: Snake Eater — PS5

Metal Gear Solid Delta at roughly $25 (from a $69.99 MSRP) is a deal that shouldn't exist this soon after release. This is a ground-up remake of MGS3, not an upscale. New graphics engine, modernized controls, and the same story and voice performances that made the original one of the best games on the PS2. Snake crawling through the jungle feels as smooth here as he did in MGSV: The Phantom Pain, which is saying something for a remake of a 2004 game.

The survival mechanics are intact. You hunt for food, treat wounds between firefights, and use camouflage to blend into the environment. The boss fights against the Cobras are legendary, and they hold up with the visual upgrade. The new camera option (over-the-shoulder instead of the original's fixed angles) makes the gameplay feel contemporary without losing the tension that made MGS3 special.

What it doesn't fix: the story is still a Cold War fever dream with 20-minute codec conversations. If you found the original's cutscenes interminable, this remake won't change your mind. The menu navigation for healing and camouflage is still clunky, even with the modernized control scheme. And Kojima's peculiar sense of humor lives on, including moments that are intentionally absurd in ways that might confuse newcomers. At $25 though, you're paying less than a dollar per hour of unique, unforgettable content.

God of War Ragnarok — PS5

God of War Ragnarok at roughly $38 (from $69.99 MSRP) represents a 45% discount on one of the PS5's flagship titles. Kratos and Atreus's Norse saga concludes here with the kind of production values that make you wonder how much this cost to make. The combat is tighter than the 2018 reboot. The realm exploration is broader. The boss fights are bigger. The story actually sticks the landing, which is more than I can say for most sequels that try to raise the stakes.

The PS5 version loads between realms in seconds, runs at 60fps in performance mode without obvious visual compromises, and makes full use of the DualSense controller. Haptic feedback on the Leviathan Axe's frost attacks and the Blades of Chaos' fire adds a physical dimension to the combat that you don't get on PS4.

The gripes: the pacing sags in the middle. There are roughly 10 hours of side content that ranges from compelling to filler. Some of the puzzle sections feel like they're there to pad runtime. And the game leans heavily on the assumption that you played the 2018 entry. If you haven't, the emotional beats won't land with the same weight. But if you have, Ragnarok delivers a satisfying, emotional conclusion that justifies the 25-30 hour journey.

🃏 Best Indie Deal

Balatro Special Edition — Nintendo Switch

Balatro Special Edition on Switch at $19.99 (33% off its $29.99 MSRP) might be the most hours of fun you can get for twenty bucks on any platform. This poker roguelike became a phenomenon for a reason. You play illegal poker hands modified by Joker cards that break the rules in increasingly absurd ways. One run you're scoring millions with a straight flush boosted by a Joker that multiplies face card scores. The next run you're trying to make a high card build work because the shop didn't give you anything better. The randomness is the point.

The Special Edition gets you the physical cartridge, which matters for Switch collectors, plus a few in-game cosmetic extras and a nice steelbook case. LocalThunk, the solo developer, has been updating the game consistently since launch, adding new Jokers, challenges, and balance tweaks. The Switch version runs great in both docked and handheld modes.

The downside is that Balatro is a time black hole. You sit down to play one run, and two hours disappear. The game loop is that addictive. Visually, it's a pixel-art card game. If you need 4K set pieces, this isn't it. The difficulty curve spikes hard in later antes, and some Joker combinations are clearly better than others, which means experienced players will gravitate toward the same builds. But at $20 for the Special Edition with a physical cartridge, you're paying less than a movie ticket for hundreds of hours of strategic card chaos.

⚔️ Best JRPG Deal

Suikoden I & II HD Remaster — Nintendo Switch

Suikoden I & II HD Remaster at $29.99 (40% off the $49.99 MSRP) gives you two of the most important JRPGs ever made in one package. The original Suikoden and its sequel Suikoden II were PS1 classics that introduced the 108 Stars concept, where you recruit 108 unique characters to build your army and castle. The HD Remaster updates the visuals with crisp pixel art, widescreen support, and a gallery mode, while keeping the core gameplay identical to the originals.

These games defined what JRPGs could be. Suikoden II in particular is considered one of the greatest RPGs of all time, with a political story about war, friendship, and the cost of conflict that still resonates. The army battle system, where you command your recruited characters in large-scale tactical battles, was revolutionary in 1998 and still feels satisfying today. The castle-building mechanic, where your base grows as you recruit more characters, gives you a tangible sense of progress.

The remaster quality is solid but not transformative. These look like PS1 games running at higher resolution with cleaner text, not modern remakes. The encounter rate, typical of late-90s JRPGs, will feel high to modern players. There's no fast-forward battle option in the Switch version (the PC version has one via mods). The stories hold up, but the pacing is slow by modern standards. This is a purchase for people who love classic JRPGs or want to experience gaming history, not for someone looking for a modern RPG experience.

  • Star Wars Outlaws at $14.99. Yes, 25% off sounds like a deal. But this game launched at $69.99 and has been $19.99 or less repeatedly. The current $14.99 is barely below the typical sale price. When to Buy vs. When to Wait

If a game is at 50% off or more and it's on your wishlist, pull the trigger. Amazon's prices bounce around, and a deal at 60% off this week might be 30% off next week. The games above are all at or near their historical lows.

For newer releases under a year old, waiting usually pays off. Games that launched at $69.99 typically hit $39.99 within 6-8 months and $29.99 within a year. The exception is Nintendo first-party titles, which hold value like nothing else in gaming. Breath of the Wild still sells for $50 used. Plan accordingly.

The big sale windows on Amazon are Prime Day (typically July), Black Friday/Cyber Monday (late November), and a secondary sale in October. Between those events, individual publishers run their own promotions. Set CamelCamelCamel alerts for games you want and let the internet do the watching for you.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the best video game deal on Amazon right now?

Elden Ring on PS5 at $19.99 (60% off its $49.99 MSRP) is the best deal going. It's a complete, critically acclaimed action RPG that offers 80+ hours of content. At under twenty bucks, the price-per-hour of entertainment is absurd.

Are Amazon video game deals reliable or are they scams?

Amazon sells games both directly and through third-party sellers. Deals sold by Amazon.com (not a marketplace seller) are legitimate retail copies. Check the seller name before buying. If it says "Sold by Amazon.com," you're getting a genuine product. Third-party sellers are usually fine too, but read the listing carefully to make sure you're getting the right platform and edition.

Should I buy physical or digital game copies?

Physical copies have two real advantages: you can resell them when you're done, and Amazon's sale prices on physical discs are often lower than PlayStation Store or Nintendo eShop digital sales. The downside is shipping time and shelf space. If you want to play tonight, go digital. If you want the best price and the option to resell, go physical.

How often do Amazon game prices drop?

Major sales happen around Prime Day (July), Black Friday (November), and holiday season (December). Between those events, individual titles drop to near-historic lows unpredictably. A game at its lowest price ever one week might bounce back to full price the next. If you see a deal at 50% off or more, grab it. Those prices don't stick around.

Are older PS5 games like Returnal still worth buying in 2026?

Returnal at $29.99 is absolutely worth it. The gameplay holds up, the DualSense haptics are still among the best on the platform, and the roguelike loop offers more replay value than most $70 titles. Age doesn't diminish a good game.

What Switch games actually go on sale on Amazon?

Third-party Switch games drop regularly. Titles like Balatro, Unicorn Overlord, and Suikoden I & II HD Remaster see 40-60% discounts. First-party Nintendo games (Mario, Zelda) rarely drop more than $10-15 below MSRP, even during Prime Day. If you want Nintendo first-party titles cheap, your best bet is buying used or waiting for Black Friday bundles.
