Chapter 16: The Setup

Chapter 16: The Setup

The trap was elegant. Drevik had to give them that much.

He'd followed Mira for three days, tracking her movements through the compound, noting the way she seemed to slip into restricted areas with the casual ease of someone who belonged there. She was good. Too good. Every time he got close to confirming his suspicions, she'd vanish into a shadow corridor or get called away by some convenient emergency.

But today, she'd made a mistake. Or what looked like one.

The door to the archive chamber stood slightly ajar, light spilling through the gap in a way that violated every security protocol the Order maintained. Drevik pressed himself against the corridor wall, listening. Silence. No footsteps, no whispered incantations, nothing.

He should have known then. He should have recognized the perfection of it—the way everything aligned to draw him forward, the absence of witnesses, the timing that coincided with the Elder council's monthly recess.

Instead, he pushed through the door.

The archive chamber stretched before him, rows of crystalline tablets and bound manuscripts glowing faintly with protective wards. At the center, a reading pedestal held an open tome, its pages illuminated by a focused beam of lunar light from the ceiling. Shadow Communication Protocols, the spine read. Restricted. Eyes Only.

And scattered across the reading table, as if left in haste, were pages of transcribed symbols. Shadowlands entity codes. Contact frequencies. The kind of information that could compromise the entire Order's defensive network.

Drevik's stomach dropped. He stepped forward, reaching to examine the pages more closely—

"Step away from the materials, Acolyte."

The voice came from everywhere at once. Elder Thornwood's voice. Lexi's father.

Drevik froze, his hand hovering inches from the incriminating documents. Slowly, he turned. The chamber's shadows had resolved into figures—three Elders flanking the entrance, their faces grim, their hands already weaving containment wards.

"Elder Thornwood." Drevik kept his voice steady, though his heart hammered against his ribs. "I can explain."

"You can explain to the council." Thornwood's green eyes—so like Lexi's, but colder, harder—bored into him. "You can explain why you're standing over classified materials that detail our entire communication network with the Shadowlands border patrols. You can explain why these documents have been photographed and transmitted to an unknown recipient."

"Photographed?" Drevik's mind raced. "I haven't—"

"Silence." Elder Voss, a sharp-featured woman with silver-streaked hair, stepped forward. Her fingers moved in a complex pattern, and Drevik felt the Oath tattoo on his chest begin to warm. "The binding will determine truth. Resist, and the pain will be... considerable."

The warmth became heat. Drevik gritted his teeth as the Oath's magic activated, probing his memories, his intentions, his very thoughts. It was invasive in a way he'd never experienced—like fingers rifling through the pages of his mind.

"What is your purpose in this chamber, Acolyte Drevik Markinsoul?"

The question burned through him, demanding answer. "I was investigating a suspected traitor. I followed Mira Vance to this location."

Eyes narrowed. Elder Thornwood exchanged glances with Voss.

"Mira Vance," Voss repeated. "The same acolyte who reported suspicious behavior from you three days ago? Who provided testimony that you'd been asking questions about Shadowlands communication protocols?"

The heat intensified. Drevik felt sweat beading on his forehead. "She's lying. She's the one—I saw her accessing restricted areas. I have witnesses who can—"

"Your witnesses." Thornwood's voice cut like a blade. "Would that include the unbound civilian you've been consorting with? The exotic dancer with no connection to our Order? Or perhaps the Thornwood daughter you've been... distracting from her duties?"

The Oath seared. Drevik bit back a cry of pain.

"Answer the question, acolyte." Voss's voice was clinical, detached. "Who are your sources? Who told you to investigate Mira Vance?"

Ivy. Seraphina. Lexi, who'd noticed discrepancies in the patrol reports.

Names rose to his lips, and the Oath pushed them forward, demanding release. But Drevik clamped his jaw shut. If he named them now, without proof, without preparation, they'd be dragged into this. Ivy, with no protection from the Order's justice. Seraphina, whose position as an archivist would crumble. And Lexi—

He couldn't. He wouldn't.

The pain redoubled. Drevik dropped to one knee, gasping.

"You refuse to name your sources." Thornwood's voice held something that might have been disappointment. Or might have been satisfaction. "You stand in a restricted chamber, caught with transmitted classified documents, and you protect those who aided you. The evidence is clear, Acolyte Markinsoul. You've been feeding information to the Shadowlands entities. The question is only—why?"

"No." The word came out strangled. "I would never—I've given everything to this Order."

"Then prove it. Name your accomplices."

Drevik looked up, meeting Thornwood's eyes through the haze of pain. "I work alone."

It was a lie, and the Oath punished him for it. Fire raced through his veins, and he doubled over, hands pressing against the cold stone floor. But he held. Somehow, he held.

"Enough." A new voice—Elder Kade, the oldest of the council, his face weathered by centuries of service. He stepped forward, and his touch cooled the burning in Drevik's chest. "The boy's strong. Stronger than he should be. Killing him with the Oath won't get us answers."

"I'm not trying to kill him," Thornwood said coldly. "I'm trying to get the truth."

"The truth is complicated." Kade's ancient eyes studied Drevik with unsettling intensity. "Take him to the holding cells. Let him consider his position. Perhaps isolation will loosen his tongue."

Hands grabbed Drevik's arms, hauling him upright. He stumbled between two wardens—faces he didn't recognize, their expressions professionally blank—and they dragged him from the chamber.

The corridors blurred past. Drevik's mind churned through possibilities, looking for escape, for explanation, for anything that could change what was happening. But the Oath still throbbed against his chest, and every shadow seemed to press in on him, watching, waiting.

They threw him into a cell.

It was small—barely ten feet square—with stone walls that drank the light and a floor that chilled through his boots. The door slammed shut behind him, and he heard wards activating, sealing him in with magic as much as iron.

Drevik slumped against the far wall and let the trembling take him.

Framed. Someone had framed him.

The realization crystallized with horrible clarity. Mira had been setting him up from the beginning. The suspicious behavior, the trail he'd been meant to follow, the conveniently open door. She'd known he'd investigate. She'd known he'd walk right into it.

And now he was trapped.

Time passed strangely in the cell. Without windows, without any connection to the outside world, Drevik had only the growing ache in his chest and the racing of his own thoughts. Hours might have passed. Or minutes. Or days.

He dozed at some point, drifting in and out of uneasy dreams. His grandmother's face swam before him, disappointment in her eyes. His parents—faces he'd never known—hovered at the edges of his vision, always just out of reach.

When the door opened, he jerked awake, heart pounding.

But it wasn't the Elders. It wasn't wardens come to drag him to judgment.

It was Lexi.

She slipped through the door quickly, closing it behind her, and for a moment she just stood there, looking at him. Her auburn hair was pulled back severely, her uniform crisp and proper, but her eyes—

Her eyes were red-rimmed. She'd been crying.

"You idiot." The words came out thick, unsteady. "You absolute idiot."

Drevik pushed himself to his feet, swaying slightly. "Lexi. You shouldn't be here."

"Shouldn't be—" She crossed the cell in three quick strides and grabbed his collar, yanking him close. "My father is calling for your expulsion. For your unmaking. And you're telling me I shouldn't be here?"

"Your father." Drevik's voice cracked. "He's one of the ones who—"

"He's doing his duty." Lexi's grip tightened. "The evidence, Drevik. The photographs. They show transmissions from your personal terminal to an encrypted address in the Shadowlands. They show access logs with your credentials entering restricted archives a dozen times over the past month."

"Those are forged."

"Of course they're forged!" She released him, stepping back, running a hand through her hair in frustration. "I know they're forged. I know you. You'd never betray the Order—you'd never betray me."

The conviction in her voice cut through the fog of despair. Drevik stared at her, seeing the fear beneath the anger, the love beneath the frustration.

"Then help me," he said quietly. "Tell them—"

"I tried." Lexi's voice dropped. "I stood before the council and told them you were with me the night of the supposed transmissions. I told them your credentials must have been stolen. They said—" She swallowed. "They said I was compromised. That you'd seduced me into covering for you."

Seduced. The word hung between them, weighted with everything that had passed in her apartment, everything they'd shared.

"Lexi." Drevik reached for her, then stopped, his hand hovering in the air. "If you keep defending me, they'll—"

"I don't care." She stepped into his space, catching his hand and pressing it to her cheek. Her skin was warm, damp with tears she'd clearly been fighting. "I don't care what they do to me. I'm not losing you."

"You could lose everything." His thumb traced the line of her jaw, feeling the tension in her muscles. "Your position. Your family. Your—"

"My family." Lexi laughed bitterly. "My father looked at me like I was a stranger. Like everything I've worked for, everything I've sacrificed, meant nothing compared to my feelings for you." Her eyes met his, fierce and bright. "He's wrong. You mean more."

Drevik's chest tightened—not the Oath this time, but something deeper. Something that had been growing since that first sparring match, since the first time she'd looked at him and seen something other than a threat.

"I need you to leave." The words scraped against his throat. "You need to distance yourself from me. If you're there when they question me again, they'll—"

"I'm not leaving." Lexi's hands found his chest, pressing flat over his heart. "I don't care what they say. I don't care what evidence they've manufactured. I know you. And I know you'd rather die than betray this Order."

"I would." Drevik covered her hands with his own. "But I can't prove that. I can't explain my sources without dragging—"

"Ivy." Lexi's voice softened. "Seraphina. I know."

Drevik stilled. "How—"

"I'm not an idiot." A hint of her usual fire returned to her eyes. "I've seen the way you look at them. The way they look at you. And I know they've been helping you investigate."

"If your father finds out—"

"My father already suspects." She stepped closer, eliminating the last distance between them. "He asked me directly if you were involved with the unbound girl. I told him she was a friend from your college classes. That you helped her with her studies." A bitter smile. "He didn't believe me."

Drevik closed his eyes, feeling the warmth of her body against his, the steadiness of her presence. In this cold cell, with his world crumbling around him, she was the only solid thing.

"Lexi." Her name came out like a prayer. "I'm so sorry."

"For what?" Her fingers curled into his shirt. "For being targeted? For fighting for the truth?"

"For dragging you into this. For making you choose."

"Choose?" She laughed softly, and the sound held no bitterness this time—only wonder. "Drevik, I chose you the moment I realized you weren't what I expected. Everything since then has just been... confirming what I already knew."

He opened his eyes. Her face was inches from his, her green eyes bright with unshed tears, her lips parted slightly. She was beautiful in a way that made his chest ache—not just her features, but the strength in them, the courage.

"I don't deserve you," he said.

"Probably not." Her smile was tremulous. "But you're stuck with me anyway."

She kissed him.

It wasn't like the first time—urgent, desperate, a claiming. This kiss was slower, deeper, weighted with everything they couldn't say. Her lips moved against his with a tenderness that broke something open in his chest. Drevik's hands found her waist, pulling her closer, and she melted against him, her body fitting into his like she belonged there.

For a long moment, the cell, the accusations, the trap—all of it faded. There was only Lexi. The scent of her hair, something floral and clean. The warmth of her body pressed against his. The soft sounds she made as the kiss deepened, her fingers threading through his hair.

When she finally pulled back, her breathing was unsteady. Her forehead rested against his, her eyes still closed.

"I'm scared," she whispered. "I've never been scared of anything in my life. But the thought of losing you—"

"You won't." Drevik's voice was rough. "I'll find a way out of this."

"The evidence—"

"Is forged. Which means there's a trail. I just need to find it."

Lexi's eyes opened. "The council is meeting tomorrow morning. They'll decide your fate then." Her hands slid to his chest, pressing flat over his heart. "My father is pushing for unmaking. Elder Kade wants to wait. But if they vote to strip your magic—"

"They won't."

"Drevik—"

"I said they won't." He cupped her face, tilting it up to meet his gaze. "I didn't survive twenty-one years alone just to be taken down by a frame job. I didn't find this Order, find you, just to lose it all because Mira Vance is better at politics than I am."

Lexi's lips curved. "That's the Drevik I know."

"Get out of here." He pressed a kiss to her forehead. "Before someone notices you're gone."

"I don't care if—"

"I know." He smiled, and it felt strange on his face after so many hours of despair. "But I need you outside. I need someone who believes me on the other side of these walls."

She studied him for a long moment, then nodded. Stepped back. Straightened her uniform.

"I'll find something." Her voice was fierce. "There's a hole in their evidence somewhere. I just need to find it."

"Be careful."

"Always." She moved to the door, then paused, looking back. "Drevik?"

"Yes?"

"I love you."

The words hung in the air, simple and devastating. She was gone before he could respond, the door sealing shut behind her with a whisper of wards.

Drevik slid down the wall, his legs suddenly unable to support him. His hand pressed against his chest, over the spot where the Oath still throbbed faintly.

She loved him.

The thought should have brought joy. Instead, it brought terror. Because if she loved him, she would put herself in danger to save him. She would sacrifice everything she'd worked for, everything she'd built, on the chance that she could prove his innocence.

And if she failed, they would burn for it together.

The next hours were the longest of Drevik's life.

He paced. He sat. He tried to meditate, to access the strange future-sight that had manifested during his testing, but the wards dampened his magic, leaving him frustratingly mortal. He examined the cell, looking for weakness, but the stone was seamless and the wards were flawless.

He thought about Lexi's confession. About the way she'd said it—matter-of-fact, almost defiant, as if daring him to reject her. As if there was any universe where he could.

He thought about Ivy, alone in the outside world, probably hearing rumors about his arrest. About Seraphina, in the archives, surrounded by the very documents that had been used to frame him.

He thought about his parents. About the mystery that had drawn him to the Order in the first place. About the journal his father had left, hidden somewhere in this compound, waiting to be found.

The journal.

Drevik straightened, a thought forming. His father had been a member of the Order—his mother too, though he'd only learned that recently. They'd left him clues, breadcrumbs leading to the truth of their disappearance. What if—

The door opened.

Drevik rose, expecting wardens, expecting Elders. Instead, a small figure slipped through—barely five feet tall, with close-cropped dark hair and a round face that looked far too innocent for someone who moved like a shadow.

"Ivy?"

"Shh." She pressed a finger to her lips, her eyes darting around the cell. "I've got thirty seconds before the ward rotation. Listen carefully."

"How did you—"

"Doesn't matter." She crossed to him, pulling something from her jacket—a small folded paper. "Lexi told me what happened. She got word to me through Seraphina."

"Ivy, you can't be here. If they catch you—"

"They won't." Her voice was steady, but her hands trembled slightly as she pressed the paper into his palm. "I've been doing my own investigating. Through non-Order channels. And I found something."

Drevik unfolded the paper. It was a copy of a journal page, written in a cramped, hurried hand he didn't recognize. But the signature at the bottom—

"Your father's journal," Ivy said. "I couldn't get the whole thing—it's warded too heavily. But I found fragments. Copies that someone made before the original was sealed."

Drevik's eyes scanned the page. Dates. Coordinates. And a series of symbols that made no sense—until he looked at them from the corner of his eye, letting his Veil Sight activate.

The symbols shifted, resolved into words.

The cipher is in the last five entries. The traitor's name is hidden there. Trust only those who bear the mark of the eclipse.

"I don't understand," Drevik said. "Where is the original journal?"

"Archives. Restricted section. The same section you were supposedly caught stealing from." Ivy's voice was grim. "Which means whoever framed you knows about the journal. Knows it could clear your name."

"Or incriminate them."

"Exactly." She squeezed his arm. "The council meets in six hours. If you can get to that journal, find the cipher, decode those last five entries—you might have a chance."

"I'm locked in a cell, Ivy."

"Lexi's working on it." Ivy's eyes met his, filled with a determination that surprised him. "She's not going to let you die in here, Drevik. None of us are."

"Ivy—"

"Save it." She stepped back, moving toward the door. "We all chose to be part of this. Whatever happens next, we chose it."

She was gone before he could respond, slipping through the door just as the wards flickered through their rotation.

Drevik was alone again.

But now he had a direction. Now he had a purpose.

The journal. His father's last five entries. A cipher that could prove his innocence.

He just had to figure out how to escape an inescapable cell, break into the most restricted section of the archives, and find a document that someone had already killed to protect.

Drevik looked at the paper in his hand, at his father's signature, at the hidden message that shifted beneath the ink.

The traitor's name is hidden there.

He thought of Mira, of her too-innocent smile, of the way she'd anticipated his every move. Of the evidence that had appeared like magic, perfectly placed to destroy him.

Someone in the Order was working with the Shadowlands entities. Someone had killed his parents to protect their secret. And now that same someone was trying to silence him.

Drevik folded the paper carefully and slipped it into his boot.

Six hours.

He had six hours to prove his innocence, expose a traitor, and stay alive long enough to tell Lexi that he loved her too.

The trap had been elegant. But they'd made one mistake.

They'd left him alive.

And Drevik Markinsoul was done being a victim.
