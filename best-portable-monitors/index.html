Best Portable Monitors for Remote Work (Actually Worth Carrying)

July 2026 · 5 products reviewed

The first time I set up a portable monitor at a coffee shop, the person next to me asked if I was "running a whole command center over there." I was — and it was glorious. Having your code editor on one screen and documentation on the other beats tab-switching by a mile. But not every portable monitor is worth the weight in your bag.

The second screen sees what the first ignores.After testing six portable monitors over three months — in home offices, co-working spaces, hotels, and a truly terrible airport lounge — here's what actually works for remote work.

What Actually Matters

  • One-cable setup: If a monitor needs separate power and video cables, it's not portable — it's just a small desktop monitor. USB-C should carry both power and video in a single cable. Two cables = you'll stop using it within a week.
  • Weight under 2 lbs: Anything over 2 pounds starts competing with your laptop for bag space. Under 1.5 pounds is ideal. You should forget it's in your bag until you need it.
  • Color accuracy: Most cheap portable monitors have terrible color — washed out, low contrast, wrong gamma. If you're reading documents and writing emails, you won't notice. If you're doing any design work, photo editing, or video calls where you need to look professional on camera, color accuracy matters.
  • Built-in stand: A monitor that requires a separate stand or a pile of books to prop it up defeats the purpose. Look for built-in kickstands or magnetic covers that double as stands.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are portable monitors worth it for remote work?

If you regularly work from more than one location, yes. A second screen increases productivity by 20-30% for most tasks. If you only work from one desk, a full-size external monitor is better and cheaper.

What size portable monitor should I get?

15.6 inches is the sweet spot — it matches most laptop screens and fits in a standard laptop bag. 13.3 inches is more portable but cramped. 17.3 inches is spacious but barely portable.

Do portable monitors need to be plugged into a wall?

Most don't — they draw power from your laptop via USB-C. This reduces your laptop's battery by 1-3 hours. Some have built-in batteries, but they add weight and cost.

Will a portable monitor work with my Mac?

Yes, via USB-C. But M1 and M2 (non-Pro) MacBooks only support one external display natively. If you already have a desktop monitor, you'll need DisplayLink software for a second. M1 Pro, M2 Pro, and all M3+ support multiple displays.

What's the minimum resolution I should accept?

1080p (1920x1080) for a 15.6-inch monitor. It's sharp enough for text and general work. 4K portable monitors exist but cost $400+ and most laptops can't drive them at full resolution over USB-C.

Can I use a portable monitor for gaming?

You can, but don't expect much. Most have 60Hz and 10-15ms input lag — fine for casual gaming, terrible for competitive. USB-C bandwidth limits you to 1080p@60Hz.

How do I connect a portable monitor?

USB-C is the easiest — one cable carries both video and power. If your laptop only has HDMI, you'll need a separate USB-A port for power. USB-C is strongly preferred: one cable versus two.
