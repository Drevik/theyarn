Chapter 20: Confrontation

Chapter 20: Confrontation

The chamber beneath the university had never felt so small.

Drevik stood at the center of the circular room, stone walls pressing inward like ribs around a heart. Above him, the vaulted ceiling disappeared into shadow, but it was the semicircle of elevated seats that made his chest tight. Seven thrones carved from obsidian, and six of them occupied by figures whose faces he couldn't quite see—features blurred by the same veil of power that had hidden the Order's true nature for centuries.

The seventh seat stood empty.

His hands remained steady at his sides, though every instinct screamed to reach for the shadows coiling in his chest. He'd dressed carefully for this: dark shirt, no jewelry, nothing that could be construed as a challenge. But everything about his presence here was a challenge, and they all knew it.

"Drevik Markinsoul." The voice came from the center throne, a woman's voice like wind through dead leaves. "You stand before the Council of Elders accused of harboring forbidden knowledge, consorting with entities beyond the Veil, and willfully undermining the authority of this Order."

He kept his eyes forward, not bowing. Not quite.

"I stand here because you summoned me," he said. "But we all know the real reason isn't what's written in your charges."

A rustle of robes from his left. One of the Elders leaned forward, and he caught the glint of eyes behind the obscuring veil.

"You speak boldly for an acolyte who hasn't earned the right to address this Council."

"I speak because staying silent hasn't worked." Drevik let his gaze drift across the assembled Elders, counting the ones who shifted, the ones who remained still. "Three members of this Order are dead. The Shadowlands are bleeding through in places they shouldn't. And every time I've tried to report what I've discovered, I've been dismissed, redirected, or threatened."

"Threatened?" The woman in the center throne—Elder Thornwood, Lexi's grandmother, though she'd never acknowledged that connection in front of him—tilted her head. "You mistake guidance for threat, acolyte. A mistake born of youth and arrogance."

"Then let's talk about guidance." Drevik's voice hardened. "Like being guided to investigate a chapter that had already been cleansed of evidence. Being guided away from the Shadowland breaches that didn't match the official narrative. Being guided to believe that Mira's chapter had gone rogue when they were actually trying to expose what you've been doing."

The silence stretched like a held breath.

Behind him, the heavy wooden doors groaned open. He didn't turn—couldn't, not without showing how badly he wanted to see who had entered. But the familiar rhythm of footsteps, one set quick and determined, another measured and deliberate, told him everything.

Lexi's voice cut through the chamber like a blade.

"He's right."

The words landed in the quiet like stones dropped into still water. Elder Thornwood's veiled head turned toward the entrance, and Drevik finally allowed himself to look.

Lexi stood in the doorway, shoulders squared, her auburn hair pulled back in a severe knot that made her features look sharper. Beside her, Ivy's silver-white hair caught the candlelight, her expression unreadable but her presence a statement in itself.

"Alexa Thornwood." The Elder's voice remained flat, controlled. "This Council did not summon you."

"No." Lexi's chin lifted. "You summoned Drevik to stand trial for crimes he didn't commit while the real criminals sit on their thrones and pretend the Veil isn't tearing apart."

"Careful, granddaughter." The word dripped with ice. "Blood may grant you entrance to this chamber, but it doesn't grant you immunity from consequence."

Drevik watched the exchange with his chest tight. He'd known Lexi would come—had seen it in one of the fragmentary futures that flickered at the edges of his consciousness—but knowing hadn't prepared him for the reality of her standing against her own blood.

"I've spent my entire life being careful." Lexi stepped forward, moving to stand at Drevik's side. Not behind him. Beside him. "Careful not to embarrass the family. Careful not to ask questions. Careful not to see what was right in front of me." Her hand found Drevik's, fingers interlacing with a grip that felt like an anchor. "I'm done being careful. I'm choosing to be honest instead."

Elder Thornwood rose from her throne.

The temperature in the chamber dropped. Shadows gathered in the corners, drawn by power older than the university above them. Drevik felt the pressure against his chest, the weight of authority that the Elders wielded like a weapon.

"You defy this Council?"

"I defy corruption." Lexi's voice didn't waver. "I defy lies. I defy whatever you've been doing to the Veil that's causing breaches in locations that should be protected." Her eyes found her grandmother's veiled face. "I'm not the only one."

The doors opened again.

Mira entered first, her dark skin luminous in the candlelight, the sigils of her chapter still visible on her collarbones. Behind her came others—acolytes and seekers that Drevik recognized from late-night gatherings, from whispered conversations, from the faction that had been building since he'd first started asking the wrong questions.

Ivy moved to Drevik's other side, her shoulder brushing his arm as she positioned herself. When she spoke, her voice carried the precise diction that made every word feel calculated.

"I have documentation."

Every eye in the room fixed on her.

She produced a leather satchel from beneath her cloak, extracting papers that had been folded and refolded, their edges soft with handling. "Correspondence between Elder Vance and the chapter in Prague. Financial records showing transfers to accounts that don't exist in any mortal banking system. And testimony"—she paused, letting the word settle—"from three seekers who witnessed the deliberate weakening of protective wards in the eastern quadrant."

The Elders stirred. One of them, a man whose throne sat at the far end, spoke for the first time.

"Where did you obtain these documents?"

"Where I always obtain information." Ivy's smile held no warmth. "From the places you forgot to look. From the people you dismissed as unimportant. From the shadows you thought belonged only to you."

Drevik felt a surge of something that wasn't quite pride, wasn't quite fear. Ivy had been gathering evidence for weeks, working in silence while he'd been playing the visible role. The trust she'd shown by bringing this forward now—

It was more than strategy. It was faith.

Elder Thornwood descended from her throne, each step deliberate, the shadows parting before her like curtains. She stopped an arm's length from Drevik, and for the first time, he could see past the veil just enough to make out the lines around her eyes, the silver hair pulled back in a style that mirrored Lexi's.

"You've assembled quite a following." Her voice had lost its otherworldly echo, becoming something almost human. "Tell me, acolyte, what exactly you believe this will accomplish?"

"I believe," Drevik said carefully, "that the Order has been compromised from within. Not by Shadowland entities, but by people who should be protecting the Veil. I believe you've been using breaches as weapons, controlling where the shadows bleed through to eliminate enemies and consolidate power." He met her gaze through the veil. "And I believe you brought me here because I can see futures—possible paths that others can't—and that scares you."

The silence that followed pressed against his ears.

"You don't know what you're meddling with." Elder Thornwood's voice had dropped to barely a whisper. "The Shadowlands are evolving. Changing. What we've done has been necessary to—"

"Necessary?" Mira's voice cracked through the chamber. "My entire chapter was slaughtered because they discovered what you were doing with the breach in the catacombs. Don't talk to me about necessary."

Another Elder spoke from the far end—Elder Vance, Drevik remembered, the one whose handwriting appeared on Ivy's documents. "The Prague chapter was compromised. They had to be contained before their reckless experiments destabilized the entire Veil."

"Contained." Mira's hand went to the hilt of the blade at her hip. "You mean murdered. You mean sixteen seekers and acolytes who trusted the Order, slaughtered and then blamed on 'rogue elements' so no one would ask questions."

"The truth of the Veil requires sacrifice." Elder Vance's veil rippled with what might have been a shrug. "You cannot comprehend the pressures we face, the decisions that must be made—"

"Then help us comprehend." Drevik stepped forward, breaking the invisible line that separated accused from accuser. "Show us what you're protecting. Let us see the full scope of the threat so we can help you fight it instead of standing here debating whether murder counts as sacrifice."

Elder Thornwood's head turned slowly.

"You would trust us? After everything you've discovered?"

"I would trust the truth." Drevik spread his hands. "Whatever that truth is. But I won't trust orders that come wrapped in lies, and I won't follow leaders who treat members as disposable resources."

The chamber seemed to hold its breath.

Then Elder Thornwood did something that rippled through the assembled watchers like a shockwave. She reached up and pulled away her veil.

The face beneath was older than Drevik had expected, weathered by decades of struggle he couldn't imagine. Her eyes were the same green as Lexi's, but where Lexi's sparked with defiance, these held a weariness that seemed to go down to the bone.

"You want truth?" She spoke to the room, but her eyes stayed on Drevik. "Then truth you shall have. But understand—the truth I offer you today cannot be unheard. Once you know what we protect, you become responsible for protecting it as well."

She turned to face the other Elders.

"Brothers. Sisters. The time for secrecy among our own has passed." Her voice carried an edge that hadn't been there before. "We either bring this acolyte and his faction into the full light of our purpose, or we destroy them here and now and hope the shadows take their secrets before they can spread."

Drevik felt Lexi's hand tighten on his.

Elder Vance rose from his throne. "You would break centuries of tradition? Reveal our innermost workings to children who haven't even completed their training?"

"I would prevent a war within our own ranks." Elder Thornwood's voice hardened. "Something you seem determined to provoke."

"I'm preventing catastrophe." Vance's shadows gathered around him, dark and thick. "These children have already meddled with forces beyond their understanding. They've communicated with entities that should never have been contacted, opened doors that should have remained sealed—"

"Because you opened them first." Drevik's voice cut through the gathering darkness. "The breach in the library basement. You didn't contain it—you created it. You've been pulling Shadowland entities through intentionally, testing them, trying to weaponize them."

The shadows around Vance froze.

"Where did you hear this?"

"I didn't hear it." Drevik let his own shadows rise, feeling them respond to his will in a way they never had for other acolytes. "I saw it. One of the possible futures that I'm still learning to read. You've been experimenting on sentient beings, Elder Vance. Treating entities that have thoughts and feelings and families as tools to be used and discarded."

"They are monsters." The word came out twisted, ugly. "Creatures of shadow and malice that would devour our world if given the chance."

"Would they?" Drevik stepped closer, and he felt something shift in the air—a recognition from the shadows themselves. "Or have we been fighting a war that was started by the very people supposed to prevent it?"

Vance's hand shot up, shadows coalescing around his fingers in a pattern Drevik recognized as a binding. But before he could complete the gesture, Elder Thornwood moved.

She was faster than her age suggested, shadows lashing out to intercept shadows, the collision sending a shockwave through the chamber that knocked several acolytes off their feet. Drevik grabbed Lexi, steadying her as the floor shook.

"Enough!" Elder Thornwood's voice filled the room with authority that went beyond the physical. "This Council has debated in shadow long enough. I call for a vote—here, now, before witnesses. Do we continue down the path of secrets and manipulation, or do we embrace a new way? Do we trust our members with the truth, or do we rule through fear?"

One by one, the Elders turned to face each other.

Drevik watched the invisible lines shift, alliances forming and breaking in the silence. Beside him, Ivy leaned close.

"Three will support Thornwood," she whispered. "Two will follow Vance. One is undecided."

"Which means—"

"Whoever controls the undecided controls the Order."

He found the uncertain Elder at the end of the row—an older man whose face remained hidden, whose body language screamed discomfort. Elder Zhao, if he remembered correctly. The one who'd always taught history with a focus on the costs of secrecy.

Drevik moved before he could second-guess himself.

He crossed to Elder Zhao's throne, aware of every eye tracking his movement, aware of Lexi positioning herself to intercept if Vance tried anything. He stopped at the base of the older man's seat and looked up.

"Elder Zhao. You taught my introductory history course. You spoke about the Purification of 1847, when the Order nearly destroyed itself from within because different factions couldn't trust each other with the truth."

The Elder didn't respond.

"You said that the worst atrocities in our history came from leaders who believed they were protecting their followers by keeping them ignorant." Drevik's voice dropped. "Is this that moment? Are we standing at the edge of another Purification?"

Slowly, Elder Zhao reached up and lowered his veil.

His face was weathered, tired, but his eyes—his eyes held something Drevik recognized from his own mirror. The desperate desire to do the right thing, tangled with the fear of making things worse.

"I remember your paper on the Shadowland treaties," Zhao said quietly. "You argued that the old agreements weren't failures—they were simply incomplete. That entities we call monsters might have been trying to communicate rather than invade."

"I still believe that."

"Then you understand why I've remained silent." Zhao's gaze drifted to where Vance stood surrounded by his shadows. "When you see something wrong, but speaking would tear apart the only organization keeping the world safe from what lies beyond... you learn to live with compromise."

"Compromise is different from complicity." Drevik kept his voice low. "There's a point where staying silent becomes the same as supporting the thing you hate. The question is whether you've reached that point."

Zhao was quiet for a long moment.

Then he stood.

"I cast my vote with Elder Thornwood," he said, his voice carrying through the chamber. "And I further move that Elder Vance be suspended from Council duties pending investigation of the allegations presented."

The chaos that followed was immediate.

Vance's shadows exploded outward, but Elder Thornwood was faster, her power slamming into his like waves against a cliff face. Drevik grabbed Lexi and dove behind one of the stone pillars, pulling Ivy with them. Around them, the chamber erupted into motion—acolytes scattering, seekers drawing weapons, the air itself seeming to tear apart with the force of the Elder's clash.

"Stay down!" Lexi shouted, but Drevik was already moving.

His shadows weren't as strong as the Elders', but he'd learned things in the past months that they hadn't. He'd learned to listen to the darkness rather than forcing it to obey. And right now, the darkness was angry.

He reached for the shadows gathering around Vance's feet—not to control them, but to understand them. And what he found there made his blood run cold.

The shadows weren't natural. They'd been bound, twisted, forced into service through methods that left them screaming at the edges of his perception. These weren't the cooperative shadows he'd learned to work with—they were slaves.

"Stop!" He launched himself forward, placing himself between the fighting Elders. "Both of you, stop!"

A bolt of shadow struck him in the chest.

He felt it like ice spreading through his ribs, cold that burned, darkness that tried to take hold. But his own shadows rose to meet it, wrapping around the intrusion and—instead of fighting—simply absorbing it.

The chamber went silent.

Drevik stood in the center of the chaos, shadows wreathing his arms, his chest, his face. He could feel them like extensions of his own will, feel the bound shadows Vance had enslaved reaching toward him with something that might have been hope.

"You've been torturing them." His voice came out strange, layered with echoes. "Binding them against their will. Using pain to force obedience."

Vance's face contorted. "They are tools—"

"They are living beings." Drevik felt the shadows pulse with agreement. "And you've been treating them like property."

He reached out—not with power, but with intention. The shadows surrounding Vance hesitated, then began to drift away, moving toward Drevik like iron filings to a magnet. Not because he forced them. Because they wanted to.

Vance staggered as his shadows abandoned him, the sudden loss leaving him pale and suddenly ordinary.

Elder Thornwood lowered her hand, the fight going out of her posture. Around them, the other Elders watched with expressions ranging from horror to wonder.

"What are you?" Vance whispered.

"I'm an acolyte who listens." Drevik let the shadows settle around him like a cloak. "Who asks before he takes. Who understands that the power we wield comes from somewhere, and that somewhere deserves respect."

He turned to face the Council.

"I came here to confront you about your lies. But I'm starting to think the bigger problem is that you've forgotten what we're supposed to be." His voice steadied. "The Order of the Eclipsed Veil doesn't exist to control the Shadowlands. We exist to maintain the balance. To protect both worlds from those who would exploit either one."

Elder Thornwood studied him with an expression he couldn't read.

"You speak of balance while standing in the center of our sacred chamber, wielding power that should be beyond any acolyte, surrounded by shadows that answer to you rather than any Elder." Her voice was neutral, but something flickered in her eyes. "What would you have us do, Drevik Markinsoul? The threats we face are real. The sacrifices we've made have kept this world safe for centuries."

"Then let us help you face them." He gestured to Lexi, to Ivy, to Mira and the others who had gathered behind him. "We're not children. We're not resources to be managed. We're members of this Order who deserve to know what we're fighting for."

The silence stretched.

Then Elder Thornwood did something that made every person in the chamber freeze.

She knelt.

Not to Drevik—to the shadows that surrounded him, the ones that had been bound and were now free, the ones that had been used and were now heard. She knelt to them in acknowledgment of what had been done wrong.

"Your words will be taken to the full Council," she said, rising. "All of them. Elder Vance will face judgment for his actions, and the truth of our purpose will be shared with every member who wishes to hear it." Her eyes found Drevik's. "But this does not end here. The threats we face are real, and now you've made yourself visible to them. The entities that seek to breach the Veil will know your name. The powers that move in shadow will see you as either an obstacle or an opportunity."

"I know."

"And you still choose to stand in the light?"

Drevik looked at Lexi, her hand finding his again. At Ivy, whose careful mask had cracked to show something vulnerable underneath. At Mira, who had crossed an ocean and lost everything to stand in this chamber.

"I choose to stand with them," he said. "Whatever light or shadow that means."

Elder Thornwood nodded slowly. Then she turned to Elder Zhao, who had moved to stand beside her, the lines of alliance clearly drawn.

"The Council is dissolved for tonight," she announced. "Elder Vance will be confined to quarters. All evidence presented will be examined by a tribunal of neutral parties. And you"—she fixed her gaze on Drevik—"will return tomorrow. There is much to discuss about your future in this Order."

The dismissal was clear. Drevik felt the tension drain from his shoulders as the chamber began to empty, acolytes and seekers filing out in whispers and glances. Lexi stayed at his side, her grip on his hand almost painful.

"That was insane," she muttered as they moved toward the doors. "You stood between two Elders and absorbed shadow energy that should have killed you."

"Should it have?"

"Theoretical consensus says yes."

He smiled slightly. "Then I guess theory needs updating."

They emerged into the corridor that led back to the surface, the stone walls giving way to the familiar architecture of the university's basement level. Behind them, Ivy and Mira fell into step, the four of them moving together in a silence that felt heavy with unspoken words.

It was Ivy who finally broke it.

"Your grandmother just lost significant political standing," she said to Lexi. "And she's not the type to forgive easily."

"I know."

"And you publicly chose Drevik over your family's legacy."

"I know that too." Lexi's voice was steady, but Drevik felt the tremor in her hand. "I chose correctly."

They reached the stairs leading up to the main building, the late afternoon light filtering down through the windows above. Drevik stopped, turning to face the three women who had risked everything to stand with him.

"I need you to know—" he started.

"No." Lexi cut him off, stepping close enough that he could see the flecks of gold in her green eyes. "Whatever you're about to say about gratitude or obligation or how we didn't have to come—stop. We came because we chose to. Because we believe in what you're doing." Her hand came up to rest against his cheek. "Because you're worth following."

Drevik felt something shift in his chest—not shadows this time, but something warmer. Something that had been building for months and had finally found its moment.

"I've been terrified this whole time," he admitted quietly. "Every step, every confrontation, every time I saw a possible future where things went wrong. I kept waiting for someone to tell me I was making a mistake."

"You were making a mistake," Mira said from behind them. When Drevik turned, she was smiling slightly. "You should have let us help sooner. You should have trusted that we could handle the truth. You should have known that you don't have to carry everything alone."

"I'm learning."

"Good." Ivy's voice was dry. "Learn faster. We have an Order to rebuild."

The afternoon sun hit them as they emerged from the building, casting long shadows across the quad. Students walked past, oblivious to the ancient conflict that had nearly erupted beneath their feet. A group of freshmen laughed near the fountain. A professor hurried toward the science building with papers clutched to his chest.

Normal life. The world continuing while everything had changed.

"Drevik."

He turned at Elder Zhao's voice, finding the older man standing in the doorway, his veil gone entirely now, his face exposed to the fading light.

"A word before you go."

Drevik gestured for the others to continue, then moved back toward the entrance. Zhao's expression was unreadable, but there was something in his eyes—something that made Drevik's shadows stir in anticipation.

"I told you that I remembered your paper," Zhao said quietly. "What I didn't mention is that I also remember your parents."

The world seemed to stop.

"They were members of this Order," Zhao continued. "Brilliant researchers who saw things in the Shadowlands that no one else could see. They believed, as you do, that the entities beyond the Veil were capable of communication. Cooperation. They spent years building bridges, learning to understand the shadow beings that we had always treated as enemies."

Drevik's voice came out hoarse. "What happened to them?"

"They discovered something." Zhao's voice dropped further. "A truth about the Veil itself that certain parties wanted buried. They tried to bring their findings to the Council, but not every Elder was interested in the truth. Some were invested in maintaining the status quo, in keeping the Shadowlands as enemies rather than allies."

"Vance."

"Vance was among them, yes. But there were others. Others who are still on this Council, still making decisions, still hiding what they know." Zhao's hand came up to rest on Drevik's shoulder. "Your parents were killed in what was described as a research accident. But I've never believed that. And I've spent eighteen years waiting for someone who might finally uncover what they died to protect."

The words landed like stones in still water.

"Why tell me this now?"

"Because tomorrow, the Council will offer you advancement." Zhao's eyes held something that might have been hope. "They'll make you a Seeker—the youngest in Order history. They'll give you access to archives and resources and training that you've earned through today's events." He squeezed Drevik's shoulder. "But that's not why you should accept."

"Then why?"

"Because your parents left something for you. A message, hidden in the archives, that only a Seeker can access. A message that I've been guarding for eighteen years, waiting for the right moment to reveal." Zhao's voice dropped to barely a whisper. "Your parents didn't just discover a truth about the Veil. They discovered the source of the corruption that's been eating this Order from the inside. And they died to keep that knowledge from the people who would use it to destroy everything."

Drevik felt his shadows surge, felt them wrap around him in a protective embrace that he hadn't consciously summoned.

"Who killed them?"

"That's not for me to say." Zhao stepped back. "But the answer is in that message. And once you have it..." His expression hardened. "Be careful who you trust, Drevik Markinsoul. Not everyone who stands with you today will remain an ally when the truth comes out."

He turned and disappeared back into the building before Drevik could respond.

The walk across campus felt longer than usual, shadows lengthening as the sun continued its descent. Drevik's mind raced through everything he'd learned, everything that had changed, everything that still lay hidden.

Tomorrow he would become a Seeker. Tomorrow he would have access to his parents' final message. Tomorrow the real work would begin.

But tonight—

Tonight, he found Lexi waiting for him at the edge of the quad, her auburn hair catching the last of the golden light, her expression soft in a way she rarely allowed in public.

"Zhao told you something," she said. Not a question.

"He told me enough to know that this is just beginning."

"It always is." She reached out and took his hand, her fingers intertwining with his in a gesture that had become natural over months of practice. "The Order doesn't do simple. The Shadowlands don't do safe. And us..." She trailed off, something vulnerable flickering in her eyes.

"Us?"

"Us is the one thing I'm sure of." Her voice dropped. "Whatever comes next, whatever truths you uncover, whatever enemies you make—I'm with you. Not because of duty or family or Order politics. Because I choose you."

Drevik felt his heart stutter in his chest.

"I've been seeing futures," he said quietly. "Fragments of what might be. And in almost every one, you're there. Standing beside me. Fighting with me. Building something that's better than what came before."

"That's not prophecy." Lexi's smile was small but genuine. "That's intention."

He pulled her close, letting his forehead rest against hers, letting the moment stretch in the fading light. Behind them, he could sense Ivy and Mira keeping watch, giving them this small piece of privacy in a world that offered very little.

"I'm going to need all of you," he murmured. "Whatever's coming—it's bigger than I thought. Bigger than the Order. Maybe bigger than the Veil itself."

"Then we'll face it together." Lexi's hand came up to rest over his heart. "That's what this is, right? Not just a cult. Not just duty. Family. The kind you choose."

"The kind you choose," he agreed.

The sun dipped below the horizon, and the shadows came alive around them, reaching, listening, waiting for what would come next.

Drevik Markinsoul had entered the Order as an acolyte, uncertain and alone.

Tomorrow, he would rise as a Seeker.

And somewhere in the archives, a message from the dead waited to tell him why he'd been chosen for this path all along.

The shadows are listening. The Veil is thinning. And the real war is just beginning.
