Chapter 15: Reunion and Reconnaissance

Chapter 15: Reunion and Reconnaissance

The knock came three hours before midnight, sharp and deliberate.

Drevik set aside his economics textbook—the mundane coursework felt increasingly absurd against the weight of what he'd learned about the Order—and crossed to his apartment door. He checked the peephole out of habit now, a reflex born from weeks of looking over his shoulder.

Lexi stood in the hallway, arms crossed, wearing the same leather jacket she'd worn the night they'd first met. Her auburn hair was longer than he remembered, brushing her shoulders in loose waves. Her green eyes met his through the small lens with an intensity that made his chest tighten.

He opened the door.

"Surprised to see me?" Her voice carried that familiar edge of challenge, but something underneath it had shifted. Something softer.

"You disappeared for two weeks." He stepped aside to let her in. "After everything that happened with the Shadowlands breach, I thought—"

"I know what you thought." She moved past him, her shoulder brushing his arm in the narrow doorway. The contact sent electricity skittering across his skin. "You thought I was avoiding you. That I couldn't handle what happened between us."

"Was I wrong?"

She turned to face him, and the lamplight caught the shadows under her eyes. She looked tired. Worn. But her chin lifted in that stubborn way he'd come to recognize as her armor.

"I was reassigned. Temporarily." Her jaw tightened. "Elder Thorne wanted me away from you while the internal investigation played out. Someone had to take the fall for the breach, and my mother—well, let's just say she has opinions about who that should have been."

Drevik's stomach dropped. "They blamed you?"

"They tried." A ghost of a smile crossed her lips. "I have allies too. Not as many as my mother, but enough. Enough to pull strings and call in favors I've been saving for years."

He studied her face, reading the exhaustion beneath the defiance. "What kind of favors?"

"The kind that got me reassigned." She stepped closer, close enough that he could smell the cold night air clinging to her jacket, mixed with something warm and distinctly her. "As your handler. Officially. Permanently."

The words hung between them, weighted with implication.

"Lexi, I don't—"

"I know what you're going to say." She held up a hand, forestalling his protest. "You don't need a handler. You've proven yourself. You're more powerful than half the Seekers in this chapter, and everyone knows it. But that's exactly why you need someone watching your back."

"Because I'm a target."

"Because you're valuable." Her eyes softened, just slightly. "And because I wasn't going to let anyone else do it."

The admission cost her something. He could see it in the way her shoulders tensed, the way her gaze flickered to the window and back. Lexi Thornwood didn't admit weakness. Didn't ask for permission.

He wanted to kiss her. The realization hit him with the force of shadow-walking—that sudden displacement of reality, the vertigo of wanting something he shouldn't.

"Thank you," he said instead. "For fighting for this."

Something flickered in her expression—surprise, maybe, or relief that he hadn't made it harder. "Don't thank me yet. The regional gathering is tomorrow night, and you're going to need me more than you know."

The Order's regional gatherings were held in rotating locations—always beneath the veneer of mundane society. This month's host was the Blackwood family, whose ancestral estate had been converted into a "private events venue" that catered to the wealthy and oblivious.

Drevik stood in the entryway of the restored Victorian mansion, adjusting the cuffs of the suit Lexi had insisted he wear. The charcoal fabric was expensive—borrowed from another acolyte who owed her a favor—and it fit well enough to make him look like he belonged.

"You clean up nice." Lexi appeared at his elbow, and his breath caught.

She'd traded her usual leather for a deep green dress that hugged her athletic frame, leaving her shoulders bare. Her auburn hair was swept up, exposing the curve of her neck. She looked elegant. Dangerous. Utterly untouchable.

"You look incredible," he said, the words escaping before he could filter them.

Her lips curved. "Eyes forward, Acolyte. We have a job to do."

The interior of the mansion had been transformed. Candles floated in the air—actual floating candles, held aloft by subtle shadow magic—casting flickering light across the assembled crowd. Order members from six regional chapters mingled in clusters, their conversations a low hum of politics and power.

Drevik recognized the dynamics immediately. The way Elders gathered in tight circles, their body language exclusionary. The way Seekers orbited them, angling for promotion. The way acolytes like him kept to the margins, watching and learning.

"Who are we looking for?" he asked, offering his arm.

Lexi took it, her fingers curling around his bicep with a familiarity that felt dangerously natural. "Mira Blackwood. Warden rank, third in her family's hierarchy. She's been flagged by internal investigations as a potential leak."

"Flagged how?"

"Orders from the Shadowlands have been traced back to someone in her network. Not directly to her—she's too careful for that—but close enough to warrant attention." Lexi guided him through the crowd, nodding at faces Drevik didn't recognize. "She's ambitious. Charming. And if she's the traitor, she's been playing this game for years without getting caught."

"How do I approach her?"

"You don't. You let her approach you." Lexi's grip tightened on his arm. "She's competitive, remember. She'll see you as a threat—or an opportunity. Either way, she'll want to figure out which."

They moved deeper into the gathering, and Drevik felt eyes following them. Whispers at the edges of conversations. He was still new enough to be a curiosity—the acolyte who'd survived a Shadowlands breach, who'd displayed power beyond his station.

"Smile," Lexi murmured. "Look like you belong."

He smiled. It felt like wearing a mask.

The ballroom opened before them, a vast space with vaulted ceilings and crystalline chandeliers. Shadow magic shimmered at the edges of his vision—protective wards, surveillance enchantments, the subtle glow of heart bonds linking couples across the room.

"Heart bonds?" Drevik asked quietly.

"Conservative chapters love them. Ensures loyalty, tracks emotional states." Lexi's expression flickered. "My parents have one. My mother uses it to monitor my father's devotion."

The clinical way she said it made his chest ache.

They stopped near a table laden with refreshments, and Lexi pressed a glass of champagne into his hand. "Drink. You look too tense."

"I am too tense."

"Then pretend better." She leaned into him, her shoulder brushing his chest, and lowered her voice. "Eleven o'clock. Dark hair, silver dress, talking to the Elder from the Northern chapter."

Drevik glanced without turning his head. A woman stood near the far wall, her posture languid, her smile sharp. She was beautiful in a calculated way—high cheekbones, dark eyes that missed nothing, a silver gown that caught the candlelight like water.

"Mira Blackwood."

"Watch how she moves through the room. See how everyone she speaks to walks away feeling like they've won something, even when they haven't?"

He tracked her path through the crowd. The way she touched arms, leaned close, laughed at the right moments. She was collecting secrets, he realized. Every conversation was an exchange.

"She knows we're watching," Lexi said. "She's known since we walked in."

As if on cue, Mira's gaze lifted, finding Drevik across the room. Her smile widened, and she began moving toward them with the unhurried grace of a predator who knew her prey wasn't going anywhere.

"Ready?" Lexi asked.

"No."

"Perfect."

Mira Blackwood reached them in twelve steps—Drevik counted—and stopped at a distance that was intimate without being improper. Up close, she was older than she'd appeared from across the room. Late twenties, maybe. Experienced.

"Lexi Thornwood." Mira's voice was honey over glass. "I heard you'd been reassigned. Congratulations on... landing on your feet."

"Mira." Lexi's tone matched hers, sweet with an edge. "The gathering is lovely. Your family outdid themselves."

"We try." Mira's attention shifted to Drevik, and he felt the weight of her assessment. "And this must be the famous acolyte everyone's talking about. The one who walked through a Shadowlands breach and came back with more power than he started with."

"I had good teachers," Drevik said, keeping his voice neutral.

"Did you?" Mira stepped closer, and he caught her scent—jasmine and something darker, something that reminded him of the Shadowlands themselves. "I wonder if that's the whole story."

"Should it be more?"

Her eyes lit with interest. "Direct. I like that." She extended her hand, palm down, as if expecting him to kiss it. "Mira Blackwood. My family has been with the Order for six generations. We don't often see new blood rising so quickly."

Drevik took her hand and shook it, deliberately ignoring the expected gesture. Her smile flickered—annoyance or approval, he couldn't tell which.

"Drevik Markinsoul. I'm still learning the ropes."

"Modest too." She didn't release his hand. Her fingers were cool, her grip firm. "Tell me, Drevik—may I call you Drevik?—what do you think of the Order's current direction?"

"Careful," Lexi said, her voice low. "That's a loaded question."

"Every question is loaded at these gatherings." Mira's thumb traced a circle against Drevik's palm. "I'm simply curious about the perspectives of our newest talent. The Elders can be so... set in their ways. Fresh eyes might see possibilities they've missed."

He understood what she was doing. Testing him. Looking for cracks in his loyalty, or perhaps recruitment opportunities if his ambition matched hers.

"I think the Order exists to protect the balance," he said carefully. "Anything beyond that is above my rank."

Mira laughed—a genuine sound, surprisingly warm. "The diplomatic answer. Smart." She finally released his hand, but her gaze remained fixed on his face. "You'll go far, Drevik. Or you'll burn out trying. The Order has a way of consuming those with too much potential."

"Is that a warning or an offer?"

The question surprised her. He saw it in the slight widening of her eyes, the momentary stillness of her smile.

"Both, perhaps." She stepped back, adjusting her stance. "I'd like to talk more when you're not so... chaperoned. There are conversations worth having, if you're interested."

She turned and walked away without waiting for a response, her silver dress catching the light as she disappeared into the crowd.

"Well," Lexi said, releasing a breath Drevik hadn't realized she was holding. "That was interesting."

"Did I pass?"

"You didn't fail." She took his arm again, her grip tighter than before. "She's careful. Whatever she's hiding, she won't give it up easily."

They moved away from the refreshment table, drifting toward the edge of the ballroom. The crowd thickened around them, faces blurring together—Elders in elaborate robes, Seekers in formal wear, acolytes watching from the shadows.

"There's someone else you need to meet," Lexi said quietly. "Someone who's been watching you all evening."

"Who?"

"Zara Nightshade. Veiled One."

Drevik's stomach tightened. The Veiled Ones were the Order's highest rank—those who'd mastered shadow magic so completely they could exist partially in the Shadowlands without losing themselves. They were legends. Myths.

"You're serious."

"Very." Lexi's expression was unreadable. "She asked about you last month. Before the breach. I've been trying to figure out why."

They climbed a curved staircase to the second floor, where the crowd thinned and the air grew cooler. Shadow magic pooled in the corners here, darker and more concentrated. Drevik felt it pressing against his awareness like a living thing.

A woman waited at the top of the stairs.

She was unlike anyone he'd seen in the Order. Her skin was pale—not the paleness of illness, but the color of moonlight on snow. Her hair was black and cropped short, exposing the elegant lines of her neck and jaw. Her eyes were silver, without iris or pupil, reflecting the candlelight like mirrors.

She wore a simple black dress that seemed to absorb the light around it, and when she moved, Drevik could see the Shadowlands moving with her—a flicker of twilight at the edges of her form, a whisper of something vast and eternal.

"Zara Nightshade," Lexi said, her voice carefully neutral. "May I present Acolyte Drevik Markinsoul."

The Veiled One's silver eyes fixed on Drevik, and he felt himself being seen—not just his body, but something deeper. Something that made him want to look away and unable to.

"Markinsoul." Her voice was soft, layered with harmonics that seemed to come from somewhere else entirely. "The one who walks between possibilities."

"Excuse me?"

"Your gift." She tilted her head, studying him. "You don't just see shadows. You see what could be. Futures that branch and twist and turn back on themselves. It's rare. Beautiful."

Drevik glanced at Lexi, but her expression gave nothing away.

"I don't understand," he said.

"You will." Zara stepped closer, and the temperature dropped around them. "The Order values power, but it fears vision. Those who can see what's coming are dangerous to those who benefit from things staying the same."

"Is that why you're talking to me?"

"I'm talking to you because I see what you're looking for." Her silver eyes flickered, and for a moment, Drevik could have sworn he saw futures reflected in them—paths branching and dividing, possibilities stretching into darkness. "The traitor. The one who's been feeding information to the Shadowlands."

His breath caught. "You know who it is?"

"I know where the trail leads." Her expression remained serene, but something in her voice shifted—a note of warning, perhaps, or regret. "The traitor is protected by someone higher than you can imagine. Be careful who you trust, little Acolyte."

Her gaze moved past him, settling on Lexi with an intensity that made his skin prickle.

"Even her."

The words hung in the cold air, and Drevik felt the world tilt beneath him. He turned to look at Lexi, but her face was a mask—shock and denial and something else, something that might have been fear.

"Zara—"

"The truth has many shadows, Drevik Markinsoul." The Veiled One began to fade, her form dissolving into twilight at the edges. "Learn to tell them apart before they swallow you whole."

She was gone.

The candlelight returned to normal. The warmth seeped back into the air. But the weight of her words remained, pressing down on Drevik's chest like a stone.

"What was she talking about?" he asked, turning to Lexi fully.

"I don't know." Her voice was steady, but her eyes were too bright. "Veiled Ones speak in riddles. They see futures, not facts. She could have meant anything."

"Did she?"

The question hung between them. Drevik searched her face, looking for the Lexi he'd come to know—the woman who'd fought for him, who'd admitted she wanted to protect him, who'd stood against her own family's politics to be his handler.

He saw all of that. He also saw shadows.

"Drevik." She stepped closer, and her hand found his. Her fingers were cold, trembling slightly. "I have secrets. Everyone in the Order does. But I would never—"

"I know." He squeezed her hand, making a choice he couldn't take back. "I trust you."

Her eyes searched his face, looking for the lie. When she didn't find it, something in her expression cracked—relief and fear and something deeper, something she wasn't ready to name.

"Thank you," she whispered.

Below them, the gathering continued. Mira Blackwood moved through the crowd, collecting secrets. Zara Nightshade's warning echoed in the shadows. And somewhere in the maze of politics and power, a traitor watched and waited.

But for this moment, in the cold air at the top of the stairs, Drevik held Lexi's hand and chose to believe.

The night was far from over.
