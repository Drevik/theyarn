Core idea

The goal of sex in an ethical non-monogamous context is to move away from "performance" and toward "clean love." This means stripping away the layers of possessiveness, desperation, and the need to impress a partner. You must learn to decouple sexual activity from "Goal Orientation," where orgasm is treated as a pass/fail metric that inadvertently kills spontaneous pleasure. Instead, focus on removing cultural obstacles—like body shame, rigid gender roles, and sex-negative disinformation—to create a space where pleasure is an exploratory process rather than a destination.

Key concepts and terms

  • Clean Love: Sex characterized by an absence of ownership, competition, or the need to "win" through sexual prowess. It is sex performed without the baggage of trying to prove your worth or secure a partner's commitment.
  • Goal Orientation: The psychological trap of treating orgasm as the only successful outcome of a sexual encounter. This creates performance anxiety and prevents the enjoyment of the intermediate sensations.
  • Sex-Negative Cultural Messages: The internalized societal scripts that label certain desires as "wrong," "dirty," or "deviant," which act as psychological brakes during intimacy.
  • The "Maybe" Condition: A middle-ground state of desire that is not a "No" but is not a "Yes" until specific environmental or emotional criteria are met.
  • Process-Free: An approach to intimacy that prioritizes the sensations and connection of the moment over the achievement of a specific physical result.

Techniques worth drilling

  1. De-escalating the Goal (Slow Down)

When you feel the pressure to "perform" or reach a specific climax, intentionally pivot the focus.

  • Step 1: Identify the moment you feel "performance anxiety" or a "need to finish."
  • Step 2: Physically slow your movements by 50%.
  • Step 3: Shift your focus from genital stimulation to sensory input (the feeling of skin, the sound of breathing, or the weight of your partner).
  • Step 4: Use "Talk Dirty" not just as roleplay, but as a way to narrate your actual physical sensations to keep yourself grounded in the present.
  1. The Sensory Connection Protocol (Get Connected First)

Use this technique before any sexual activity to bypass "Goal Orientation."

  • Step 1: Engage in non-sexual touch (massaging hands, stroking hair) for at least ten minutes.
  • Step 2: Practice "In Bed" mindfulness: focus on the temperature of the room, the scent of your partner, and the texture of the sheets.
  • Step 3: If sex is no longer feeling "sexy," do not force it; use this time to transition back to simple companionship.
  1. The Expansion of Choice (The Three-List Method)

This is a structural way to communicate boundaries and desires without the fear of rejection.

  • Step 1: Create three distinct columns on a piece of paper: Yes, No, and Maybe.
  • Step 2: Populate the Yes list with acts that are unconditionally enjoyable.
  • Step 3: Populate the No list with hard boundaries that are non-negotiable.
  • Stepram 4: Populate the Maybe list with acts that are conditional. For every item in the "Maybe" column, you must write a corresponding "If" clause (e.g., "Oral sex: if I feel safe enough," or "Roleplay: if we have a backup plan/safeword").
  1. Vocalizing Presence (Get Loud)

Use sound to bridge the gap between internal sensation and external communication.

  • Step 1: Identify moments of high pleasure.
  • Step 2: Instead of internalizing the feeling, use vocalizations (moans, sighs, or verbal affirmations) to signal to your partner that the current "process" is working.
  • Step 3: Use this to reinforce "Clean Love"—communicating what is happening now rather than what you want to happen next.

Practice drill

The "Maybe" Seduction Forecast

This week, do not surprise your partner with a new sexual request or an "ambush" of a new kink. Instead, perform the following sequence:

  1. Review your List: Look at your "Maybe" list and select one item that has a clear condition (e.g., "Impact play: if we go slow enough").
  2. Build the Fantasy: Spend ten minutes mentally constructing a scenario where this "Maybe" becomes a "Yes" because the condition is met.
  3. The Verbal Delivery: During a non-sexual moment (not while in bed), tell your partner: "I was thinking about [Item from Maybe list]. I would be interested in trying that if [Your Condition] is met. What do you think?"
  4. The Goal: The objective is to practice communicating a desire while simultaneously providing the "safety rails" (the condition) that make the desire feel safe to explore.

Critiques and caveats

  • The Risk of Over-Conditioning: While attaching conditions to "Maybes" is essential for safety, be careful not to create a list so restrictive that you effectively turn every "Maybe" into a "No." The goal is to find the threshold of safety, not to build a fortress.
  • The "Self-Correction" Trap: While "Good Sex Starts with You" emphasizes personal responsibility, do not use this to gaslight yourself or your partner. If a partner's behavior consistently makes you feel unsafe, the issue may not be your "body image" or "internalized shame," but a lack of respect for your boundaries.
  • Safety Note: When practicing "Toys for Everybody," always ensure that any new equipment or substances are discussed and vetted for hygiene and physical safety before introduction.
