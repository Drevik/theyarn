Chapter 22: Ascendant

Chapter 22: Ascendant

The weight of the Seeker's mantle settled across Drevik's shoulders like armor made of midnight itself.

He stood in the central chamber of the Order's compound, surrounded by the faces of those who'd shaped his journey—from skeptical initiate to whatever he was becoming now. The ceremonial robes shifted against his skin, warm despite their shadowy appearance, and power hummed beneath his sternum like a second heartbeat.

"Kneel," Elder Thornwood commanded.

Drevik dropped to one knee, never breaking eye contact with the man who'd once threatened to end him for touching his daughter. Some things you couldn't forget. Some things you couldn't forgive, either, but the Order didn't require forgiveness. It required utility.

Lexi's father pressed his palm against Drevik's forehead. Words poured from his mouth in a language that predated human speech—guttural, melodic, wrong in ways that made Drevik's hindbrain scream at him to run. He held his ground.

Power flooded him.

Not the gentle trickle he'd felt as an acolyte. This was a tidal wave, drowning out everything except the sensation of shadows wrapping around his bones, filling spaces he hadn't known existed. His Veil Sight sharpened until he could see the threads connecting everyone in the room—silver bonds of loyalty, gold threads of love, black strings of hidden enmity.

And beneath it all, the Shadowlands pressing against reality like a bruise through silk.

"Rise, Seeker Markinsoul."

He rose.

The chamber erupted in applause, but Drevik barely heard it. His attention fixed on four women standing together against the far wall—Lexi with her arms crossed and fierce pride radiating from every line of her body, Seraphina mysterious and unreadable as always, and the others he'd collected along the way. Mira, whose healing hands had saved his life twice now. Katryn, whose information networks had proven invaluable during the crisis they'd just survived.

Four women. Four bonds he could feel pulsing in his chest like echoes of his own heart.

What the hell was he supposed to do with that?

The ceremony's aftermath blurred into handshakes and murmured congratulations, most of which Drevik dismissed with appropriate humility while his mind raced ahead. Seeker. He'd made it. One step closer to the truth about his parents, one step further into a world that seemed determined to kill him.

"You're brooding already." Lexi appeared at his elbow, her familiar presence a balm against the chaos. "Can't you enjoy five minutes of victory before planning the next campaign?"

"I'm not brooding. I'm strategizing."

"Same thing with you." She bumped his shoulder with hers, casual in a way that would have seemed impossible months ago. "My father's watching."

"Let him watch."

"He's trying to decide if you're worth the headache of having you in the family."

Drevik turned to face her fully, taking in the controlled tension in her jaw, the way her hands wanted to clench but wouldn't allow it. "What do you think?"

"I think—" She stopped, something vulnerable flickering across her features before her familiar armor slammed back into place. "I think we need to talk. All of us."

"All of us?"

She gestured toward the other three women, who'd drifted closer during their conversation. "This isn't going to work if we're all pretending the others don't exist. Whatever this is—" she waved at the space between them, encompassing bonds that defied easy explanation "—we need to figure it out before it blows up in our faces."

He'd never loved her more than in that moment. Practical, fierce, unwilling to let awkwardness fester into something worse.

"Tonight," he said. "My place. The Order's given me new quarters, and I'm told Seekers rate actual privacy."

A smile cracked through her composure. "You're moving up in the world, Markinsoul."

"Don't I know it."

Seeker quarters were a significant upgrade from the acolyte cells Drevik had called home for the past months. A sitting room with actual furniture, a bedroom spacious enough to pace in, a small kitchen, and—most importantly—wards so thick he could taste them on his tongue. Shadowland entities would find no easy entry here.

He stood in the center of his new domain, still processing the reality of his promotion. The crisis that had nearly destroyed the Order's local chapter had been resolved, but at costs he was only beginning to understand. Three acolytes dead. Elder Vance revealed as a traitor and subsequently vanished into the Shadowlands, his fate unknown. And Drevik himself...

He touched his chest where the power now lived, vast and strange. The ascension ritual had changed something fundamental. He could feel possibilities branching from every choice, futures flickering at the edge of his vision like heat distortion.

A knock interrupted his contemplation.

Lexi entered first, of course. She'd never wait for permission when she could simply take it. The others followed—Seraphina gliding like smoke, Mira with her ever-present medical bag, Katryn bringing up the rear with a tablet already in hand.

"Nice place," Katryn said, her tone carefully neutral. "Bigger than my apartment."

"Perks of nearly dying repeatedly." Drevik gestured at the seating. "Make yourselves comfortable."

Nobody moved.

The air thickened with unsaid things. Drevik looked at each woman in turn, seeing clearly what he'd been avoiding for weeks. Lexi, whose feelings had grown from rivalry to respect to something deeper. Seraphina, whose mysterious connection to the Shadowlands matched his own in ways he didn't understand. Mira, whose healing touch carried intimate knowledge of his body. Katryn, whose sharp mind had saved him more times than he could count.

Four women. Four bonds. No clear path forward that didn't end with someone getting hurt.

"So," Lexi said into the silence. "This is awkward."

"Brilliant observation." Seraphina's voice carried its usual sardonic edge. "Shall we state more obvious truths? The sky is blue. Water is wet. We've all fallen for the same man."

"Not the same." Mira spoke quietly, but her words carried weight. "Different facets. Different needs."

"Still a problem," Katryn added. "Unless someone's planning to bow out."

Nobody did.

Drevik let out a breath he hadn't realized he was holding. "I don't... this isn't something I planned. Any of it. I came here looking for answers about my parents, and instead I found—" He gestured helplessly. "This. You. All of you."

"Life rarely follows plans." Seraphina moved to the window, her silhouette cutting sharp lines against the fading daylight. "The bonds we form aren't always convenient. Sometimes they're not even logical. But they are what they are."

"And what are they?" Lexi demanded. "Because I didn't sign up for a harem arrangement, and I'm pretty sure neither did anyone else."

"Would it be so terrible?" Mira's question hung in the air. "The Order has traditions—"

"The Order has a lot of traditions I don't give a damn about." Lexi's hands clenched. "I care about what's real. What's true. And what's true is that I—" She stopped, jaw tight.

Drevik crossed to her, taking her hands in his despite her obvious desire to pull away. "You."

Her green eyes met his, fierce and vulnerable in equal measure. "I love you, you idiot. I've loved you since you nearly got yourself killed saving those initiates. I've loved you through every stupid risk, every secret you kept, every moment you pushed me away because you thought you weren't worth the trouble."

His heart clenched. "Lexi—"

"Let me finish." She pulled one hand free to press against his chest. "I love you, and I know I'm not the only one. I see how you look at Seraphina. I see how Mira touches you when she thinks no one's watching. I see how you and Katryn finish each other's sentences. And I can either fight battles I'll lose, or I can figure out how to live with reality."

"It's not losing," Seraphina said quietly. "It's expanding. There's a difference."

"Is there?"

The question hung between them, heavy with genuine uncertainty. Drevik looked around at the women who'd somehow become essential to his existence, trying to find words for feelings that defied language.

"I can't choose," he finally said. "I won't. Not because I'm greedy or indecisive, but because each of you holds something the others don't, and losing any of you would leave me incomplete in ways I can't survive." He met each of their eyes in turn. "If that makes me selfish, then I'm selfish. But I'd rather be selfish and honest than noble and lying to everyone, including myself."

Silence stretched. Then Katryn laughed, the sound breaking tension like a stone through glass.

"Finally. Someone said it." She tucked her tablet away. "For the record, I've run the numbers on this kind of arrangement. Success rates are higher than monogamous partnerships within the Order—something about distributed emotional load and multiple perspectives on problem-solving."

"Romantic," Lexi muttered, but a smile tugged at her lips.

"I'm a data person. Sue me."

Mira moved to Drevik's side, her hand finding his. "We'll figure it out. Together. That's what matters."

Seraphina remained by the window, her expression unreadable. "There will be complications. Jealousy. Misunderstandings. Times when one of us needs something the others can't provide."

"Name a relationship without those things," Lexi countered.

"Point taken."

The evening light caught Seraphina's features, illuminating something beneath her usual composure. Drevik crossed to her, conscious of the others watching.

"What do you need?" he asked quietly.

"Something I'm not sure you can give." Her fingers found his, cool and certain. "But I'm willing to wait and see."

"That's not an answer."

"No. It's not." She pressed closer, her breath warm against his ear. "But this is."

Her kiss was different from Lexi's fire—deeper, slower, carrying the weight of shadows and secrets. Drevik felt the Shadowlands respond, power stirring in recognition of something he couldn't name. When they separated, her smile held genuine warmth beneath its mystery.

"Later," she promised. "When we have time to do this properly."

Night fell in earnest, and with it came the responsibilities Drevik had been promoted to bear.

A Seeker's duties, he discovered, were less glamorous than the title suggested. Reports to review, patrol schedules to coordinate, the endless administrative machinery that kept the Order functioning. He bent over his desk in the small hours, trying to make sense of supply requisition forms, when a presence stirred at the edge of his awareness.

Not hostile. Familiar.

"You should be resting." Mira's voice came from the doorway, soft with concern. "The ascension takes a toll."

"So does paperwork, apparently."

She crossed to his desk, her healer's hands pressing against his temples. Cool relief spread through his skull, easing a headache he hadn't consciously registered.

"You're pushing too hard."

"There's a lot to push through."

"Always will be." Her fingers traced down to his shoulders, working knots with professional efficiency. "But you're no good to anyone if you burn out in your first week."

He caught her hands, pulling her around to face him. In the dim light, her features held the same quiet strength that had drawn him to her from the beginning—steady, grounding, asking nothing while giving everything.

"Mira."

"Drevik." Her smile held gentle amusement. "If you're about to apologize for something, don't. I chose this. All of it."

"Did you? Choose?"

"I walked into that warehouse knowing I might die. I stayed when I could have transferred to another chapter. I'm standing here now, knowing you have feelings for three other women, and I'm not walking away." She freed one hand to cup his cheek. "That's choice. Every single moment of it."

He turned his head to press his lips against her palm. "You deserve better than being one of four."

"Probably." Her thumb traced his cheekbone. "But deserving and wanting rarely align. I want you. The rest is logistics."

"Logistics." A laugh escaped him, surprised by his own lightness. "Is that what we're calling it?"

"What would you call it?"

He pulled her into his lap, her weight settling against him with easy familiarity. "I'd call it the most complicated thing I've ever attempted. I'd call it terrifying and wonderful and probably doomed to spectacular failure."

"Romantic."

"I'd call it—" He stopped, searching for words. "I'd call it worth the risk."

Her kiss tasted like healing herbs and quiet devotion. She moved against him with the easy confidence of someone who knew his body intimately, and he let himself sink into the sensation, paperwork forgotten, responsibilities fading to background noise.

"Bed," she murmured against his mouth. "You need actual rest, and I'm not having you fall asleep at that desk."

"Is that a medical opinion?"

"It's a demand from someone who's seen what sleep deprivation does to newly ascended Seekers." She stood, pulling him with her. "Come on. I'll stay. Make sure you actually sleep."

"Just sleep?"

Her smile turned wicked. "Maybe not just sleep. But sleep eventually."

Dawn found him tangled with Mira in sheets that smelled like her herbs, her steady breathing a counterpoint to his own thoughts. He slipped from bed without waking her, padding to the sitting room in search of coffee.

Lexi was already there, mug in hand, reading something on her phone.

"Early," she said without looking up.

"Couldn't sleep."

"Liar. You slept fine until you snuck out." She finally met his eyes, amusement warring with something deeper. "Mira looks peaceful when she's unconscious. Less worried."

"She carries a lot."

"We all do." She set her phone aside. "Katryn wants to set up a household management system. Color-coded calendars, designated nights, the whole bureaucratic nightmare."

"That sounds... practical."

"It sounds like Katryn." Lexi stood, crossing to him with purpose. "But before we turn this into a committee meeting, I need something else."

"What?"

"This."

Her kiss hit like a thunderclap, all the fire he associated with her packed into a single moment. She pressed him against the kitchen counter, hands everywhere, claiming what she'd staked out with unmistakable clarity.

"Mine," she murmured against his lips. "Regardless of who else you're with. Some part of you is mine."

"Lexi—"

"Don't argue. Just accept it." She pulled back, eyes fierce. "I'm not good at sharing. I'm making an exception because you're worth the exception. Don't make me regret it."

"I won't."

"See that you don't." She kissed him once more, hard and demanding, then stepped back as if nothing had happened. "Now. Coffee's getting cold and Katryn will be here in twenty minutes with charts."

The day unfolded in a blur of orientation and duty. Drevik's first official Seeker assignment: investigating a Shadowlands breach in the university's library, minor enough to be handled alone but significant enough to prove his new status. He walked through the stacks, power humming through him, and found the thin place between worlds with an ease that would have been impossible weeks ago.

The entity that slipped through was small—barely a nuisance—but he dispatched it with efficiency that surprised even him. New strength, new senses, new responsibilities.

He returned to find his quarters transformed. Someone—probably Katryn—had reorganized the space into something more livable. Someone else—Mira's touch, he suspected—had added plants and soft textiles. Lexi's weapons rack now occupied one corner, her presence declared through steel and leather.

Four women, making their marks on his space. His life.

The thought should have terrified him. Instead, it felt like coming home.

Night fell again, and with it came the message that would change everything.

Drevik stood at his window, watching shadows shift in ways only he could perceive, when the air itself seemed to whisper. A presence pressed against his wards—not hostile, but familiar in a way that made his chest ache.

The voice came from everywhere and nowhere.

"My son."

He froze. Recognition flooded him, bone-deep and impossible.

"Mother."

"They're coming for what you carry. The truth you seek is buried deeper than you know. Your father and I hid you for a reason—hid what you are."

"What am I?"

"The key. The bridge. The final piece of a war that started before your birth." A pause, weighted with sorrow. "Trust no one with this truth—not even the women you love. Some secrets must remain hidden until the final ascension."

"Where are you? Let me help you—"

"I'm beyond your help. Beyond everyone's help. But you—you have a chance to finish what we started. To end the shadow war before it consumes everything."

The presence began to fade.

"Wait! Tell me more!"

"Find the Veiled One's journal. It holds the answers you need. And Drevik—"

The voice dropped to a whisper.

"The heart bonds you've formed are real. Don't let the Order's politics cheapen them. But remember—anyone can be turned. Anyone can be broken. The only person you can trust completely is yourself."

Silence. The presence vanished entirely, leaving Drevik alone with shadows that suddenly seemed darker, deeper, more threatening than before.

He stood motionless for a long moment, processing. His mother—alive, or something like it. A war he'd never known about. A truth about himself that others would kill to possess.

The door opened behind him. Lexi's voice, sharp with concern.

"Drevik? What's wrong?"

He turned to face her, and the others gathering in the doorway. Four women who'd become essential to his survival, his sanity, his heart. Four potential weaknesses, according to his mother's warning.

But also four strengths. Four anchors. Four reasons to keep fighting.

"Nothing," he said, and the lie tasted like ash. "Just adjusting to the new normal."

She studied him, clearly unconvinced, but let it pass. "Katryn made dinner. Try to eat something before you collapse."

He nodded, following them to the small kitchen table, taking his place among them as they argued over seating arrangements and schedules and all the mundane logistics of building a life together.

His mother's warning echoed in his mind.

Trust no one.

But he looked at Lexi's fierce protection, Seraphina's mysterious devotion, Mira's quiet strength, Katryn's brilliant mind—and knew he couldn't follow that advice. Wouldn't. Some risks were worth taking.

He just hoped he wouldn't live to regret it.

The shadows outside his window deepened, and somewhere in the distance, something howled.

The next chapter of his ascent was about to begin.

End of Book Two
