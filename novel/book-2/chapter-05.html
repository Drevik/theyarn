Chapter 5: Breach Point

Chapter 5: Breach Point

The scream cut through his sleep like a blade through silk.

Drevik's eyes snapped open, his body already moving before his mind caught up. The dorm room was dark—too dark. The shadows pooling in the corners weren't natural. They writhed and pulsed with a rhythm that made his stomach clench.

His phone buzzed on the nightstand. Then again. Then a third time in rapid succession.

Emergency. Science Building. Now.

The text glowed on his screen, sent from a number he'd memorized during his initiate days. Elder Thornwood's personal line.

Drevik was out of bed and pulling on his jeans before the fourth buzz came. His heart hammered against his ribs as he grabbed his jacket—the one with the concealed pockets holding his ritual components—and shoved his feet into boots without bothering with socks.

The hallway outside his door was chaos. Students streamed toward the exits, some crying, others shouting into phones. The alarm system hadn't triggered, but something had these people running. Animal instinct, maybe. The same instinct that made prey animals flee before the predator stepped into view.

He caught a glimpse of Mira's door as he passed. Already open. Already empty.

Good. She was smart. She'd have gotten out.

The night air hit him like a slap when he burst through the ground floor exit. Cold and wrong, carrying a smell that didn't belong on a college campus—rotting vegetation and something metallic underneath. Copper and decay.

The science building loomed three hundred yards away, its modern glass facade reflecting emergency lights that flickered in an irregular pattern. Blue and red strobes that created a strobing effect making his vision swim.

Drevik broke into a run.

He made it fifty yards before a hand grabbed his arm and yanked him sideways into the shadows between buildings.

"Wait."

Lexi's voice was low and controlled, but he could hear the tension underneath. Her auburn hair was pulled back in a tight braid, and she wore tactical clothes that looked nothing like what a college student should own. Black cargo pants, fitted top, boots that had seen actual combat.

"What's happening?" He didn't fight her grip. She was stronger than she looked—Order training did that to people.

"Breach." Her green eyes met his, and for a moment he saw something beneath the professional mask. Fear, maybe. Or something else. "Science building. Multiple entities. The ward matrix collapsed about fifteen minutes ago."

"How many acolytes are responding?"

"Everyone within a five-mile radius. But Drevik—" She tightened her grip on his arm. "The breach isn't contained. It's growing."

He processed that. A breach that grew meant the barrier between realms was destabilizing. Meant whatever had punctured through was still actively tearing the wound wider.

"Ivy," he said suddenly. "She was being questioned. Where—"

"Safe. For now." Lexi released his arm and stepped back. "Elder Voss has her in a secure location. But that's not why I grabbed you."

The way she said it made cold settle in his stomach. "Then why?"

"Because I need you to trust me." Her jaw tightened. "And because I'm about to do something that might get us both in serious trouble."

Before he could ask what she meant, she grabbed his hand. Her fingers intertwined with his, palm to palm, and he felt the familiar tingle of shadow magic responding to contact with another practitioner.

"We're shadow-walking into the building," she said. "The main doors are being watched by entities that feed on fear. They'll sense any acolyte coming and scatter. But if we go through the shadows—"

"We can get past them." He finished her thought. "Lexi, that's risky. If the breach has corrupted the shadow paths—"

"It has." Her expression was grim. "That's why I need your help. Your talent with shadow-walking is stronger than mine. If we link, I can guide while you stabilize."

It was against protocol. Against everything they'd been taught about breach response. Acolytes weren't supposed to engage directly with uncontained entities. They were supposed to support, contain, and let the Wardens handle the actual threat.

But the Wardens weren't here yet. And every minute they waited, the breach grew.

"Show me the path," he said.

Lexi's smile was brief and fierce. She squeezed his hand once, then pulled him into the shadow of the building beside them.

The world inverted.

Walking through shadow felt like being underwater without the pressure. Colors bled into grayscale, sounds became muffled echoes, and time moved strangely—stretching and compressing in alternating waves. Drevik had done this hundreds of times since his initiation, but never into an active breach zone.

The corruption hit him immediately.

It felt like oil sliding across his skin. Dark and slick and wrong. The shadow paths that usually felt cool and welcoming now had an infection running through them, something that made his magical senses scream in protest.

Hold, Lexi's voice came through their linked hands. Not spoken aloud—impossible in the shadow realm—but projected through their connection. I can see the contamination. It's coming from the northeast corner of the building. Guide us around it.

He closed his eyes—not that it mattered in this place—and focused on the shadow paths. They spread out before his awareness like a network of veins, some clear and bright, others clouded with the same oily corruption. The breach was poisoning everything it touched.

Drevik pulled them left, then down. The shadow realm had its own geography, one that didn't always match the physical world. A straight line in reality might require a dozen turns through shadow.

There. Lexi's mental voice carried a note of triumph. I can see the exit point. Science building, second floor. The entity concentration is lower there.

They materialized in a hallway that should have been familiar but looked like a war zone. Lockers had been torn from walls, their contents scattered across blood-smeared tile. The fluorescent lights flickered overhead, casting the corridor in alternating pools of yellow and shadow.

Blood. Not a lot, but enough. Someone had been hurt.

"Stay close," Lexi whispered. Her hand found his again, but this time she was pulling a blade from the sheath at her hip. The metal was dark and seemed to drink the light around it—Order-forged steel, designed to cut through entities that weren't entirely physical.

They moved through the hallway in a crouch, checking corners with practiced efficiency. Drevik's shadow senses were stretched thin, reaching out for any sign of the entities that had come through the breach. He could feel them—vague shapes of hunger and malice—but they seemed concentrated elsewhere. Drawn to something.

"The main lab," Lexi breathed. "That's where the breach is centered. And that's where the entities are feeding."

"Feeding on what?"

She didn't answer. Her expression told him she didn't want to guess.

They reached the stairwell without incident, but the moment Drevik's foot touched the first step, the temperature plummeted. His breath misted in front of his face, and the shadows in the corners of the stairwell began to move.

Not naturally. Not randomly.

Something was pulling them together, gathering darkness into a shape that shouldn't exist.

"Lexi."

"I see it." Her blade came up, the dark steel catching what little light remained. "Don't engage. We go around. Fast and quiet."

But the shadow-thing was already forming. A torso, elongated and wrong. Limbs that bent in too many places. A face that was barely a face—just a suggestion of features around a mouth that gaped too wide.

It pulled itself out of the corner like something climbing out of a pool, and when it saw them—saw them, though it had no eyes—its mouth stretched wider in what might have been a smile.

Drevik felt the fear hit him like a wave. Not his own fear—the entity's. It was projecting, pushing terror into his mind, trying to freeze him in place so it could feed.

Don't listen to it. Lexi's grip on his hand tightened painfully. This is what they do. They make you feel things that aren't yours.

He knew that. The Wardens had drilled it into every acolyte during training. But knowing and resisting were different things when the fear was clawing at the inside of his skull, when every instinct was screaming at him to run or freeze or—

Lexi moved.

She didn't run. She didn't freeze. She stepped forward and drove her blade through the thing's chest in a motion so fast Drevik barely tracked it.

The entity shrieked—a sound that existed somewhere between hearing and feeling—and dissolved into wisps of shadow that scattered like smoke.

"Move," Lexi ordered, already pulling him down the stairs. "That will have drawn attention."

They ran. The next floor was worse—more blood, more damage, and the shadows themselves seemed to press inward like a living thing. Drevik could feel the breach now, a wound in reality that throbbed with wrongness. It was close.

They found the source in the main laboratory.

The room was a disaster—overturned tables, shattered equipment, and in the center, a tear in reality that looked like someone had cut a hole in the air itself. Through it, Drevik could see the Shadowlands. Twilight sky. Twisted trees. Movement in the distance that suggested things coming to investigate the opening.

Three Wardens stood in a triangle around the breach, hands raised, chanting in a language that predated human speech. Sweat beaded on their faces. One of them—the woman leading the ritual—had blood running from her nose.

"Containment team is on their way," one of the men said without breaking his chant. "We're holding it open but we can't close it. Something's anchoring it from the other side."

Drevik's stomach dropped. "Anchoring it?"

"Something came through." The lead Warden's voice was strained. "Several somethings. We can't close the breach while they're still in this realm. They're using the opening to feed, and every minute they're here, the tear gets wider."

That was why the entities had been drawn elsewhere. They weren't just randomly attacking—they were spreading out, hunting, feeding. And every person they touched made the breach worse.

"We need to find them," Lexi said. "All of them. And we need to do it before—"

"Before more students die," Drevik finished. "How many came through?"

The Warden's silence was answer enough.

They split up. Lexi took the east wing, Drevik took the west. It went against every protocol—acolytes weren't supposed to hunt alone—but there weren't enough Wardens to cover the building, and every second they waited, the situation deteriorated.

Drevik moved through the corridors with his shadow senses stretched to their limit. The fear-projecting entity in the stairwell had been a minor one. The things that came through a breach this size could be significantly more dangerous.

He found the first one in a bathroom.

It had cornered a student—a girl he vaguely recognized from his chemistry lecture—and was pressing her against the wall with tendrils of shadow that wrapped around her wrists and ankles. Her eyes were wide and vacant, her mouth open in a silent scream. The entity was feeding on her terror, and from the look of her, it had been feeding for a while.

Drevik didn't hesitate. He pulled the shadow knife from his jacket—the one every acolyte carried—and stepped into the room.

The entity turned. Its face was barely formed, just a suggestion of features, but he could feel it assessing him. Deciding whether he was worth abandoning its current meal.

"Hey." His voice came out steadier than he felt. "You want something to eat? Try me."

The girl's eyes moved—just barely—but the entity didn't release her. It was smarter than the one in the stairwell. Cautious.

Drevik reached for his shadow-walking ability, the talent that had marked him as unusual since his initiation. He could feel the shadows in the room, could sense the entity's connection to them. It was using shadow to maintain its form, shadow to bind its prey.

If he could disrupt that connection...

He reached out with his mind and pulled.

The entity shrieked as the shadows that composed its form unraveled. It wasn't destruction—the thing was still there, still dangerous—but it was distracted, trying to pull itself back together, and Drevik was already moving.

His knife found the center of its mass three times in rapid succession. Not Order-forged steel like Lexi's blade, but enough to disrupt the entity's cohesion. It dissolved into fragments that scattered across the ceiling and walls.

The girl collapsed. Drevik caught her before she hit the ground, lowering her gently to the tile. Her breathing was shallow, her pulse thready. Alive, but barely conscious.

He pulled out his phone and typed a quick message to the coordinating Warden: West bathroom, second floor. One student down, one entity dispersed. Medical needed.

The response came immediately: Confirmed. Three more entities detected. Continue sweep.

Three more. And that was just in this building. If the breach had been growing for fifteen minutes before anyone responded...

He left the girl in the bathroom and kept moving. The next entity was two corridors away—he could feel its presence like a stain on his senses. This one was bigger. Stronger.

It had found another meal.

Drevik rounded the corner and stopped cold.

Mira stood in the center of the hallway, her back against the wall, her hands raised in a defensive posture. The entity facing her was massive—at least seven feet tall, with shadow-limbs that seemed to flow and reform constantly. It wasn't just feeding on fear. It was playing with her.

Toying with its food.

"Mira." Her name came out sharper than he intended. "Get down."

She didn't move. Her eyes were locked on the entity, and he could see the terror in them—real terror, not the projected kind. She'd seen something like this before. She knew what it could do.

The entity turned toward him, and Drevik felt the fear-wave hit him like a physical force. This one was stronger than the others. Older. More powerful.

Don't engage. We go around. Lexi's words echoed in his memory.

But there was no going around. Not with Mira in the thing's path.

He reached for his shadow-walking ability again, tried to pull at the entity's form like he had with the others. But this one resisted. It had better control of its shadows, more cohesion. It was anchored to the Shadowlands in a way the minor entities weren't.

Drevik's knife suddenly felt very small.

The entity lunged.

He moved on instinct—ducked under the first swipe, rolled away from the second. A tendril of shadow caught his shoulder and he felt cold spread through his arm like poison. His knife hand went numb.

"Your shadow-walking won't work on me, little acolyte." The entity's voice was a whisper that seemed to come from everywhere at once. "I've eaten a hundred of your kind."

It wasn't bluffing. He could feel the truth in the words, could sense the weight of experience behind them. This thing had killed Order members before. Had fed on them.

Drevik's mind raced. His knife was useless. His shadow-walking was blocked. He was outmatched, outpaced, and running out of options.

But he wasn't alone.

Lexi's blade took the entity in the back.

She'd come out of nowhere—shadow-walked directly into the thing's shadow, which was apparently still possible even when direct manipulation failed. The Order-forged steel sank deep into the entity's mass, and it screamed. Actually screamed, a sound that rattled the windows and made Drevik's ears ring.

"Move!" Lexi shouted.

He didn't need to be told twice. He grabbed Mira's arm and pulled her toward the nearest exit while Lexi engaged the entity. The thing was thrashing, trying to reach the blade buried in its back, but Lexi was already moving—circling, striking, forcing it to split its attention.

Drevik glanced back once and saw her face in the flickering light. Focused. Fierce. Beautiful in a way that made his chest ache.

He pushed Mira through the exit door and turned back toward the fight.

Lexi was holding her own, but barely. The entity had recovered from the initial strike and was pressing hard. Its shadow-tendrils whipped through the air, forcing her to dodge and weave, and he could see her starting to slow. Starting to tire.

He reached for his shadow-walking ability again. Not to disrupt the entity—he'd already tried that—but to change the battlefield.

The shadows in the hallway responded to his pull. They deepened, spread, created pools of darkness where there hadn't been any before. Not much—just enough to give Lexi more weapons.

She understood immediately. Her next dodge ended in a shadow-walk, reappearing behind the entity for another strike. Then again. And again. Using the shadows he was creating, moving faster than the thing could track.

"End it!" Drevik called. "I can't hold this forever!"

Lexi drove her blade into the entity's core. The thing convulsed, its form destabilizing, and she twisted the blade before yanking it free. Shadow exploded outward, dissipating into nothing.

The hallway went quiet.

Lexi stood in the center of it, breathing hard, her blade dripping with something that wasn't quite blood. Her eyes found his across the space between them, and for a moment, neither of them moved.

Then she was walking toward him, and her hand was on his face, and she was checking him for injuries with a focus that made his heart stutter.

"You're hurt." Her fingers found the wound on his shoulder—the one the entity's tendril had touched. "This needs treatment. Shadow-taint. If it spreads—"

"I'm fine." He caught her wrist. "Lexi. I'm fine. Are you?"

She stared at him for a long moment. Her eyes were too bright, her jaw too tight. The fear she'd been suppressing was starting to show through the cracks.

"That was stupid," she said finally. "Trying to fight a greater shadow entity with a knife. What were you thinking?"

"I was thinking about Mira." He nodded toward the other woman, who was standing frozen by the exit door. "I couldn't leave her."

Lexi's expression flickered. He couldn't read it—there was too much happening behind her eyes. But her grip on his shoulder gentled.

"You need to be more careful." Her voice was quiet. "You're not expendable, Drevik."

Neither are you, he wanted to say. But the words stuck in his throat.

The Elder council convened at midnight.

Drevik stood in the center of the circular chamber, his shoulder bandaged and aching, and tried not to feel like he was on trial. The Elders sat in their elevated positions around him, faces half-shadowed, expressions unreadable.

Elder Thornwood—Lexi's father—spoke first. "The breach has been contained. Four entities eliminated, six students injured, no fatalities. We were fortunate."

"Fortunate?" Elder Voss's voice dripped with contempt. "Our defenses were compromised. Someone disabled the ward matrix. This wasn't a random breach—it was an attack."

"An attack that was detected and responded to with appropriate speed." Elder Thornwood's tone remained neutral. "The acolytes performed admirably."

"Acolytes should not have been in a position to perform at all." Voss leaned forward, and Drevik saw the cold calculation in his eyes. "Where were the Wardens? Why was the response left to untrained children?"

"The Wardens were dispatched to the wrong location." Another Elder spoke—Drevik didn't know her name. "The initial alert was... redirected."

The implication hung in the air. Someone had intentionally sent the Wardens away from the breach. Someone had wanted it to grow.

"We are investigating." Elder Thornwood's voice hardened. "But for now, we must address a more pressing concern."

His eyes moved to Drevik.

"The breach source remains open. We've contained it, but we cannot close it. Something is anchoring it from the Shadowlands side, and until that anchor is removed, the breach will continue to expand. Every hour it remains open, the risk of another incident increases."

"What are you proposing?" Voss asked.

"A mission." Thornwood's gaze didn't waver. "Someone needs to enter the Shadowlands and find the anchor. A small team, moving quickly. In and out before the local entities can respond."

Drevik's stomach tightened. Shadowlands missions were dangerous under the best circumstances. During an active breach, they were nearly suicidal.

"Who would lead this mission?" someone asked.

Thornwood's expression was unreadable. "The acolyte Drevik."

Silence. Then Voss laughed—a cold, sharp sound. "An acolyte? You want to send an untrained boy into the Shadowlands during a breach event?"

"His shadow-walking abilities are exceptional." Thornwood's voice carried a note of challenge. "And he has experience with Shadowlands navigation that many Wardens lack."

"He's expendable, you mean." Voss's smile didn't reach his eyes. "If he fails, we lose nothing of value."

Drevik kept his face neutral, but the words stung. He knew how the Order viewed him—the outsider, the scholarship student, the one who'd stumbled into membership rather than being born to it. To Elders like Voss, he was a resource to be spent, not a person to protect.

"I'll do it," he said quietly. "I'll go."

"You don't have a choice in the matter." Voss waved a dismissive hand. "The council has decided—"

"I volunteer." Lexi's voice cut through the chamber like a blade.

She stepped out of the shadows at the edge of the room—Drevik hadn't even seen her arrive—and moved to stand beside him. Her jaw was set, her eyes bright with determination.

"Lexi." Her father's voice was carefully controlled. "This isn't a discussion."

"No, it's not." She crossed her arms. "Drevik isn't going into the Shadowlands alone. I'm going with him. That's not negotiable."

Voss's smile had returned, and Drevik didn't like the satisfaction in it. "How touching. The Thornwood heir volunteering to die alongside her—" He stopped, reconsidered his words. "Her partner."

"You need someone who knows breach dynamics." Lexi's voice was steady, but Drevik could see the tension in her shoulders. "You need someone who can fight while he looks for the anchor. I'm the best qualified acolyte for this mission."

"She's not wrong," another Elder murmured. "Her combat scores are exceptional."

"The mission is dangerous enough without sending two acolytes." Voss's tone was skeptical, but Drevik saw the calculation behind his eyes. The man wanted them both out of the way. Lexi's presence would make the mission more likely to succeed, but also more likely to result in casualties.

And Voss wanted casualties.

"Two acolytes can cover more ground than one." Elder Thornwood's voice was carefully neutral. "And Lexi's skills complement Drevik's. They work well together."

"Is that what we're calling it?" Voss murmured.

Lexi's hand found Drevik's. Not holding—just touching. A reminder that she was there. That she wasn't going to let him face this alone.

"The mission is approved." Thornwood's voice was final. "Drevik will lead. Lexi will provide support. They leave at dawn."

Dawn. Less than six hours away.

Drevik looked at Lexi, saw the determination in her eyes, the fear she was trying to hide. She knew what they were walking into. She knew the odds.

She was going anyway.

"I need to make arrangements," he said quietly. "Preparations."

"Take what time you need." Thornwood's gaze was heavy on him. "But be ready. The Shadowlands won't wait for anyone."

They found Ivy in a secure room beneath the Order's compound.

She looked smaller than Drevik remembered. Fragile, somehow, despite the defiance in her eyes. Her interrogator had left bruises—visible ones, on her wrists and arms, and Drevik suspected there were others hidden under her clothes.

"They wanted to know about my research," she said when they were alone. Her voice was hoarse. "About the texts I found. About the things I've seen."

"What things?" Lexi asked from the doorway.

Ivy's eyes moved between them, calculating. Trusting, but only so far.

"Things that shouldn't exist." Her voice dropped. "Things the Order has hidden. Things about what's really happening to the barrier between our world and theirs."

Drevik thought about the breach. About the disabled wards. About Voss's cold smile and the way he'd looked at Lexi when she volunteered.

"Someone inside the Order is working against it," he said quietly. "Someone wanted that breach to happen."

Ivy nodded slowly. "I know. And I think I know who."

She told them what she'd found in her research. The patterns. The money trails. The communications that shouldn't exist between Order members and entities that lived in shadow.

By the time she finished, Drevik's hands were shaking.

"We have to tell Thornwood," Lexi said. "This changes everything."

"Does it?" Ivy's smile was bitter. "Or does it just confirm what some of us have suspected all along? The Order is rotting from the inside. And the people in charge are too busy protecting their own positions to see it."

Drevik thought about the mission. About walking into the Shadowlands with enemies behind him and unknown dangers ahead.

"Whatever's happening," he said slowly, "we need to survive this mission first. Find the anchor. Close the breach. Then we deal with the traitors."

Lexi nodded, but her expression was troubled. "And if the traitors are the ones sending us in?"

The question hung in the air, heavy and uncomfortable.

Drevik didn't have an answer. But as he looked at the two women—the one who'd volunteered to die beside him, and the one who'd risked everything to uncover the truth—he knew one thing for certain.

He wasn't going to let any of them die. Not if he could help it.

And if that meant walking into the Shadowlands and facing whatever waited there...

Then that's what he would do.

Dawn came too quickly.

Drevik stood at the breach point, watching the tear in reality pulse and breathe. In the twilight beyond, he could see shapes moving. Waiting.

Lexi appeared at his side, geared for combat. Her face was pale but determined.

"Last chance to back out," she said quietly.

"Same to you."

She shook her head. "Not a chance. Where you go, I go."

It should have sounded like a threat. Instead, it sounded like a promise.

They stepped through together, and the Shadowlands swallowed them whole.
