Chapter 17: Escape and Proof

Chapter 17: Escape and Proof

The confinement cell had no windows, no clocks, nothing to mark the passage of time except the slow fade of the suppressor runes etched into the walls. Drevik had counted the cracks in the stone ceiling forty-seven times when the shadows in the corner rippled.

He was on his feet before Lexi fully materialized, her form bleeding out of the darkness like ink spreading through water.

"You're late," he said.

"Traffic was terrible." She stepped closer, and even in the dim light, he could see the tension in her jaw, the way her hands trembled slightly at her sides. "We have exactly eleven minutes before the next patrol rotation. Can you shadow-walk in your condition?"

He tested his connection to the shadows—the suppressor runes made it feel like reaching through mud, but his core still pulsed with power. "I can manage. What's the plan?"

"I told Elder Sevestian I spotted an intruder near the eastern perimeter. He's pulling every available Warden to investigate." A ghost of a smile crossed her lips. "You're welcome."

"Lexi, if they catch you—"

"They won't." She moved closer, close enough that he could smell the night air clinging to her skin, the faint trace of jasmine from whatever soap she used. "I've been planning this for three days, Drevik. While you've been counting ceiling cracks, I've been memorizing patrol routes, bribing kitchen staff, and forging documents. You're not the only one who can be clever."

Three days. He'd lost track somewhere after the second interrogation, after Warden Blackwood's questions had circled around his father like a vulture waiting for a dying animal to stop twitching. Tell me about the journal, acolyte. What did Marcus Markinsoul hide in his final entries? Who did he trust?

They'd taken everything from him—his communication stone, his student ID, the small knife he kept in his boot. But they hadn't found the journal. He'd hidden it too well for that.

"The journal's in my quarters," he said. "Behind the loose stone near the window, the one with the crack running through it."

"I know. I checked." Her eyes met his, and something flickered there—something that looked almost like hurt. "Why didn't you tell me about your father's research? About what he was investigating before he died?"

"Because I didn't trust you."

The words hung between them. Lexi's expression didn't change, but her shadows writhed at her feet, agitated.

"And now?" she asked.

"Now I'm beginning to think I was an idiot."

"You were." She stepped back, creating distance, and the cold that rushed into the space between them felt like a physical thing. "We need to move. Follow my shadow and stay quiet."

She dissolved into darkness, and Drevik reached for his own power, letting it wrap around him like a familiar cloak. The suppressor runes screamed in protest, but he pushed through, following the thread of Lexi's essence through the twisting paths of shadow-walking.

They emerged in a supply closet on the third floor of the acolyte dormitory. Drevik pressed his ear to the door, hearing nothing but the distant hum of the building's old heating system.

"Clear," he whispered.

Lexi was already moving, her body low, her footsteps silent on the worn carpet. She moved like someone who had done this a hundred times—like someone born to the shadows in ways Drevik was only beginning to understand.

His quarters were three corridors away. Three corridors that could be filled with Wardens at any moment, every shadow a potential threat.

They made it through the first without incident.

The second corridor brought voices—two acolytes arguing about something mundane, their words echoing off the stone walls. Lexi pulled Drevik into an alcove, their bodies pressed together in the narrow space.

Her breath was warm against his neck. Her hand rested on his chest, feeling the rapid beat of his heart.

"Relax," she murmured. "They're just passing by."

"I am relaxed."

"You're vibrating." Her lips twitched. "Your heart's trying to escape your ribs."

"Maybe I'm just excited to be this close to you."

The words slipped out before he could stop them. Lexi's eyes widened slightly, and for a moment, the fierce, controlled mask she wore cracked. He saw the girl beneath—the one who had been watching him for months, the one who had fought beside him and trained him and pushed him harder than anyone else.

"Drevik—"

The voices faded. The corridor fell silent.

She pulled away first, her expression smoothing back into neutral territory. "We should keep moving."

They reached his quarters without further incident. The door was still sealed with the Order's confinement ward, a shimmering barrier that would trigger an alarm if breached by anyone but a Warden.

"Can you break it?" Lexi asked.

Drevik studied the ward's structure, tracing the threads of power woven into the barrier. It was complex, but he could see the anchor point—a small knot of energy near the lock, tied to the suppressor runes throughout the building.

"I don't need to break it." He placed his hand against the door. "I just need to convince it I'm supposed to be here."

His father had taught him that much, in the months before his death. Wards are like people, Drevik. They have patterns, habits. Learn to speak their language, and they'll open any door for you.

The barrier shivered, recognized something in his touch, and dissolved.

"That's new," Lexi breathed. "I've never seen anyone talk down a confinement ward before."

"It's a family trick." He pushed the door open and slipped inside.

His room was exactly as he'd left it—the narrow bed with its standard-issue gray sheets, the desk covered in textbooks and notes, the small window overlooking the eastern courtyard. Nothing had been disturbed.

Which meant they hadn't found the journal.

Drevik crossed to the window, running his fingers along the stone frame until he found the crack Lexi had mentioned. The loose stone came away easily, revealing the hollow space behind it.

The leather-bound journal was still there, its cover worn soft by years of handling. Marcus Markinsoul's final research, hidden in a space his son had carved out when he was twelve years old.

"I found it," he said, turning to show Lexi—

And froze.

The shadows in the corner of his room were moving. Not the gentle ripple of shadow-walking, but something darker, more violent. They coalesced into shapes—vaguely humanoid, with too-long limbs and faces that were nothing but gaping voids.

"Lexi, get back—"

The first shadow creature lunged.

Drevik threw himself sideways, feeling the brush of something cold and wrong against his shoulder. The thing's touch left frost on his shirt, ice spreading through the fabric.

"What are these?" Lexi shouted, her hands wreathed in shadow as she prepared to fight.

"Shadowstalkers." Drevik scrambled to his feet, the journal clutched against his chest. "They're from the Shadowlands. Someone summoned them."

Shadowstalkers were assassins—the Order used them rarely, only for executions, only for targets deemed too dangerous to approach directly. The fact that someone had sent them meant his death had been ordered.

By someone with the authority to command entities from the Shadowlands.

A Warden. Or higher.

The second creature attacked from the left. Drevik's foresight kicked in—a flash of possible futures, each one showing a different angle of the strike. He dodged left instead of right, feeling the creature's claws pass through the space where his throat would have been.

"There are too many," Lexi gasped. Four of them now, spreading through the room, cutting off the door. "We can't fight them in here."

"The window." Drevik grabbed her arm, pulling her toward the small opening. "Can you shadow-walk us both?"

"From inside a warded building? Not reliably."

"Then we go out the hard way."

He didn't give her time to argue. The journal went into his jacket, pressed flat against his ribs, and then he was throwing himself at the window, leading with his shoulder.

Glass shattered. Cold air rushed in. They fell.

Drevik reached for his power, wrapping it around both of them, turning the fall into something almost like flight. They hit the courtyard grass rolling, and behind them, the shadow creatures poured through the broken window like smoke from a fire.

"Run," Lexi snarled, pulling him to his feet. "I'll hold them here."

"Like hell."

"Drevik, you have the journal. That's proof of everything your father found. If you die here, his work dies with you." Her eyes were fierce, bright with something that looked almost like tears. "Go. Get it to Elder Sevestian. I'll catch up."

The first Shadowstalker landed between them, its void-face turning toward Lexi. Drevik didn't think—he moved. His power surged, and he shadow-walked the three steps needed to place himself at Lexi's side.

"I told you," he said, his voice raw, "I'm done doing things alone."

She looked at him, really looked at him, and the Shadowstalkers closed in.

They fought together.

It shouldn't have worked—Drevik was still an acolyte, his training incomplete, and Lexi was exhausted from the effort of breaking him out of confinement. But there was a rhythm to it, a synchronization that felt less like practice and more like instinct.

Lexi struck high while Drevik swept low. She created openings with her shadows, and he filled them with precise strikes. When a creature came at Drevik from behind, Lexi was already there, her blade of solidified darkness cutting through its form.

It was like fighting with an extension of himself.

"Left," she called, and he was already moving, his foresight showing him the attack a second before it came. "Two more incoming."

The Shadowstalkers weren't endless, but they were relentless. Every time Drevik dissolved one into mist, another seemed to take its place. The cold was seeping into his bones, making his movements sluggish, slowing his reactions.

"Drevik!" Lexi's voice cut through the haze. "Your power—use it to track the summoning anchor."

Right. Shadowstalkers didn't just appear—they had to be anchored to the mortal realm by a summoner's will. If he could find that thread and sever it—

He reached for his foresight, pushing past the immediate futures to the deeper currents beneath. There. A thin line of power running from the largest Shadowstalker, threading back toward the dormitory, toward—

His breath caught.

The thread led to Warden Blackwood's office.

"The summoner is Corvin Blackwood," he gasped. "Lexi, the Warden who interrogated me—he's the one who—"

A Shadowstalker's claw caught him across the ribs, and pain exploded through his side. He staggered, his concentration breaking, and the futures scattered like leaves in a storm.

"Drevik!" Lexi was there, her body between his and the creatures, her shadows forming a protective barrier. "Stay with me."

"I'm fine." He wasn't fine. Blood was soaking through his shirt, and his vision was starting to blur at the edges. "The anchor—I can break it, but I need time."

"Then take it." She planted her feet, and her shadows exploded outward, forming a wall of darkness between them and the Shadowstalkers. "I'll hold them. Do what you need to do."

He wanted to argue. He wanted to pull her back, to protect her the way she was protecting him. But the Shadowstalkers were pressing against her barrier, their void-faces pressing through like fingers through wet paper, and he knew he didn't have time for pride.

Drevik closed his eyes and reached for the thread.

It was slippery, coated in something that felt like oil and wrongness. Blackwood's will held it tight, but Drevik could feel the places where it frayed—the corners the older man hadn't bothered to reinforce because he'd never expected anyone to look.

Arrogant, Drevik thought. You sent these things to kill an acolyte, and you didn't even bother to do it properly.

He wrapped his own power around the thread and pulled.

The backlash was immediate and violent. Drevik's body arched as energy surged through him—Blackwood's will meeting his, the Warden's power crushing down on him like a physical weight. For a moment, he felt the older man's surprise, his fury, his cold calculation as he realized what was happening.

And then he felt something else: recognition.

Marcus's boy, came the thought, pressing through the connection like a knife. You have your father's eyes. And his foolishness.

The thread snapped.

Drevik crashed to the ground, his body screaming in protest, every nerve ending on fire. The Shadowstalkers dissolved instantly, their forms collapsing into wisps of darkness that scattered on the wind.

Silence.

"Drevik?" Lexi was at his side, her hands pressing against his wound, her face pale. "Stay awake. Look at me."

He looked at her. Her green eyes were bright with unshed tears, her auburn hair coming loose from its braid, and there was a cut across her forehead where something had grazed her. She looked fierce and fragile and terrified all at once.

"I told you," he managed, his voice rough, "I learned from you."

"What?"

"Don't do anything stupid." He tried to smile, but his face didn't seem to want to cooperate. "That's what you always tell me."

Her laugh was half a sob. "You absolute idiot." Her hands pressed harder against his side. "You could have died. You almost died. For a journal?"

"For my father's work." He reached up, his hand shaking, and touched the cut on her forehead. "You're hurt."

"I'm fine. You're the one bleeding out in a courtyard."

"I'm not bleeding out." He wasn't, probably. The wound was deep, but it had missed anything vital. He'd had worse in training accidents. "The journal's safe. That's what matters."

"That's not—" She broke off, her jaw tightening. "You matter, Drevik. You stubborn, reckless, infuriating man. You matter."

There was something in her voice that made the pain recede, just a little. Something that made the cold night air feel warmer.

"Lexi—"

"I'm going to kill whoever did this." Her voice had gone flat, dangerous. "Blackwood. You said it was Blackwood."

"He's the one who summoned the Shadowstalkers." Drevik tried to sit up, winced, and let Lexi push him back down. "He interrogated me. He asked about the journal, about my father's research. He wanted to know what I knew."

"Because your father found something." Lexi's hands were gentle despite the pressure she was keeping on his wound. "Something dangerous enough to kill for."

"Let's find out."

He pulled the journal from his jacket, wincing at the movement. The leather was stained with his blood now, the cover slick under his fingers. But the pages inside were still intact.

"We need somewhere safe," Lexi said. "Somewhere the Wardens won't look."

"The old archives." Drevik had found the abandoned section during his first month, when he'd been exploring the compound's forgotten corners. "No one goes there anymore. The preservation wards are still active, but the location isn't monitored."

Lexi nodded, her expression grim. "Can you walk?"

"With help."

She pulled his arm over her shoulder, taking most of his weight as they moved toward the shadows at the courtyard's edge. Her body was warm against his, solid and real, and despite everything—the pain, the blood loss, the creatures that had just tried to kill him—Drevik felt something shift between them.

She'd come for him. She'd risked everything—her position, her safety, her family's standing in the Order—to break him out of confinement. And then she'd stood between him and death without hesitation.

"I owe you," he said quietly as they moved through the darkness. "For all of this."

"You don't owe me anything." Her voice was soft, barely audible. "I did it because I couldn't—because watching them take you was—"

She didn't finish. She didn't need to.

The old archives were exactly as Drevik remembered: dusty, forgotten, and utterly silent. The preservation wards hummed at a frequency just below hearing, keeping the worst of the decay at bay. They found a corner hidden behind collapsed shelves, and Lexi helped him settle against the wall before taking position by the entrance, her shadows spreading out to warn of any approaching threat.

Drevik opened the journal with shaking hands.

His father's handwriting covered the pages—familiar strokes that brought a lump to Drevik's throat. He'd avoided reading the final entries for months, unable to face the words Marcus Markinsoul had written in the days leading up to his death.

But now, with blood still seeping through his shirt and Lexi standing guard in the darkness, he turned to the last pages.

The pattern is clear now, his father had written. The breaches aren't random. Someone in the Order is deliberately weakening the Veil, allowing Shadowlands entities through for study. Or worse—for power.

Three Wardens have access to the necessary protocols. Three have the authority to suppress investigation. I've tracked the authorization seals to a single source, but I need more proof before I can bring this to the Veiled Ones.

The summoning rituals match ancient texts—pre-Order texts. Someone isn't just using Shadowlands creatures. They're trying to bring something through. Something that was banished centuries ago.

If you're reading this and I'm gone, trust no one above the rank of Warden. The corruption goes deep. The evidence is in the eastern archive, behind the Veiled One's seal. Use the cipher below to decode the authorization trails.

And Drevik—if you've found this, I'm sorry I couldn't be there to teach you what you need to know. Your mother and I always knew you'd be special. The bloodline runs strong in you, son. Stronger than it ever did in me.

Be careful. Trust your instincts. And remember: the shadows aren't just our tools. They're our responsibility.

—M

Below the signature was a string of characters—a cipher that Drevik recognized from the codes his father had taught him as a child. He could decode it, but it would take time.

Time they didn't have.

"Lexi." He looked up, finding her watching him from across the room. "My father found evidence of corruption. Someone in the Order is deliberately weakening the Veil, letting Shadowlands entities through. And they killed him to keep it secret."

"The Shadowstalkers."

"Sent by Blackwood. But there's more." Drevik turned the page, showing her the cipher. "This will tell us who else is involved. Who's behind everything. But I need to decode it first."

"Then decode it." She moved to his side, settling next to him against the wall. "I'll keep watch."

He should have argued. He should have told her to rest, to let him handle it, to stop taking care of him when she'd already done so much. But his body was heavy with exhaustion, and her presence was warm at his side, and he found himself leaning into her as he worked.

The cipher was complex but not impossible. His father had used a variant of an old Order code, one that Drevik had learned in his first year of training. Letter by letter, he translated the string into something readable.

Authorization source: WB

Co-conspirators: RV, STH, MK (deceased)

Target: Veiled One ascension

Next breach: 3 days after next new moon

Entity designation: THE HOLLOW KING

Drevik's blood ran cold.

"WB," Lexi breathed, reading over his shoulder. "Warden Blackwood."

"And RV, STH..." Drevik's mind raced through the Order's hierarchy. "Ruth Vane, the head of acolyte training. Samuel Thorn—"

"Thornwood." Lexi's voice was hollow. "That's my mother's maiden name. She was a Thornwood before she married my father."

The implications hung between them. If Lexi's maternal family was involved in the conspiracy...

"My father," she whispered. "He'd know. If Uncle Samuel was part of this, my father would have known."

"Maybe he didn't." Drevik reached for her hand, his fingers finding hers in the darkness. "Maybe he was kept in the dark like everyone else."

"Or maybe he's part of it too." Her grip tightened on his, almost painful. "Maybe this whole time, when he was telling me to stay away from you, to report on you, to keep my distance—he was protecting the conspiracy, not the Order."

The pain in her voice was palpable. Drevik pulled her closer, ignoring the protest from his wounded side, and let her press her face against his shoulder.

"We'll find out," he said quietly. "We have proof now. We take this to the Veiled Ones, and—"

"And what? Hope they believe an acolyte and a disgraced Seeker over three Wardens?" Her laugh was bitter. "Drevik, the moment we show this to anyone, Blackwood will know. He'll have us killed before we can say a word."

"Then we find another way." He turned to face her, his free hand coming up to cup her jaw. "Lexi, look at me."

She looked up, her green eyes bright with unshed tears.

"I've been alone my whole life," he said quietly. "Even when my father was alive, I knew he was hiding things from me. I learned to rely on myself, to trust no one, to keep everyone at arm's length. But you—" He stopped, swallowed hard. "You broke through that. You forced your way in and refused to leave, and I don't want to do this alone anymore. Whatever happens next, I want you by my side."

Her breath caught. "Drevik—"

"I know this is complicated. I know your family might be involved, and I know you're risking everything by helping me. But I need you to know that—" He stopped, the words catching in his throat. "That I—"

She kissed him.

It was nothing like the first time—nothing like that careful, tentative exploration in the training room. This was desperate and raw, her hands fisting in his hair, his grip pulling her closer despite the pain in his side. She tasted like night air and adrenaline, and she kissed him like she was drowning and he was the only air in the world.

When they finally broke apart, both of them were breathing hard.

"I thought I told you not to do anything stupid," she whispered against his lips.

"I learned from you."

Her laugh was shaky, but real. "We're going to figure this out. Together."

"Together."

The word felt strange on his tongue—foreign, unfamiliar, but right in a way that made his chest ache. For the first time since his father's death, Drevik felt something other than alone.

But the journal was still open in his lap, and the cipher still glared up at him from the page. WB. Next breach: 3 days after next new moon. Entity designation: THE HOLLOW KING.

They had proof now. They had names.

And somewhere in the compound, Warden Corvin Blackwood was learning that his Shadowstalkers had failed.

The game had changed.

And Drevik was finally ready to play.
