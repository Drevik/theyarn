Chapter 12: The Full Moon Ceremony

Chapter 12: The Full Moon Ceremony

The moon hung above the university's clock tower like a silver coin waiting to be spent, and somewhere beneath Drevik's feet, two hundred members of the Order of the Eclipsed Veil were preparing to spend it.

He stood at the entrance to the hidden compound, the weight of ceremonial robes heavy on his shoulders. The fabric was midnight blue, embroidered with silver thread that seemed to shift and move in his peripheral vision—shadows given form, the elder who'd presented them explained. The robe of an Initiate transitioning to Acolyte.

"You're not moving," said a voice behind him.

Drevik turned to find Marcus Chen, the senior Warden who'd been assigned as his sponsor for the evening. The man's salt-and-pepper hair was pulled back in a formal knot, his own robes a deep charcoal that marked his higher station.

"Nervous," Drevik admitted.

"Good." Marcus clapped him on the shoulder. "Means you understand what's about to happen. The full moon ceremony isn't just pageantry, boy. It's the one night each month when the Veil thins and every member of the Order can draw power directly from the Shadowlands itself. Tonight, you'll be baptized in that power. It will change you."

"I've already been changed."

A thin smile. "Then prepare to be changed again."

They descended together through the hidden passage beneath the library's eastern wing. The stairs spiraled down into darkness, but torches flickered to life as they passed—actual flames, not electric lights, burning without fuel or smoke.

"The old ways," Marcus explained, noting Drevik's glance. "On this night, we honor tradition."

The central chamber opened before them like the throat of some vast creature, vaulted ceilings lost in shadow, walls carved with symbols that seemed to breathe. Drevik had been here before, during his initial initiation, but tonight it was transformed. Candles lined every surface—hundreds of them, perhaps thousands—their light reflecting off crystals embedded in the stone until the entire space shimmered like the inside of a geode.

And the people.

Members of the Order filled the chamber in a spiral pattern, their robes creating a gradient from the pale silver of the Veiled Ones at the center to the deep midnight of the initiates at the outer edge. Drevik counted perhaps two hundred figures, faces he recognized and faces he didn't, all standing in silence with their eyes fixed on the domed ceiling.

Where a perfect circle of glass revealed the full moon, already beginning to glow with an intensity that defied physics.

"Your place is there," Marcus said, pointing to a gap in the outer ring. "When the Elder calls your name, step forward. Until then, observe. Learn. And whatever you do, don't break the circle."

Drevik moved to his position, acutely aware of the eyes tracking him. Some curious, some hostile, most simply assessing. He was, after all, an outsider—the scholarship student who'd stumbled into their ranks rather than being born to them. Every step felt like walking across a stage under hot lights.

Then he saw her.

Lexi stood three rows inward, her auburn hair loose around her shoulders for the first time since he'd known her. Her robes were the deep green of a Seeker, and they fit her differently than her usual practical clothes—less utilitarian, more ceremonial, hugging her form in ways that made his throat tighten. Candles flickered around her like attendants, and her green eyes found his across the chamber.

For a moment, neither looked away.

She looked powerful. Not the capable, athletic power he was used to seeing from her, but something older, deeper. She looked like someone who could command shadows and summon darkness, because she was. And she was looking at him with an expression he couldn't quite read—something between pride and fear and want.

Want.

The realization settled into his bones. He'd spent weeks watching her push him away, pull him close, alternate between coldness and warmth until he couldn't tell which was the real Lexi. But tonight, stripped of pretense, surrounded by ancient magic, she wasn't hiding anything.

She wanted him.

And gods help him, he wanted her too.

The sound of a bell broke the moment—a deep, resonant tone that seemed to come from the walls themselves. The chamber fell into absolute silence, and Drevik turned toward the center of the spiral.

The Veiled Ones had emerged.

Three figures in pale silver robes, their faces obscured by gauzy fabric that shimmered like moonlight on water. They moved in perfect synchronization, their steps leaving ripples of shadow in their wake. Drevik felt the power radiating from them like heat from a forge, and understood for the first time just how far he had to climb.

"Welcome, children of the Veil." The voice came from all three figures simultaneously, layered and strange. "The moon reaches its zenith. The boundary weakens. We gather to renew our bonds and strengthen our purpose."

Another bell tone, and the moonlight through the glass ceiling intensified, turning from silver to pale gold. Drevik felt it in his blood—a pull, a hunger, a connection to something vast and dark and patient.

"Who presents for the rank of Acolyte?"

Marcus's voice rang out. "Drevik Markinsoul, sponsored by the Wardens, tested and found worthy."

Tested. He thought of the past weeks—the trials, the study, the battles against shadow creatures that had slipped through the Veil. He thought of the blood on his hands and the fear in his gut and the moments when he'd nearly broken. All of it leading here.

"Step forward, Drevik Markinsoul."

He walked the spiral path inward, passing rows of robed figures who watched in silence. The air grew thicker as he approached the center, heavy with power and possibility. When he reached the Veiled Ones, they parted to reveal a basin of black stone filled with what looked like liquid shadow.

"Kneel."

He knelt on cold stone, his eyes level with the basin's rim. The shadow-substance rippled without any wind or movement, responding to his presence.

"Drevik Markinsoul, you came to us as an outsider. You have proven yourself through trial and tribulation. Tonight, the moon blesses you with its full power. Tonight, you become one with the Order in truth."

One of the Veiled Ones produced a silver blade and drew it across Drevik's palm. The cut was shallow, but his blood—dark and red and impossibly bright against his skin—dripped into the basin.

The shadow erupted.

Not violently—more like a sigh, a release. The liquid darkness swirled upward, forming a column that wrapped around Drevik's arm, his shoulder, his chest. He felt it searching, probing, tasting him with something like curiosity.

And then it poured into him.

The pain was immediate and absolute—not physical, but something deeper. The shadow was weighing his soul, measuring his worth, and it was not gentle about it. He felt his memories flashing through his mind: his grandmother's hands, his parents' faces in the one photograph he owned, every failure and triumph and moment of weakness laid bare.

He didn't break.

He couldn't have explained why, but he held on. Not through strength—the shadow was stronger than any strength he had—but through surrender. He opened himself to it, let it see everything, and refused to flinch.

Accepted, something seemed to whisper. Marked.

The shadow receded, leaving him gasping, his robes now marked with patterns of silver thread that hadn't been there before. He looked down at his hands and saw faint lines of darkness beneath his skin, like veins of ink.

"Rise, Acolyte Drevik Markinsoul. The Shadowlands recognize you."

He rose on unsteady legs, and the chamber erupted—not in applause, that would be too mundane, but in a low, resonant hum that seemed to come from every throat simultaneously. The sound vibrated through his bones, welcoming him.

Then the ceremony shifted.

The Veiled Ones began a new chant, and the moonlight intensified again. Drevik felt power flowing into everyone present—each member drawing from the thinned Veil, each feeding on the energy that seeped through from the Shadowlands.

"Pair for the sharing," the Veiled Ones intoned. "Let power flow between you. Let bonds strengthen and grow."

Drevik watched as members turned to each other, forming pairs based on some system he didn't understand. The pairs placed their hands on each other's shoulders, and he could see the power flowing between them—dark and light, shadow and moonlight, moving in circuits that made the whole chamber glow.

"You."

He turned to find Lexi behind him, her face unreadable.

"Me?"

"The pairing is determined by magical affinity. Ours is..." She hesitated. "Strong. The strongest I've felt with anyone."

He didn't know what to say to that, so he said nothing.

"Turn around. Hands on my shoulders."

He obeyed, feeling the warmth of her through the thin ceremonial fabric. She placed her own hands on his shoulders in turn, and for a moment they simply stood there, two people connected by formal necessity.

Then the magic began to flow.

It started as a tingle, a spark where their skin touched. But it quickly became more—a current of power that ran from her to him and back again, building in intensity with each cycle. Drevik gasped as he felt her magic intertwining with his own, their energies learning each other, merging.

And with the merge came something else.

He felt her.

Not just her power, but her. Her emotions, her thoughts, the shape of her consciousness pressing against his own. He felt her pride in her position, her fear of failing her family's expectations. He felt her loneliness—a profound, aching loneliness that had been with her for years, masked by competence and determination.

He felt her attraction to him, raw and unfiltered.

You, she was thinking, the word carrying a weight of meaning beyond language. It's you. I've been fighting it and I can't anymore.

He opened his eyes—when had he closed them?—and found her staring at him, her green gaze luminous in the candlelight. Her lips were parted, her breath coming fast. She was feeling him too—his confusion, his desire, his desperate need for belonging.

I see you, she whispered in his mind. I see all of you.

And I see you.

The magic between them intensified, becoming something more than ritual. This was intimate in ways that transcended the physical, two souls touching directly. Drevik felt himself harden in response, his body reacting to the closeness even as his mind reeled from the emotional connection.

Lexi—

Don't. Her mental voice was ragged. Don't say anything. Just feel.

The chamber around them faded. There was only her—her presence in his mind, her hands on his shoulders, her breath across his face as they swayed closer together. He could have kissed her. He wanted to kiss her. Every fiber of his being ached to close the distance and—

The vision hit him like a fist.

He gasped, his grip tightening on her shoulders as the world shattered around him. Suddenly he wasn't in the chamber anymore—he was elsewhere, watching events unfold in fractured, overlapping possibilities.

The ceremony continues. The moon reaches its peak. A figure in shadow moves toward the Veiled Ones, a blade in hand—

An alarm sounds. Members rush toward a breach in the outer ward. The chamber empties, leaving the Veiled Ones unprotected—

A scream. Blood on silver robes. The Veiled Ones fall, one by one, and with each death, the Veil tears further open—

Shadow creatures pour through. Dozens. Hundreds. The Order is slaughtered—

Drevik tries to stop it. He reaches for the assassin, but he's too slow—

He reaches again. He makes it in time, but the assassin is someone he knows, someone he trusted—

He reaches a third time. He stops the blade. The breach is sealed. But the cost—

The visions fractured into a dozen more possibilities, each one showing him a different path, a different outcome. He saw victories that cost lives, sacrifices that saved no one, choices that led to ruin. He saw Lexi dying in his arms, the Veiled Ones falling, the Order burning.

He saw, through it all, one constant: the alarm was a distraction.

The real attack came from within.

"Drevik!"

He came back to himself with a start, finding Lexi's face inches from his own, her expression tight with concern. The magic between them had faded—when had the ritual ended?—but she was still holding him, still close.

"You collapsed," she said quietly. "Your nose is bleeding."

He touched his face, felt the wetness. Around them, other members were watching with curiosity and concern, but the ceremony continued, the Veiled Ones moving through the crowd.

"What did you see?" Lexi's voice was barely a breath. "Your magic spiked. I felt it. What did you see?"

He wanted to tell her. He wanted to warn her, to share the terrible knowledge pressing against his skull. But the vision had been clear—if he acted openly, if he revealed what he'd Seen, everything would fall apart.

"Nothing," he lied. "Overwhelmed. Too much power."

Her eyes narrowed. She knew he was lying. Their brief connection had been too intimate for deception. But she didn't push, didn't expose him.

"Take a moment," she said instead. "The ceremony continues for another hour. Stay close to me."

Stay close to me. He felt the weight of those words, the promise hidden within them. Later, when this was over, they would talk. They would have to.

But first—

An alarm split the air.

It came from everywhere at once—the walls, the ceiling, the floor itself—a shrieking sound that sent every member of the Order into motion. Drevik felt the chamber's energy shift from ritual calm to battle readiness in an instant.

"Breach," someone shouted. "Outer ward breach!"

Members were running now, streaming toward the exits in organized patterns that spoke of long practice. The Veiled Ones remained at the center, their silver robes shimmering with protective magic.

"Come on." Lexi grabbed his wrist. "We're needed at the perimeter."

Drevik let her pull him toward the exit, but his mind was racing. The vision had shown him this—the alarm, the rush to respond, the chamber emptying. It was happening exactly as he'd foreseen.

And the real attack was still coming.

"Lexi, wait—"

"There's no time for—"

"I know." He stopped, pulling her to a halt in the flow of bodies. "I know this is a distraction."

Her eyes searched his face. "How?"

"I told you. The power was overwhelming." He couldn't explain, not here, not now. But he needed her to believe him. "Lexi, the real attack is inside. Someone's going after the Veiled Ones."

Her expression flickered—disbelief, then something else. She'd felt his magic, felt the strength of his gift. She knew, on some level, that he was capable of seeing things others couldn't.

"The Veiled Ones have guards," she said, but her voice was uncertain.

"Not enough. Lexi, please—"

Another alarm sounded, different from the first. This one came from within the chamber itself.

Drevik spun around, his blood running cold.

The crowd had thinned significantly—perhaps half the members had rushed to respond to the outer breach. Those who remained were turning toward the center of the spiral, where the Veiled Ones stood frozen in place.

Where a figure in dark robes was moving through the shadows, a blade gleaming in their hand.

"Stop!" Drevik shouted, breaking away from Lexi and running toward the center.

He felt the magic in his blood responding, the power he'd absorbed during the ritual rising to meet the moment. He didn't know what he was doing—only that he had to reach them, had to stop what was coming.

The figure in dark robes turned, and Drevik caught a glimpse of a face—

No.

The recognition hit him like ice water. He knew that face. He'd trusted that face.

The assassin smiled, raised their blade, and stepped through a shadow, vanishing completely.

"No!" Drevik reached the Veiled Ones, his chest heaving. "They're inside the shadows! They can Shadow Walk!"

The remaining members were converging now, the ceremony forgotten. Lexi appeared at his side, her face pale.

"Drevik, who was that? You saw their face."

He had. And the knowledge was a weight he wasn't ready to carry.

"Later," he said. "Right now, we have to—"

The lights went out.

Every candle, every magical flame, every source of illumination in the chamber vanished simultaneously. Darkness descended like a physical weight, absolute and complete.

Drevik felt Lexi's hand find his in the dark.

"Stay close to me," she whispered, echoing his earlier words.

Then the screaming started.
