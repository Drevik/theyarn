Chapter 12: Mother's Trail

Chapter 12: Mother's Trail

The Shadowlands didn't smell like death.

Drevik had expected decay, rot, something that spoke of the endless twilight that bathed this parallel dimension. Instead, the air carried notes of ozone and something sweeter—night-blooming jasmine, maybe, though he'd never seen jasmine grow in eternal darkness.

"Stop cataloging and start moving." Lexi's voice cut through his thoughts, sharp but not unkind. Her hand found the small of his back, a touch that would have been purely functional two weeks ago. Now it carried weight. Memory. The press of her fingers against his skin carried echoes of the night before—her breath hot against his neck, the way she'd whispered his name like a promise she hadn't meant to make.

He stepped forward, letting the shadows fold around him.

The transition between worlds had become easier with practice. What once felt like drowning in cold water now felt more like sliding into a bath—still strange, still requiring adjustment, but no longer terrifying. Lexi followed a heartbeat behind, her presence a warmth against the perpetual chill of the Shadowlands.

"Which way?" Mira called from behind them. The dark-haired seeker had insisted on joining despite barely being cleared after her injuries. Drevik suspected she didn't trust him alone with Lexi. Or maybe she didn't trust Lexi alone with him.

The dynamic between the three of them had shifted since he and Lexi had finally stopped dancing around each other. Mira's glances carried something sharper than professional concern, though he couldn't quite read what lay beneath.

Drevik pulled the worn photograph from his pocket—his mother's face smiling up at him, young and bright before everything went wrong. Behind her in the image, barely visible, stood a structure he'd only just recognized during his research in the Order's archives. A spire of black stone that shouldn't exist in the mortal realm.

"North," he said, though cardinal directions meant little here. The Shadowlands operated on different principles—intent, connection, the pull of significant locations. "The spire should be about two miles. If the archives were right."

"And if they weren't?" Lexi's hand pressed more firmly against his back.

"Then we walk until we find something that doesn't want to eat us."

"Comforting." Mira moved up beside them, her movements still carrying a slight favor to her left side. "I feel like I should mention that this is a terrible idea. The Elders specifically forbade anyone from investigating the deeper Shadowlands without full seeker escort."

"Good thing we have a seeker escort, then." Drevik gestured at Lexi.

"An acolyte and a seeker who's currently supposed to be teaching introductory shadow-walking to initiates." Mira's voice carried a thread of something that might have been amusement. "Ivy would be furious if she knew."

At the mention of Ivy's name, something tightened in Drevik's chest. The communication crystal hung against his sternum, warm but silent. Ivy had been pulled away on urgent business—something about a breach at the northern perimeter—and her absence felt like a missing tooth. A constant, dull awareness of absence.

"She'd tell us to be careful and then give us seventeen contingency plans." Lexi's voice softened. "I miss her too."

The acknowledgment surprised him. Lexi had never been particularly warm toward Ivy, their relationship defined by professional respect and occasional friction. But something in her tone suggested she understood what he was feeling.

"Let's just find what we came for," Drevik said, starting forward. "Then everyone can argue about protocols later."

The Shadowlands stretched before them in waves of gray and purple, the landscape folding in on itself in ways that defied mortal geometry. Paths that looked straight curved unexpectedly. Distances compressed or expanded based on factors he couldn't identify. The Order's teachings spoke of the Shadowlands as a realm of intention—where willpower shaped reality as much as physical movement.

They walked in silence for what felt like twenty minutes but could have been hours. Time moved differently here, another reason the Order limited exposure. Extended stays in the Shadowlands could age a person unpredictably, or not at all. Some seekers returned after apparent days to find years had passed in the mortal realm.

"There." Drevik pointed toward a shadow that didn't quite match its surroundings. The darkness pooled in a way that suggested structure rather than absence. "Do you see it?"

Lexi squinted, her eyes shifting into the enhanced vision that came with her training. "A building. Maybe. It's hard to tell with the way light behaves here."

"I see it clearly." Mira moved past them, her earlier caution apparently forgotten. "A tower of some kind. Black stone, just like your photograph."

The confirmation sent a jolt through Drevik's system. For weeks, he'd been chasing fragments—archival references, secondhand accounts, the mysterious circumstances of his mother's disappearance. The Order had told him she'd been lost to the Shadowlands during a routine mission. Just another casualty of the eternal war between dimensions.

But the archives had told a different story. Notes hidden in margins, supply requisitions for locations that didn't exist on official maps, and finally, the photograph—taken years after her supposed death.

"Stay close." Lexi's hand found his again, their fingers intertwining without conscious thought. The newness of the gesture still caught him off guard. Three days of physical intimacy hadn't erased years of rivalry and mistrust, but it had created something new—a foundation they were still learning to walk on.

As they approached the structure, details emerged from the shadows. The spire rose from a base of twisted rock, its surface carved with symbols Drevik didn't recognize. Not the Order's traditional glyphs, something older. Something that made his skin prickle with recognition he couldn't explain.

"She was here." The words left his mouth before he could question them. "I can feel it."

"Shadow sensitivity," Mira said, her voice careful. "Your mother's bloodline carried strong talents. It makes sense you'd pick up on her residual presence."

"Not just presence." Drevik moved toward the entrance, a doorway carved into the base of the spire. "She left something for me. I can feel it."

The interior of the structure defied its external dimensions. What should have been a cramped tower opened into a vast chamber, its ceiling lost in shadow. Candles lined the walls—not burning, but glowing with their own internal light. Someone had prepared this place. Someone had been maintaining it.

"By the Veiled Ones," Lexi breathed, her grip tightening on his hand. "This is a sanctuary."

Drevik had read about sanctuaries during his training—hidden spaces within the Shadowlands where members of the Order could find refuge during extended missions. They were supposed to be rare, heavily guarded secrets passed down through generations.

But this one wasn't on any map he'd studied.

"Look at the walls." Mira moved deeper into the chamber, her voice hushed with something approaching reverence. "These carvings... they're not Order work. This predates the Order."

Drevik followed her gaze, studying the symbols carved into the stone. They seemed to shift under his attention, revealing layers of meaning that slid away before he could grasp them. But one image remained constant—a figure surrounded by radiance, arms outstretched, with something emerging from the chest.

"Is that...?"

"A heart-bond ritual." Lexi's voice carried an edge of disbelief. "But that's impossible. The Order developed heart-bond magic only three hundred years ago."

"Apparently not." Mira traced one of the carvings with her fingertips. "Someone's been teaching us things we claimed to discover."

A sound echoed from deeper within the structure—not threatening, but deliberate. Footsteps, slow and measured, approaching from a passage Drevik hadn't noticed.

"Someone's here." He pulled Lexi behind him, instinct overriding training. The protective gesture felt natural now, a reflex born from their new connection.

"Wait." Mira held up a hand. "Look at the shadows."

The darkness in the passage wasn't moving toward them—it was retreating. Drawing back like a curtain to reveal a figure made entirely of shadow and light, its form shifting between humanoid and something less defined.

Drevik's heart stopped.

The entity's face held no features, yet he could feel its attention fixed on him. Not hostile. Not hungry. Curious.

"You carry her blood." The voice came from everywhere and nowhere, resonating in his bones rather than his ears. "The mother's line continues."

"You knew my mother?" Drevik stepped forward despite Lexi's hand trying to hold him back. "What happened to her? Where is she?"

"Safe. Hidden. Protecting what must be protected." The entity's form flickered, showing glimpses of something vast behind the humanoid shape. "She left preparations for you. Knew you would come."

The shadows near the eastern wall rippled, and a small alcove revealed itself. Inside sat a wooden box, aged but intact, carved with the same symbols that covered the walls.

"That's hers." Drevik didn't question the certainty. He knew it the way he knew his own name. "She left that for me."

"Then take it, blood of her blood." The entity began to fade, its form dissolving back into the ambient shadow. "But know that truth carries weight. What you learn here cannot be unlearned."

"Wait—I have questions—" Drevik moved toward the fading presence, but Lexi caught his arm.

"Let it go. Shadow guides don't give straight answers. It's like pulling information from a particularly cryptic riddle." Her voice carried forced lightness, but her eyes remained fixed on the alcove. "Whatever your mother left, it's been waiting for you. Don't keep it waiting longer."

He approached the alcove slowly, aware of Mira and Lexi flanking him. The box sat on a pedestal of black stone, unmarked by the dust that covered everything else in the Shadowlands. Whatever protected this place had kept it pristine.

The lid opened easily, revealing contents wrapped in cloth that shimmered with protective enchantments. Drevik's hands trembled as he pulled back the fabric.

A journal. A small crystalline orb. A sealed envelope with his name written in handwriting he recognized from birthday cards and homework help sessions—his mother's careful script.

"Drevik." Mira's voice cut through his frozen moment. "The crystal. It's activated. Someone's trying to contact us."

Not us. Him. The crystal pulsed with light, and Drevik realized it was a communication device—but more sophisticated than anything the Order currently used. The enchantments layered through it spoke of power he'd only read about in theoretical texts.

He touched the crystal's surface, and Ivy's face materialized in the air above it.

"There you are." Her voice carried relief mixed with frustration. "I've been trying to reach you for an hour. The communication crystals we carry don't work in the deep Shadowlands."

"Ivy." Her name came out rougher than intended. "We found something. A sanctuary. My mother—"

"I know." Her image flickered, showing the strain of maintaining connection across dimensions. "I've been researching since you left. Drevik, your mother didn't disappear. She was hidden. By someone inside the Order."

The words hit like a physical blow. "What?"

"The archives have gaps—deliberate gaps. Someone's been removing information about her for years. But I found fragments. She was placed in the Shadowlands deliberately, given sanctuary status, and then..." Ivy's image wavered. "And then the records just stop. Like she ceased to exist."

"Because she was hiding." Lexi's voice cut through, her mind clearly working through implications. "Someone in the Order wanted her gone, but couldn't kill her. So they hid her instead."

"Or she chose to hide." Mira moved to examine the journal Drevik had set aside. "From someone. Or something."

Ivy's image stabilized, her eyes finding Drevik through the connection. "There's more. The Elders have called a meeting about you. Tomorrow night. They're discussing your 'rapid advancement' and whether it represents a threat to Order stability."

"They know I'm asking questions."

"They know you're getting close to something." Ivy's expression shifted to something softer, the professional concern giving way to personal worry. "Be careful. Whatever you find there, think twice before you share it with anyone."

The connection faded before he could respond, leaving Drevik staring at empty air.

"We need to move." Lexi's hand found his shoulder, squeezing with reassurance. "If the Elders are already suspicious, we can't be found here."

"She's right." Mira had opened the journal, scanning pages with trained speed. "This is... Drevik, your mother was researching something. Something about bloodlines and activation rituals. I don't understand half of it, but she references 'the vessel' repeatedly. And she mentions you."

The envelope sat in his hands, heavier than paper should be. His mother's handwriting on the front, his name in careful letters. Whatever was inside had been meant for him alone.

"Take everything." Lexi gathered the crystal and journal, tucking them into the pack she'd brought. "We can examine it somewhere safer."

Drevik barely registered her words. His attention was fixed on the envelope, on the possibility of answers contained within. After years of wondering, of being told his mother was simply gone, of accepting that he might never know the truth—

He opened it.

The letter inside was brief, written in the same careful hand. But the words it contained made the air leave his lungs.

My dearest Drevik,

If you're reading this, you've found the sanctuary I prepared for you. You've inherited my gifts—gifts that make you special and dangerous in equal measure.

I didn't disappear. I was hidden. And I chose to stay hidden because the alternative was watching you become a weapon for people who see you as nothing more than a vessel for power they can't access themselves.

The Order isn't what it seems. The Elders serve purposes they've kept secret for generations. They don't protect the balance—they manipulate it. And they've been waiting for someone with your specific heritage to appear for a very long time.

Trust the women who choose to love you. Your heart will be your guide when nothing else can be trusted.

And above all, remember: Don't trust the Elders. They know what you are. They're not sending you to save anyone—they're sending you to activate what's inside you.

I'm still watching. Still protecting what must be protected. When the time is right, we'll meet again.

All my love, Your mother

Drevik read the words twice, three times, letting them sink into his understanding. When he finally looked up, he found Lexi watching him with an expression that mixed concern with something deeper.

"What does it say?"

He handed her the letter without speaking. Mira moved to read over her shoulder, and the silence in the sanctuary stretched like a held breath.

Lexi finished first, her jaw tightening as she processed the implications. "This changes everything."

"Or confirms what we already suspected." Mira's voice carried a hard edge. "The Order has always had secrets. But this..."

"They're using me." Drevik's voice sounded hollow to his own ears. "Everything they've told me about rising through the ranks, about proving myself—they have another agenda entirely."

"Then we need to figure out what it is before they succeed." Lexi folded the letter carefully, tucking it into the pack with the other items. "We need to get back. We need to plan."

"We need to tell someone." Mira's eyes met Drevik's, and he saw the question she wasn't asking aloud. The question about trust and who deserved it.

"Not yet." He surprised himself with the certainty in his voice. "Not until we understand more. My mother said to trust the women who love me. That means we keep this between us for now."

Lexi's hand found his, her fingers threading through with practiced ease. "Then we keep it between us. All of it."

The return journey passed in tense silence. Drevik felt the weight of the journal in his pack, the crystal, the letter—evidence of a conspiracy that reached the highest levels of the Order. Every shadow seemed to hold watching eyes. Every step felt like it carried him further from the person he'd been when he entered the Shadowlands.

By the time they emerged in the mortal realm, the sun had set. Hours had passed in what felt like a single afternoon—a reminder of how differently time moved in the Shadowlands.

Mira split from them almost immediately, claiming exhaustion that Drevik suspected was an excuse. He watched her go, noting the rigid set of her shoulders.

"She's struggling." Lexi spoke softly, standing close enough that her shoulder pressed against his arm. "With us. With what we've become to each other."

"I know." Drevik turned to face her, taking in the exhaustion written in her features, the worry she was trying to hide. "Are you struggling?"

The question surprised her—he could see it in the slight widening of her eyes. But she recovered quickly, falling back into the directness that defined her.

"I'm struggling with how much I care." The admission came reluctantly, as if the words fought against her natural reserve. "I didn't expect this, Drevik. I didn't expect you."

"Is that a problem?"

"It's a complication." She stepped closer, close enough that he could feel the warmth radiating from her skin. "I've spent years defining myself by my training, my rank, my ability to protect others. And now there's someone I want to protect more than any mission. More than my position."

Her hand came up to rest against his chest, over his heart. The gesture was simple, intimate in a way that went beyond physical.

"I'm not asking you to solve this tonight," she continued. "I'm just... telling you where I am. So you know."

Drevik covered her hand with his own, feeling her pulse jump at the contact. "I know where I am too. Standing right beside you."

The words were inadequate for the weight of what he felt, but her smile suggested she understood anyway. She rose on her toes, pressing her lips to his in a kiss that started gentle and deepened into something more urgent. When they finally separated, both slightly breathless, he saw something new in her eyes—not just affection, but commitment. Partnership.

"We should debrief," he said, though his body argued against anything that required moving away from her.

"We should." She didn't move either. "Tomorrow. After we've processed everything."

"After the Elders' meeting."

"That too." Her hand slid from his chest to his jaw, cupping his face. "Be careful tomorrow. If what your mother wrote is true..."

"I know." He turned his face into her palm, pressing a kiss to the center of her hand. "I'll have you watching my back."

"Always."

They stood in silence for another moment, the weight of the day pressing against them. Tomorrow would bring new challenges, new dangers, new revelations. The Elders were watching. His mother was hiding. And somewhere inside him, something was waiting to be activated—something the Order had been planning to use him for since before he knew they existed.

But for now, in this moment, he had Lexi's hand in his and the knowledge that he wasn't facing any of it alone.

"Let's go home," he said finally.

She nodded, her fingers tightening around his. "Together."

As they walked back toward campus, the shadows seemed to follow—watching, waiting, patient as only eternal darkness could be. And somewhere in the depths of the Shadowlands, in a sanctuary built by hands that predated the Order itself, the echo of his mother's presence lingered like a promise.

Trust the women who love you.

He intended to.

The communication crystal pulsed again as they reached the edge of campus. Ivy's voice emerged before her image, rushed and worried.

"Drevik, I found something else. There's a prophecy in the restricted archives—mentioning someone with your exact heritage. It says 'when the vessel carries the shadow's heart, the gates will open of their own accord.'" A pause. "Drevik, I think you're the vessel. And I think the gates they're trying to open aren't for protection—they're for something else entirely."

The connection cut before he could respond, leaving him standing in the darkness with Lexi's hand still wrapped in his and the weight of revelation settling onto his shoulders.

The Elders weren't protecting anyone.

They were waiting for him to open doors that should remain closed.

And tomorrow night, they would learn just how much he knew about their plans.
