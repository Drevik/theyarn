Chapter 14: Evidence and Escalation

Chapter 14: Evidence and Escalation

The message waited for him in the last place anyone would think to look.

Drevik stood in his new quarters—his own quarters, a privilege of his promotion to Acolyte—and stared at the leather-bound journal Seraphina had pressed into his hands three days ago. He'd searched it twice already, finding nothing but her cramped notes on shadow manipulation techniques and observations about lunar phase correlations. Nothing useful.

But something had nagged at him since his conversation with Ivy. She wouldn't have risked meeting you just to pass along study materials.

He flipped to the back of the journal, running his fingers along the inside of the leather binding. The spine crackled under his touch—older than the pages, slightly uneven. His thumbnail caught on a seam he hadn't noticed before.

A hidden compartment.

The folded paper that slid out was thin as onion skin, covered in Seraphina's precise handwriting.

Names, he realized, his heart hammering against his ribs. She gave me names.

The list was devastating in its simplicity.

Seven names. Seven members of the Order of the Eclipsed Veil who Seraphina believed were working to weaken the barrier between the mortal realm and the Shadowlands. Each entry included a location, a role within the Order, and a single line of corroborating evidence.

His eyes caught on the fourth name and locked there.

Mira Blackwood — Warden, Western Chapter. Based in Seattle. Attends all regional gatherings. Present at the Breach incident in Portland, 2019. No official record of her presence exists.

A Warden. Seraphina had implicated a Warden.

Drevik's mouth went dry. Wardens were the Order's elite operatives, the ones who hunted Shadowlands entities that breached into the mortal realm and kept the peace between chapters. Accusing one of treason was akin to accusing a general of aiding the enemy.

He read the next line and nearly dropped the paper.

Mira Blackwood was the investigating officer on the Markinsoul case. She closed it as 'natural causes' within forty-eight hours.

His parents. This woman had signed off on the investigation into his parents' deaths.

The room seemed to tilt around him. He'd spent three years believing his parents had died in a car accident—an unfortunate collision with a drunk driver on a rain-slicked highway. The Order had told him there was nothing suspicious, nothing to suggest Shadowlands involvement.

And now Seraphina's list suggested the person who'd made that determination might be compromised.

He thought of his father's journal, the one his grandmother had passed down to him. He'd only skimmed it, assuming it contained personal reflections rather than anything relevant to the Order. But if his parents had been getting close to something dangerous—

A knock at his door made him shove the paper back into the hidden compartment, heart racing.

"Drevik?" Lexi's voice, muffled through the wood. "You decent?"

"Coming." He slid the journal under his pillow and crossed to the door.

Lexi stood in the hallway wearing her training gear—tight black leggings, a fitted tank top, her auburn hair pulled back in a severe ponytail. She looked ready for combat. The expression on her face suggested she'd already fought several battles this morning.

"We need to talk." She pushed past him into his room without waiting for an invitation.

"Please, come in," he said dryly, closing the door behind her.

She turned to face him, arms crossed over her chest. "You've been avoiding me."

"I've been busy. There's a difference."

"Three days, Drevik. You've had three days since your promotion ceremony, and you've been impossible to find." Her green eyes searched his face with an intensity that made him want to look away. "What's going on?"

He could tell her about the list. He should tell her about the list. But Seraphina's warning echoed in his memory: Trust no one. Not even the people you want to trust.

"I'm adjusting to my new responsibilities," he said instead. "They've got me shadowing Elder Corvin for the next week. Learning the administrative side of chapter operations."

Lexi's jaw tightened. "That's not what I'm asking about, and you know it."

"What do you want me to say, Lexi?"

"The truth." She stepped closer, close enough that he could smell the faint traces of lavender soap on her skin. "I thought we were past the point where you hide things from me."

The accusation stung because she wasn't wrong. Since his initiation, Lexi had been his guide, his trainer, the person who pushed him to be better even when he wanted to quit. They'd bled together, fought shadow creatures together, shared a bed in a safe house when the alternative was freezing to death during a winter mission gone wrong.

She'd seen him at his worst. He owed her more than deflection.

"I found something," he admitted quietly. "Something Seraphina gave me before she disappeared."

Lexi's expression shifted, suspicion giving way to sharp attention. "What kind of something?"

"Names. Of people inside the Order who might be working with the Shadowlands."

Her eyes widened. "How many names?"

"Seven." He held her gaze. "Including a Warden."

"Shit." She ran a hand through her hair, the composed mask cracking. "Drevik, do you understand what you're saying? You can't just accuse a Warden of—"

"I'm not accusing anyone of anything yet. I'm telling you what I found."

"Who else knows?"

"Just you." He paused. "And whoever Seraphina was working with. She mentioned a contact before she went to ground, but she didn't give me a name."

Lexi absorbed this in silence, her mind clearly racing through the implications. Drevik watched her process the information, watched her weigh the risks of knowing against the risks of ignorance.

Finally, she exhaled. "Okay. We figure this out together. Just like always."

"Lexi..."

"Don't." Her voice sharpened. "Whatever you're about to say, don't. I've been with you since the beginning of this mess, and I'm not going to let you face down a potential conspiracy alone because you're afraid of putting me in danger."

"I'm not afraid for you," he said, the words slipping out before he could stop them. "I'm afraid of what happens if I'm wrong. If I drag you into something that blows back on both of us."

Something flickered in her expression—surprise, maybe, or something softer. She stepped even closer, reducing the space between them to inches.

"You don't get to make that choice for me," she said quietly. "I decide what risks I'm willing to take. And I'm telling you right now: I'm willing to take this one."

The air between them felt charged, heavy with unspoken words. Drevik was suddenly, acutely aware of how close she was, of the rise and fall of her chest beneath the thin tank top, of the warmth radiating from her skin.

"Lexi." Her name came out rougher than he intended.

Her gaze dropped to his mouth, then back up to his eyes. "Yeah?"

He wanted to kiss her. The realization hit him with the force of a physical blow. He wanted to close the remaining distance, to taste her lips, to feel her body against his. Three days of avoiding her hadn't dimmed the pull between them—it had only sharpened it.

But before he could move, before he could act on the impulse, another knock shattered the moment.

"Drevik Markinsoul?" An unfamiliar voice, formal and clipped. "Elder Corvin requests your presence in the assignment chamber."

Lexi stepped back quickly, her expression smoothing into neutrality. "That's your summons. You should go."

"Lexi—"

"We'll talk later." She moved toward the door, pausing with her hand on the frame. "And Drevik? Whatever happens in that meeting, remember that you've earned your place here. Don't let anyone make you feel like you don't belong."

She was gone before he could respond, leaving him alone with the ghost of almost-something and the weight of secrets pressing down on his shoulders.

The assignment chamber was located in the oldest part of the compound, deep beneath the university's administration building. Drevik had never been granted access before, and the heavy iron doors and ward-etched walls made it clear why. This was where the Order made decisions that shaped lives.

Elder Corvin waited for him at a long table, flanked by two other figures Drevik didn't recognize. The Elder's silver-streaked hair was pulled back severely, and his dark eyes held the weight of decades of service.

"Acolyte Markinsoul," Corvin said, his voice resonating in the stone chamber. "Please, sit."

Drevik took the indicated chair, acutely aware of the scrutiny from the two strangers—a severe woman with iron-gray hair and a younger man with a scar that bisected his left eyebrow.

"Your promotion to Acolyte comes with new responsibilities," Corvin continued. "And new oversight. As you are no doubt aware, the transition from initiate to acolyte requires a change in handler assignment."

Drevik's stomach dropped. "Sir?"

"Alexa Thornwood has served as your guide during your initiation period. However, chapter regulations prohibit a handler from overseeing an acolyte with whom they share... personal entanglements."

The word 'entanglements' hung in the air like an accusation. Drevik kept his face neutral, but heat crept up the back of his neck.

"I wasn't aware there were any personal entanglements, Elder."

Corvin's expression didn't change. "The Order makes it our business to be aware of such things. Your connection to Acolyte Thornwood has been noted since your first month of training. It is not a condemnation—such bonds can strengthen our order—but it is a conflict of interest for oversight purposes."

Drevik absorbed this in silence. They'd been watching. Of course they'd been watching. The Order saw everything.

"Who will be my new handler?" he asked.

The severe woman spoke for the first time, her voice as cold as her gray eyes. "I am Warden Thessaly. I will be overseeing your continued training and assignment completion."

Warden. They'd assigned him a Warden as a handler. The oversight was either a tremendous honor or a prison sentence dressed up as protocol.

"Acolyte Markinsoul has shown unusual promise," the scarred man added. "His shadow-walking abilities are developing faster than any initiate in the past decade. The Order wishes to... nurture that potential."

There was something in his tone that made Drevik's skin prickle. Nurture wasn't quite the right word. Control felt closer to the truth.

"You will move to independent quarters effective immediately," Corvin said. "Your new duties include attending chapter briefings, contributing to regional intelligence analysis, and preparing for the upcoming gathering in Seattle. Warden Thessaly will provide your full assignment roster."

Seattle. The city where Mira Blackwood was based. The city where the regional gathering would take place in two weeks.

"Will there be an opportunity to meet with other chapters at the gathering?" Drevik asked carefully.

Corvin's eyes sharpened. "The gathering is a formality. Acolytes do not participate in inter-chapter discussions."

"Of course, Elder. I only meant—"

"You meant to assess the scope of your exposure." Corvin's smile didn't reach his eyes. "An appropriate question for an ambitious young acolyte. But remember: ambition without discretion is merely a faster path to failure."

The warning was clear. Stop asking questions.

"I understand, Elder."

"Good." Corvin stood, signaling the end of the meeting. "Warden Thessaly will escort you to your new duties. Welcome to the next phase of your service, Acolyte Markinsoul."

Drevik rose and bowed, the motion automatic after months of training. As he followed Warden Thessaly from the chamber, he felt the weight of the Elder's gaze on his back like a physical thing.

His new quarters were larger than his previous room, but they felt smaller. The stone walls pressed in from all sides, and the single window looked out onto an interior courtyard rather than the campus beyond.

More space, less freedom, he thought bitterly. The Order's idea of a promotion.

Warden Thessaly had left him with a stack of assignment briefings and a warning about curfew that felt more like a threat. "You are being watched, Acolyte Markinsoul. The Order has invested considerable resources in your development. Do not disappoint us."

Now alone, Drevik pulled out his father's journal—the one his grandmother had given him, not Seraphina's—and flipped to the pages he'd only skimmed before.

His father's handwriting was cramped and hurried, the letters slanting aggressively to the right. The early entries were mundane: observations about shadow-walking techniques, notes on lunar phase correlations, complaints about chapter politics. But as Drevik read deeper, the tone shifted.

September 14th — Met with M.B. today. She's concerned about the Portland chapter, says they've been compromised. I told her she was paranoid. She said I'd change my mind when I saw the evidence. She's sending a dossier through channels.

September 21st — The dossier arrived. I don't know what to make of it. If the information is accurate, this goes higher than I ever imagined. I need to verify before I bring it to the Elders.

September 28th — Verification is proving difficult. Records that should exist don't. Witnesses who should be available have been reassigned or retired. Someone is covering tracks, and they're doing it thoroughly.

October 5th — Elara wants me to stop. She's worried about what happens if the baby comes while I'm chasing shadows. I promised her I'd be careful. But I can't let this go. Whatever's happening inside the Order, it's bigger than one chapter. Bigger than one region.

October 12th — Meeting with M.B. at the regional gathering. She says she has names. I'll finally know who I'm dealing with.

The entry after that was dated October 15th—three days after the regional gathering.

Something went wrong. M.B. is dead. They're saying Shadowlands breach, but I was there. I saw what happened. That wasn't a breach. That was an assassination. I have the names she gave me, but I don't know who to trust with them. Elara says we should run. Maybe she's right.

His father had been investigating the same conspiracy. His father had met with someone—M.B., Mira Blackwood?—who had evidence of Shadowlands sympathizers within the Order. And three days after that meeting, his father was dead.

Drevik's hands shook as he turned to the final entry, dated the day before the car accident.

I've hidden the evidence. If you're reading this, I didn't make it. The names are in the usual place. Trust the Veiled One who walks between chapters. She knows more than she's said. She's the only one who might still be alive who knows the truth.

The usual place. Drevik had no idea what that meant. His father's belongings had been returned to his grandmother after the accident, but he'd never thought to search them for hidden compartments. And after his grandmother's death last year, everything had been boxed up and stored.

He needed to get those boxes. He needed to find whatever his father had hidden.

His door opened without a knock, and Drevik spun, shoving the journal under his pillow.

Lexi stood in the doorway, her expression tight with controlled anger.

"They told me I'm not allowed to be your handler anymore," she said flatly. "Apparently, I'm too personally entangled."

"They told me the same thing."

"Did they also tell you they're reassigning me to the Seattle chapter? Effective immediately?"

The words hit him like a physical blow. "What?"

"Two weeks before the regional gathering. I'm being sent ahead as part of the advance security team." She crossed her arms, the gesture more protective than aggressive. "They're separating us, Drevik. Deliberately."

He stood, moving toward her without conscious thought. "Lexi, I—"

"Don't apologize." Her voice cracked slightly. "Just... don't."

"Then what do you want me to say?"

She looked up at him, her green eyes bright with emotions she was clearly fighting to contain. "Tell me you're going to be careful. Tell me you're not going to do something stupid while I'm gone."

"You're the one who always does the stupid things."

A ghost of a smile crossed her face. "Yeah, well. Someone has to keep you interesting."

The silence stretched between them, heavy with everything they weren't saying. Drevik thought about the almost-kiss from earlier, about the way she'd looked at him, about the months of tension that had built between them.

"I'll come find you," he said quietly. "At the gathering. Whatever they're trying to do by separating us, it won't work."

Lexi studied him for a long moment. Then, without warning, she closed the distance between them and kissed him.

It wasn't a gentle kiss. It was desperate and fierce, all the things she couldn't say pressed against his mouth in a single, searing moment. Drevik's hands found her waist, pulling her closer, and she made a sound against his lips that sent heat flooding through his veins.

She tasted like mint and something darker, like the edge of a blade. Her fingers tangled in his hair, and he walked her backward until her shoulders hit the wall beside his door.

"Lexi," he breathed against her mouth.

"Shut up." She pulled him back to her, kissing him harder, deeper. Her body pressed against his, all lean muscle and desperate intensity. "You talk too much."

He laughed against her lips, the sound surprised out of him. "That's rich, coming from the woman who just—"

She silenced him with another kiss, and this time he let her. His hands slid under the hem of her tank top, finding the warm skin of her back, and she shivered at his touch.

They stayed like that for long minutes, kissing in the dim light of his spartan quarters, learning each other in ways they never had before. Drevik felt like he was drowning in her, in the scent of her skin and the taste of her mouth and the desperate way her fingers clutched at his shoulders.

Finally, Lexi pulled back, her breathing ragged. Her lips were swollen, her hair mussed from his hands.

"I should go," she whispered. "Before I do something that gets us both in more trouble."

"Stay."

The word slipped out before he could stop it. Her eyes softened, and for a moment he thought she might actually agree.

"I can't." She touched his cheek, the gesture almost tender. "They're watching, Drevik. They're always watching."

"Then let them watch."

She smiled, but it was sad. "You don't mean that. Not yet." She stepped back, putting distance between them. "I leave in the morning. Early. I probably won't see you before..."

"I'll find you at the gathering."

"You better." She moved toward the door, then paused with her hand on the frame. "And Drevik? Don't do anything stupid while I'm gone. That's my job."

Her voice wavered on the last word, and then she was gone, leaving him alone with the taste of her still on his lips.

He didn't sleep.

Instead, he lay in his narrow bed, staring at the ceiling, his mind racing through everything he'd learned. His father's journal. Seraphina's list. The promotion that felt more like a leash. Lexi's kiss and the way she'd looked at him before she walked away.

Around midnight, a shadow moved in the corner of his room.

Drevik sat up, power gathering in his hands, but a familiar voice stopped him.

"Relax, Acolyte. It's just me."

Ivy materialized from the darkness, her dark hair loose around her shoulders, her expression grave.

"Ivy. What are you doing here?"

"Warning you." She moved closer, keeping her voice low. "The gathering in Seattle is a trap. Multiple chapters, multiple factions. Everyone has an agenda, and half of them involve using you as a pawn."

"Using me for what?"

"Your abilities. Your connection to the Markinsoul line." Her eyes held his. "Your father wasn't just an ordinary member, Drevik. He was the last of a bloodline that the Order thought had died out centuries ago. When they discovered what he could do..."

"What could he do? What can I do?"

Ivy hesitated. "I don't know all of it. But I know it involves the Veil itself—the barrier between our world and the Shadowlands. Your father could manipulate it in ways no one else could. And from what I've seen of your training reports, so can you."

The implications washed over him like cold water. "They're not training me to fight Shadowlands creatures, are they? They're training me to control the Veil."

"They're training you to be a weapon." Ivy's voice was soft. "The question is: whose weapon?"

Drevik thought of the list hidden in Seraphina's journal. Of Mira Blackwood and the seven names. Of his father's desperate final entries.

"I have names," he said quietly. "Seraphina gave me names of people inside the Order who might be working with the Shadowlands."

Ivy's expression flickered. "How many names?"

"Seven. Including a Warden."

"Mira Blackwood."

Drevik went still. "You know her."

"I know of her. She's dangerous, Drevik. More dangerous than you can imagine. And she'll be at the gathering."

"Then the gathering is my chance to get close to her. To find out what she knows about my parents."

Ivy gripped his arm, her fingers digging into his skin. "That's exactly what they want. You walking into a room with a woman who's killed everyone who's ever gotten close to the truth. You'll be dead before the night is over."

"Then what do you suggest?"

"Trust no one." She paused, something shifting in her expression. "Except maybe Lexi. She's requested transfer back to you."

Drevik blinked. "What?"

"After the gathering. She put in a formal request to be reassigned as your partner rather than your handler. It would give her proximity without the conflict of interest." Ivy's mouth twisted wryly. "She's smarter than I gave her credit for."

The news settled into Drevik's chest like a warm weight. Lexi had found a way. Despite everything the Order was doing to separate them, she'd found a way back.

"The gathering is in two weeks," he said. "What do I do until then?"

"Stay quiet. Do your assignments. Don't give anyone a reason to suspect you know more than you should." Ivy moved toward the shadows she'd emerged from. "And Drevik? Your father's evidence—the 'usual place' he mentioned. It's not in his belongings."

"Then where?"

Ivy's form was already fading, her voice coming from somewhere deep in the darkness. "The same place all secrets are kept in this Order. The archives. Behind the Veiled One's portrait in the eastern wing. The eyes are hollow."

And then she was gone, leaving Drevik with more questions than answers and the burning certainty that everything was about to get much, much worse.

He dreamed of his parents that night.

Not the vague, soft-focus memories he usually held onto—the way his mother laughed, the steady strength of his father's hands. No, this dream was sharp and terrible.

He stood in a dark room, shadows pressing in from all sides. His father knelt before a figure in ceremonial robes, the Veiled One's mask gleaming in the dim light.

You understand what we're asking? The voice was neither male nor female, young nor old.

I understand. His father's voice was steady, but Drevik could see the fear in his eyes. But my wife—my son—

Will be protected. That is our covenant.

And if I fail?

The masked figure tilted its head. Then the Veil falls. And everything you love dies.

His father reached out, pressing his palm against a stone tablet covered in symbols that seemed to writhe and shift under Drevik's gaze. Light blazed from the contact—

And Drevik woke, gasping, his palm burning as though he'd touched fire himself.

He stared at his hand in the darkness, half-expecting to see blisters forming. Instead, he found a mark he'd never noticed before: a thin silver line that curved from his wrist to his palm, glowing faintly in the moonlight.

The mark of a covenant.

His father had made a deal with the Veiled One. And somehow, Drevik had inherited the terms.

What the hell did you do, Dad?

He wouldn't find answers by staying in bed. Pulling on clothes in the darkness, Drevik grabbed his father's journal and Seraphina's hidden list, tucking both into the inner pocket of his jacket.

The archives were in the eastern wing. The Veiled One's portrait. The hollow eyes.

And somewhere in Seattle, a Warden who had killed his parents was waiting for him to walk into a trap.

Two weeks. He had two weeks to find the evidence his father had hidden, figure out who among the Order's leadership could be trusted, and prepare for a confrontation that might kill him.

But as Drevik slipped out of his quarters and into the shadowed corridors of the compound, he felt something he hadn't felt since his initiation: certainty.

He was going to find the truth. And when he did, the Order would never be the same.
