Chapter 15: The Darkness Speaks

Chapter 15: The Darkness Speaks

The darkness didn't creep anymore.

It poured.

Drevik gasped awake at 3:47 AM, his bedroom swallowed whole by shadow that moved like water, like blood, like something alive. His lungs burned. His skin prickled with cold fire. And beneath it all, beneath the panic clawing up his throat, a voice whispered directly into his mind.

Found you.

He threw himself upright, sheets tangling around his legs, heart hammering against his ribs. The shadows retreated—not fading, but receding, pulling back like a tide going out, leaving him stranded on the shore of his own trembling body.

"Shit." He pressed his palms against his eyes. "Shit, shit, shit."

Moonlight filtered through his window. The new moon had passed three days ago, which meant the slender crescent now hanging in the sky held barely any power. His abilities should be at their weakest.

Instead, they were screaming.

The whispers slithered through the edges of his consciousness, too fast to parse, too loud to ignore. Not words, exactly—impressions. Hunger. Curiosity. Something vast and patient turning its attention toward him like a spotlight in an infinite dark.

His phone buzzed against his nightstand.

Lexi: You're projecting. Wake up.

Lexi: NOW.

He grabbed the phone, thumbs shaking. I don't know what's happening.

Lexi: Stay there. Don't open any doors. Don't let anything IN.

Too late. The thought surfaced unbidden, terrible certainty settling into his bones. Something had already found its way inside—not through his door, but through him. Through the darkness that had been growing in his shadow since the night he'd first walked between worlds.

Twenty minutes later, Lexi arrived with Seraphina in tow. They'd come through the shadows directly into his apartment, a feat that should have been impossible for anyone but a Veiled One—and certainly not for an acolyte still learning to control her abilities.

"You're glowing." Seraphina's voice cut through the silence, her dark eyes fixed on him with an intensity that made his skin prickle. "Look at his shadow, Lexi. Really look."

Drevik stood in the center of his living room, arms wrapped around himself, trying to stop the trembling. Lexi circled him slowly, her auburn hair loose around her shoulders, her green eyes narrowed in concentration.

"Shadows cling to him." Her voice was barely above a whisper. "They're not following him. They're... emanating from him."

"Yes." Seraphina moved closer, her silver jewelry catching the moonlight and throwing strange reflections across the walls. "They are."

"What does that mean?" Drevik's voice cracked. "What's happening to me?"

Seraphina exchanged a look with Lexi—a loaded glance that spoke of shared history and unspoken conversations.

"Show me your hand," Seraphina said.

He extended his arm, trying to steady his fingers. She took his wrist in her grip, her touch cool and firm, and turned his palm upward. The shadows pooling at his feet surged upward, wrapping around his arm like vines seeking purchase.

Seraphina didn't flinch. She watched the darkness with something that might have been recognition. Might have been fear.

"You're not becoming shadow-touched," she said quietly. "You're not being corrupted by the Shadowlands."

"Then what—"

"You're awakening." Her eyes lifted to his face. "The darkness inside you isn't an infection, Drevik. It's a birthright."

Lexi made tea.

It was such a mundane response to the incomprehensible that Drevik almost laughed. But he sat at his kitchen table anyway, watching her move through his small kitchen with practiced efficiency, finding the mugs and the honey without asking where they were kept. She'd been here before—several times now—and the intimacy of that knowledge settled something in his chest.

Seraphina remained standing, arms crossed, her expression unreadable.

"Start from the beginning," Lexi said, setting a steaming mug in front of him. "What did you hear?"

"Voices. Whispers." He wrapped his hands around the ceramic, letting the heat seep into his frozen fingers. "They said... they said they found me."

"Anything else?"

"Hunger." The word tasted wrong in his mouth. "And patience. Like something very old had noticed me for the first time."

Seraphina's breath caught. "Describe the feeling. Not the words—the feeling."

Drevik closed his eyes, reaching for the sensation that had woken him. "Like being underwater. Like the moment before a lightning strike. Like—" He stopped, a specific image crystallizing. "Like standing in a doorway and realizing the door opens both ways."

"Shit." Seraphina pressed her hand against her face. "Shit. He's actually doing it."

"Doing what?" Lexi's voice sharpened. "Seraphina, you need to tell us what you know. Right now."

"I need to tell him." Seraphina pulled out a chair and dropped into it, her composure cracking for just a moment. "The Order has secrets. That's not surprising—we're literally built on secrets. But some secrets are kept even from the Elders."

Drevik leaned forward. "What secrets?"

"The Shadowlands isn't just a dimension." Seraphina's voice dropped low. "It's a consciousness. A collective awareness made of billions of entities, all connected, all thinking and feeling as one."

"That's not—"

"It's not in any of the texts. I know." She cut Lexi off. "Because the texts were written by the Order, and the Order doesn't want anyone to know the truth."

"What truth?"

Seraphina met his eyes. "The Veiled Ones don't banish entities back to the Shadowlands. They feed them. They send back power, knowledge, pieces of mortal souls—and in exchange, they receive abilities that no human should possess."

The words landed like stones in still water, ripples spreading through Drevik's understanding.

"You're saying—"

"I'm saying the Order has been making deals with something ancient and hungry for centuries. And the entities they've been sending back?" Seraphina's voice went flat. "They're not random. They're carefully selected. They carry pieces of the Order's enemies, dissolved into shadow, fed to the darkness piece by piece."

Lexi stood very still. "How do you know this?"

"Because I've seen someone go through what Drevik is going through." Seraphina's hands tightened around the edge of the table. "Someone who was born with a connection to the Shadowlands so strong that the darkness recognized him as one of its own."

"What happened to them?"

Seraphina's jaw tightened. "The Order happened to them."

The silence stretched between them, thick and suffocating.

Drevik's mind raced through the implications. "If the Order knows what I am—if they find out—"

"They'll either try to use you or destroy you." Seraphina didn't sugarcoat it. "Probably both. A human with an actual connection to the Shadowlands consciousness? You'd be the most valuable asset the Order has ever acquired. And the most dangerous."

"I can't let them know." He looked between them. "Either of you. If they find out you helped me—"

"We're already helping you." Lexi's hand found his across the table, her fingers intertwining with his. "We're already in this. Whatever this is."

"You don't understand—"

"I understand that you're not facing this alone." Her grip tightened. "I didn't spend months training you, fighting beside you, falling—" She stopped, a flush creeping across her cheeks. "You're not doing this alone, Drevik. That's not how this works."

Seraphina watched them with an expression Drevik couldn't read. "There's something else. Something I haven't told anyone."

Both of them turned to her.

"The entities in the Shadowlands—the ones who can communicate through the consciousness—they have a hierarchy. Not all of them are equal. Some are ancient beyond measure. Some are newly formed from the echoes of mortal deaths." She paused. "And some... some were never mortal at all."

"Never mortal?" Lexi frowned. "What does that mean?"

"It means the Shadowlands existed before humanity. Before the earth, even. It's always been there—the space between worlds, the darkness that connects all things." Seraphina leaned forward. "And the entities that were born there, the ones who've never been human? They don't think like us. They don't want like us. But they can recognize those who carry their blood."

Drevik's blood went cold. "Their blood?"

"Half-breeds exist," Seraphina said quietly. "Humans with one parent from the Shadowlands. They're rare—impossibly rare—but they're not impossible. And when they start to awaken to their heritage..." She gestured at the shadows still pooling at Drevik's feet. "This is what it looks like."

The whispers surged at the edge of his consciousness, louder now, more urgent. He could almost parse them—almost understand what they were trying to say.

You are claimed. You are called. You are ours.

"I'm half—?" He couldn't finish the sentence.

"I don't know." Seraphina's voice gentled. "But someone in your lineage wasn't human. And whoever they were, whatever they were, their blood is waking up inside you."

Hours later, after Seraphina had left through the shadows and Lexi had promised to return before dawn, Drevik lay in bed staring at the ceiling.

The darkness had receded again, pulled back into the corners of his room, but it hadn't disappeared. It never really disappeared anymore. It just... waited.

You are claimed.

What did that mean? Claimed by whom? By the Shadowlands consciousness itself? By something older, deeper, more dangerous?

His phone buzzed.

Unknown Number: We need to talk. Meet me at the old library. Come alone. Come through shadow.

He stared at the message, trying to parse whether it was a threat or an invitation. The number wasn't saved in his contacts, but somehow, he recognized the energy behind it. The intention.

Unknown Number: You have questions. I have answers. The Order won't give you either.

Another message followed immediately.

Unknown Number: Your mother is still alive.

His hand tightened around the phone until his knuckles went white.

Unknown Number: Midnight. Come alone. Or don't come at all.

The old library was a building on the edge of campus, abandoned for years, slated for demolition that never seemed to arrive. Drevik had passed it hundreds of times without really seeing it. Now, standing before its weathered doors at midnight, he could feel the shadows pooled inside—shadows that went deeper than the absence of light.

He stepped into darkness and let it carry him.

Shadow Walking had become instinct over the past months, but this felt different. The shadows welcomed him, cradled him, carried him through the space-between-spaces with a tenderness that made his chest ache. When he emerged in the library's depths, he wasn't disoriented.

He was home.

The thought was so jarring he nearly missed his footing. This wasn't home. This was an abandoned building filled with darkness and danger and—

"You came."

The voice came from everywhere and nowhere. Drevik spun, shadows gathering around his hands, ready to strike.

A figure emerged from the darkness between the bookshelves. Tall, gaunt, dressed in black robes that seemed to drink the light. Their face was obscured by a veil of shadow that moved like living smoke.

"The Order calls me the Whisperer." The figure's voice was layered, multiple tones overlapping. "But you can call me what I am."

"What are you?"

"A messenger." The figure moved closer, and Drevik realized with a start that they weren't walking—the shadows were carrying them, the same way they'd carried him. "The consciousness has noticed you, Drevik Markinsoul. It has questions."

"What kind of questions?"

"The kind that require answers you don't have yet." The Whisperer stopped an arm's length away. "Your mother. You want to know where she is."

Drevik's shadows flickered with his emotion. "You mentioned her."

"I know where she's been for the past twenty-one years. I know why she left. I know what she was running from—and what she was running toward." The Whisperer's veiled face tilted. "But information has a price."

"What price?"

"A conversation. Nothing more. The consciousness wishes to speak with you directly."

"The consciousness?" Drevik took a step back. "You mean the Shadowlands itself?"

"I mean the awareness that binds all entities in our realm. The collective that thinks and feels and hungers as one." The Whisperer's voice dropped lower. "It has been watching you since you were born. It has been waiting for you to awaken. And now that you have..."

"What?"

"It has plans for you."

Drevik knew he should refuse. He knew he should turn around, walk away, report this encounter to the Order. But the Whisperer had mentioned his mother—had dangled that information like bait on a hook—and he was starving for answers.

"How do I speak with it?"

"You already are." The Whisperer raised one hand, and the shadows around them surged. "Close your eyes. Reach for the darkness inside you—not with fear, but with acceptance. The consciousness speaks to those who embrace what they are."

He didn't want to. Every instinct screamed against it. But his mother's face floated in his memory—her smile, her voice, the way she'd looked at him the last time he'd seen her, with love and terror and something that might have been recognition.

He closed his eyes.

The darkness rushed in.

It wasn't like Shadow Walking. It wasn't like the Veil Sight that let him perceive the Shadowlands. This was something else entirely—a surrendering of self, a dissolution of the boundaries between him and not-him.

For a moment, he was nothing. Just awareness suspended in infinite black.

Then the consciousness wrapped around him.

Child of shadow. Child of light. You carry our blood in your veins.

The voice was everything—the whisper of wind through dead leaves, the murmur of water in deep caves, the echo of footsteps in empty halls. It spoke without speaking, communicated in concepts that bypassed language entirely.

We have waited for you. So long. So very long.

"Where is my mother?" The words formed in his mind, projected outward.

She is in the place between. The space where your Order cannot reach. She is... protected. And imprisoned.

"By whom?"

By those who fear what she carries. What she created. A pause that felt like the held breath of a dying star. You.

Drevik's awareness flickered. "Me? What do you mean?"

Your mother was one of ours. Not by blood—by choice. She carried the shadow-gift stronger than any human in generations. And when she conceived a child with a mortal man...

"She created me."

She created something unprecedented. A bridge. A living door between our realm and hers. The consciousness pulsed with something that might have been wonder. You are not half-Shadowlands, child. You are something new. Something that has never existed before.

"What am I?"

The answer. The question. The key. The darkness swirled around him. You can walk between worlds in a way no one else can. You can carry our voice into places we cannot reach. You can see futures that branch from choices no one has made yet.

"Why are you telling me this?"

Because the Order will try to destroy you when they learn what you are. And we... we would prefer you survived.

Drevik emerged from the consciousness gasping, his body wracked with cold and trembling. He was on his knees in the abandoned library, the Whisperer standing over him, shadows still swirling.

"You spoke with it." The Whisperer's voice held something like reverence. "You actually spoke with the consciousness."

"What..." Drevik swallowed, trying to find his voice. "What happens now?"

"Now you choose." The Whisperer knelt beside him. "You can take what you've learned back to the Order and hope they believe you—and hope they don't decide you're too dangerous to let live. Or you can keep this secret and learn to use what you are."

"And my mother?"

"Is in the deep Shadowlands. Beyond the reach of the Order, beyond the reach of the entities that hunt at the borders." The Whisperer paused. "But not beyond your reach. Not anymore."

Drevik's head snapped up. "I can find her?"

"You can always find her. The connection between a mother and child of the shadow-blood cannot be broken—not by distance, not by dimension, not by death itself." The Whisperer stood. "Close your eyes. Reach for her with everything you are. Not your mind. Not your magic. Your heart."

He didn't want to. The consciousness had already shown him more than he could process. But his mother's face floated behind his eyes, and the ache of twenty-one years of absence pressed against his chest.

He reached.

And found her.

It was like a thread of light in infinite darkness—a tether connecting him to something vast and distant and achingly familiar. He could feel her heartbeat, feel the rhythm of her breathing, feel the love she still held for him across impossible distances.

She was alive. She was there.

And he knew, with certainty that bypassed thought, exactly where she was.

The Whisperer was gone when Drevik opened his eyes.

The library stood empty around him, shadows pooled in corners, moonlight filtering through broken windows. He was alone—but not alone. The consciousness still lingered at the edges of his perception, watching, waiting.

He pulled out his phone with shaking hands.

Lexi: I need to tell you something. In person. Now.

The response came immediately.

Lexi: I'm on my way. Are you safe?

He considered the question. Was he safe? He'd just learned that the Order might want to kill him, that he was some kind of hybrid bridge between dimensions, that his mother was trapped in a place that shouldn't exist.

I don't know.

It was the most honest answer he had.

Lexi arrived in fifteen minutes, her hair disheveled, her eyes sharp with worry. She'd come through shadow directly into his apartment—something she'd only done a handful of times, always with careful precision. Tonight, she arrived like a storm, shadows swirling around her in agitation.

"What happened?" She crossed to him in three strides, her hands finding his shoulders, steadying him. "You're freezing. Your shadows are everywhere—I could feel them from across campus."

"I spoke with something." He let her guide him to the couch, let her pull a blanket around his shoulders. "Something old. Something that lives in the Shadowlands."

"The consciousness." It wasn't a question. "Seraphina mentioned it."

"It told me what I am." The words came easier than he expected. "What my mother was. Why she left."

Lexi's expression shifted through several emotions—surprise, concern, determination—before settling on something fierce and protective. "Tell me everything."

So he did. He told her about the Whisperer, about the consciousness, about the blood that ran in his veins and the bridge he apparently was. He told her about his mother—trapped but alive, protected but imprisoned, waiting for him in a place beyond the Order's reach.

When he finished, Lexi was silent for a long moment.

"The Order will kill you if they find out." Her voice was steady, but her hands trembled where they rested on his arm. "The Whisperer was right about that. An acolyte who can commune with the Shadowlands consciousness? Who carries shadow-blood? You'd be a threat to everything they've built."

"I know."

"What are you going to do?"

He looked at her—really looked, taking in the worry in her green eyes, the tension in her shoulders, the way she'd come to him without hesitation despite the danger. She'd been his rival once, then his mentor, then something more complicated and precious.

"I'm going to find my mother." The decision crystallized as he spoke it. "But first, I need to learn to control this. Whatever I am, whatever the consciousness wants from me, I can't let it use me. I need to be stronger."

Lexi's jaw tightened. "Then we'll make you stronger."

"We?"

"You didn't think I was going to let you do this alone, did you?" A ghost of a smile crossed her face. "I've invested too much time in your training to watch you get yourself killed by ancient shadow entities."

"Lexi—"

"Shut up." She leaned forward and pressed her forehead against his, her breath warm on his skin. "Whatever you're becoming, whoever you're becoming, you don't have to face it alone. That's not how this works. That's not how we work."

Her lips found his before he could respond—not demanding, not claiming, just there. Present. Steady. A reminder that he wasn't alone in the darkness.

He kissed her back with everything he had.

The kiss deepened, heated, and Drevik felt the shadows respond to his emotion. They swirled around them both—not threatening, but protective, creating a cocoon of darkness that blocked out everything else.

Lexi pulled back just enough to speak, her voice breathless. "Your shadows—"

"I know. I can't control them when I—"

"I don't want you to control them." Her green eyes caught the darkness and reflected it back. "I want you to feel them. I want you to feel everything."

She kissed him again, and this time there was nothing gentle about it. Her hands found the hem of his shirt, pulled it over his head, traced the lines of his chest with fingers that trembled. His own hands found her waist, her back, the curve of her spine.

"Tell me if you want to stop," he whispered against her mouth.

"I don't want to stop." She pulled back long enough to meet his eyes. "I've never wanted to stop with you."

The confession hung between them—raw, vulnerable, honest. Drevik had always known Lexi felt something, had always felt the pull between them, but hearing her say it out loud was different. It made the connection real.

He kissed her slowly this time, savoring the feeling of her lips against his, the warmth of her body pressed close. She made a soft sound in her throat, and the shadows around them pulsed in response.

"Your magic," she breathed. "It's reacting to—"

"I know." He could feel it too—the darkness reaching for her, wrapping around her, not to harm but to connect. "I don't know how to stop it."

"I don't want you to stop it." Her hands found his face, cupping his jaw, forcing him to look at her. "I want to feel what you feel. I want to be part of what you're becoming."

"Lexi, that's dangerous—"

"Everything about you is dangerous." A smile curved her lips. "That's never stopped me before."

She kissed him again, and this time, Drevik stopped fighting. He let the darkness flow through him, let it reach for Lexi, let it create a connection between them that went deeper than skin and bone. He felt her heartbeat, her breath, the rhythm of her thoughts—and knew she felt his in return.

It was intimate in a way that transcended the physical. They were sharing something that couldn't be put into words, something that changed the shape of the space between them.

When they finally broke apart, both breathing hard, Drevik knew with certainty that something had shifted. The bond between them had deepened—had become something more than friendship, more than attraction, more than simple affection.

"What was that?" Lexi's voice was hushed.

"I don't know." He touched her face, traced the line of her cheek. "But I don't regret it."

"Neither do I." She leaned into his touch. "We'll figure this out. Together."

Dawn found them tangled together on his couch, shadows pooled around them like sleeping animals. Lexi's head rested on his chest, her breathing slow and even, her hand curled over his heart.

Drevik stared at the ceiling, processing everything that had happened. The consciousness, the Whisperer, the revelation about his mother—and the new connection forming between him and Lexi.

His phone buzzed. He reached for it carefully, trying not to wake her.

Unknown Number: You've taken the first step. There are many more to come. We will speak again soon.

Unknown Number: Your mother is waiting. Don't make her wait too long.

He stared at the messages, the weight of them settling into his bones. The path ahead was clear, even if the way was shrouded in shadow.

He would find his mother.

He would learn to control what he was becoming.

And he would do whatever it took to protect the people he loved from the Order that would destroy them.

Lexi stirred against him, her eyes fluttering open. "What is it?"

"Nothing." He set the phone aside and pulled her closer. "Nothing you need to worry about right now."

She studied his face, reading the lie in his expression, but didn't push. Instead, she pressed closer, her hand finding his, their fingers intertwining.

"Whatever it is," she murmured against his chest, "we'll face it together."

Drevik closed his eyes and let the darkness carry him into something that felt, for the first time in his life, like belonging.

But even as sleep claimed him, a distant part of his awareness felt something else—a tug, a pull, a thread connecting him to a presence far beyond the walls of his apartment. A presence that waited in the depths of the Shadowlands, patient and ancient and somehow familiar.

His mother was out there.

And now, finally, he knew how to find her.
