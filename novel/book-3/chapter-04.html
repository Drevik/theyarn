Book 3: The Veiled Truth

Chapter 4: Crossfire

The footage played on a loop in his mind. The figure's gait. The angle of the shoulders. The precise way they navigated the corridor.

Seraphina's movements. Seraphina's posture. Seraphina's ghost walking through a restricted archive while the real Seraphina stood beside him, fighting entities in the compound.

Drevik sat on the edge of his bed, elbows on his knees, staring at the wall. Dawn light—manufactured, consistent, reliably false—crept through the warded window. He hadn't slept. The cold weight of the drugging revelation sat in his gut alongside the newer, sharper suspicion.

Mira's movements carried from the kitchen—quiet, efficient, someone who moved through spaces like a field hospital. Katryn's screens hummed from the corner. Lexi had left for early training without waking him—a mercy, or a statement. He couldn't tell which.

The figure moved like Seraphina.

But it wasn't Seraphina. It couldn't be. She'd been beside him during the breach, shadow-walking through entities with the kind of power that left no room for doubt.

Unless she'd been somewhere else too. Unless the Seraphina beside him wasn't entirely her.

He pressed his palms against his eyes. His mother's warning. The compound in his blood. The key inside him. The cage being built around him. Too many threads, all pulling in different directions, none of them leading to answers.

"You're thinking too loud again."

He dropped his hands. Seraphina stood in the doorway, her dark hair loose, her expression watchful. She'd been avoiding him since last night—he'd noticed, even through the exhaustion and the chaos.

"We need to talk."

"Those words never precede anything good." But she crossed to him, settling on the edge of the desk across from him. Her eyes held that particular depth—the Shadowlands staring back through human irises. "What is it?"

He told her about the footage. About the figure. About the way it moved.

She didn't flinch. She didn't deny. She went still, the kind of stillness that spoke of something freezing inside her.

"I have gaps." Her voice came out flat. "In my memory. Nights where I can't account for where I was or what I did. I assumed they were side effects of the Shadowlands contact. I assumed—" She stopped. Swallowed. "I assumed wrong."

"You think you've been—what? Controlled?"

"My heritage makes me susceptible. I've always known that." Her hands curled around the edge of the desk. "The Shadowlands don't just give power. They take. And when they take enough, there's less of me left to resist."

The admission cost her. The tight line of her jaw, the way her knuckles went white against the desk's edge—she'd been living with the possibility that she was compromised, and she'd said nothing.

"Why didn't you tell me?"

"Because I hoped I was wrong." Her dark eyes met his. "And because if I'm right—if something is using me—then I'm the most dangerous person in this room."

"You're not—"

"I might be." No hesitation. "The bond between us doesn't guarantee loyalty. Your mother said so. Anyone can be turned." She looked away. "I'm asking you to watch me. Monitor me. If I have gaps again, I want to know."

The trust in that offer hit harder than any accusation. She was handing him the weapon and turning her back.

"We'll figure it out," he said.

"We don't have time." She stood. "Your meeting with Thornwood is in an hour. You need to decide what you're going to say."

Thornwood's office felt smaller this time. The bookshelves pressed inward. The shadows that bent away from the Elder were thicker, more deliberate.

Drevik sat in the deliberately low chair and didn't let it intimidate him. He'd spent the night being drugged, attacked, and forced to confront the possibility that one of his partners might be an unwitting traitor. A power-play chair didn't rank.

"I'll cooperate." He opened with it, stripping away negotiation's preamble. "But not as your subordinate. As an ally."

Thornwood's expression didn't change. He steepled his fingers, green eyes assessing. "You're not in a position to set terms."

"I'm in the only position that matters. I'm the one being targeted. Whoever's behind this wants me compromised, not eliminated. That means I have value. And valuable people negotiate."

A beat of silence. Then, faintly: approval. "What are your terms?"

"Full access to restricted files. Your technical resources. And you don't use my partners as leverage."

"You're very protective of them."

"I'm very aware of what they bring to this." He held the Elder's gaze. "Lexi included."

The name landed. Thornwood's jaw tightened—the only crack in his composure. "My daughter has chosen her loyalties."

"Her loyalties include you."

Something shifted in the Elder's face. Not softening—Drevik wouldn't call it that—but something that might, in a different man, have been regret.

"Your mother," Thornwood said, changing course. "She was investigating the Veiled One before she vanished. Not the journal—the Veiled One herself. The last person to hold that rank. Your mother believed she'd found evidence of something buried in the Order's foundation."

"Buried how?"

"Hidden in plain sight. A secret that the Order was built to protect—or to contain." Thornwood leaned back. "The Veiled One before the current one disappeared without explanation. Your mother discovered that disappearance and started asking questions. Then she vanished too."

Two disappearances. Two Seekers who got too close. The pattern wasn't subtle.

"There's a name," Thornwood continued. "It surfaces in encrypted communications, in records that shouldn't exist, in the whispers of Elders who've been in this Order longer than I have. 'The Architect.' Someone—or something—operating within our ranks for decades. I've never been able to confirm it exists."

"Until now."

"Until the breach pattern. Until someone used your credentials. Until the Veiled One's journal went missing." Thornwood's eyes sharpened. "These events didn't happen in isolation. They're connected. And they all orbit around you."

Drevik absorbed that. "You're giving me access to restricted files."

"Conditional access. I'll know what you're looking at, and I'll have questions of my own." A thin smile. "You'll be my eyes inside this investigation. And in exchange, I'll make sure the Council doesn't scapegoat you when the political fallout lands."

It wasn't trust. It was leverage, exchanged in both directions. Drevik could work with that.

He left the office with a data chip containing access codes and the weight of a new name rattling in his skull. The Architect. Someone inside the Order. Someone who had been here for decades.

Someone who might be standing right next to him.

Katryn had been busy while he was gone.

Her screens displayed a revised map of the compound, the breach points highlighted in red, connected by geometric lines that formed a shape Drevik recognized from last night. But she'd added something new—a second layer, pulsing beneath the first.

"It's not a summoning circle." She tapped the screen. "I was wrong. The pattern isn't drawing something in from the Shadowlands."

"Then what is it?"

She pulled up a third overlay, this one showing energy signatures at each breach point. The readings spiked and dipped in a rhythm that looked almost like—

"A lock." Mira's voice came from the doorway. She'd been running blood analyses since dawn. "You're looking at a lock being opened, one pin at a time."

"The breaches aren't summoning entities." Katryn enlarged the pattern. "They're unlocking something that's already here. Something that's been sealed inside the compound for decades."

Drevik stared at the screen. The shape the breaches formed—a sigil that had looked random, chaotic—resolved into something deliberate. Precise. Engineered.

"Someone built this compound on top of a prison," he said. The words came out hollow. "And they've been slowly letting it out."

"The original construction records are sealed." Katryn's fingers flew across her tablet. "Thornwood gave you access?"

"Conditional."

"Then use it." She projected a data stream onto the main screen. "I've been cross-referencing the breach locations with the compound's original blueprints. There's a gap."

"A gap?"

"Three hundred square feet of unaccounted space beneath the old rituals chamber. It doesn't appear on any official floor plan. Either someone forgot to include it, or someone deliberately excluded it."

His gut twisted. Three hundred square feet. Hidden. Right beneath the place where the Order conducted its most important ceremonies.

Mira stepped closer, her expression clinical in the way it got when she was holding back something difficult. "I have more. The blood analysis. There's a second compound in Drevik's system."

"A second one?"

"The first suppresses. This one amplifies." She pulled up a chart on her own device. "Whoever's drugging you isn't just trying to weaken you. They're tuning you. Suppressing your defenses, then amplifying your Seeker abilities in controlled bursts."

The room went quiet.

"They want me powerful," Drevik said. "But controllable."

"Like an instrument." Katryn's voice held grim admiration. "Someone's been playing you."

His jaw locked. The image of a figure moving like Seraphina through the archive. The gaps in her memory. The compound in his blood, placed there by someone with access to his quarters. His mother's voice, distorted through the Shadowlands: The key is already inside you.

Not a metaphor. Literal. They'd put something inside him, and they were adjusting the dosage until he performed the way they wanted.

"Can you trace the second compound?"

"I can try." Mira's hand found his arm—a brief touch, steadying. "The molecular structure is unusual. Not something from the Order's pharmacies. It's older. Pre-Order, maybe."

"Like the disabler you found before."

"Related. Part of the same system." She paused. "Drevik, whoever is doing this has medical knowledge that rivals or exceeds mine. This isn't amateur work."

Before he could respond, the alarms shrieked.

The fourth breach was worse than the previous three combined.

Drevik ran through corridors filled with running acolytes, the compound's emergency lighting bathing everything in red. His Seeker abilities flared—unstable, the amplification compound making everything sharper and more chaotic at once. The breach blazed from three corridors away, a wound in reality pulsing with sickly light.

Entities poured through. Not one. Not three. A swarm, their forms shifting and writhing as they pushed into the mortal realm.

He sealed the first breach by sheer force, his power lashing out with more intensity than he'd intended. The amplification compound. It was pushing his abilities past their safe limits, burning through reserves he didn't have.

His vision blurred. His knees buckled.

Lexi was at his side, her sword already singing through the nearest entity. "Focus!"

He focused. The second breach collapsed under his will. The third required everything he had—every ounce of power, every thread of connection to the Shadowlands, every stubborn refusal to let the darkness win.

When it was over, he was on his knees, gasping. Blood ran from his nose. His hands shook.

Mira reached him first, scanner already in hand, her expression tight with controlled fear. "Your readings are off the chart. The amplification compound—it spiked during the breach. Your body used it as fuel."

"Is that bad or good?"

"Neither. It's designed." She wiped the blood from his upper lip. "Someone knew there would be a breach tonight. They dosed you to make sure you could handle it."

The implication settled like ice in his veins. Not just surveillance. Not just manipulation. Predictive control. They knew the breaches were coming before they happened, and they'd prepared him like a weapon they intended to fire.

The lockdown came an hour later. Order leadership, concerned about the escalating attacks, restricted all non-essential movement. Drevik and his partners found themselves confined to his quarters—five people in a space designed for one, tension crackling like a live wire.

Lexi waited until Katryn and Seraphina were absorbed in their respective screens and shadows before she rounded on him.

"You met with my father alone."

"You were training when—"

"I would have come." Her voice was low, controlled, and angrier than he'd heard her in weeks. "Instead, I find out from Katryn's monitoring feed that you walked into his office by yourself. After everything we discussed about not going alone."

"He's your father."

"He's the man who offered you a deal that ties your survival to his political agenda." Her jaw set. "And you took it."

"I negotiated."

"You accommodated." She stepped closer. "There's a difference."

The argument that followed cut deep. Lexi's fear wore the mask of anger—it always had—but this time the mask was thinner, cracked in places that let the fear leak through. She was terrified. Not of the breaches or the conspiracy, but of losing him to a game she couldn't play.

"You don't get to make deals with my father without telling me." Her voice broke on the last word. "He's already used me as leverage once. You just gave him permission to do it again."

"I was trying to protect—"

"Protect me?" She laughed, sharp and brittle. "By handing him more power over both of us? That's not protection. That's surrender."

"I was trying to keep you out of it."

"Then you weren't listening." Her hand pressed against his chest, right over the bond. "I don't want to be kept out. I want to be in. With you. Even when it's dangerous. Especially when it's dangerous."

He looked at her—really looked. The tension in her shoulders. The fear she was barely containing. The woman who'd fought at his side since before he understood what he was fighting for.

"I heard you," he said. "I just didn't want to believe you meant it."

"I've always meant it."

She kissed him like she was trying to prove something. Maybe she was. The argument dissolved into something more desperate—hands pulling at fabric, breath coming fast, the urgent need to feel something real when everything else was falling apart.

Lexi was fierce in everything she did. This was no different. She pushed him against the wall, her mouth hard on his, her hands claiming territory she'd already mapped a hundred times. He matched her intensity, pulling her closer, feeling the bond pulse between them like a second heartbeat.

There was nothing gentle about it. This was assertion, not tenderness—a refusal to let the conspiracy, the suspicion, the fear take this from them. Every touch was a word she couldn't say: I'm here. I'm not leaving. You don't get to push me out.

They didn't make it to the bed. The wall would have to do.

After, she lay with her head on his chest, her breathing slowing. The fierceness had drained out of her, leaving something softer underneath.

"My father told me something," she said quietly. "About your mother."

His hand stilled on her back. "What?"

"She worked with another Seeker. A man named Corvin. He vanished around the same time she did—same circumstances, same lack of explanation." She traced a pattern on his skin. "My father said Corvin was obsessed with the Veiled One. He believed the current Veiled One was hiding something fundamental about the Order's origins."

"Did your father know Corvin?"

"Everyone knew Corvin. He was one of the best Seekers the Order ever produced. Charismatic, brilliant, completely convinced that the Order was built on a lie." She paused. "My father says he went mad. Disappeared into the Shadowlands and never came back."

Two Seekers. Two disappearances. Two people who got too close to the same secret.

"Or someone made sure they disappeared."

She was quiet for a long time. "That's what I think too."

He couldn't sleep.

The lockdown pressed in, the compound's artificial night feeling more claustrophobic than usual. His Seeker abilities buzzed under his skin, the amplification compound still pushing them past their normal limits. The barriers between worlds felt thin and fragile, like glass about to crack.

Something was pushing at those barriers. Not the entities from the breaches—something else. Something deeper, older, and patient.

He followed the sensation.

The abandoned section of the compound wasn't on any map—or rather, it was on maps that had been deliberately removed from the Order's archives. He found it anyway, his Seeker senses pulling him through corridors that felt wrong, past rooms that hadn't been used in years.

Beneath the old rituals chamber, behind a door that shouldn't have been there, he found a room.

Three hundred square feet of hidden space. A cot in the corner, still warm. Supplies stacked against the walls. And every flat surface covered in calculations—symbols he recognized from the breach sigils, equations that spiraled across the walls like mathematical madness.

Someone had been living here. Recently. For months, maybe longer, right beneath the Order's nose.

The room smelled of old paper and something else—something that reminded him of the Shadowlands, but muted, like a memory of a smell rather than the thing itself. The cot's blanket was thin but clean. The supplies were organized with military precision. Whoever had been living here wasn't just hiding. They were working.

He moved through the space with careful steps, his Seeker abilities picking up traces of recent occupancy. A meal half-eaten. A ritual circle inscribed on the floor, its lines still faintly glowing. And on a small table, a journal.

Not the Veiled One's journal. Thinner, newer, filled with cramped handwriting that catalogued schedules, movements, patterns of behavior. His schedules. His movements. Detailed notes on his Seeker abilities—their range, their limitations, their fluctuations.

And then, tucked between the pages like a pressed flower, a single sheet in a different hand.

His mother's handwriting. He knew it instantly—he'd grown up studying the few surviving letters she'd left behind. The script was unmistakable.

The message was short. Five words.

"They're not destroying you, Drevik."

He turned the page over. One more line, written in the same hand but with a tremor that suggested fear, or urgency, or both.

"They're replacing you."

The words hung in the air. The room pressed in around him. And somewhere far away, the barriers between worlds shuddered, as if something on the other side had heard him read them.
