Best Cast Iron Skillets (And How to Season Them Right)

July 2026 · 5 products reviewed

I bought my first cast iron skillet at a garage sale for $3. It was rusted, crusted, and looked like it had been buried in a yard for a decade. After three hours of scrubbing, reseasoning, and wondering why I bothered, I cooked an egg in it the next morning and it slid around like it was on butter. That $3 skillet is still the best pan I own.

The seasoning is older than the pan.Cast iron isn't complicated, but the internet makes it feel like you need a degree in metallurgy to cook with one. You don't. You need a good skillet and about 10 minutes of knowledge. Here's both.

What Actually Matters

  • Pre-seasoned vs bare: Most modern cast iron comes preseasoned from the factory. It's not a perfect nonstick surface yet — you'll build that up over time — but it's enough to start cooking immediately. Bare cast iron requires seasoning from scratch, which takes 2-3 rounds of oiling and heating.
  • Weight matters more than you think: A 12-inch skillet full of food weighs 8-10 pounds. If you have wrist issues or do a lot of one-handed pan flips, look at lighter options. Stargazer is the lightest in this category at 6.5 lbs.
  • Smooth cooking surface: Vintage cast iron is prized because it was machined smooth. Modern Lodge has a pebbly texture that improves with use but never gets glass-smooth. If you want smooth out of the box, look at Field, Stargazer, or Smithey.
  • Handle length and shape: Short handles stay cooler but give less leverage. Long handles give better control but can be awkward in the oven. The helper handle on a 12-inch skillet is essential for lifting a hot, heavy pan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I really need to season cast iron?

Modern preseasoned skillets come ready to cook on. What you need to do is maintain and improve the seasoning: cook fatty foods (bacon, steak), wipe with a thin oil layer after cleaning, and avoid soaking. Over months of regular use, the seasoning builds into a natural nonstick surface.

Can I use soap on cast iron?

Yes. Modern dish soap doesn't strip seasoning — that's a myth from when soap contained lye. A little Dawn and a sponge won't hurt your pan. What WILL hurt it is soaking it, putting it in the dishwasher, or scrubbing with steel wool.

Why does my cast iron food stick?

Three reasons: not enough fat in the pan, heat too high, or the seasoning isn't built up yet. Cast iron needs more oil than nonstick. Medium heat is usually enough. New pans will have some sticking for the first month — cook bacon and steak to build seasoning.

How do I remove rust from cast iron?

Scrub with steel wool until the rust is gone. Rinse, dry completely (stove on low for 5 minutes), then reseason: thin coat of vegetable oil, wipe almost dry, oven at 450°F for 1 hour. Cool in oven.

Is cast iron better than nonstick?

For searing, baking, and high-heat cooking, cast iron is better. For eggs and delicate items, well-seasoned cast iron works but nonstick is easier. The real advantage: nonstick lasts 1-3 years, cast iron lasts generations.

What's the best oil for seasoning cast iron?

Grapeseed oil (420°F smoke point) is the best choice. Vegetable and canola work fine. Flaxseed creates a beautiful patina but can flake. Never use olive oil for seasoning — it smokes too low and leaves sticky residue.

Can I use metal utensils on cast iron?

Yes. Metal spatulas, tongs, and forks won't damage the cooking surface. The seasoning is polymerized oil bonded to iron — metal won't scrape it off. Avoid metal scrubbers for cleaning, but cooking utensils are fine.
