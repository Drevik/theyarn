Best Silk Pillowcases 2026: Tested for Hair, Skin, and Wash Durability

September 2026 · 5 products reviewed

I bought my first silk pillowcase because my hairdresser told me to. Not asked — told. "Your ends are breaking," she said, pointing at my split ends like a doctor showing me an X-ray. "Get a silk pillowcase." Three years and five pillowcases later, I can confirm: it makes a real difference for hair. The skin benefits, I'm less sure about. But the hair thing is real, and after testing pillowcases from $23 to $115, I can also confirm that not all silk is created equal.

The market is full of misleading labels. "Silk-like" means polyester satin, which is not silk. "Mulberry silk" without a momme count could be 16 momme, which is thin enough to see through. And "charmeuse" refers to the weave, not the quality. I tested five pillowcases across the price range, washed each one ten times, slept on each for at least a week, and measured what actually changed about my hair and skin. Here's what I found.

What to Look For in a Silk Pillowcase

  • Momme count: This is the single most important spec. Momme measures silk weight per unit area. Below 16 is too thin for regular use. 19 is acceptable but will thin out within a year. 22 is the sweet spot — durable enough for daily use, soft enough to feel luxurious. 25+ is premium and will last 2+ years. If a product doesn't list the momme count, assume it's under 19.
  • 100% mulberry silk: Mulberry silk has longer, more uniform fibers than wild silk, resulting in a smoother surface and better durability. Look for "100% mulberry silk" on the label. "Silk blend" means it's mixed with cotton or polyester, which reduces the hair and skin benefits.
  • Closure type: Zipper closures keep the pillow fully enclosed and prevent the case from sliding off. Envelope closures are easier to put on but can gap open at the back. Hidden zippers are the best of both worlds.
  • Color fastness: Dark-colored silk pillowcases are more prone to color transfer on sweaty or damp nights. Light colors (ivory, champagne, blush) are safer bets. If you want dark colors, look for color-fast dyed silk and wash before first use.
  • Washability: Silk is delicate but not fragile. Most pillowcases can be machine washed on gentle with cold water. The real test is how they hold up after 10+ washes. High-momme silk retains its luster and smoothness. Low-momme silk starts to pill and thin.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are silk pillowcases actually good for your hair?

Yes, and the mechanism is straightforward. Silk creates less friction than cotton, so your hair glides over the surface instead of catching and pulling. This matters most for curly, coily, or fragile hair types that are prone to breakage and frizz. Cotton pillowcases absorb moisture from your hair and create drag, which is why you wake up with tangled, frizzy strands. Silk lets hair move freely, reducing breakage and preserving styles longer between washes.

What does momme mean for silk pillowcases?

Momme (pronounced "mummy") is the weight measurement for silk. A higher momme count means denser, more durable silk. 16-19 momme is entry-level — it feels smooth but thins out after repeated washing. 22 momme is the sweet spot for pillowcases, offering a good balance of softness and durability. 25-30 momme is luxury grade — thicker, more opaque, and significantly more expensive. Anything below 16 momme is too thin for a pillowcase and won't last.

Can you machine wash silk pillowcases?

Most silk pillowcases can be machine washed on a gentle cycle with cold water and a pH-neutral detergent. The key is avoiding hot water, bleach, and the dryer. Air dry flat or hang dry. High-quality silk (22+ momme) holds up well to machine washing. Cheaper silk (under 19 momme) may pill or lose its luster faster. Some brands recommend hand washing for the first few cycles to set the fibers, but honestly, most people machine wash from day one with no issues.

Is mulberry silk better than regular silk?

Mulberry silk is the gold standard for pillowcases. It comes from silkworms fed exclusively on mulberry leaves, which produces longer, more uniform fibers. This results in smoother, stronger, and more durable fabric. "Wild silk" or "tussah silk" comes from wild silkworms with a varied diet, producing shorter, rougher fibers. When you see a pillowcase labeled "100% mulberry silk," that's the quality you want. If the label just says "silk" without specifying the type, it's likely a blend or lower-grade silk.

How long do silk pillowcases last?

A good quality silk pillowcase (22+ momme, 100% mulberry) lasts 1-2 years with regular use and proper washing. Higher momme counts (25-30) can last 2-3 years. Budget silk pillowcases (16-19 momme) typically show thinning or pilling within 6-12 months. To extend the life of any silk pillowcase, wash it in cold water on a gentle cycle, skip the fabric softener, and never put it in the dryer. Rotating between two pillowcases also helps each one last longer.
