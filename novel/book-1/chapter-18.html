Chapter 18: Alliance and Assault

Chapter 18: Alliance and Assault

The vision struck Drevik like a blade between the ribs.

He staggered against the brick wall of the alley, gasping as possibilities cascaded through his mind—dozens of futures branching from this moment, each one a fragment of what could be. In some, he watched Elder Thornwood fall to Corvin's shadow blade. In others, Lexi bled out on the compound floor, her green eyes going dull. In one particularly vicious thread, he stood alone in the aftermath, everyone he cared about scattered like broken dolls around him.

"Drevik!" Lexi's hand gripped his shoulder, anchoring him to the present. "What do you see?"

He blinked, the futures fading like morning mist. His hands were shaking. They'd been shaking since they'd fled the warehouse with the evidence that could bring Corvin down—scrolls detailing his dealings with Shadowlands entities, names of Order members he'd corrupted, dates and locations of planned breaches.

"The compound's on lockdown," he managed. "Corvin's people control the eastern entrance. The western approach is mined with shadow-traps." He swallowed hard. "And Elder Thornwood is running out of time."

Lexi's jaw tightened. She looked as ragged as he felt—auburn hair escaping her practical braid, a cut above her eyebrow crusted with dried blood from their escape. But her eyes remained fierce, determined. She'd been the one to pull him through the warehouse window when Corvin's men cornered them. She'd killed two shadow-walkers in the process.

"Then we find another way in," she said.

"There is no other way in." Ivy's voice came from behind them, and Drevik turned to find the willowy blonde emerging from the shadows of the alley. She clutched a leather satchel to her chest, her pale face drawn tight with anxiety. "I've been watching the compound for hours. Every entrance is either sealed or guarded by Corvin's people."

"Ivy." Relief flooded through him at the sight of her. She'd separated from them at the warehouse, taking a different route to avoid the pursuit that had chased Drevik and Lexi. "You made it."

"Barely." She moved closer, and he caught the faint scent of lavender beneath the metallic tang of fear. "They're hunting anyone loyal to Elder Thornwood. Three seekers were dragged from their quarters an hour ago. I don't know if they're still alive."

Lexi moved to stand beside Drevik, her shoulder brushing his in a gesture that was both protective and possessive. "How many are left? People we can trust?"

"A handful, maybe. Most are too afraid to move. Corvin's been planning this for months—he's already turned half the Wardens to his side." Ivy's voice cracked. "Drevik, if we go in there, we might be walking into a tomb."

Before he could respond, the temperature in the alley dropped. Shadows pooled in the corner, darker than the night around them, and Drevik felt the familiar pull of someone shadow-walking nearby. His hand went to the knife at his belt, but Lexi was faster, positioning herself between him and the emerging figure.

The shadows parted like a curtain.

Seraphina Vane stepped into the dim light of the alley.

She looked like she'd been through a war. Her usually immaculate dark hair hung loose around her shoulders, tangled and streaked with something that might have been ash. A tear in her fitted jacket revealed an angry red scratch across her ribs, and she moved with a slight limp that she was clearly trying to hide. But her dark eyes were sharp as ever, and when they met Drevik's, something flickered there—something that looked almost like relief.

"Seraphina." Her name left his mouth before he could stop it.

"Did you miss me?" A ghost of her usual smirk crossed her face, but it didn't reach her eyes. "You look terrible, by the way."

"You disappeared." He stepped forward, ignoring Lexi's warning growl. "After the warehouse, you just—"

"I know what I did." Seraphina's voice hardened. "I had to make sure Corvin didn't have eyes on me before I could bring you this."

She reached into her jacket and withdrew a folded parchment, sealed with black wax. The seal of the Shadowlands.

"What is it?" Ivy moved closer, her curiosity overriding her caution.

"Insurance." Seraphina held it out to Drevik, her fingers brushing his as he took it. The contact sent a spark through him—her touch was cold, but the look in her eyes was anything but. "Corvin's been communicating with something in the Shadowlands. A void walker. It's been feeding him information about the Order's weaknesses in exchange for... well. You can read it yourself."

Drevik broke the seal and scanned the contents. His stomach turned.

Names. Dates. The locations of shadow-breaches that Corvin had orchestrated—each one timed to weaken Elder Thornwood's position, to make it seem like the old man was losing his grip on the Order's power. And at the bottom, a signature that made his blood run cold: a mark that belonged to something that had no business existing in either realm.

"This is enough to hang him," Lexi breathed, reading over his shoulder. Her body pressed against his back, warm and solid. "If we can get this to your father—"

"My father is exactly the problem." Seraphina's voice dripped with bitterness. "He's been helping Corvin. Not willingly—I'm sure Corvin has something on him—but that doesn't change the facts. We can't trust any of the Elders except Thornwood."

"Then we get to Thornwood." Drevik folded the parchment and tucked it into his jacket. "Tonight. Before Corvin makes his move."

"And how do you propose we do that?" Lexi crossed her arms, fixing Seraphina with a glare that could have frozen fire. "In case you haven't noticed, the compound is locked down. Corvin's people are crawling over every entrance."

Seraphina's lips curved. "That's why I brought the map."

She withdrew another parchment from her jacket—this one covered in intricate lines and symbols. A layout of the compound, but with passages Drevik had never seen before.

"Ventilation shafts," Seraphina explained, tracing a thin line with her finger. "They run through the entire compound. Most of them are too small for shadow-walking, but there's a maintenance corridor here—" she tapped a section near the eastern wing "—that connects to the Elder's private chambers. It hasn't been used in decades. Corvin won't have it guarded."

Ivy leaned in, her brow furrowing. "How did you get this?"

"I have my sources." Seraphina's smile didn't reach her eyes. "Let's just say my father's position has certain advantages."

The tension in the alley was palpable. Lexi hadn't moved from her protective stance beside Drevik, and Seraphina seemed to take pleasure in that fact, deliberately stepping closer to him when she spoke. Ivy watched the exchange with careful eyes, her fingers twitching toward the satchel she carried.

Three women. Three very different connections to him.

And right now, all three of them were looking at him like he held the answers.

"Okay," Drevik said, forcing his voice to remain steady. "Here's what we're going to do."

The planning session took place in Ivy's apartment—a cramped space above a bookstore that served as her unofficial sanctuary. The walls were lined with books and maps, and the air smelled of old paper and the herbs Ivy used in her minor workings.

Drevik spread the materials across her table: Seraphina's map, the evidence from the warehouse, the Shadowlands parchment. Four people against an entire compound of corrupted Order members. The odds were so bad they would have been laughable if they weren't so terrifying.

"Ivy, you'll take the eastern approach," Drevik said, pointing to a section of the map. "There's a shadow-trap here, here, and here. Can you disable them?"

Ivy nodded, her fingers tracing the positions. "Give me twenty minutes. I've been studying the new trap configurations—Corvin changed the patterns, but the underlying structure is the same."

"Good. Once the traps are down, you shadow-walk to this junction and wait. If anyone follows us, you're our exit strategy."

"Understood." Ivy's pale eyes met his, and something passed between them—gratitude, maybe, or the weight of things unsaid. She'd been preparing for this moment for weeks, gathering intelligence, mapping the compound's defenses. All without anyone asking her to.

"Lexi." Drevik turned to the auburn-haired woman who had been silently fuming since Seraphina's arrival. "You and I go through the maintenance corridor. We reach Elder Thornwood, get him the evidence, and protect him until we can call in loyal seekers."

"And what about her?" Lexi jerked her chin toward Seraphina, who was examining her nails with studied nonchalance.

"I go where I'm needed." Seraphina's voice was cool, but Drevik caught the edge beneath it. "Someone has to create a distraction at the main entrance. Unless you'd prefer to fight your way through Corvin's guards?"

Lexi's hands curled into fists. "I'd prefer you weren't part of this at all."

"And yet here I am." Seraphina rose from her chair, moving to stand in front of Drevik. This close, he could see the exhaustion beneath her composed mask, the shadows under her eyes. "Look, I know you don't trust me. You have no reason to. But Corvin killed my mother, and he's been using my father as a puppet for years. I want him to burn as badly as you do."

The raw honesty in her voice made Lexi hesitate. Drevik watched the two women face each other—Lexi with her fierce protectiveness, Seraphina with her careful walls finally showing cracks.

"Fine," Lexi said finally. "But if you betray us—"

"You'll kill me yourself. I know." Seraphina's lips twisted. "Get in line."

The tension broke, and Drevik exhaled slowly. They had a plan. A terrible, desperate plan, but it was something.

"I need a moment," he said. "Before we move."

He walked to the window, staring out at the city lights. The moon was three-quarters full, its light lending strength to his abilities. He would need every advantage tonight.

Soft footsteps approached behind him. He didn't need to turn to know it was Lexi.

"You don't have to do this." Her voice was low, meant only for him. "We could find another way."

"There is no other way." He turned to face her, struck as always by the fierce beauty of her—the sharp angles of her face, the determination in her green eyes. "You know that."

"I know you're the one taking all the risks." She stepped closer, close enough that he could feel the heat radiating from her. "Every vision you've had, every path you've seen—you're always at the center. Always the one in danger."

"Lexi—"

"I can't lose you." The words came out rough, almost angry. "Do you understand? I've lost too much already. My mother, my brother—I can't—"

He silenced her with a kiss.

It wasn't gentle. He cupped her face in his hands and pressed his mouth to hers with a desperation that surprised them both. She responded instantly, her fingers twisting in his shirt, pulling him closer. The kiss tasted like fear and determination and something deeper—something that had been growing between them since the day she'd been assigned to evaluate him as an acolyte.

When they broke apart, both of them were breathing hard.

"I'm coming back," he said against her forehead. "I promise."

"Don't make promises you can't keep." But her arms wrapped around him, holding tight for a moment before she pulled away. Her eyes were bright, but her voice was steady when she spoke. "We should move. The longer we wait, the worse our chances."

He nodded, but before he could turn, Ivy appeared at his elbow.

"Drevik." She held out a small pouch. "Shadow-dust. It'll mask your presence for a few minutes—long enough to get through the maintenance corridor without triggering any alarms."

He took it, their fingers brushing. "Thank you. For everything."

"I didn't do it for thanks." Her pale eyes studied his face. "I did it because you're the first person in this Order who saw me as more than a researcher. Who trusted me to be more."

"You've always been more, Ivy."

She smiled—a small, fragile thing that made something twist in his chest. Then she stepped back, her expression returning to its usual careful neutrality.

Seraphina was the last to approach. She waited until the others were occupied before sliding up beside him, her voice pitched low.

"I wasn't entirely honest earlier." She pulled something from her pocket—a small crystal that pulsed with faint light. "This is a truth-stone. Elder Thornwood gave it to my mother years ago. If you get it to him, he'll know the evidence is genuine. Corvin can't counterfeit its light."

"Why are you giving this to me?"

"Because you're the one walking into the dragon's den." Her dark eyes met his, and for once, there was no smirk, no practiced seduction. "Because if anyone can pull this off, it's you."

He took the stone, feeling its warmth against his palm. "Seraphina—"

"Don't." She held up a hand. "Whatever you're going to say, save it until this is over. One way or another."

She turned to leave, but he caught her wrist. Her pulse jumped under his fingers.

"Thank you," he said simply.

She hesitated, then covered his hand with hers. A brief squeeze—almost too quick to notice.

"Stay alive, Drevik Markinsoul. I haven't decided what to do with you yet."

Then she was gone, slipping out the door to begin her distraction.

Lexi appeared at his shoulder. "We should move."

"Yeah." He took a breath, centering himself. "Let's go."

The maintenance corridor smelled of dust and old magic.

Drevik moved through the darkness with Lexi close behind, one hand on the wall to guide him. The shadow-dust Ivy had given him created a thin veil around his presence, but it wouldn't last forever. Every step was a countdown.

The corridor was barely wide enough for one person, carved from the same stone as the rest of the compound but untouched for decades. Cobwebs brushed his face, and he tried not to think about what might have made them.

"Stop." Lexi's whisper cut through the darkness. "There's a ward ahead."

He froze, extending his senses. She was right—a thin thread of power stretched across the corridor, invisible to normal sight but shimmering with warning to anyone with Veil Sight. If they passed through it, alarms would sound throughout the compound.

"Can you disable it?"

"Maybe. Give me a moment." She moved past him, her fingers tracing the air. He felt her power reaching out, shadows responding to her call. A soft pop echoed through the corridor, and the ward collapsed. "There. Move quickly."

They emerged into a wider passage, and Drevik recognized the eastern wing of the compound. The walls here were hung with tapestries depicting the Order's history—scenes of shadow-walkers battling void creatures, of Elders raising barriers against breaches. The irony wasn't lost on him. The organization that had protected the mortal realm for centuries was now being corrupted from within.

Voices drifted from around the corner. Drevik pressed himself against the wall, Lexi beside him.

"—searched the lower levels. No sign of them."

"Keep looking. Corvin wants them found before midnight."

Footsteps retreated, and Drevik let out a breath he hadn't realized he'd been holding.

"Elder Thornwood's chambers are just ahead," Lexi whispered. "But there will be guards."

"I know." He closed his eyes, reaching for his gift. The futures flickered before him—possibilities branching like lightning. He saw himself fighting through the guards, saw Lexi fall to a shadow blade, saw Elder Thornwood's door breached before they could reach it. He saw them turn back, abandoning the mission. He saw—

There. A thread of possibility, thinner than the others but shining with potential.

"Follow me," he breathed. "And stay quiet."

He moved through the compound with new purpose, taking turns that seemed random but weren't. The futures had shown him a path—a servant's corridor, a hidden door, a moment when the guards changed shifts. They slipped through gaps in the patrol routes, shadow-walked through stretches that would have been impossible to cross physically.

And finally, they stood before Elder Thornwood's door.

It was carved from dark wood, inlaid with silver runes that pulsed with protective magic. Two guards stood on either side, their faces masked, shadow blades at their hips.

"Four of them," Lexi murmured. "We can take them."

"No." Drevik's mind raced through possibilities. "There's a better way."

He reached into his pocket and withdrew the truth-stone Seraphina had given him. Its light was faint but steady. He closed his eyes, focusing his will, and pushed.

The stone's light flared.

The guards turned, hands going to their weapons, but Drevik was already moving. He stepped into the shadows between one heartbeat and the next, emerging behind the guard on the left. His knife found the gap in the man's armor, and the guard crumpled without a sound.

Lexi took the second one before he could raise an alarm.

The other two guards came at them together, shadow blades gleaming in the corridor's torchlight. Drevik ducked under a slash, feeling the cold whisper of the blade pass inches from his face. He drove his elbow into his attacker's stomach, then brought his knife up under the man's chin.

Lexi was a whirlwind of motion beside him, her training showing in every precise strike. She disarmed her opponent with a twist of her wrist and brought him down with a blow to the temple.

Four guards. Four bodies on the floor.

"Inside," Drevik gasped. "Now."

He pushed open the door and stumbled into Elder Thornwood's chambers.

The old man sat at his desk, surrounded by scrolls and artifacts, his weathered face turning toward them in surprise. His white hair was disheveled, his robes marked with the signs of a long siege. But his eyes—those piercing blue eyes that had seen decades of Order politics—were sharp as ever.

"Drevik." Elder Thornwood rose slowly, leaning heavily on his staff. "I wondered if you would come."

"Sir." Drevik stepped forward, pulling the evidence from his jacket. "Corvin is planning to overthrow you tonight. He's been working with a void walker from the Shadowlands—here's the proof."

The Elder took the scrolls, his eyes scanning the contents. His expression darkened with each line.

"The foolish boy," he breathed. "He would risk everything for power."

"There's more." Drevik handed over Seraphina's truth-stone. "This will confirm the evidence is genuine."

Elder Thornwood's fingers closed around the stone, and its light flared brighter—confirmation that the documents were real, that the signatures were authentic.

"I had hoped..." The old man shook his head. "I had hoped there was another way. But this leaves no room for doubt."

He turned to Lexi. "Your father—"

"Is compromised," Lexi said, her voice strained. "I know."

"I'm sorry, child." The Elder's tone softened. "I know what this costs you."

"Save your sympathy for after we survive." Lexi's jaw tightened. "Can you call in loyal seekers? The ones who haven't turned?"

"I've already sent word to those I trust. But they're hours away, and Corvin's people control the compound." Elder Thornwood moved to his window, looking out at the night sky. "He'll move soon. The moon is nearly at its peak—his power will be at its height."

"Then we take the fight to him." Drevik stepped forward. "Before he can bring his forces to bear."

The Elder turned to face him, something like pride flickering in his eyes. "You've grown much since your initiation, Drevik Markinsoul. Your parents would be proud."

The words hit him like a physical blow. "My parents?"

"Another conversation for another time." Elder Thornwood gripped his staff. "For now, we have a traitor to confront."

The explosion shook the entire compound.

Drevik staggered, catching himself on the desk as debris rained from the ceiling. The sound of shouting and running feet filled the corridor outside.

"What—"

"Seraphina." Lexi's voice was grim. "Her distraction."

"It's working." Elder Thornwood moved toward the door. "We must reach the central chamber before Corvin completes his ritual. If he succeeds in opening a full breach, we'll have more than political intrigue to worry about."

They moved through the chaos together, the Elder's staff clearing a path through the wreckage. Order members ran past them—some loyal, some corrupted, all too confused to tell friend from foe. Drevik's shadow-dust had worn off, but it hardly mattered now. Stealth had given way to survival.

The central chamber loomed ahead, its great doors carved with the symbols of the Order. Power pulsed from within—dark, alien power that made Drevik's skin crawl.

"He's already started," Elder Thornwood growled. "The fool."

The doors burst open.

Corvin stood at the center of the chamber, surrounded by a ring of corrupted acolytes. His arms were raised, shadows swirling around him in a vortex that seemed to drink the light from the room. And beyond him, in the space where the veil between worlds was thinnest, something was pushing through.

Something hungry.

"You're too late, old man." Corvin's voice echoed with power. "The breach is already opening. Soon, my ally will walk in this realm, and the Order will belong to those with the strength to seize it."

Drevik saw the futures branching before him—dozens of ways this could end, most of them in darkness. But there, in the chaos of possibilities, one thread shone brighter than the others.

He reached for Lexi's hand.

Then for Ivy's, as she materialized beside them, her face pale but determined.

And finally, surprisingly, for Seraphina's, as she emerged from the shadows on his other side.

Four people. Four connections. Four paths converging.

The truth-stone in Elder Thornwood's hand flared, and Corvin's ritual faltered.

"Now!" the Elder shouted.

Drevik closed his eyes and reached for the one future that might save them all.

The vision hit him like a tidal wave—possibilities collapsing into a single thread. He saw the path forward, clear and terrible. He saw what needed to be done.

And he saw, with cold certainty, that one of the people standing beside him would not survive the night.

The question was: who?

He opened his eyes, meeting Corvin's mad gaze across the chamber.

"Let's finish this."
