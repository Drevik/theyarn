Chapter 9: Questions of Loyalty

Chapter 9: Questions of Loyalty

The pain hit Drevik like a knife between his ribs, sharp and sudden, just as Professor Hendricks called on him.

"Mr. Markinsoul? Care to explain the significance of the Marshall Court's decisions on federal Indian policy?"

Drevik gripped his desk, knuckles white. The ache radiated outward from his chest, spreading through his shoulders, crawling up his neck. His vision blurred at the edges. He'd felt the Oath's punishment before—mild warnings, brief flares of discomfort when he'd skirted too close to forbidden topics. This was different. This was the Oath sinking its teeth in and refusing to let go.

"I..." He swallowed hard. The right answer sat somewhere in his notes, which he'd barely skimmed before class. He'd meant to study last night. Instead, he'd spent hours testing the boundaries of what he could and couldn't say about Seraphina, about the shadows he'd seen moving in the library, about the file Ivy had shown him with his parents' names redacted.

Each attempt to articulate the truth had brought pain. Each lie by omission had made it worse.

"Mr. Markinsoul?" Hendricks adjusted his glasses, frowning. "Are you feeling alright? You look rather pale."

Someone behind him snickered. Drevik recognized the sound—Marcus Webb, perpetual thorn in his side since freshman year, currently dating the girl Drevik had tutored through remedial history last semester.

"I need—" Drevik started, then stopped. Speaking felt like swallowing glass. The Oath coiled tighter every time he thought about explaining his distraction, because the explanation would lead to questions, and questions would lead to truths he wasn't permitted to share.

This is ridiculous. He was an adult. A student. A member of a shadowy magical organization that apparently had no problem with him failing his classes as long as he kept their secrets.

"Mr. Markinsoul, perhaps you should visit the health center." Hendricks had moved on, calling on another student, a girl with perfect notes and no magical obligations weighing on her soul.

Drevik gathered his things with shaking hands. His notebook slid off the desk, papers scattering across the floor. He knelt to retrieve them, and the motion sent a fresh wave of agony through him. For a moment, he stayed there, forehead nearly touching the cold tile, breathing through the pain.

Get up. Get out. Figure this out.

He made it to the hallway before his legs gave out.

The student lounge was crowded for a Tuesday afternoon. Drevik found a corner table, far from the windows where sunlight streamed too bright for his shadow-adjusted eyes. He'd managed to grab a coffee on the way, but his hands were still trembling too badly to drink it without spilling.

"You look like absolute hell."

He looked up to find Jenna Torres hovering over him, backpack slung over one shoulder, concern etched into her features. They'd had World Civ together last semester, studied in the same library corners, exchanged the kind of casual friendship that came from shared academic suffering.

"Thanks," Drevik managed. The pain had settled into a dull throb, bearable if he didn't think too hard about anything important. "I've had a rough couple days."

"Rough couple days?" Jenna dropped into the chair across from him, setting her bag down with a thump. "Drevik, you look like you haven't slept in a week. Are you sick? Should I call someone?"

"No." The word came out sharper than intended. He softened his tone. "No, I'm fine. Just—stress. You know how it gets."

Jenna studied him, unconvinced. "I brought my poli-sci notes if you want to study together. Professor Chen's exam is Thursday, and you look like someone who forgot it existed."

Thursday. The word landed like a blow. Three days away. He'd completely forgotten.

"Actually—" He started to decline, but the Oath tightened again, a warning pulse. He couldn't explain why he needed to be alone, couldn't articulate that studying felt impossible when his brain was being squeezed by magical restrictions. "Yeah. Yeah, that'd be great."

Jenna's smile was sympathetic. She pulled out her laptop, opened a document covered in color-coded highlights. "I've got the study guide pretty well mapped out. We can start with the foundational concepts and work our way up to the contemporary applications."

Drevik nodded, reaching for his own notebook. His hand brushed the coffee cup, knocked it sideways. Dark liquid spread across the table, dripping onto his notes, staining the pages he'd managed to salvage from his earlier disaster.

"Shit." He grabbed napkins from the dispenser, dabbing frantically at the spreading mess. His chest constricted, and for a horrible moment, he thought the Oath was punishing him for his clumsiness.

But no—this was just frustration. Real, ordinary, non-magical frustration.

"Here." Jenna had grabbed more napkins, helping him clean. "It's just coffee, Drevik. It's not the end of the world."

He laughed, a hollow sound. "No. No, it's not."

They settled into studying, or tried to. Jenna would explain a concept, ask him a question, and Drevik would reach for the answer only to find his thoughts scattered by pain. Every time he tried to focus, his mind drifted to Seraphina—to her warning about traitors in the Order, to the fear in her eyes when she'd said they'll kill me if they find out I talked to you.

If he told Lexi, she'd have to report it. That was her duty as his handler. And Seraphina would die.

If he didn't tell Lexi, he was violating his Oath to the Order. And the Oath was making sure he knew it.

"Drevik?" Jenna's voice cut through his spiraling thoughts. "You're doing that thing again."

"What thing?"

"The spacing out thing. The 'I'm somewhere far away and unhappy about it' thing." She closed her laptop. "Look, I don't know what's going on with you, but maybe you need a break. Have you eaten today? Drank water? Done anything that doesn't involve staring into space and looking miserable?"

The question caught him off guard. He tried to remember the last time he'd eaten. Breakfast? Had he eaten breakfast? The day blurred together, a smear of classes and pain and the constant weight of secrets.

"I don't think so," he admitted.

"Then we're getting food." Jenna stood, slinging her bag over her shoulder. "Come on. My treat. And you can tell me what's actually bothering you, or you can not tell me, but at least you'll be fed while you're being mysterious."

Drevik opened his mouth to refuse, but another spike of pain drove the words back down his throat. He couldn't explain. Couldn't even decline properly without triggering the Oath's restrictions.

"I need to—" He gestured vaguely toward the bathroom. "I'll meet you there."

Jenna's expression shifted, concern flickering into something harder to read. "Yeah. Sure. The café on Fifth?"

He nodded, not trusting himself to speak. She left, and he stayed in his chair for a long moment, breathing through the pain.

Then he walked to the bathroom, locked himself in a stall, and pressed his forehead against the cold metal door.

What am I supposed to do?

The Oath didn't answer. It never did. It simply punished, restricting, binding him in chains he couldn't see and couldn't break.

He thought of Lexi. Of her hands on his face when she'd healed him after the warehouse, the way her eyes had softened before she'd pulled away. He wanted to tell her everything. Wanted to trust her with the secrets eating him alive.

But Ivy's warning echoed in his memory: There are people in the Order who want you to fail. Who wanted your parents gone.

If Lexi was one of them...

He couldn't finish the thought. Wouldn't.

Drevik splashed cold water on his face, gripped the edges of the sink, and stared at his reflection. Dark circles shadowed his eyes. His skin had gone grey. He looked like a corpse waiting to happen.

"Get it together," he muttered to himself. "You've survived worse."

The lie felt hollow even as he spoke it.

He didn't make it to the café.

Drevik made it halfway across the quad before the pain crested again, dropping him to his knees on the grass. Students streamed past, a few casting curious glances, most ignoring him entirely. He'd become adept at being invisible, at blending into the background even when his world was falling apart.

This is getting worse. The realization settled cold in his stomach. The Oath wasn't just warning him anymore. It was actively hurting him, punishing him for secrets he couldn't share.

"Hey! Hey, are you okay?"

Hands gripped his shoulders, steadying him. He looked up into familiar green eyes, auburn hair falling across a worried face.

Lexi.

Of course it was Lexi.

"I'm fine," he managed, but the words came out strangled. He wasn't fine. He was the opposite of fine, and the Oath was making sure he couldn't even lie about it properly.

"You're not fine." Her voice was sharp, commanding, the voice of someone used to being obeyed. "You're on your knees in the middle of the quad, Drevik. That's not fine. That's 'I need a doctor' or 'I need to tell me what the hell is going on.'"

"I can't—" He gasped as the pain spiked again. "I can't tell you."

Lexi's expression hardened. "Can't or won't?"

"Can't." The word came out as a whisper. "The Oath. It won't let me."

Something shifted in her eyes. The anger didn't fade, but it was joined by understanding, and beneath that, a fear she was trying to hide. She helped him to his feet, supporting his weight against her side.

"We need to get you somewhere private." She glanced around the quad, scanning for threats Drevik couldn't see through the haze of pain. "Can you walk?"

He nodded, though he wasn't sure it was true. Lexi took his arm anyway, guiding him across the grass, past the clusters of students, toward a building he didn't recognize.

"Where are we going?" he asked.

"Somewhere the Order's eyes won't follow." Her voice was low, meant only for him. "There are places on this campus that aren't monitored. Old buildings. Forgotten rooms. You're not the first person who's needed to hide from the Oath's restrictions."

The revelation struck him, even through the pain. "Other people have—"

"Had secrets they couldn't share? Yes." Lexi's grip tightened on his arm. "The Oath is supposed to protect the Order, but sometimes it protects the wrong people. Sometimes it protects the ones who are hurting us."

They entered a building Drevik vaguely recognized—one of the oldest on campus, brick and ivy, scheduled for renovation that never seemed to start. Lexi led him down a hallway, through a door that looked like it hadn't been opened in years, up a narrow staircase that creaked with every step.

The room at the top was small, dusty, lit by a single window letting in the afternoon light. Lexi guided him to a worn couch against the wall, pushed him down with gentle pressure.

"Lie back," she ordered. "Let me see if I can help."

Drevik obeyed, too exhausted to argue. The pain had settled into a constant ache, flaring whenever he thought about Seraphina, about the file, about anything that might lead to truth he couldn't share.

Lexi knelt beside him, her hands hovering over his temples. "This might hurt at first. The Oath's restrictions are woven into your energy. I can try to ease the pressure, but I can't remove it without your vow."

"Do it," he breathed. "Please."

Her fingers touched his temples, cool against his feverish skin. For a moment, nothing happened. Then heat built at the points of contact, spreading through his skull, and the pain shifted.

It didn't disappear. But it moved, settled into something less consuming. Like a wound that had gone from bleeding to scabbed over.

"Better?" Lexi's voice was soft now, intimate in the quiet room.

"Better." Drevik opened his eyes, finding her face inches from his own. This close, he could see the flecks of gold in her green irises, the faint scar at the corner of her mouth, the way her breath came slightly faster than normal.

She didn't pull away.

"You scared me," she admitted. "I saw you go down from across the quad. I thought—" She stopped, swallowed. "I thought something had attacked you. A shadow creature. One of the things from the other side."

"Just the Oath," Drevik said. "Just my own choices, coming back to hurt me."

Lexi's expression flickered. "What did you do? What are you hiding that's worth this?"

He opened his mouth to answer, but the pain flared again, a warning. Not that far. Not yet.

"I can't tell you." The admission felt like defeat. "I want to, Lexi. I want to trust you with everything. But the Oath—"

"The Oath is a tool," she cut in, sharp. "It's not supposed to torture you. It's supposed to ensure loyalty, not punish you for having thoughts the Order doesn't approve of."

"Maybe the Order doesn't approve of my thoughts."

The words hung between them. Lexi's hands were still on his face, her thumbs brushing against his cheekbones in a gesture that felt far too tender for the conversation they were having.

"Then maybe the Order is wrong." Her voice was barely a whisper. "Maybe you're not the one who needs to change."

Drevik's heart stuttered in his chest. She was too close. He was too aware of her—the warmth of her palms, the slight tremble in her fingers, the way her lips parted as she spoke.

"Lexi," he started, but he didn't know how to finish.

"I know." She pulled back slightly, but her hands remained, framing his face. "I know you can't tell me. I know the Oath won't let you. But Drevik, whatever this is—whatever secret you're carrying—I need you to know that I'm on your side. Always. Even when the Order isn't. Even when the Oath makes you doubt it."

"How can you say that?" He searched her face for deception, for any sign of the traitor Ivy had warned him about. "You don't know what I'm hiding."

"I know you." Her thumb traced the line of his jaw. "I know you wouldn't keep a secret unless you had to. I know you wouldn't protect something that would hurt the Order unless the Order was wrong to begin with. And I know—" She paused, something vulnerable passing across her features. "I know I've spent the last three months trying not to care about you, and I'm failing spectacularly."

The confession hit him like a physical blow. He'd suspected, maybe, in the way she looked at him, the way she always seemed to appear when he needed help. But hearing it spoken aloud, in a dusty room in an abandoned building, with her hands still cradling his face—

"Lexi."

"I'm not asking you to tell me," she said quickly. "I'm not asking you to break your Oath. I'm just asking you to believe that when you're ready—when you can—I'll be here. And I won't betray you. I won't use your secrets against you. I won't be the person you're afraid I might be."

Drevik reached up, covering one of her hands with his own. The contact sent a shiver through him, her magic intertwining with his, the shadow energy that bound them both humming with recognition.

"I want to believe that," he said. "I want to trust you."

"Then trust me with what you can." Her eyes searched his. "Test the boundaries. Find the edges of what the Oath will allow. And let me help you carry whatever's hurting you."

He considered it. The Oath restricted specific truths—names, locations, details that could expose the Order's secrets. But what about feelings? Suspicions? Questions without answers?

"There's someone," he said slowly, testing each word before speaking it. "Someone who warned me about traitors in the Order. Someone who said there are people who want me to fail. Who wanted my parents gone."

Lexi went very still. "Who?"

"I can't tell you that." The Oath tightened, but not painfully. A warning, not a punishment. "But I can tell you that I don't know if they're telling the truth. I don't know if the traitor is them, or someone else, or no one at all."

"Your parents." Her voice was careful. "The file Ivy showed you—that's what this is about?"

Drevik nodded. "It was redacted. Pages removed. I don't know what they were hiding, but someone went to a lot of trouble to make sure I couldn't find out."

"Your handler." Lexi's expression darkened. "She's supposed to guide you, not use you for her own investigations."

"She's trying to help."

"She's trying to use you to expose something that might get you killed." The words were harsh, but her tone held more fear than anger. "Drevik, the Order doesn't forgive betrayal. If you're caught digging into classified files, if you're caught conspiring with someone who's already under suspicion—"

"I know." He closed his eyes, feeling the weight of everything pressing down on him. "I know the risks. But Lexi, these are my parents. Someone removed information about their deaths. Someone wanted me to stay ignorant. How can I ignore that?"

Her hand moved from his face to his chest, resting over his heart. The contact was grounding, her magic seeping into him, easing the last traces of the Oath's punishment.

"You can't ignore it," she said quietly. "But you can't face it alone either. And you can't keep destroying yourself to protect secrets that might not even matter."

"They matter to me."

"Then let me help you find a way to share them." Her thumb traced small circles over his heart. "There are rituals—ways to share information under the protection of a blood bond. The Oath can't restrict what's shared between bonded partners."

Drevik's eyes snapped open. "A blood bond?"

"It's not—" Lexi pulled back slightly, color rising in her cheeks. "It's not permanent. It's a temporary connection, a way to share information without speaking it aloud. It's used for sensitive missions, when handlers and acolytes need to communicate without risking exposure."

"And you're offering to do this with me?"

"I'm offering to help you." Her voice was firm, but her eyes dropped, unable to meet his gaze. "Whatever you need. However I can give it."

The air between them felt thick, charged with possibility. Drevik was acutely aware of how close she still was, of the warmth of her hand against his chest, of the way her breathing had quickened.

This is dangerous, he thought. Not the blood bond—though that carried its own risks—but the way he wanted to say yes for all the wrong reasons. The way he wanted her hands on him, her magic inside him, her trust wrapped around him like armor.

"I need to think about it," he said finally. "The bond, the secrets—all of it. I need to figure out who I can trust before I start making magical commitments."

Lexi withdrew her hand, and he felt the loss like a physical ache. "I understand."

"Lexi—" He caught her wrist before she could pull away completely. "I'm not saying no. I'm saying I need time. This isn't something I can rush into."

"Time." She laughed, a hollow sound. "That's something we might not have much of. The Order doesn't wait for acolytes to feel ready. The next trial is in two weeks, and if you're distracted, if you're hiding things, if you're compromised by Oath restrictions—"

"Then I'll fail."

"Then you'll die." The words were blunt, brutal. "The trials aren't academic exercises, Drevik. They're tests of survival. And if you go in distracted, in pain, carrying secrets that are actively hurting you—"

"I know." He released her wrist, running a hand through his hair. "Believe me, I know."

She stood, moving toward the window, her back to him. The afternoon light caught the red in her hair, turned it to fire. "Then let me help you. Let me be the ally you need. Because whatever you're hiding, whatever the Oath is protecting—it's not worth your life."

Drevik wanted to believe her. Wanted to trust that the woman standing in the dusty abandoned room, offering him a magical bond that would let him share his secrets, was exactly who she appeared to be.

But Ivy's warning echoed in his mind. Traitors in the Order. People who wanted your parents gone.

And Lexi was the daughter of an Elder.

He made it through the rest of the day in a fog.

Drevik attended his remaining classes, took notes he couldn't focus on, answered questions he barely understood. The Oath's pain had faded to a dull ache, manageable as long as he didn't think too hard about anything important.

By evening, he'd retreated to his dorm room, door locked, lights off. He sat on the edge of his bed, staring at nothing, trying to organize thoughts that kept slipping away from him.

The blood bond. Lexi's offer circled in his mind. A way to share information without speaking, to bypass the Oath's restrictions, to finally unburden himself of the secrets eating him alive.

But also a way to expose himself completely. To let another person into his mind, his memories, his fears. To trust someone with everything, including the truths that could get him killed.

His phone buzzed. Drevik glanced at the screen, expecting Lexi, or maybe Jenna asking why he'd never made it to the café.

Instead, he found a message from a number he didn't recognize.

I found what was removed from your parents' files. Meet me tomorrow night. Come alone.

His heart stuttered. He didn't need a signature to know who'd sent it. Ivy had found something. Something worth risking everything to see.

I'll be there, he typed back.

The response came immediately: Midnight. The old cemetery on Thornwood Road. Don't be followed.

Drevik set the phone down, staring at the dark screen. Tomorrow night. Another secret. Another risk. Another thread in the web of lies he was weaving around himself.

But somewhere in those redacted files lay the truth about his parents. And he would face anything—Oath restrictions, traitor accusations, even death—to find it.
