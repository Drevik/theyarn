Chapter 10: The Truth About His Parents

The restricted section of the Order's library smelled of dust and secrets.

Drevik stood at the threshold, his heart hammering against his ribs as Ivy's pale hand pressed against the small of his back, urging him forward. The archive room stretched before them, shelves climbing toward a ceiling lost in shadow, each volume bound in leather that seemed to drink the dim light.

"We shouldn't be here," he whispered.

"We have to be." Ivy's voice carried a steel edge he'd never heard from her before. The shy girl who barely met his eyes during training had vanished, replaced by someone with purpose burning behind her violet gaze. "What I'm about to show you doesn't exist. Not officially."

She led him past rows of categorized texts—Shadowlands Bestiary, Lunar Rites and Their Applications, *The History of the Veiled Ones—until they reached a section marked only with a symbol. A crescent moon pierced by a dagger.

Drevik's blood chilled. He'd seen that symbol before. In his grandmother's things, after she passed. He'd assumed it was decorative.

"Your parents were Wardens," Ivy said, pulling a leather-bound journal from the shelf. Her fingers trembled, but her voice remained steady. "High-ranking ones. They weren't killed in a Shadowlands breach like the official records state."

She opened the book and laid it on a reading table. The pages were yellowed, covered in precise handwriting that made Drevik's throat tight. He recognized that script. He'd seen it on the back of a photograph he'd kept hidden for years.

"These are my mother's notes." His voice came out raw.

Ivy nodded, her dark hair falling forward to curtain her face. "They were investigating corruption within the Order. Someone was deliberately weakening the barriers between realms during specific lunar phases. Creating breaches on purpose."

Drevik's hands shook as he turned the pages. His mother's handwriting detailed meetings, financial transactions, names. Some he recognized from Order ceremonies. Others were coded, marked with symbols he couldn't decipher.

"They found proof," Ivy continued. "Evidence that someone at the Elder level was orchestrating the breaches. But before they could present their findings to the Veiled Council—"

"They were silenced." The word tasted like ash.

"The breach that killed them was engineered. Your father's Shadow Walking was sabotaged. Your mother was trapped on the other side of a closing veil." Ivy's voice cracked. "I'm sorry, Drevik. I'm so sorry."

He gripped the edge of the table, his knuckles white. For years, he'd told himself their deaths were an accident. A tragedy. Something random and cruel that the universe had thrown at him. But this—

"How long have you known?"

Ivy's cheeks flushed. "My mother was their friend. She suspected something was wrong when the official story didn't match the magical signatures at the site. She started digging. When she got too close—" The flush drained from her face, leaving her pale. "The same thing happened to her. 'Natural causes,' they said. But I knew."

Drevik looked at her with new eyes. The quiet girl in the back of his acolyte classes. The one who excelled at research and avoided combat training. She'd been carrying this weight alone, just like him.

"Why tell me now?"

"Because the pattern is repeating." Ivy flipped to a later section of the journal, pointing to a series of dates. "The breaches your parents identified? They happened during full moon ceremonies. When the Order's leadership is gathered and focused on the ritual. When security is concentrated in one place."

The full moon ceremony. Three days away.

Drevik's stomach dropped. "Someone's planning another breach."

"I believe so. And I think—" Ivy hesitated, chewing her lower lip. "I think they're going to use you to do it."

The words hit him like a physical blow. "Me?"

"Your Shadow Walking ability. It's exceptional. The Veiled Ones themselves have commented on it." Ivy's gaze dropped to the journal. "If someone wanted to create a catastrophic breach, they'd need a powerful walker to anchor it. Someone who wouldn't realize what they were doing until it was too late."

Drevik thought of the strange attention he'd received since his initiation. Elder Thornwood's personal interest in his training. The extra lessons, the specialized techniques. He'd assumed it was favoritism. Recognition of his talent.

What if it was something else entirely?

"Who?" The question came out harsh. "Who's behind this?"

Ivy's fingers hovered over a list of names in the journal. Drevik followed them with his eyes, and his blood ran cold.

Elder Thornwood's name appeared three times.

"There are others," Ivy said quickly, seeing his expression. "Elder Vance. Warden Corith. But Elder Thornwood—he's the one who's been positioning you. Suggesting you for advanced rituals. Ensuring you're at the center of the full moon ceremony."

Lexi's father.

Drevik's mind spun. Lexi, with her fierce loyalty and protective nature. Lexi, who'd trained him, challenged him, pushed him harder than anyone. Lexi, who'd kissed him in the shadow of the last moon ritual with desperate intensity.

She couldn't know. She couldn't be involved. The thought of her being aware of this, complicit in—

"I don't believe it," he said, but doubt gnawed at the edges of his certainty.

Ivy touched his arm gently. "I don't want to believe it either. Elder Thornwood has a daughter close to our age. Lexi. She's—" A pause, loaded with meaning. "You know her."

The implication hung between them. Drevik thought of Lexi's warnings about trusting too easily. Her suspicious nature. The way she'd questioned his presence from the beginning.

Had she been trying to protect him? Or had she been monitoring him for her father?

Footsteps echoed in the corridor outside.

Drevik's combat instincts flared. He grabbed Ivy's hand and pulled her toward the shadows between shelves, pressing them both into the narrow gap between two massive bookcases. The darkness swallowed them as he wrapped shadow around them like a cloak—a technique he'd mastered beyond what any acolyte should be capable of.

Ivy's body pressed against his in the tight space. Her breath came quick and shallow, her heart racing so hard he felt it against his chest. Her wide violet eyes found his in the darkness, inches away.

"Don't move," he breathed against her ear. "Don't make a sound."

She nodded, her hair brushing his cheek. Her fingers curled into the fabric of his shirt, holding on.

The footsteps grew closer. Measured. Confident. Someone who belonged here.

Drevik held his breath, acutely aware of every point of contact between their bodies. Ivy's soft curves fitted against his harder lines. The warmth radiating from her skin. The subtle scent of her—jasmine and old books and something uniquely her.

A figure passed the end of their aisle. Tall, robed, moving with the authority of rank. Drevik caught a glimpse of silver hair.

Elder Vance.

He waited until the footsteps faded completely before releasing the shadow cloak. But he didn't step back. Neither did Ivy.

"Thank you," she whispered. "For hiding us."

"Thank you for telling me." The words came out rough with emotion he couldn't contain. "You've been carrying this alone. Risking everything. Why?"

Ivy's gaze held his, fierce and vulnerable at once. "Because someone should have helped them. Your parents. My mother." Her voice dropped to barely audible. "Because when I look at you, I see someone who might finally be strong enough to make it matter."

Drevik's hand came up without conscious thought, cupping her face. Her skin was soft, warm, trembling slightly under his touch.

"I won't let their deaths be meaningless," he said. "Either of them."

Ivy's eyes glistened. "I know. That's why I chose you."

She rose on her toes and pressed her lips to his.

The kiss was soft, tentative, nothing like the firestorm of Lexi's kisses. But there was something deeper in it—shared grief, shared purpose, a connection born of secrets and sorrow. Drevik found himself responding, his other hand coming to rest on her hip, steadying her as the kiss deepened.

Ivy made a small sound against his mouth, her fingers tightening in his shirt. She tasted of mint tea and desperation. Of loneliness finally acknowledged.

When they broke apart, both of them breathing harder, Ivy's cheeks were flushed pink.

"I'm sorry," she breathed. "That was—I shouldn't have—"

"Don't apologize." Drevik's thumb stroked her cheekbone. "Not for that."

The words hung between them, weighted with promise. Ivy's lower lip trembled.

"The full moon ceremony," she said, her voice steadying as she stepped back slightly. "Whatever they're planning, it happens then. You need to be prepared. You need to know what to watch for."

She spent the next hour walking him through the journal's contents. His mother's coded notes. The financial irregularities. The pattern of deaths that had been dismissed as accidents over the past two decades. All of it pointed to a conspiracy that reached the highest levels of the Order.

"They're not just creating breaches," Ivy explained, her academic demeanor returning even as her lips remained swollen from their kiss. "They're harvesting shadow essence from the entities that come through. Selling it to outside interests. The barrier between worlds is being deliberately weakened for profit."

Drevik thought of the Shadowlands. The endless twilight realm that pressed against the edges of reality. If the barrier fell, creatures would pour through unchecked. Thousands would die.

"Who's buying it?"

"I don't know yet. But there are references to a 'client' in the later entries. Someone powerful outside the Order." Ivy's expression darkened. "Your parents were close to identifying them when they were killed."

Drevik's jaw tightened. "And Elder Thornwood?"

"Has been the primary advocate for expanding Shadow Walking training. Specifically for acolytes with unusual talent." Her gaze met his. "Specifically for you."

The pieces clicked together with sickening clarity. The extra sessions. The advanced techniques. The way Elder Thornwood had personally overseen his progress.

He was being groomed. But not for advancement.

For sacrifice.

"I need to tell Lexi," Drevik said, though the words tasted bitter. If she wasn't already compromised. If she wasn't already part of this.

Ivy's expression flickered. "Be careful. If she's loyal to her father—"

"I know." He touched Ivy's hand briefly. "I'll be careful."

They left the restricted section separately, timing their exits to avoid notice. Drevik's mind whirled with everything he'd learned. His parents, murdered. A conspiracy spanning decades. And somewhere in the center of it all, the man who had taken a personal interest in his development.

The man whose daughter had become the first woman Drevik had let past his defenses.

He needed time to think. To plan. To figure out who he could trust.

The message was waiting for him when he returned to his quarters.

A folded parchment, sealed with wax impressed by a crescent moon and dagger. The symbol that marked his mother's journal. The symbol of the conspiracy that had killed her.

Drevik broke the seal with steady hands, forcing himself to remain calm.

ACOLYTE MARKINSOUL,

Your presence is requested in the Elder chambers before the full moon ceremony. Elder Thornwood wishes to discuss your upcoming role in the rituals.

Fail to appear at your peril.

No signature. No indication of who had delivered it.

Drevik read the message three times, each pass making the implications clearer. Elder Thornwood wanted to speak with him. Alone. Before the ceremony where, if Ivy was right, they planned to use him to create a catastrophic breach.

He could run. Use his Shadow Walking to escape the compound entirely. Disappear into the world beyond the Order's reach.

But that would mean leaving his parents' deaths unavenged. Leaving Ivy alone with her dangerous knowledge. Leaving Lexi—

Lexi, who might be innocent. Who might be just as much a pawn as he was.

Drevik crumpled the message in his fist.

His parents had died trying to expose the truth. They'd been silenced before they could act.

He wouldn't make the same mistake.

The full moon ceremony was in three days. Elder Thornwood wanted a private meeting.

Drevik would give him one.

But he wouldn't walk in blind. And he wouldn't walk in alone.

He thought of Ivy's fierce determination beneath her shy exterior. Of Lexi's protective instincts and suspicious nature. Of the two women who had, in very different ways, claimed pieces of his heart.

If he was going to survive this, he'd need both of them.

The question was whether either of them could truly be trusted.

Drevik stared out his window at the moon, already swelling toward full. In three days, it would hang heavy and silver in the sky, pulling at shadow magic and dark ambitions alike.

In three days, he'd find out who his real enemies were.

And he'd make them pay for what they'd done to his parents.

His mother's journal sat heavy in his jacket pocket, a dead woman's testimony against the Order's leadership. Evidence that could expose everything—if he lived long enough to use it.

Drevik closed his eyes and let the shadows gather around him, drawing strength from the darkness that was both his gift and his heritage.

His parents had died for the truth.

He would not let their sacrifice be in vain.

The knock came at midnight.

Drevik had been lying in bed, staring at the ceiling, running scenarios through his mind until they blurred together. The quiet tap on his door made him sit up instantly, shadows coiling around his fists.

"Who is it?"

"Me." Ivy's voice, barely audible through the wood. "Let me in. Please."

He crossed the room in three strides and pulled the door open. Ivy stood in the hallway, wrapped in a dark cloak that swallowed her slight frame. Her eyes were red-rimmed, her expression tight with fear.

"I had to come," she said, slipping past him into his room. "I couldn't stay in my quarters after—after everything."

Drevik closed the door, suddenly aware that they were alone. In his room. At midnight. With the weight of their shared secrets pressing them together.

"What happened?" He kept his voice low, conscious of the thin walls.

"I found something else." Ivy pulled a folded paper from her cloak, pressing it into his hands. "A letter. Hidden in my mother's things. It's from your father."

Drevik's world narrowed to the paper in his grip. His father's handwriting—bolder than his mother's, more angular—covered the page.

Caroline,

If you're reading this, I've failed. The conspiracy goes deeper than we imagined. Thornwood is not the architect—he's a pawn. The true enemy wears a face we trust. Trust no one with the silver mark.

Tell our son the truth. Tell him his gift is his inheritance and his weapon. Tell him—

The letter ended there. Torn, as if someone had interrupted the writing. Or the writer had been interrupted permanently.

"Silver mark," Drevik murmured. "What does that mean?"

"I don't know." Ivy's voice shook. "But your father trusted my mother. He sent her this letter as insurance. A dead man's switch."

Drevik looked up from the paper to find Ivy watching him with those wide violet eyes. Tears tracked silently down her cheeks.

"I'm scared," she admitted. "For the first time since my mother died, I'm actually scared. And I don't know what to do."

Drevik set the letter aside and opened his arms.

Ivy stepped into them without hesitation.

Her body pressed against his, trembling with suppressed sobs. Drevik held her, one hand cradling the back of her head, feeling her warmth seep into him. She felt fragile in his embrace, but he knew that was an illusion. Ivy had been carrying the weight of her mother's murder for years. She was stronger than anyone gave her credit for.

"I won't let anything happen to you," he said against her hair. "I promise."

"You can't promise that." Her voice was muffled against his chest. "None of us are safe."

"Then we'll be unsafe together." He pulled back slightly, tilting her chin up to meet his eyes. "You're not alone anymore, Ivy. You hear me? Whatever comes next, you don't face it alone."

Her lower lip quivered. "Why are you being so good to me?"

"Because someone should have helped you." He echoed her earlier words back to her. "Because when I look at you, I see someone who might be strong enough to make it matter."

Ivy's laugh was watery but genuine. "That's my line."

"I'm stealing it." Drevik brushed a strand of hair from her face, his thumb lingering on her cheek. "Consider it payment for everything you've risked for me."

Her eyes dropped to his mouth. The air between them thickened with awareness.

"Drevik," she breathed. "I—"

He kissed her before she could finish.

This kiss was different from the one in the library. Less tentative, more certain. Ivy responded instantly, her fingers curling into his shirt as she pressed closer. Drevik's hands slid down her sides, feeling the curves hidden beneath her shapeless cloak, the way her body fitted against his.

She made a soft sound of need that went straight to his core.

"Stay," he murmured against her lips. "Tonight. Stay with me."

Ivy pulled back just enough to meet his eyes. Her expression was torn—want and fear and hope tangled together.

"I shouldn't," she whispered. But her hands didn't release his shirt. "We have so much to do. So much to prepare."

"The world might end in three days." Drevik's voice was rough. "I don't want to spend tonight alone."

Something shifted in Ivy's face. The fear receded, replaced by determination.

"Then don't."

She let her cloak fall to the floor.

Drevik woke to gray dawn light and an empty bed.

The sheets beside him were still warm, carrying Ivy's jasmine scent. A note lay on the pillow where her head had rested.

I'll see you at breakfast. Act normal. We'll find a way through this.

Ivy

He read the note twice, memorizing the shape of her handwriting. Another piece of evidence to add to his collection—though this one he'd guard more jealously than any journal.

The full moon ceremony loomed. Elder Thornwood's summons waited. His father's warning about a silver mark echoed in his mind.

But for the first time since his parents died, Drevik felt something other than grief or rage.

He felt purpose.

And he wasn't alone anymore.

That had to count for something.

The day passed in a blur of normalcy that felt increasingly surreal. Training sessions. Classes. Meals in the communal hall where he sat across from Lexi, acutely aware of her father's name in his mother's journal.

Lexi watched him with sharp green eyes throughout lunch, her expression unreadable.

"You're distracted," she said, stabbing at her food. "Something wrong?"

"Nothing." The lie came easily. "Just pre-ceremony nerves."

Lexi's gaze narrowed, but she let it go. "The full moon ritual is intense. You'll need your focus."

"I know."

A pause. Then, softer: "My father wants to meet with you. Before the ceremony."

Drevik's heart stuttered, but he kept his face neutral. "I received the summons."

"He's taken a special interest in your development." Lexi's voice carried an edge he couldn't interpret. "It's an honor."

"Is it?"

The question came out sharper than intended. Lexi's eyes flashed.

"What does that mean?"

Drevik forced himself to relax. "Nothing. I'm just wondering why an Elder would bother with an acolyte."

"You're not just any acolyte." Lexi leaned closer, lowering her voice. "You're talented. Powerful. The Order needs people like you."

Needs me to be a sacrifice, Drevik thought. Needs me to create a breach.

Needs me dead.

But he smiled and nodded, playing the part of the grateful initiate.

"Of course. I'm honored by the attention."

Lexi studied him for a long moment, her expression troubled. Then she stood abruptly.

"I'll see you at training."

She walked away, leaving Drevik with the weight of secrets he couldn't share and suspicions he couldn't voice.

Ivy caught his eye from across the hall, a subtle question in her gaze.

Drevik gave an almost imperceptible nod.

They were in this together now. Whatever came next.

The full moon rose in two days, carrying with it the promise of revelation or ruin.

Drevik intended to be ready for either.
