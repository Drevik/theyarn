October Video Game Deals 2026: Six Picks That Survived Amazon's Prime Sale

October 14, 2026 · 6 deals tracked · By Marcus Chen

Amazon's October Prime Big Deal Days ran October 6 and 7. I wrote down the gaming prices during the sale, fully intending to publish the usual roundup. Then the sale ended and the roundup became useless, because most of those prices evaporated within 48 hours. Pragmata went from $39.99 back to $59.99. Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds jumped from $19 to $44.95. Resident Evil Requiem, which had been $49.99, snapped right back to $69.99.

So this is the article that nobody else seems to write: the follow-up. Everything below was re-checked against Amazon's live buyboxes on October 14, a week after the sale closed. These are the prices that held. Some are Prime-sale leftovers that Amazon simply never revoked. Some are ordinary discounts that happened to coincide with the sale and outlasted it. Either way, they're real, the links point at the exact listings I verified, and every one of them carries a genuine downside section because no game deal is flawless.

I judge game deals on one metric above all others: dollars per hour of play. A games console is hardware, but a game is content, and content has a performance spec just like a CPU does. It's called campaign length. Let's do the math.

This is the deal I'd spend my own money on this week. During the sale Square Enix briefly gutted Rebirth to $16.99, and even the deal sites said it wouldn't survive. They were right. What did survive is a $33.98 buybox straight from Amazon, which lands a dollar under the lowest physical price I tracked at any point this year. For a game that launched at $69.99, that's half off with free Prime shipping.

The value math is silly. Forty hours of main story puts you under $0.85 an hour, and if you're the type who clears every region and plays the card games, you're at $0.42 an hour. That's the best price-to-content ratio on this page. Rebirth is also the middle chapter of the remake trilogy operating at the absolute peak of its ambition, huge open regions, a party system with real depth, and combat that finally clicks about ten hours in.

The downsides are real, so here they are. You must play Final Fantasy VII Remake first, or the opening hours will make no sense. The open-world checklist design will feel familiar if you've played any Ubisoft game from the last decade. The minigame density is aggressive; there is a beach resort section that hijacks the plot for hours. And the ending is a literal cliffhanger, so you're buying into a trilogy, not a complete story. If any of that bothers you, grab Remake first (it's on this list too) and come back.

Here's the quiet steal of the season. Dragon Quest VII Reimagined shipped in February at $59.99, and eight months later it's sitting at $34.99 with no sale event to blame. That's the kind of cut you usually see on a three-year-old game, not a current-year JRPG from Square Enix. I checked the Prime sale listings twice expecting this price to be a flash deal. It just stayed put.

The game itself is a strange and wonderful thing. The PS1 original was a hundred-hour marathon about islands restoring a drowned world, one fragment at a time, and this remake keeps that island-by-island structure while fixing the tedium that kept most players from finishing it. If you like classic turn-based combat and you want your money measured in dozens of hours, this is the cheapest big new JRPG on Amazon, full stop. At $34.99 over even 50 hours, you're paying about 70 cents an hour.

Downsides, honestly stated. The current seller on this listing charges $5.49 for delivery; when the buybox flips back to Amazon retail, shipping goes free, so check before checkout. The pacing is still slow-burn even by design, the first island takes hours to resolve, and the famous story twist lands the same way it did in 2000, which some returning players will see coming. Turn-based combat is also a genuine dealbreaker for a lot of modern players. Know yourself before you buy.

Fifty-five percent off a first-party shooter eight months after launch. That's the deepest cut still standing from the October sale window, and there's no asterisk on it: the buybox says $31.49 outright. The PS5 copy runs a few dollars higher right now, which makes the Series X version the cheapest way into the game for anyone with both consoles.

Dark Ages chose a direction and committed: heavier, slower, ground-bound combat built around a shield saw you throw and recall, plus a mech section and a dragon-flying sequence that break up the rhythm. I found the middle third sags under repetition, and players who loved Eternal's ballet will miss the double jump badly. It's a more deliberate game than its predecessors, and deliberate isn't for everyone.

One warning that saves you money entirely: if you're on Game Pass Ultimate, this game is already in the library. Paying $31.49 for a copy you technically already subscribe to is the kind of double-dip a price nerd has to call out. This deal is for PS5 owners who want the cheapest copy, or Xbox players who've let their subscription lapse.

Half price for two remakes of the two best Tony Hawk games ever made. The pure dollars-per-hour math is unflattering, the combined campaign is maybe 10-12 hours, which works out to over $2 an hour. But that metric undersells a sports game, because a sports game is a toy, not a story. THPS is built to be replayed in 15-minute bursts forever, ideally with two people trading the controller on a couch. Am I describing myself in 2001? Yes. Has the formula aged? Not really.

Honest downsides: the fourth game's career structure was rebuilt into the timed-level format of the third, which veterans of the original THPS4 free-roam mode have loudly resented, and they're not wrong to. If your memories are specifically of THPS4's open structure, that memory has been edited in this remake. The soundtrack is packed, but your mileage on any licensed setlist is pure taste. And if you've never clicked with trick-line games, nothing here will convert you.

And if you only want one RPG this month and don't plan to continue the trilogy, buy Rebirth and backfill later instead. The sequel is the better game.

How I Verify These Prices

Every price above was scraped from the actual product page buybox the morning of October 14, not copied from another outlet's roundup. When a deal site says a game is $20 off, I want to know whether that's the Amazon buybox or some third-party marketplace seller with $6 shipping and a two-week delivery window, because those are very different purchases.

Three habits carry most of the weight. First, wishlist anything you're watching; Amazon's wishlist alerts do the vigilance for you. Second, run the ASIN through a price-history tracker before believing a "discount," because a fake list price is the oldest trick in retail. Third, decide your ceiling before the sale starts. Requiem at $49.99 is fair for 12 hours of horror. Requiem at $69.99 is not, and knowing that in advance is what stops you from rage-buying at full price two days after the sale ends.

On the calendar question: for games specifically, Black Friday is usually the better event. October's sale was stronger on hardware and accessories, while the game discounts it ran mostly dissolved within the week. The two Square Enix prices above are the exception, and they're live now, which is why this list exists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are the October Prime Day game prices still available?

Most are not. Prime Big Deal Days ran October 6-7 and was Prime-member exclusive, and most game prices snapped back within days. When I re-checked on October 14, Pragmata had jumped from $39.99 back to $59.99, Sonic Racing: CrossWorlds went from $19 to $44.95, and Resident Evil Requiem returned to $69.99. The picks in this guide are the ones that held, verified against live Amazon buyboxes on October 14.

Should I wait for Black Friday instead of buying now?

For most games, yes. Amazon's October sale historically repeats or beats these prices in November, and Requiem at $49.99 will very likely return to that range or better. The exceptions: FF7 Rebirth at $33.98 already matches its lowest physical price this year, physical deals can sell out, and if a game is already at a price you'd be happy paying, waiting costs you the weeks you could have been playing it.

Do I need to play Final Fantasy VII Remake before Rebirth?

Yes, unreservedly. Rebirth is the direct continuation of Remake's story and assumes you know the party, the combat system, and the ending. Play Remake Intergrade first, at $31.89, then Rebirth at $33.98. That's about $66 total for two 30-plus hour RPGs, which is still less than one new full-price release.

Is DOOM: The Dark Ages better on Xbox or PS5?

The game is identical on both, but the Xbox Series X copy is cheaper right now at $31.49, with the PS5 version a few dollars higher. One warning: if you subscribe to Game Pass Ultimate, Dark Ages is already in the library, so buying a copy would mean paying twice for the same game. This deal makes the most sense for PS5 players, or Xbox players without a subscription.

Why do game deals die so fast after Amazon sales end?

Prime Big Deal Days pricing is a temporary, member-exclusive flag on the buybox, and Amazon's systems automatically revert it when the event closes. Prices that survive, like the Square Enix pair on this list, are ordinary discounts that happened to overlap with the event. That's also why deal roundups go stale within 48 hours and why the publish date of any deals article matters more than its headline.

How do I catch the next drop on a game not listed here?

Wishlist it on Amazon with alerts on, run the ASIN through CamelCamelCamel to learn its real price history, and set a target price in advance. For a 10-12 hour game like Requiem, my ceiling is $50. For 40+ hour RPGs I'll go higher per copy because the math works. Buy the moment your number hits, because on Amazon it won't stay there.

Dollar per hour, nothing else on this page comes close: a 40-to-80-hour trilogy centerpiece at half its launch price, sold by Amazon with free Prime shipping. One dollar under its best physical price all year, and it's the pick I'd spend my own money on this week.
