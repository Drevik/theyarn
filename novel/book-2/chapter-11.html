Chapter 11: Shadows Within

Chapter 11: Shadows Within

The Shadowlands had teeth.

Drevik felt them sinking into his consciousness with every step—small, insistent bites that nibbled at the edges of his sanity. The realm of eternal twilight pressed against his skin like a living thing, and somewhere deep in his chest, something was waking up.

Something hungry.

"Stop," Seraphina commanded, her voice cutting through the oppressive silence. She held up one hand, silver rings catching light from no visible source. "We're not alone."

Drevik froze, his boots sinking into ground that wasn't quite solid. Around them, the landscape twisted—ancient trees with bark like charred bone stretched toward a sky that held no stars, only swirling curtains of deep purple and black. The air tasted of copper and forgotten memories.

Lexi moved to his left flank, her auburn hair plastered to her neck with sweat despite the cold. Her green eyes scanned the shadows with practiced intensity. "I don't see anything."

"That's rather the point." Seraphina's通常 composed features held an edge of tension Drevik hadn't seen before. "The things that hunt here don't wish to be seen. Not until they're ready."

Mira clutched her jacket tighter, the young initiate's face pale but determined. She'd insisted on coming despite the risks—something about proving herself. Drevik admired her courage even as he questioned her judgment.

Just like mine.

"What do we do?" Mira asked, her voice smaller than she probably intended.

Drevik felt the answer before anyone spoke it. The darkness inside him stirred, stretching like a cat waking from a long nap. It recognized this place—not the specific location, but the nature of it. The Shadowlands weren't foreign territory.

They were home.

"We move forward," he said, and the words came from somewhere deeper than his throat. "Whatever's watching us has already made its decision. Running won't change anything."

Lexi shot him a look that carried a dozen arguments, but she didn't contradict him. Her jaw tightened. "Fine. But stay close, and if I tell you to run—"

"I run. I know." Drevik managed a thin smile. "You've only told me thirty times since we entered."

"Thirty-one," she corrected. "I counted."

He would have laughed under different circumstances. Would have let himself appreciate the way her dry humor cut through his fear. But the thing inside him was too busy reaching—extending tendrils of awareness toward the shadows around them like a plant turning toward sunlight.

Let me out, it seemed to whisper. Let me see. Let me hunt.

Drevik pressed his palm against his sternum, feeling his heartbeat thunder beneath his ribs. "Which way?"

Seraphina consulted the small obsidian disc in her hand, its surface swirling with faint luminescence. "Northeast. The breach point should be less than a mile. But the readings are... strange." She frowned. "The path keeps shifting."

"Shifting how?" Lexi moved closer to Drevik, her shoulder brushing his. The contact sent warmth flooding through him—sharp contrast to the cold seeping into his bones.

"Like something is redirecting us." Seraphina's dark eyes met Drevik's, and something passed between them. Recognition, perhaps. Or warning. "The Shadowlands sense us. And they're... curious."

"Curious." Mira's voice cracked slightly. "About what?"

"About him." Seraphina didn't look away from Drevik. "The darkness in you—it's not entirely yours, is it? It's been here before. Part of it remembers."

Drevik's mouth went dry. "I don't understand."

"No. You wouldn't yet." Seraphina tucked the disc away. "But you will. Come. We're losing time."

They moved deeper into the Shadowlands, and with every step, Drevik felt the realm pressing closer. Not hostile—curious. Like a child poking at a strange insect to see what it would do.

The darkness inside him responded in kind. It unfurled through his veins, cold and familiar, and he let it. Let it spread through his fingers and toes, let it settle behind his eyes like a second sight.

The shadows around them became clearer. Sharper. He could see the way they moved—not randomly, but in patterns. Flows and eddies like water around stones.

"There," he breathed, pointing. "The path isn't shifting. It's being directed. Someone's channeling the shadows."

Seraphina stopped, her head tilting. "You can see that?"

"I can see... something." Drevik blinked, trying to find words for what he was perceiving. "Like currents. And there's a source—something's pulling them."

Lexi's hand found his wrist, her grip tight. "Drevik. Your eyes."

He touched his face reflexively. "What about them?"

"They're black." Her voice held a steadiness that her expression didn't match. "Completely. Even the whites."

Mira made a small sound of distress.

"It's fine." Drevik surprised himself with how calm he felt. "It doesn't hurt. It's just... letting me see."

"Letting you see what the Shadowlands see." Seraphina's voice was quiet, thoughtful. "That's not supposed to be possible for an acolyte. Even Elders struggle with True Sight in this realm."

"I don't think I'm doing it on purpose."

"No. I don't think you are either." Seraphina studied him with an intensity that made him want to step back. "The question is whether that makes it better or worse."

Before he could respond, the ground beneath them shuddered. The shadows convulsed, writhing like wounded things, and from somewhere ahead came a sound that wasn't quite a roar—more like a scream stretched into infinity.

"Everyone down!" Lexi yanked Drevik to the ground as something massive passed overhead. He caught only fragments—leathery wings, too many limbs, eyes that burned like dying stars.

It moved faster than should have been possible, vanishing into the twisting trees without pause. Hunting something else. Something that wasn't them.

Yet.

"What in the hells was that?" Mira's whisper was barely audible.

"A Hollow." Seraphina rose slowly, brushing dirt from her robes. "They're... collectors. They take things from this realm to others. Sometimes they bring things back."

"Things?"

"Best not to ask." Seraphina's usually warm voice had gone flat. "We need to move. Now. Before it circles back."

Drevik didn't need to be told twice. But as they moved through the twisting landscape, following currents only he could see, the darkness inside him grew stronger. More demanding. It wanted to take—to spread through him completely and use his body as a vessel.

You could control it, a voice whispered from somewhere deep. Not the darkness itself, but something adjacent. Something that felt almost like memory. You could make it serve you instead.

"Drevik." Lexi's voice cut through the haze. "You're shaking."

He looked down at his hands. She was right—tremors ran through his fingers, and his skin had gone pale as moonlight. The darkness was trying to consume him, and his body was fighting back.

"I'm fine."

"You're a terrible liar." Lexi positioned herself in front of him, forcing him to stop. "Let me see your eyes."

"Lexi—"

"Let me see." Her voice brooked no argument.

He met her gaze, letting her look. The concern in her face shifted to something harder—fear, maybe, or determination. Her fingers came up to brush his cheek, cool against his feverish skin.

"Your pupils are blown, but the black is receding." She spoke quietly, for him alone. "Whatever's happening, you're fighting it. Keep fighting."

"I don't know how much longer I can."

"Then let us help." Her thumb traced his cheekbone, a gesture so tender it made his chest ache. "You don't have to do everything alone, Drevik. No matter what the shadows tell you."

He leaned into her touch despite himself. Just for a moment. Just long enough to remember what warmth felt like.

"Path's clear ahead," he managed. "About half a mile. Something's... different there. The currents are pooling."

"Pooling?" Seraphina moved closer. "Like a nexus?"

"Maybe. I don't know enough to say for certain." Drevik forced himself to straighten, to pull back from the darkness. It retreated reluctantly, like a sullen child denied a treat. "But it's the most stable path I've seen. Everything else keeps shifting."

"Then we follow it." Seraphina glanced at the sky—which held no sun, but somehow still showed passage of time. "We have perhaps three hours before we need to return. The veil weakens at night, and I'd rather not be here when the Hollows wake fully."

They pressed on, and Drevik led them through the currents he could sense. Twice more they had to hide—once from a creature that looked like a wolf made of crystallized shadow, once from something that had no visible form at all, only a presence that made the air itself scream.

Each time, Drevik felt the darkness inside him respond. I could destroy that, it whispered. Let me out. Let me show you.

Each time, he pushed it back. But the effort was cumulative. Each refusal cost him something.

By the time they reached the clearing, he was barely standing.

The space opened suddenly—one moment they were pushing through trees that seemed to breathe, the next they stood in a perfect circle of bare earth. In the center, a single stone pillar rose from the ground, its surface covered in symbols that hurt to look at directly.

"The breach point," Seraphina breathed. "It's... intact. That's not possible."

"The Order sealed these months ago." Lexi moved to the pillar, running her fingers over the symbols without touching them. "This one should have collapsed."

"Someone's maintaining it." Drevik didn't know how he knew, but the certainty settled in his bones. "The currents here—they're being fed. Deliberately."

"By who?"

"By—" He stopped, the darkness suddenly surging. Not attacking. Showing.

The world around him went still. The shadows peeled back, revealing layers of reality that had been hidden. He could see the breach—really see it—a wound in the fabric between worlds that someone was keeping open with deliberate care. Power flowed through it, power that tasted familiar.

Power that tasted like him.

"Drevik!" Lexi's voice reached him from somewhere far away. "Drevik, your nose is bleeding."

He blinked, the vision fading. Warm liquid trickled over his lips. His hand came away red.

"I saw it," he rasped. "The breach. It's anchored to something in the mortal realm. Something that's... connected to me."

Seraphina's expression had gone carefully blank. "Are you certain?"

"The power signature. It's similar to mine. Similar to the darkness I carry." He wiped the blood away, but more came. "Someone is using my connection to keep this open. Using me."

Lexi's grip on his arm tightened. "That's not possible. You've been with the Order for months. No one could—"

"I don't think they need my permission." Drevik looked at his hands, still trembling. Still pale. "I think the darkness in me is old. Older than I am. And I think someone has been using it for a very long time."

The silence that followed was broken only by Mira's quickened breathing.

"We need to go back," Seraphina said finally. "Now. We have what we came for."

"We don't have anything useful—"

"We have more than you realize." Seraphina was already moving toward the path they'd come by. "And your acolyte is about to collapse. Look at him, Lexi. Really look."

Lexi did. Her sharp intake of breath told him everything about how bad he must look.

"Drevik, your skin..." She touched his face again, and this time her fingers came away marked with something dark. Something that moved.

Shadow. He was bleeding shadow.

"We need to get him through the veil." Lexi's voice had gone hard with command. "Mira, support his other side. Seraphina, get us out of here."

"I'm trying." Seraphina held up the obsidian disc, now pulsing rapidly. "The path is unstable. The Shadowlands don't want to let him go."

"Then make them." Lexi's tone left no room for argument. "Whatever it takes."

Drevik wanted to tell them not to bother. Wanted to tell them the darkness was winning, was spreading, was finally taking what it had been promised. But his voice wouldn't work.

His legs stopped working too.

The last thing he saw before the shadows took him was Lexi's face—fierce and terrified in equal measure. The last thing he heard was her screaming his name.

Black.

Endless, suffocating black. The kind that existed before light was invented. The kind that would exist after the last star died.

Drevik floated in it—or stood, or knelt, the distinction didn't seem to matter here. There was no up or down. No sensation except the cold that had seeped into his bones and the hunger that had taken residence in his chest.

"You're fighting." The voice came from everywhere and nowhere. "You don't have to."

"Who are you?" His thoughts formed words without his mouth moving.

"I'm the part of you that knows what you want." The darkness rippled, and a figure emerged from it. His own face looked back at him—but different. Older. Eyes completely black. Smile too wide. "I'm the part that's been waiting."

"Waiting for what?"

"For you to stop pretending you don't want power. Stop pretending you don't want revenge against everyone who's ever hurt you. Stop pretending you don't want to take what's yours." The other-him circled slowly. "I know what you dream about, Drevik. I know what you think when you look at Lexi. When you look at Seraphina. When you think about all the ways they could be yours."

"That's not—I don't—"

"Lies." The figure's smile widened. "You've been alone your whole life. Your grandmother tried, but she couldn't understand. The other children at school couldn't see you. The Order sees you only as a tool. But I see you. I see everything."

"Why should I trust anything from this place?"

"Because I'm the only one being honest." The figure stopped in front of him. "The darkness in you isn't a curse, Drevik. It's a birthright. Your mother gave it to you before she disappeared. Your father tried to steal it back. You think it's an accident you ended up with the Order? You think any of this is random?"

"What do you know about my parents?"

"Everything." The other-him leaned close. "But I won't give it away. That's not how this works. You want answers? You have to come get them yourself. Wake up. Find the path. And when you're ready to stop being afraid of what you are..."

The figure pressed a hand to Drevik's chest—right over the hunger, the cold, the thing that had been growing inside him.

"...let me out."

He woke to soft sheets and candlelight.

Drevik blinked, disoriented. The ceiling above him was stone, crisscrossed with shadows that seemed to dance of their own accord. His body felt heavy, like it had been filled with sand, and his head pounded with the remnants of the vision.

"Easy." A hand pressed against his shoulder. "You've been out for two days."

He turned his head. Lexi sat beside the narrow bed, her auburn hair loose around her shoulders, dark circles under her eyes. She looked like she hadn't slept since he'd collapsed.

"Two days?" His voice came out as a croak.

"Thirty-seven hours, technically. But who's counting?" She tried for a smile, but it didn't reach her eyes. "How do you feel?"

"Like someone filled my veins with ice water."

"That's not far from accurate. Seraphina had to purge the shadow contamination. It..." Lexi's voice caught. "It nearly killed you. Would have, if she hadn't acted fast enough."

Drevik tried to sit up. His arms shook with the effort.

"Don't." Lexi pushed him back down, gentle but firm. "Your body is still recovering. You need rest."

"I need answers." He met her gaze. "What happened to me?"

Lexi's jaw tightened. "We don't know exactly. Seraphina's theory is that the Shadowlands recognized the darkness in you and tried to... claim it. Activate it. Whatever the right word is."

"And the breach?"

"Still open. Sealed from our side, but whoever's maintaining it has resources we didn't anticipate." She looked away. "The Order is sending more acolytes. But there's something else."

"Something else?"

Lexi's silence stretched. When she finally spoke, her voice was barely audible. "You told us your parents were dead."

"They are." But even as he said it, doubt crept in. The figure in his vision had mentioned his mother. Had said she'd given him the darkness.

"Seraphina did some digging while you were unconscious. The records she found..." Lexi handed him a folded piece of paper. "Your mother was a member of the Order. A Seeker. She didn't die, Drevik. She vanished. Into the Shadowlands. Twenty-one years ago."

The paper trembled in his hands. A photograph had been printed on it—faded, grainy, but clear enough to make out. A woman with dark hair and familiar green-gold eyes stood in acolyte robes, her expression serious but her eyes laughing.

"She looks like me," he breathed.

"She looks exactly like you." Lexi's voice was gentle. "Same eyes. Same bone structure. She was researching something in the Shadowlands when she disappeared. No body was ever found."

The darkness inside him stirred—not with hunger this time, but with recognition. Memory. Something deep and old and wanting.

Find me, it seemed to whisper. I've been waiting.

"Drevik?" Lexi's hand covered his. "What is it?"

He looked at her—really looked, letting himself see past the fear and exhaustion to the woman beneath. The one who'd stayed by his bedside for thirty-seven hours. The one who'd screamed his name when he'd fallen.

"I saw something," he said slowly. "In the vision. Someone. He told me to find a path."

"What path?"

"I don't know yet." He set the paper aside, his hand finding hers. "But I know I can't do this alone. Any of it."

Lexi's fingers intertwined with his. "You're not alone. You haven't been alone for months. You just keep refusing to see it."

"Maybe I'm done refusing."

Her breath caught. "What does that mean?"

He didn't answer with words. Instead, he pulled her toward him—gently, giving her every chance to resist. She didn't. Her lips met his with a desperation that matched his own, months of tension and fear and wanting coalescing into a single point of contact.

"Wait—" She pulled back, her forehead against his. "You're recovering. You nearly died. This isn't—"

"Lexi." He cupped her face, feeling the slight tremor in her jaw. "I nearly died. I might die tomorrow. Or the day after. And I'm tired of pretending I don't want you when every moment I spend with you is a moment I'm fighting not to touch you."

Her eyes searched his, looking for something. Whatever she found made her decision.

"To hell with it," she whispered, and kissed him again.

This kiss was different. Deeper. The gentle exploration of before gave way to something hungrier. Her hands found his chest, pushing aside the thin fabric of whatever shirt someone had dressed him in. His fingers tangled in her hair, pulling her closer.

She broke away long enough to shed her jacket, her tank top, the practical clothes she wore like armor. In the candlelight, her body was all muscle and softness, scars from old battles mapping her skin like constellations.

"You're staring," she said, a hint of her usual sharpness in her voice.

"I'm appreciating." He sat up properly, ignoring the protest of his muscles, and pulled her onto the bed with him. "There's a difference."

"Is there?"

"Let me show you."

He kissed her collarbone, her shoulder, the curve of her breast. She made a sound that wasn't quite a moan—more like a surrender. Her fingers found his hair, his back, pulling him closer.

"Tell me if it's too much," he murmured against her skin. "Tell me to stop."

"Don't you dare."

He laughed, the sound surprising him. When had he last laughed? When had he last felt anything other than the weight of responsibility and fear?

Her hands were everywhere, tracing the lines of his chest, his stomach, lower. He returned the favor, learning her body with the same intensity he'd learned everything else about her—carefully, thoroughly, with complete focus.

When he finally pressed inside her, they both went still. The sensation was overwhelming—not just physical, but emotional. This was Lexi, who'd fought him and trained him and pushed him and protected him. Lexi, who'd stayed.

"Look at me," she whispered.

He opened eyes he hadn't realized he'd closed. Her gaze was fierce, challenging, just like always.

"Don't disappear on me," she said. "Stay. Stay right here, with me."

"I'm not going anywhere."

He moved, and she moved with him, and for a few moments the darkness inside him went quiet. There was only Lexi—her breath, her skin, her voice saying his name like it meant something. Like he meant something.

After, they lay tangled together in the narrow bed. Her head rested on his chest, her fingers tracing absent patterns on his skin.

"We should get up," she said eventually. "Seraphina will want to know you're awake."

"Seraphina can wait."

"Drevik—"

"I mean it." He tilted her chin up, forcing her to meet his eyes. "I know this doesn't solve anything. I know we still have a breach to close and a mystery to untangle and probably a dozen other crises I don't know about yet. But right now, in this moment, I just want to stay here. With you. Can I have that?"

Her expression softened in a way he'd rarely seen. "Yeah. You can have that."

They lay in silence as the candles burned lower. Eventually, Drevik's eyes began to droop—not from exhaustion this time, but from peace. From warmth.

"Drevik?" Lexi's voice was soft, drowsy.

"Hmm?"

"I'm glad you didn't die."

He smiled into her hair. "Me too."

He dreamed again, but this dream was different.

The darkness didn't press in. Instead, it parted—revealing a path he hadn't seen before. Symbols floated past him, coordinates and coordinates and coordinates, burning themselves into his memory.

And at the end of the path, a woman waited.

She looked older than the photograph. Lines of exhaustion around her eyes, silver threading through dark hair. But the green-gold eyes were the same. The quiet intensity was the same.

Drevik, she said, though her lips didn't move. You found your way.

Who are you?

You know who I am.

You're dead. The records—

Are wrong. Deliberately so. She smiled, and it was a tired smile, but real. I've been waiting for you, my son. You have so much to learn. So much to become. But first, you have to find me.

Where are you?

The coordinates burned brighter. Remember these. When you're ready—truly ready—follow them. Not your body. Your shadow. Walk where light cannot go.

Why can't you come to me?

Because I'm not in the mortal realm anymore, Drevik. I'm not in any realm you know. Her expression shifted. The darkness in you is a gift. But it's also a key. Use it wisely. Trust carefully. And whatever you do—

—don't let your father find you first.

Drevik woke with a gasp, the coordinates still burning in his mind. Beside him, Lexi stirred, her hand finding his automatically.

"What is it?" she mumbled.

He stared at the ceiling, his heart racing, his thoughts a chaos of questions and fear and desperate hope.

"Lexi." His voice came out hoarse. "I know where she is. I know where my mother is."

She was awake instantly, sitting up, her exhaustion forgotten. "What? How?"

He turned to look at her, and whatever she saw in his face made her go still.

"She's alive," he said. "She's been waiting for me. And I know how to find her."

The shadows in the corner of the room seemed to shift, to lean closer. For once, Drevik didn't fight them.

Soon, he promised. Soon I'll understand.

But whether he was speaking to the darkness or to himself, he couldn't say.
