The Best PS5 Game Deals for November 2026: Six Games Sitting at Their Price Floors

November 7, 2026 · 6 games tracked

I've been chasing hardware prices since the 90s, back when a 3dfx card cost a kidney and rebates came in the mail. The one rule that survived every decade: almost nothing is worth its launch price. Games are the purest example. A new PS5 release walks the same curve every time, $69.99 at launch, a slow leak of sales, then a floor price it keeps revisiting for the rest of its life. Your job as a buyer is simple. Recognize the floor, ignore everything above it.

November is floor season. Sony's PlayStation Store Black Friday sale opened November 4 with cuts up to 75% across thousands of PS5 and PS4 titles, running through November 30. Amazon started moving on physical game prices weeks before that. What follows is not a list of whatever looked impressive this morning. Every game here sits at or near its all-time tracked Amazon price as of the first week of November 2026, and I pulled the number history behind each pick.

How I Tracked These Prices (So You Can Track Yours)

Price history is free to everyone, which makes paying full price harder to excuse. I ran each listing through a price tracker and looked at three numbers: the all-time low, the average, and the current price. A deal only makes my list when the current price is close to the floor, not 40% above it with a countdown timer pretending otherwise.

One honest warning about six-day-old numbers. Game pricing on Amazon moves fast in November, and the buy box can bounce between Amazon direct and third-party sellers overnight. Borderlands 4, for example, scanned at $22.99 from Amazon direct during the first week of November but had already touched $18.99 in late October. Treat the prices here as a floor map, check the live listing before you buy, and never pay above a game's documented floor without a good reason.

🏆 Best Overall

Final Fantasy VII Rebirth (Exclusive Amazon Edition, PS5)

This is the floor story of the month. Rebirth launched at $69.99 in 2024 and held its value longer than almost any modern big-budget release. The Amazon-exclusive edition has since returned to $19.99 about once a month since June, and price trackers logged that floor again in late October. Between floor hits, the same listing settles at $29.99. Even the upper number is 57% off launch, which tells you how the market has repriced the whole trilogy.

You get the second chapter of the Final Fantasy VII remake project, a campaign that takes most people 100-plus hours with side content, plus an exclusive full-coverage controller skin with Cloud and Sephiroth art that the standard copy doesn't include. The honest case for buying at $29.99 instead of waiting for $19.99: Final Fantasy VII Revealation is due next year, and finishing Rebirth before the third game ships is worth a few dollars to a lot of people.

  • Amazon-exclusive edition includes the PS5 Cloud/Sephiroth controller skin at no price premium
  • Launch price was $69.99; average tracked price is about $39

💥 Best New-Release Deal

Borderlands 4 (PS5)

Sixteen months after release, the newest game on this shelf has already collapsed from $69.99 to the low twenties. A price scan in the first week of November put Amazon direct at $22.99, and the all-time floor is $18.99, set October 28. Either number is aggressive for a 2025 AAA release, and it's why this pick beats every other new-gen deal I tracked.

You're buying Gearbox's looter-shooter formula at scale: four-player co-op, billions of gun rolls, and an endgame loop that eats entire weekends. Downsides are real. The story is filler between shootouts, the endgame grind repeats itself, and the base PS5 takes a frame-rate hit in crowded co-op fights where the PS5 Pro doesn't. If you loved Borderlands 3, this is the same meal with more of everything. If the formula wore out on you, $22.99 won't fix that.

  • Amazon direct scanned at $22.99 in early November; all-time floor is $18.99 from October 28
  • 67% below the $69.99 launch price roughly 16 months after release
  • Full four-player co-op with cross-play built in
  • Loot tables and endgame content received post-launch tuning patches

Best for: Co-op groups who treat loot-shooters as a second job and already know what they're getting.

🧟 Best Horror Value

Resident Evil 2 (PS5)

Capcom's 2019 remake of the 1998 classic has reached the cheapest tier of its life. The PS5 physical copy scanned at $15.19 from Amazon direct in early November, the tracked floor is $12.99, and the game has spent most of 2026 in the $13 to $16 band. It's a native PS5 edition now, no disc-swap-in tricks.

This is the slow-burn pick of the list, the one I recommend to people who haven't touched a Resi game since the 90s. The RPD station's interlocking map, two full campaigns, and scripted dread hold up beautifully. The downsides after seven years: it's the shortest major remake in the series, second run replays the same map with tweaks, and some textures show their 2019 age up close on a 4K screen. At fifteen dollars, none of that matters. This also happens to be the game that recently passed Resident Evil 5 to become the franchise's best-selling entry, and the price curve reflects a publisher that knows the audience is basically infinite.

  • Scanned at $15.19 from Amazon direct in early November; floor is $12.99
  • Now the best-selling entry in the Resident Evil series
  • PS5 physical edition with faster loads than the 2019 PS4 release
  • Two campaigns, Leon and Claire, with the same map reworked for each

Best for: Horror fans who want the series' best remake-to-dollar ratio on the console.

👊 Best Fighter Value

Street Fighter 6 (PS5)

Fighting games age differently. Street Fighter 6 launched at $59.99 in 2023, and the PS5 physical edition has drifted down to a $17.29 scan from Amazon direct in early November, with a tracked floor of $15. For a game with an active online scene and a roster that keeps growing through season passes, that price is remarkable.

The three-year-old ecosystem is actually the value argument. Character balance is settled, the meta is documented in a thousand guides, and World Tour mode doubles as a long single-player RPG if ranked matchmaking intimidates you. The honest downsides: the tutorial barely prepares you for ranked ladder brutality, World Tour takes hours before the good content unlocks, and most of the DLC roster sits behind season pass purchases that add up fast if you catch the collecting bug.

  • Scanned at $17.29 from Amazon direct in early November; tracked floor is $15.00
  • 71% off the $59.99 launch price with a live competitive scene
  • World Tour single-player mode included on disc
  • Straightforward upgrade path if you later buy a season pass

Best for: Anyone who wants a fighter that still has a community, at a price that finally matches its age.

🐉 Best RPG Value

Like a Dragon: Infinite Wealth (PS5)

The quirkiest deal on the list needs an honest explanation. Amazon direct currently lists Infinite Wealth high while the stock churns, but third-party new sellers on the same listing have run it near $17 since September, which matches the listing's average price. The all-time Amazon direct floor is $13.50, set in December 2025. If the buy box shows a third-party seller at $17 or so, that's still a great number for a 90-plus-hour RPG.

Infinite Wealth is the biggest mainline Yakuza game ever built, with a Hawaii-set story that jumps the series into a turn-based sequel to Ichiban's first adventure. It's dense with minigames, karaoke, and side stories that parody everything they touch. Downsides for the skeptical: turn-based combat takes time to click if you grew up on real-time brawlers, the middle third sags as you commute across Hawaii, and third-party buy boxes mean checking your seller's rating before checkout. The savings pay for the caution.

  • Third-party new sellers run near $17; Amazon direct floor was $13.50 in December 2025
  • Mainline series entry with roughly 90 hours of campaign and side content
  • Dense minigame lineup: karaoke, Sujimon battles, and an island-management parody
  • Check the buy box seller before checkout; returns route through that seller

Best for: RPG players who clock value in hours-per-dollar and don't mind verifying a seller.

🩸 Best Action-Horror Sequel

Resident Evil 4 (PS5)

The 2023 remake of Leon's Spanish vacation rounds out the Capcom corner of this shelf. Amazon has recently tracked the PS5 physical edition at $15.99, close to its all-time floor of $15, against a current shelf price of $29.99. Capcom keeps these remakes in the $15 to $16 band about every couple of months, so the deal's return is reliable.

Where RE2 is dread, RE4 is momentum. The parry-and-knife counter system turned the 2005 classic into something closer to an action brawler with jump scares, and the village fights remain some of the best set pieces Capcom has ever shipped. Genuine downsides: side content is thinner than RE2's campaigns, the pacing front-loads its best hour, and mastering the parry timing on hardcore difficulty is a genuine skill wall. If you only want one Resi remake, decide between slow dread and loud adrenaline, then buy accordingly. If you can't decide, both together still cost less than one launch-era game.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do PS5 games get cheaper on Black Friday itself, or is the best price already out?

For most of the games on this shelf, the floor price already exists. Borderlands 4 dipped to $18.99 on October 28, and Street Fighter 6 has traded as low as $15. Sony's digital Black Friday sale (November 4 through 30, with a refresh on November 16 and some offers expiring November 20) tends to match those October floors rather than beat them. If a game is at or near its tracked floor in early November, waiting for Thanksgiving week rarely saves you money.

Should I wait for Final Fantasy VII Rebirth to hit $19.99 again?

You can, and it works, because the deal keeps coming back. The Amazon Edition has returned to $19.99 roughly monthly since June 2026 and was spotted at that price again in late October. But waiting is a gamble with no schedule: between floor hits, the price sits at $29.99. Both numbers are far below the $69.99 launch price, so if you want the game this month, $29.99 is still a reasonable entry. For the price-alert type of buyer, set the alert at $19.99 and ignore the game until the email arrives.

Is buying a PS5 game from a third-party Amazon seller safe?

Usually, yes, with two checks. Confirm the listing says New and reads as factory sealed, and look at the seller rating history before clicking. Amazon's standard 30-day return window still applies to third-party orders, though returns route through the seller instead of Amazon itself. The Like a Dragon: Infinite Wealth deal in this guide typically comes through a marketplace seller priced near $17 while Amazon direct sits higher, so this matters most for that pick.

Physical or digital PS5 game deals this Black Friday?

Digital wins on headline discounts during the PlayStation Store sale, with cuts up to 75% across thousands of titles. Physical wins on everything else: floor prices like the ones tracked in this guide, resale or trade-in value later, and no storage juggling on the console. If you're buying one of the six games here anyway, the physical deals are within a few dollars of the digital prices and you keep a disc.

What's different about the Final Fantasy VII Rebirth Amazon Edition?

Only the extras on the outside. The Amazon Exclusive Edition bundles a full-coverage PS5 controller skin with Cloud and Sephiroth artwork; the game data itself is identical to the standard retail copy. Historically, this edition is also the cheapest Rebirth listing Amazon tracks, so you're not paying a premium for the skin.

How do I know if a game deal will come back if I miss it?

Check the price history before you buy anything. A free tracker like camelcamelcamel shows the lowest, highest, and average prices for a listing going back years. If a current sale price is at or close to the all-time low, buy it. If it's floating above the floor, set an alert at the floor price and walk away. Every game in this guide has a documented floor it returns to, which is the whole reason buying in November 2026 beats buying in September.
