Late Summer Video Game Deals 2026: Seven Picks From a Price-Per-Performance Nerd

August 29, 2026 · 7 products reviewed · By Marcus Chen

I've been hunting game deals since the days of Circuit City clearance racks and $29.99 Best Buy misprice posts on FatWallet. Back then, the rule was simple: if a game dropped below $20, you bought it sight unseen. That instinct is still wired into me, but the landscape has shifted. Sales come and go faster. Amazon reprices games multiple times a day. And Sony just hiked the PS5 by a hundred bucks because the economy is, well, the economy.

So here's the deal. I went through the current crop of game discounts on Amazon and picked seven that actually move the needle on price-per-performance. No $5-off-a-$70-game filler. No "deals" that are just MSRP dressed up with a fake list price. Every game on this list is selling for meaningfully less than its original price, and every one of them is worth owning at the sale price, even if some have real downsides I'll get into.

Let's get into it.

Let me be blunt: getting a FromSoftware game for $15 is absurd. Nightreign takes the combat DNA of Elden Ring and stuffs it into a three-day roguelite cycle where you and up to two friends sprint across a shrinking map, fight minibosses, level up, and then face a night boss that will absolutely flatten you the first dozen times. It's fast, it's aggressive, and it forces you to play nothing like a traditional Souls game.

The downside is real, though. If you're a solo player who doesn't want to matchmake with strangers, the experience is significantly harder. The game was designed around co-op, and while you can play alone, it feels like doing a group project by yourself. The story is also thinner than Elden Ring proper. There's lore, but you won't find the same sprawling narrative tapestry.

At $15, those complaints barely register. You're paying lunch money for a game that will eat 40+ hours of your time if you let it. This is the single best price-per-performance deal on this list — and it's not close.

MGS Delta is the rare remake that doesn't feel like a cynical cash grab. Konami actually put effort into this one: the jungle environments are dense and beautiful in UE5, the CQC system feels responsive, and the voice acting from the original is intact. If you played MGS3 on PS2 and want to experience it again without the janky 2004 camera, this is your chance.

But I won't pretend it's perfect. The game still has some of that old Kojima pacing that modern players will find glacial. Codec calls go on forever. The ladder sequence is still in there. And at 57% off, you're paying $30 for a 15-hour game with limited replay value unless you're a speedrun obsessive. That's not bad. It's just not the same dollar-per-hour proposition as Nightreign.

Still, for the nostalgia hit alone, $30 is fair. Just go in knowing that some things from 2004 are better left as memories.

Requiem is the biggest name on this list, and at $54 it's the smallest discount. Let's get that out of the way. Twenty-three percent off a $70 game is not the kind of deal that makes you cancel weekend plans. But Resident Evil games hold their value stubbornly. RE4 Remake still hovers around $30 two years later, so seeing Requiem dip below $55 this early in its lifecycle is notable.

The game itself delivers. Dual campaigns with two distinct playstyles: Grace's survival horror and Leon's action set pieces, giving you two games in one. The RE ENGINE makes every environment drip with atmosphere. Sweat droplets on terrified faces. Real-time lighting that makes you paranoid about every shadow. It's the scariest mainstream game I've played in years.

The downside? Some of the Leon sections feel like a retread of RE4's greatest hits without the novelty. And the game is linear — very little of the exploration or puzzle-solving that defined the series' golden era. You're paying $54 for a 12-15 hour rollercoaster. A good rollercoaster, but still.

If you're a horror fan who's been waiting for this one to dip, now's a reasonable time. If you're price-sensitive, wait for Black Friday. It'll hit $40 by then.

Ragnarök at $40 is one of those "why don't you own this yet?" prices. This game launched at $70 in late 2022, and it's been circling the $40 mark for a while now. If you played the 2018 reboot and somehow didn't get around to the sequel, this is your sign.

The combat is deeper, the realms are bigger, and Atreus's sections add genuine variety rather than feeling like filler. The story sticks the landing, which is more than I can say for a lot of Norse mythology narratives that trip over their own foreshadowing.

What keeps this from a higher ranking is simple: it's a sequel that plays it safe. The core loop of axe-throw-chain-pull-runic is largely the same. Some of the puzzle design feels recycled. And the pacing sags in the middle when you're doing yet another "find the MacGuffin in the realm" quest. At $40, none of that is a dealbreaker. It's still one of the best PS5 exclusives. But go in expecting refinement, not revolution.

Here's the wildcard pick. Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 is not a household name, and that's exactly why it's on this list. This turn-based RPG from a small French studio punches way above its weight class. The art direction, inspired by Belle Époque paintings and sculpture, looks like nothing else on the market. Every screenshot could be a print on your wall.

The combat system layers real-time QTE inputs on top of traditional turn-based strategy, which keeps fights engaging without demanding fighting-game reflexes. It's the kind of system that makes you lean forward in your chair during boss fights.

Downsides: the story takes about three hours to find its footing, and some of the side quests feel like filler content from a game twice its size. The studio is small, and it shows in the occasional rough edge: a misaligned lip sync here, a texture pop-in there. But at $37-40, you're getting a 30-hour RPG with genuine artistic ambition for less than the cost of a new AAA release's in-game currency pack.

Ten dollars. That's what Lost Soul Aside costs right now on Amazon. A game that launched at $70 less than a year ago is sitting at 86% off, and I'll be honest: that kind of price drop usually means one of two things. Either the publisher is clearing inventory, or the game didn't find its audience. In this case, it's the latter.

Lost Soul Aside is a perfectly competent character action game with some genuinely flashy combat. The weapon-switching system lets you chain different attack types mid-combo, and when you're in the zone, it looks like a playable anime fight scene. The China Hero Project pedigree means it got real Sony backing during development.

The problem is that "perfectly competent" is a tough sell in a year with Elden Ring Nightreign, Clair Obscur, and God of War Ragnarok all fighting for your time. The story is forgettable. The world design is linear to a fault. And the combat, while stylish, doesn't have the depth of a Devil May Cry or the weight of a Souls game. It's a solid 7/10 that plays like a proof of concept for a better sequel.

But at $10? Who cares. That's impulse-buy territory. You could spend $10 on worse things at a gas station. If you enjoy stylish action games and have a weekend to burn, Lost Soul Aside delivers more entertainment per dollar than anything else on this list except Nightreign.

The camera accessory is sold separately. And the 256GB storage fills up fast when individual games can be 15-20GB. Budget another $25-50 for a microSD Express card if you plan to go digital.

Still, if you're buying a Switch 2 anyway, the bundle is the way to go. You were going to buy Mario Kart regardless. Might as well save a few bucks.

How I Pick These Deals

I've been building PCs and tracking game prices since the 90s. Back then, you'd wait for the Sunday circular, drive to the store, and hope they hadn't sold out of the $9.99 clearance copy by noon. Now the deals move in real time, and most "deal roundups" you see online are either outdated by the time you read them or padded with games that were never really discounted in the first place.

Here's my process: I track price histories on CamelCamelCamel and similar tools. I verify current Amazon prices before listing them. I exclude anything where the "deal" is just a third-party seller listing that's $2 below MSRP. And I don't recommend anything I wouldn't spend my own money on at the listed price.

That last part matters. Lost Soul Aside at $10? Easy recommendation even with its flaws. Resident Evil Requiem at $54? I'd only bite if you're a horror fan who's been waiting. The price-per-hour math doesn't work in its favor the way it does for Nightreign or Ragnarök.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are these game prices still available right now?

Prices were verified in late August 2026. Amazon changes game prices constantly, sometimes several times a day. The $15 Elden Ring Nightreign deal was confirmed live at time of writing, but it could jump back to $40 or higher at any point. If a price looks good to you, pull the trigger. Don't wait for a better deal that may never come.

Should I buy physical or digital game copies?

Physical copies almost always win on price during sales, and you can resell them later. Digital locks you into a single ecosystem with no resale value. The exception is Nintendo Switch 2, where physical cartridges sometimes cost more than digital. For PS5 games on this list, physical is the clear value play.

Is Elden Ring Nightreign worth buying if I never played the original Elden Ring?

Yes. Nightreign is a standalone game, not DLC. It's a co-op roguelite spinoff set in the Elden Ring universe, but you don't need to have played the original. At $15, it's one of the cheapest entry points into FromSoftware's world, and the three-player co-op makes it more approachable than the notoriously difficult main game.

Why is the PS5 Slim more expensive now than at launch?

Sony raised console prices globally in April 2026, blaming economic pressures. The PS5 Slim disc edition went from $449 to $549 at retail. Some Amazon listings still show the old price, but that could change. If you see a PS5 Slim at $499 or below, that's actually a deal in the current market.

Are Switch 2 games really worth $70 to $80?

Some are, some aren't. First-party Nintendo titles like Mario Kart World and Pokémon Pokopia deliver hundreds of hours of content. But $80 for a racing game stings no matter how you slice it. If you're buying a Switch 2, look for bundle deals that include a game. That's where the real savings are.

What's the best single deal on this list?

Elden Ring Nightreign at $15 offers more hours of gameplay per dollar than anything else here. It's a full co-op action game from one of the best studios in the business, priced like a clearance rack afterthought. Grab that one first if you own a PS5.
