Core idea

The "Unethical Slut" is not a person who has sex with many people, but a person who uses others as tools to satisfy their own ego. This chapter serves as a diagnostic tool to identify when your pursuit of freedom has devolved into predatory or self-serving behavior. It highlights the moment you stop seeing a partner as a human being with their own agency and start seeing them as an object to be acquired, a trophy to be displayed, or a resource to be mined for validation. The transition from ethical to unethical occurs when the "hunt" becomes more important than the connection and when honesty is used as a shield to bypass true consent.

Key concepts and terms

  • The Big-Game Hunt: A mindset where sexual conquest is treated as a sport or a challenge. In this mode, the goal is not intimacy, but the successful "taking" of someone, often targeting those who are unaware of the true nature of the interaction.
  • Self-Esteem Theft: A parasitic psychological mechanism where you attempt to repair your own sagging self-worth by consuming the attention or validation of others. Because this is a deficit-based strategy, it requires an escalating frequency of conquests to achieve the same temporary "high," leading to a cycle of dissatisfaction.
  • Set Collecting / Trophy Fucking: The practice of treating partners as items in a collection. This involves seeking out specific "types" or "ranks" to complete a perceived set (e.g., the "family set" example in the text) or to prove your status.
  • The Hierarchy of Shallow Values: A ranking system used to judge partners based on external, non-relational metrics such as youth, physical fitness, wealth, or social status. In this framework, a partner's value is derived from how much they "upgrade" your own perceived social standing.
  • Disclosure as a Substitute for Consent: A critical failure mode where you believe that simply stating "I am polyamorous" or "I am an open person" absolves you of the responsibility to ensure the other person is actually enthusiastic and informed. You use "honesty" about your lifestyle to mask the coercion of someone who did not agree to those terms.

Techniques worth drilling

  1. The Agency Audit
  • Step 1: Identify a recent sexual or romantic encounter.
  • Step 2: Strip away the physical sensations and the "story" you told yourself.
  • Step 3: Ask: "If this person had a different set of desires, would I still be here, or would I be looking for the next 'win'?"
  • Step 4: If the answer is that you would be looking for the next win, categorize the encounter as "Instrumental" (using them) rather than "Relational" (connecting with them).
  1. The Value Deconstruction
  • Step 1: List the top three qualities that attracted you to your last three partners.
  • Step 2: Analyze those qualities. Are they internal traits (kindness, intellect, humor) or external metrics (fitness, wealth, social clout)?
  • Step 3: If the list is dominated by external metrics, recognize that you are practicing "Trophy Fucking."
  • Step 4: Intentionally seek out an interaction where the primary draw is a non-quantifiable, internal human trait to break the hierarchy.
  1. The Consent Verification Loop
  • Step 1: After disclosing your relationship structure (e.g., "I am poly"), pause for a minimum of 24 hours before assuming agreement.
  • Step 2: Ask a direct, follow-up question: "Does this structure work for you, or does it feel like a pressure to conform?"
  • Step 3: Watch for "compliance" vs. "enthusiasm." If they are agreeing just to keep you around, you are using disclosure as a tool for coercion.
  • Step 4: If you detect hesitation, withdraw the proposal and allow them the space to say "no" without losing access to you.

Practice drill

The Annual Retrospective Audit

This week, sit in a private space with a notebook and a pen. You must perform a retrospective audit of your sexual and romantic encounters from the last 12 months. Do not attempt to justify your actions or frame them in a way that makes you look "evolved." You are looking for patterns of exploitation.

Answer these three questions with brutal, unadorned honesty:

  1. Did I want this person, or did I want the proof? (Did the attraction stem from a desire to know them, or was the encounter a way to prove to yourself that you are still desirable, powerful, or "good" at the game?)
  2. Would they recognize my description of what happened? (If you told the partner exactly how you viewed them during the encounter—as a trophy, a way to boost your ego, or a notch in a belt—would they feel respected or used?)
  3. What did I do when a "no" was available? (Identify moments where a partner expressed hesitation, boundary-setting, or lack of interest. Did you respect the boundary, or did you use charm, persistence, or the "honesty" of your polyamory to navigate around it?)

Rule of the Drill: If you find yourself writing "But I told them the truth, so it's fine," stop. You are failing the drill. The drill is not about your level of disclosure; it is about your level of objectification.

Critiques and caveats

  • The Risk of Hyper-Self-Criticism: There is a danger in this diagnostic that you may begin to pathologize normal sexual desire or the natural ego-boost that comes from attraction. The goal is to identify instrumental use of others, not to eliminate the pleasure of being desired.
  • The "Honesty" Trap: Do not mistake "being transparent about my kinks/poly/openness" for "being ethical." You can be 100% honest about your intentions and still be using those intentions to manipulate others into a dynamic they cannot sustain.
  • Safety Note: If you identify patterns of "The Big-Game Hunt," the remedy is not just self-reflection but a fundamental change in how you initiate contact. If you cannot interact without a "score" in mind, you should pause active pursuit until you can re-establish a person-centered framework.

Reading notes

This chapter serves as the "dark mirror" to the rest of the book. While other chapters focus on building communication and structure, "The Unethical Slut" provides the necessary boundary by defining what the ethical framework is not. It prevents the "freedom" of polyamory from being used as a psychological loophole for predatory behavior.
